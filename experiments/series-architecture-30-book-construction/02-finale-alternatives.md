# 02 — Finale alternatives: three ways to coordinate sustained return labour

Status: **working / noncanonical.** Three mechanisms for the Book 30 finale. None is the author's choice. The author decides whether the shared accounting binds, and whether any threshold or trigger exists at all. The mechanisms rest on the Arc IV exports in [01](01-causal-construction.md).

---

## A — Noncoercive coordination by mutual dependence

**Principle.** Care is counted, but nothing is owed. Draw no longer depends on hands. Participation holds because each household's own water depends on its neighbours' labour, and the schedule that follows the balance makes that dependence visible in each household's own release.

**Mechanisms.**
1. **Gate dependence.** Each practice's release depends on the Gate schedule the other practice agrees to. A terrace that stops returning labour to the shared channels loses its own release at the next thaw. Nobody issues a penalty. The schedule follows the balance reading, which anyone can check, so a terrace that stops returning labour shows in its own release at the next thaw.
2. **Pair-hands.** Each household is paired with a neighbour across the Gate. In a bad season the partner covers the missing labour. It is repaid in kind, not as a recorded debt. The tally records pairing as care, which is the gift reading applied to daily work.
3. **Capability spread.** The lineage's Special Draw Right from Arc IV is conditional on training apprentices. Channel skill spreads out of the lineage.
4. **Visibility.** The public tally, the Balance Stones and the Gate schedule let everyone see the balance and each household's care.
5. **A care floor.** No household loses draw for lacking hands. This reverses Arc IV's hands-to-draw conversion. The floor is funded by the lineage's Right, and the Gate Table administers it. The Table has no authority over shares or the record.
6. **An institution with no record authority.** The Gate Table, with rotating seats, sets the schedule. Disputes about meaning go to the Reading Hall.

**How it differs from the Balance Stones.** The Stones inform everyone equally and bind no one. Mechanisms 1 and 2 turn the same information into dependence. A household's water now depends on a particular neighbour's labour. The information does not change; the structure of who depends on whom does.

**Credible failure modes.**
- **Correlated shock.** The Long Return hits every terrace at once. Pair-hands fail when no partner has spare hands, and the Gate cannot cover the shortfall.
- **Free-riding under visibility.** Visibility without enforcement lets a household see its neighbours' gaps and wait.
- **Patronage.** A lineage partner covers a pair, and dependence becomes deference.
- **Craft withdrawal.** Lineage crews withdraw their skill in protest. The rebuild window is short, so the withdrawal is effective.
- **Surveillance.** The tally is read as stock rather than flow. The Hall's record returns in a new form.
- **The lowland gap.** Compact draw carries no hands, and A has no lever on it. The lowland share-plot settlement draws water without labour. A either accepts unhanded lowland draw as a cost or fails there.
- **The declined share.** A household that exits at the lowest terrace leaves that terrace's channel unrepaired when the pulse comes.

**Effect on the governing variable.** Draw is decoupled from hands, so the Arc IV conversion is reversed. Labour is still asked for, but it is no longer owed.

**Cost.** High relational labour: pairing, shared meals, reading the tally. Low institutional cost. The tally's ambiguity persists, and Hesper's generation must choose the practice.

## B — Allotted, binding rota under a bounded authority

**Principle.** Obligation is explicit, time-limited and distributed by a published rule. Binding is confined to the rebuild window and to the Gate. The record stays with the Reading Hall.

**What binds.**
1. **The Long Rota.** For the rebuild window, hands are allocated per share by a published formula: proportional to draw, with the floor. A hand-light household receives fewer hands, not zero draw.
2. **The Gate schedule.** The Table's schedule binds the water at the Gate for every practice, including the gift practice. It binds the water, not the labour: the gift practice's labour stays uncounted.
3. **The Compact's hands in kind.** Purchased draw owes hands. The Compact pays grain-labour into the rebuild. This settles its claim in a form the valley can count, and it is the only mechanism that reaches lowland draw.

**What does not bind.** A household's internal practice. The interpretation of the record, which belongs to the Reading Hall. The three readings of the Stones.

**Legitimate authority.** The Gate Table, with its seats redrawn: all by lot from households, rather than the Arc IV mix of one seat for each practice and one seat by lot. Terms last one cycle, and the same house cannot serve consecutive terms. The Table may allocate hands for the window and set the schedule. It may not amend shares, the record or the readings. Its allocation expires at the end of the cycle unless the terraces ratify a renewal.

**Distribution of obligation.** Hands follow draw, with the floor. Channel priority is set publicly: the first repairs go to channels that serve hand-light households. This prevents the valley from silently sacrificing its lowest channel. It also makes the routing choice visible, so the author's flood decision is made in the open.

**What prevents capture.** Lot seats. One-cycle terms, with no consecutive service for the same house. A sunset on allocations. A public record kept separately from the schedule. The Compact has no seat.

**Freedom, fairness and feasibility.**
- **Freedom.** Refusal is allowed at a cost: a household forfeits the window's draw, not its share.
- **Fairness.** The floor means a hand-light household does not lose water for lacking hands.
- **Feasibility.** The rota must deliver the labour before the pulse arrives. If it under-delivers, the Table can hold water upstream to lessen the pulse. That is a real burden on the high terraces, shared openly.

**New costs and pressures.**
- The rota is the valley's first binding public duty since the Hall. Readers will hear the Hall in it.
- Lot-drawn officials did not choose the job.
- The Compact may refuse grain-labour, and then its fate is open.
- The Table holds schedule power. Capture risk remains, although the design limits it.
- Administration has a real cost in a valley that runs on meals and custom.
- A deficit owed to the rebuild re-admits the debt reading for one window.

**Effect on the governing variable.** Inside the rebuild window draw does not depend on hands. Hands are owed in proportion to draw, and a shortfall becomes a deficit to be repaid, not a cut in water. Arc IV's conversion is suspended for the window, not repealed. The deficit re-admits the debt reading for one window, which is a cost of B.

**Centralised variant (not developed separately).** The Council's option in the original, a single measured Turn, is B without lot seats, without sunset and without the separation of record. The construction flags it as the variant that most risks recreating custody.

## C — Ratified trigger (third possibility)

**Principle.** The valley decides the rule before the threshold is crossed. The rule stays dormant in normal seasons and activates only when a published threshold is crossed.

**Mechanism.** In Books 27–29, during Arc V's middle, the valley ratifies one instrument with four parts.
1. **Normal seasons:** Alternative A's arrangement. Pair-hands, the Gate schedule, the floor, and no binding.
2. **The threshold:** a level on the Balance Stones gauge, set and published in advance. The Reading Hall reads it.
3. **Activated rule:** when the threshold is crossed, B's allotted rota applies for a fixed window, with lot seats and sunset.
4. **The Compact clause:** grain-labour may be included in the same instrument. **[Open]**

**Why it is an improvement.** It answers A's correlated-shock failure, because the rule for the pulse exists before the threshold is crossed. It reduces B's standing-power risk: binding is dormant until a public threshold is crossed, it expires, and the Table cannot choose when it applies. It also answers the "crisis binds" critique. The valley makes the decision before the crisis, not during it. The crisis only triggers a rule the valley already chose.

**Costs.**
- The threshold becomes an authority in itself. The surveyor's reading decides whether the rule applies.
- Ratification in calm years is hard. Few people attend to a rule for a pulse that has not come yet.
- The rule may be stale by the time the cycle arrives.
- A pulse below the threshold still meets A's failure modes.
- C turns the Stones from informing to triggering. That changes the Book 23 function ("informs but does not bind") unless the valley ratifies the change. The author must accept it.

**Failure modes.**
- The threshold is misread, or the gauge is disputed.
- Ratification fails. The valley then faces the Long Return with A alone, and A's correlated-shock failure applies.
- A dispute about whether the threshold has been crossed becomes a legitimacy crisis at the worst moment.
- Activation by the Reading Hall's reading is unchecked. If publishing the reading activates the rule, reading and binding join, which is the Hall's old combination. Whether activation is automatic or confirmed by the Table is an author choice.

**Narrative consequence.** Book 30 becomes a test of prior civic choice rather than a single crisis. The drama moves to Books 27–29, into the ratification debate and the dispute over the gauge.

## Comparison

| Criterion | A — Noncoercive | B — Allotted binding | C — Ratified trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Causal plausibility | Plausible for ordinary seasons. Weak against a correlated pulse. | Plausible. The rota can meet the rebuild window if hands are there. | Plausible if ratified. Threshold timing is a physical question. |
| Relationship to established history | Extends the gift reading and pair-care. Repeats the Book 15 limit upstream. | Rebalances Arc IV's conversion. Reuses the Turn and the Table. | Joins the gift practice and the Turn, with a dormant rule. |
| Consequences for the Source | Depends on the Gate schedule. Fails if all terraces are short together. | Holds the rebuild window. Shifts the upstream burden to the high terraces. | Like A in normal seasons and like B once the threshold is crossed. |
| Distribution of responsibility | Spread across pairs. Uneven if pairs are unequal. | Set by formula and lot. Explicit. | Set in advance by ratification. Explicit once activated. |
| Independent agency | Strongest. Each household and practice keeps its agency. | Reduced in the window. Refusal is possible at a cost. | Agency is exercised at ratification and reduced only at the trigger. |
| Emotional and thematic payoff | Warmth and earned hope. The cost is shared labour. | Duty, fairness and visible cost. Less warmth. | Dread and relief. The payoff is the prior choice, not a single moment. |
| Opportunities for dramatic conflict | Pair breakdown. Free-riding. Patronage. | Refusals. Lot-seat injustice. Compact refusal. | The ratification debate. The threshold dispute. |
| Cost of maintaining | High relational labour. Low institutional cost. | Administrative cost and a real rota. | Both, concentrated at ratification. |
| Risk of recreating earlier failures | Voluntary return fails upstream and in the lowlands, as in Book 15. | Risk of recreating custody through schedule power, limited by lot seats and sunset. | Crisis-as-justification, and a threshold that becomes authority. |
| Capacity for future development | Pairing, apprenticeship and surveillance stories. | Authority, capture and legitimacy stories. | Time, ratification and the reading of gauges. |

## Working direction for testing (agent-proposed, not an author decision)

The agent nominates **C** for further testing. Reasons:
- It addresses the two failures this construction finds in the others: A's correlated shock and B's standing power.
- Its binding is decided by the valley before the crisis rather than during it. That is the strongest available answer to the charge that the crisis does the binding.

This is a proposal to test, not a finding. A fresh reviewer should try to break C. The most likely break is the threshold: if the gauge is the authority, C has moved the capture risk into the instrument.
