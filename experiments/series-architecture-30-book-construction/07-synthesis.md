## 1. Is Arc IV now causally sufficient to support the later Series?

**Sufficient as causal scaffolding. Not sufficient as drama. Two of its links depend on construction assumptions the author must accept or reject.**

- **Conflict A (the Counted Rota)** derives from established history: purchased draw carries no hands (Book 6), few-hands families cannot meet a share (Book 15), maintenance skill sits with lineages (Books 9–11), and the tally counts care (Book 15). The Lowfen break and the Special Draw Right are construction additions, labelled as such. Its export, that hands set part of draw from Book 20, is the counting principle Book 30 must keep, reverse, or floor.
- **Conflict B (Two Practices, One Gate)** is weaker. It derives by analogy from the Book 15 trial, and it depends on a construction addition: the Hollow Gate, and the reading that the Book 15 surfeit was the release the trial's schedule carried. The original names the cost, not the mechanism. The review found the first version's physics wrong (a dry-spring release was described as raising the store). The corrected version holds: release draws the high cistern down.
- **Book 23** now separates the three kinds of inheritance and gives each a mechanism: genuine but seasonal abundance (held water and sealed leaks), abundance drawn unevenly toward the best-handed houses (the conversion), and a belief that abundance needs no work (a cohort that came to adolescence in a full cistern). The belief's dating was wrong in the first version and is now corrected.
- **Book 30** needs both exports. The pulse needs channel labour before its date, which is Conflict A's hands, and a Gate routing that decides which channel takes the surge, which is Conflict B's schedule. The finale therefore emerges from Arc IV's exports, not from Arc V's ideals.

## 2. Are the Book 30 alternatives materially different and credible?

**Yes, materially. Credible at the level of mechanism, with costs stated. Not yet tested in prose.**

They differ on the governing variable, which is the brief's own question: who gives return labour, and who is counted for it.

- **A** counts care and owes nothing. Draw is decoupled from hands, which reverses Arc IV's conversion. Its credible failure modes are serious: a correlated pulse that leaves no pair with spare hands, and lowland draw that carries no hands and that A has no lever on.
- **B** keeps hands owed in proportion to draw, but only inside a bounded window, floor-protected, with lot-drawn seats and a sunset. It binds the water at the Gate, not the labour. Its costs are real: it is the valley's first binding public duty since the Hall, a deficit owed to the rebuild re-admits the debt reading for one window, and its Table holds schedule power.
- **C** is dormant until a published threshold is crossed, and the valley ratifies the rule in Books 27–29. It answers A's correlated-shock failure and B's standing-power risk. The reviews found three weaknesses in it, all now stated as costs: the Stones change their Book 23 function from informing to triggering; a threshold reading may activate binding without a check; and "before the pulse is visible" was wrong and is now "before the threshold is crossed".

The agent nominates **C** for further testing. That is not an author decision, and the nomination is not supported by any test beyond the review, which tried to break it and did not.

## 4. Did the agent make changes at the appropriate architectural levels?

**Mostly, with two exceptions the author should see.**

- **Arc IV** is a Multi-Book change, and it lives in the phase, the arc's nodes and its conflicts. That is the right level.
- **The finale** is a Whole-Series change. It lives in Book 30's phase, the three alternatives as nodes, and the open questions. That is also right.
- **Corrections** were made at the level of the field they lived in: a summary sentence, a phase narrative, a link's function, an open question. None rewrote the original files, which stay as the record.
- **H1** is a focused, scripted delta. Each replaced field is asserted to exist, and the review's findings were applied to the delta, not to a second full candidate.

The two exceptions:
1. **Provenance.** The agent had labelled three emotion targets `author_declared`, which is an authority field. The correction to `agent_proposed` is right for the eliciting conditions and the emotion mapping. But the experience words are the author's, and the schema cannot record both. The reviewer proposed reverting it; this record kept the correction, and it is recorded as a representation limit.
2. **The Gate Table and the Hollow Gate** are institutional and physical additions at Arc IV, made without a Series-level decision. They are labelled as construction additions. The author may reject them.

The agent also kept the candidate's summary labelled as a working construction, not author-approved, and the H1 summary labelled as hypothetical. The product copies the summary into the review rationale, so those labels are a weak authority mechanism. That is recorded as a product limit.

**Fictional design** (the author's to accept, change or reject):

- The Special Draw Right, and whether it is revocable.

**Agent reasoning** (errors the construction made and the review caught):
- The dry-spring physics were wrong. The release was described as raising stored water.
- The Brightwater cohort was "born into" a state that postdates their birth.
- B was described both as binding the gift practice (the closed option) and as letting hands set draw (the opposite of its rule).
- A's dependence was called physics when a schedule sets it, and its floor was funded by a body with no authority over shares.
- The first candidate contradicted itself across artifacts (the Book 1 reading, the Book 30 payoff, the revocability), which is the cross-artifact consistency failure PR #552 described.

**Product and representation** (not fixed by changing the fiction):
- The preserved check compares identifiers only. A blind host run's stale-item repair was therefore never checked by the product.
- The blind run's response was refused for one enum (a genre-contract role). That is a correct structural refusal, and it also shows that closed vocabularies can block an otherwise usable response.
- The schema has no institution category and no Book node. Book functions are free text in `scope` and in phase narratives.
- Link functions are capped at 120 characters, so causal reasons are compressed into labels.
- Provenance cannot record a mixed authority: an author's experience with an agent's mapping.
- The summary is the only place for hypothetical status, and the product copies it into the review rationale.
- The bridge has no `apply`.
- Open questions are capped at sixteen, so a new question must merge with an old one.

## 6. What is the single strongest remaining obstacle to long-form narrative architecture?

**Keeping the meaning of an unchanged element consistent under a revision.** Every serious error in this exercise came from that: the stale grief and bittersweet targets in PR #552, the seizure baseline, the remnant that survived its own revision, the Book 1 reading that two artifacts disagreed about, and the physics that contradicted a later claim. The product checks structure and identity, so it cannot see any of these. The only check that caught them was a reviewer reading the text.

The blind run is the most direct evidence. A fresh agent, told to check every preserved element semantically, repaired the two named items. In the same run it reintroduced the seizure baseline and the restored-monopoly phrase, and its response was refused structurally. One run is not a reliability estimate. But it shows the gap clearly: semantic consistency under revision is currently an agent's unreliable self-check, backed by a product check that does not look at meaning.

## 7. Is another product improvement warranted, or should the current architecture remain unchanged?

**Not yet, as product code. The next step, if the author wants it, is evidence.**

The strongest candidate is a semantic review capability, the one PR #552 proposed. This exercise does not justify building it yet, for three reasons:
- The failure it would address was detected here by reading, and by one blind run that succeeded partially.
- The cheapest test that could change the decision is a controlled repetition: several fresh agents, with and without the explicit preserved-check paragraph, on the same baseline and revision, scored against a fixed list of known stale items. That test costs little and would say whether the instruction is reliable. Building machinery before it runs would be guessing.
- The bridge's missing `apply` is a convenience, and it does not change the fiction.

**Recommendation.** Leave the product unchanged. Leave this architecture unchanged as a product matter: it is working, noncanonical, and not author-approved. If the author wants to continue, the next responsibilities are the author's decisions in [02](02-finale-alternatives.md) and the repeated trial above. Neither is started here, and no new Books or prose are written.
