# Causal adequacy review (fresh context)

You are an independent reviewer. Assess the causal adequacy of a working, noncanonical long-form narrative architecture for a 30-Book epic fantasy series, *The Spring Ledger*. You are not asked for literary-quality scores, prose, or an author decision.

## Scope limits
- Read only the files in this packet folder. Do not read the repository, other folders, git history, or the web.
- Text marked [WITHHELD] is unavailable. Do not speculate about it.
- References to documents not in this packet (for example "05") are unavailable.
- The fixed author brief is in 01-original-architecture.md §1 and §3. Engines and arcs are in §3 and §4.

## Questions (answer each one)
1. Do Arc IV's two conflicts (03 §2 and §3) follow from established history? For each link in each chain, quote the earlier text it depends on. Mark each link **Derived** (quoted antecedent), **Asserted** (no quoted antecedent), or **Contradicted** (the packet's text says otherwise).
2. Do the two conflicts produce different kinds of pressure? Do they change the distribution of labour, responsibility, water, legitimacy, or social relationships?
3. Does Book 23 (03 §5) distinguish genuine abundance, unevenly distributed abundance, and a belief that abundance needs no work? Does each have a causal source in the packet?
4. Does each finale mechanism (04: A, B, C) change the distribution or the reliability of maintenance? Assess each separately.
5. Does any alternative merely recreate the original Stone Hall monopoly, or the Council's centralised option? Test each alternative, and say which test you applied.
6. Compare 05-working-candidate.json with 02-original-candidate.json. List every change the Arc IV and Book 30 construction does not need, and every change that contradicts established text. Quote both versions.
7. Find any internal contradiction within the new construction (03, 04, 05), or between it and the original text (01, 02). Quote both sides.

## Rules
- Do not give a preferred verdict among A, B and C, and do not recommend an author decision.
- Quote the text for every claim. Do not say something is missing until you have checked the text that would contain it.
- Keep assessment separate from repair. Put any suggested repairs only in a final section titled "Optional repair notes", labelled as suggestions.
- Do not rewrite the record.

## Report format
Start with "# Causal adequacy report (verbatim)". Use headings for questions 1–7. End with a Findings table: id | severity (high/medium/low) | claim | quotes | classification (causal gap / contradiction / unnecessary change / overstatement / unsupported) | owner level (Series / Multi-Book / Arc / Book / Scene). Keep the report under 2000 words.
