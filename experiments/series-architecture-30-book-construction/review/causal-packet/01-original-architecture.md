## 1. Series identity

**Working title:** *The Spring Ledger*. **Scale:** a 30-Book epic; Books span roughly 1.5 years each, so the story covers about 45 years. **Scope:** local (terraces) → valley → lowland → civilizational only at the finale. **Series type:** would be `limited_series` with 30 contiguous plans if ever accepted (see [D](D-product-capability-observations.md)).

**Core question [Working]:** *Who gives back the water, and must giving be counted?*

**Reader promise [Working]:** wonder at a physical order that responds to how people keep it; belonging through shared return labour; relationships whose obligations change meaning across generations; a hope earned at a cost.

## 2. World and characters (compact, working placeholders)

- **Ammen**: a terraced highland valley cut into a mountain face. Terraces are walled and fed by channels.
- **The Source**: a glacier-fed spring system beneath the mountain's hollow. It is the commons. Its **balance** is the gap between **draw** (recorded shares honoured) and **return** (labour returned to the channels). Thinning (too much draw) dries the high channels and floods wild water below. Surfeit (too much return, or stored water with no draw) brings stagnant pools and fever.
- **Shares and hands**: a recorded **share** lets a terrace draw reach-water. Each share owes **hands**: return labour to the channels. Unrecorded water is **wild**.
- **The First Stones**: the oldest measure-slabs, sealed in the Stone Hall. Their script (the **Old Hand**) is partly unreadable.
- **Institutions with agency**: Stone Hall (hereditary Reckoners who keep the record); Terrace Council (formed Book 6); Mill Compact (lowland grain-buyers at the lower gate of Harrow Lowe); Reading Hall (founded Book 15).
- **Characters (placeholders, not commitments)**: *Idrun Sallow*, a tender of Quillmark terrace and the protagonist (22 in Book 1, about 60 in Book 30); *Dorran Vesk*, a Stone Hall apprentice who defects in Book 1; *Keeper Anselm Corrow*, head of the Hall, a reformer who later leads a dissenting remnant; *Oona Brisk*, a Compact factor with contract claims; *Ysa Trant*, an elder of a maintenance lineage; *Tarn*, a Quillmark child in Book 1 who becomes a Council surveyor; *Hesper Sallow*, Idrun's granddaughter, born about Book 13, central to Arc V.

## 3. Generative engines

Each engine follows the pattern **Current state → Meaningful choice → Consequence → Changed state → New pressure**. The three engines co-govern. Each arc gives one of them the governing role (see §4).

### 3.1 Ledger (normative accounting)

- **Current:** shares are recorded by the Stone Hall, which decides which terrace is credited.
- **Choice:** the Hall credits a favoured house and leaves Quillmark's share unrecorded (Book 1).
- **Consequence:** Quillmark's channel runs wild in the thaw; the falsification is provable against the Stones.
- **Changed state:** the Hall's sealed custody is exposed; records are opened (Book 5).
- **New pressure:** open shares can be bought and sold (Book 6); the Hall splits; custody becomes a contested authority.

### 3.2 Source (physical commons; non-character)

- **Current:** draw ≈ return; the balance is roughly stable.
- **Choice:** shares are sold to the Compact, so draw separates from return (Book 6); later, a generation takes water as abundance (Book 23).
- **Consequence:** the upper cistern thins; the lower channels flood; the Source's cycle sets a deadline (the Long Return, Book 30).
- **Changed state:** the Balance Stones (a public gauge cut in Book 23) make the balance visible.
- **New pressure:** feasibility. A share that cannot be physically honoured forces an institution to decide who returns labour. The pressure is measurable and does not depend on anyone's villainy.

### 3.3 Old Hand (interpretive legitimacy)

- **Current:** the Stone Hall reads the Stones as a debt register.
- **Choice:** scholars and the Council read the Stones publicly (partial readings Books 8 and 12; full reading Book 15).
- **Consequence:** the first compact is a **gift**: "water given; hands given back; nothing counted." Three readings then claim binding force: debt (Corrow's remnant), gift (Council), contract (Compact).
- **Changed state:** legitimacy is plural; the Reading Hall exists; the Unmeasured Return is trialled.
- **New pressure:** the gift reading is only physically safe if the return still happens. Gift without return thins the spring, and gift with unequal hands strands the poor. The legitimacy dispute and the physical balance feed each other.

### 3.4 Loops that must be exercised

- **L1 Ledger ↔ Source:** recorded shares set the draw; the balance decides which shares can honestly be honoured. Tested every season.
- **L2 Old Hand → Ledger → Source:** a reading changes what a share is; that changes whether returns are recorded; that changes the balance.
- **L3 openness → trade → Source:** publishing records makes shares tradable; trade separates draw from return; separation thins the spring.

**Rule:** no arc may be planned without exercising at least one loop and naming the pressure it exports.

### 3.5 The governing variable: distribution of hands

Across all three engines the central variable is **who gives return labour, and who is counted for it.** Victories change this variable; it is not restored. This is the series' answer to "restore versus transform": the same question returns with a different answer.

## 5. Concrete Book functions: Books 1, 6, 15, 23, 30

Each entry gives: conflict · local closure · Series change · enabled futures · closed futures · promise status.

### Book 1 — *The Stone That Would Not Read* (Arc I opener)

- **Conflict:** Quillmark's share is recorded as drawn by a Stone Hall house Quillmark never used. In the thaw its lower channel runs wild and floods a barn-house. Idrun asks the Hall to correct it; the Hall refuses.
- **Local closure:** Idrun and Dorran Vesk read the Stone by lamp and sounding, prove the false credit, and restore the lower channel with a night of return labour the Hall had not counted. The flood stops and the share is corrected. Dorran defects.
- **Series change:** the falsification becomes known across terraces. For the first time a share can be contested in public.
- **Enabled futures:** Corrow's split; Oona Brisk learns shares can be bought (Book 6 pressure); the first recorded thinning of the upper cistern; the first partial reading of the Old Hand.
- **Closed futures:** the Hall's sole custody of the Stones is never restored; a share is never again "only the Hall's word."
- **Promises:** *wonder* — the first chamber beneath the spring is glimpsed: **fulfilled in part**. *The Stone that would not read* — the script is not solved: **complicated**, with scheduled partial readings in Books 8 and 12 and the full reading in Book 15. No promise here is postponed past Book 15 without a partial reading.

### Book 6 — *Open Accounts* (Arc II opener; conflict from success)

- **Conflict:** open records made shares something one can own. Freed households sell shares to the Mill Compact for grain. Idrun wants sale ended; Dorran argues that the Hall hoarded unsold shares and that selling is a freedom; Oona Brisk holds contract claims. The upper cistern thins and the Source's cycle schedules a lower-valley flood.
- **Local closure:** the first Turn is sworn in Quillmark and two neighbouring valleys: rotating return labour by share-holders. Compact purchases are capped at a fixed proportion, not reversed.
- **Series change:** the Terrace Council forms with a mandate to schedule the Turn.
- **Enabled futures:** maintenance knowledge concentrated in old lineages (Ysa Trant), creating the Book 9–11 pressure; the Compact's contract claims return in Book 12 as a contract reading.
- **Closed futures:** a return to pre-open secrecy; any valley returning to the Hall's custody.
- **Promises:** Book 1's *fear of wild water* is now recurring by design: **complicated**. Tarn, the Book 1 child, becomes a Council surveyor: **fulfilled**, as the people the Hall ignored now measure the water.

### Book 15 — *Unmeasured Return* (Arc III centre)

- **Conflict:** the complete reading of the First Stones shows that the first compact was given, not owed: *water given; hands given back; nothing counted.* Three readings now claim binding force. Corrow's remnant holds debt (restore the Ledger); the Council holds gift (stop counting); the Compact holds contract (convert debt to tradeable terms). The Council votes a one-season Unmeasured Return in Lower Ammen. Its cost falls on the high cistern, which shows surfeit and fever, and families with few hands argue the trial is unfair to them.
- **Local closure:** the lower valley stabilises. The trial ends with a *stewardship tally* — a record of care, not a debt — kept by the Council so the high terraces do not surfeit. One high-terrace lineage leaves the Council over the cost.
- **Series change:** legitimacy becomes plural. The Reading Hall is founded to host public readings. The Ledger is no longer the only basis of legitimacy.
- **Enabled futures:** Arc IV's plurality; Corrow's dissent persists as a legitimate institution rather than a villain; Hesper is born (about Book 13) and grows up under the gift reading.
- **Closed futures:** the Ledger as sole law; any return to debt-only legitimacy.
- **Promises:** Book 1's *the Stone that would not read* is **fulfilled and reframed**: the falsified "debt" of Book 1 was theft from a gift, which complicates Book 1's moral clarity. The question *does the Source have intent?* receives a partial answer: it responds to balance, not to any character. It is not promised as a full ontology: **complicated, open**.

### Book 23 — *The Brightwater Fair* (Arc V opener; consequence of success)

- **Conflict:** Hesper Sallow, fifteen, grown under open records and unmeasured return, leads the Brightwater festival, drawing glowing high-cistern water for display and sale. The Source's lower balance thins. The young call the Turn "a tax." Idrun, now a Council steward, sees that the claim is partly right: the Turn burdens households with few hands, including Hesper's own.
- **Local closure:** Brightwater goes ahead under a limit set by the new Balance Stones, a public gauge cut by the Reading Hall. Festival labour is counted as a visible gift, not a debt. Hesper accepts an apprenticeship with the Council.
- **Series change:** the spring's balance becomes a visible civic instrument. It informs but does not bind.
- **Enabled futures:** the Compact's last bid for lowland water rights (Books 27–29); the Long Return deadline; the Turn's survival under generational pressure.
- **Closed futures:** unrecorded abundance cannot be assumed again; the Turn cannot stay unchanged; the young cannot treat the Source as inert.
- **Promises:** *who gives back the water* is reframed as a generational question, not a conversion: **complicated**. Quillmark land, once threatened by the Book 1 flood, now feeds Tarn's descendants: **fulfilled, at a cost**.

### Book 30 — *What the Water Keeps* (finale)

- **Conflict:** the Long Return, a physical cycle the Balance Stones predicted since Book 23, arrives while the valley is plural and unevenly maintained. Three options contend. The Council wants a single measured Turn (order at a cost of freedom). The Reading Hall and Hesper want shared accounting of care, which informs but binds no one. The Compact offers to take lowland shares as settlement of its claims. The flood is not an invented antagonist: it is the spring's cycle meeting an unevenly maintained valley, a consequence of Arc IV and V's uneven hands.
- **Local closure:** the valley adopts shared accounting of care, accepts the loss of the lowland settlement (the Compact takes a share-plot in lieu of claims), and rebuilds the channels with neighbours. The cycle passes. The Turn persists, changed: no longer mandatory.
- **Series change:** no single institution holds the record. The Old Hand is shared. Terraces keep different practices.
- **Closed futures:** the Hall's monopoly, the Compact's contract model, and debt-only legitimacy all stay closed.
- **Long-range questions left open (not withheld):** whether the spring intends (answered only as "responds to balance"); what the lowland towns owe Ammen; who carries the Turn when lineages end.
- **Promises:** *care without debt* is **complicated and provisional**: the finale gives a practice, not a formula. *The deepest chamber* is **fulfilled**, and its ontology is left explicitly open. Whether the flood costs lives is **an author choice**, not decided here.

## 7. Planning posture: what is committed, what is provisional

- **Committed (sparse):** the central question; the five arcs' governing engines; Books 1, 6, 15, 23, 30 functions; the closed futures listed above.
- **Provisional:** the protagonists' journeys between Books, Arc IV's conflicts, the Compact's end state, and the Long Return's human cost.
- **Not decided:** any Book outside the five focal Books has no function beyond its arc. The brief forbade per-Book outlines, so none exist.

This is the progressive-commitment posture recommended in the strategy document. It is a working proposal, not a chosen author default.
