# 4 — Renewal test: assume the Stone Hall monopoly is genuinely resolved

Status: **working / noncanonical reasoning.** This is a craft test of the architecture in [A](A-original-architecture.md). It was done by reasoning from the architecture's own state changes. It was **not** generated by a separate agent, and no prose or scene was written.

## 1. The early conflict and its assumed resolution

**Conflict chosen:** the Stone Hall's sealed monopoly over the Ledger (Books 1–5). It is substantial: it structures the whole arc, it has independent agency (Corrow, Dorran), and it is tied to physical harm (Quillmark's wild water).

**Assumed resolution at Book 5 (genuine, not a restoration):**
- The Hall's sealed custody ends. Records are public, and anyone can read a share's credit.
- The Hall splits. Dorran has defected. Corrow leads a dissenting remnant that accepts the records are open but keeps the debt reading.
- The falsified credit is corrected, and Quillmark's channel is restored.
- The monopoly is **closed**: the architecture does not allow it to return. This is graceful closure, not a deferred threat.

## 2. What the resolution creates

The resolution is not a neutral end state. It creates new options and new pressures:

| New option | New pressure it creates |
|---|---|
| Shares can be read and so can be traded | Draw can separate from return |
| Maintenance knowledge no longer sits with the Hall | Whoever holds maintenance skill gains bargaining power |
| The Stones are readable by people outside the Hall | Scholars can contest the Old Hand |
| The Hall's remnant keeps a rival reading | Legitimacy is contested rather than merely held |
| The valley is no longer governed by one account | The next generation inherits plurality, not one custody |

## 3. Later conflicts traced to the architecture's own history

Each candidate below is traced to a **changed relationship, responsibility, institution, power, reinterpreted promise, or engine**. The question for each: could it have arisen without Book 1–5 having happened? If yes, it is a reskin, not a renewal.

### R1 — Draw without return (Book 6, Compact purchases)

- **Traced to:** *records public → shares tradable* (institution rule changed).
- **Pressure type:** **feasibility**. The Source thins, a physical limit no character chose.
- **Engine:** L3 (openness → trade → Source).
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** No. Only public, tradable records create the Compact's purchases.
- **Distinct from R2–R5?** Yes, it is the only conflict of physical feasibility in the early Arcs.

### R2 — Skill without record (Books 9–11, the Turn's maintenance)

- **Traced to:** *Hall's maintenance craft diffuses; old lineages keep it* (responsibility changed).
- **Pressure type:** **power by skill**. The lineage can refuse the Turn, so the Terrace Council must negotiate with people who had no standing in Book 1.
- **Engine:** Source and Ledger (th_maintenance).
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** No. The Hall once held maintenance; with its dissolution, the craft has no owner.
- **Distinct?** Yes. It is distributional but driven by skill rather than credit.

### R3 — Rival reading (Book 12, the Compact's contract reading and Corrow's remnant)

- **Traced to:** *the Hall splits; Corrow keeps a dissenting reading; the Compact wants contract* (institution and power changed).
- **Pressure type:** **legitimacy**. The question is which reading binds, not who is evil.
- **Engine:** Old Hand.
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** Unlikely. A unified Hall would simply have ruled; a split gives two legitimate readings.
- **Distinct?** Yes. It concerns authority, not water.

### R4 — The Stones read by others (Book 8, partial readings)

- **Traced to:** *records public → scholars and the Council read the Stones* (reinterpreted promise).
- **Pressure type:** **legitimacy and knowledge**. Partial readings become contested, and no one can close the question.
- **Engine:** Old Hand.
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** No. The Hall's sealed custody prevented it.

### R5 — Abundance without memory (Book 23, the Brightwater Fair)

- **Traced to:** *the Unmeasured Return works (Book 15) and the next generation is born under open accounts and gift reading* (relationship across generations and a transformed engine).
- **Pressure type:** **forgetting**. The young draw the water as a natural right. The conflict is with the Turn's burden, not with an institution.
- **Engine:** Source and Old Hand.
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** Only in weaker form. The Book 1–5 victory and Book 15's gift reading create the inheritance of abundance.
- **Distinct?** Yes. It is the only conflict in which the antagonist is generational forgetting rather than an institution.

### R6 — Plural practice and the new Council (Arc IV, Books 19–22)

- **Traced to:** *the Council becomes an institution with its own stewardship tally; terraces diverge* (new institution and plurality).
- **Pressure type:** **plurality**. A neighbour who keeps the Turn differently may draw on or return to a shared channel, and the Council must decide who pays.
- **Engine:** co-governance.
- **Could it arise without the prior conflict?** Possibly, but only with the Council in place, which depends on the monopoly's collapse.
- **Risk admitted:** R6 can become the monopoly again. A Council that measures other terraces would be a reskin of the Hall. The architecture needs a visible guard against this: the stewardship tally must inform and not bind, which is the Book 30 rule. Without it, Arc IV collapses into a second Book 1.

## 4. Verdict on the test

**Renewal, supported for R1–R5.** Each candidate arises from a changed relationship, responsibility, institution or promise, and each brings a different pressure type from a different engine. The chain runs without a new villain: every antagonist is an institution with its own legitimate reading, or a physical or generational condition.

**Renewal, weak for R6.** It is plausible but at risk of becoming a reskin. It needs the guard above, and that guard is a design constraint, not a finding.

**Closure:** the Hall's monopoly closes permanently. It is not replaced by a successor monopoly in the architecture. The Council's stewardship tally is the nearest candidate, which is why R6's guard matters.

## 5. Limits — where filler would be needed

1. **Books 13–14 and 19–21 have no designed conflict.** R3 and R6 would have to be filled in. Filling them with plot would break the brief's rule against filler; filling them with arbitrary threats would break it again. This is the honest cost of the arc-level design.
2. **Arc IV is the weakest.** Its only derived conflict is R6, which is at risk. Without a second derived conflict, Books 19–22 would be decorative. An unresolved design question remains here.
3. **The test does not prove the conflicts are good.** It shows that they are causally derived and differ in pressure type. Whether readers would find them compelling is outside the test's evidence.
4. **The test was run by the same agent that designed the architecture.** It is therefore biased toward the design's own assumptions. An independent review might find R2 or R3 to be reskins, or find R6 already a monopoly.
5. **Generational motives (R5, Hesper) are authored, not derived from a changed state alone.** Their independence needs its own check.
