# 5 — Local versus global judgment: which level owns the Old Hand weakness

Status: **working / noncanonical reasoning.** The question is which level should own a weakness the exercise revealed. No repair is applied in the candidate. The judgment is about ownership, not about the fix's artistic quality.

## 1. The weakness

**Observed:** the Old Hand engine's state ladder is not staged across Arc III. The architecture names four readings: partial in Book 8, partial in Book 12, a trial in Book 15, and the full reading in Book 15. Only Books 8, 12 and 15 carry functions. **Books 9–11 and 13–14 carry none, and nothing in between changes the Old Hand's state.** So the Book 15 gift reading arrives without an intermediate test of legitimacy.

**Where the renewal test found it:** R3 (the Compact's contract reading, Book 12) and R4 (partial readings, Book 8) both changed what the Old Hand *is claimed to be* but did not change its *state*. They could be read as reskins of "a rival reading" rather than successive changes. Without a state change between them, Book 15's complete reading would be a reveal, which the brief forbids.

**Where the counterfactual makes it worse:** under H1, the reform committee authors the Book 8 partial reading. That gives Arc III a legitimacy problem that begins earlier, but still with no designed intermediate change.

## 2. Candidate owners

| Level | Could own the fix? | Why it does or does not |
|---|---|---|
| Local Book (rewrite Book 13 or 14) | Partly | A single scene can add a change, but the ladder spans Books 8–15. One Book cannot make the ladder. |
| Multi-Book structure (Arc III) | **Yes** | The defect is a sequence of state changes across consecutive Books. Structure owns sequences of changed state. |
| Whole-Series (engines, identity, arcs) | No | The three engines, the core question and the five arcs are intact. Only the staging of one engine changes. |
| Working Story Identity | No | No accepted Identity exists. The fixed brief is intact. |
| Realization / Expression | No | Prose cannot supply a state change the Structure does not plan. A scene written to fill Book 13 would look plausible and leave the ladder missing. |
| Creative method (planning posture) | **Contributing cause only** | The planning posture specified focal Books only. That hid the gap until the renewal test. The method should be improved, but the fix itself belongs to Structure. |

## 3. Decision

**The owning level is multi-Book structure (Arc III).** The creative method is recorded as the reason the gap was not seen earlier. It is not where the repair lives.

**Justification, in one sentence per point:**
1. The defect is a missing sequence of state changes across Books 8, 12, 13, 14 and 15, which only a multi-Book structure can express.
2. It does not touch the Series identity, the engine set or the other four arcs, so whole-Series repair would overreach.
3. A local rewrite of Book 13 would add a scene without fixing the Old Hand's ladder, which is precisely the kind of plausible-but-empty repair the brief warns against.
4. Expression cannot supply the state change; it can only render one that Structure has planned.

## 4. Proposed repair at the owning level (not applied)

Stage the Old Hand as a ladder with a changed legitimacy state at each step:

| Step | Book | Changed state of the Old Hand | Pressure it creates |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | Scholars publish a partial reading that the Hall cannot contest alone | a reading has an audience |
| 2 | 12 | The Compact introduces a contract reading that turns debt into tradeable terms | a rival reading with money behind it |
| 3 | 13 | A gift-only trial fails in a single terrace (a small, physical failure of the gift reading) | the gift reading must answer for a failure |
| 4 | 14 | The Council must argue that the failure reflects unequal hands, not the gift itself | the question moves from legitimacy to fairness |
| 5 | 15 | The full reading makes the first compact a gift, and the Unmeasured Return is trialled in Lower Ammen | the stakes are set by earlier steps, not by a reveal |

Each step changes a state and exports a pressure to the next. Books 9–11 remain unplanned except as Arc II pressure.

## 5. What would change this judgment

- If the author wants Arc III to be short (only Books 12–15), the ladder compresses, but the owner stays multi-Book structure.
- If the Old Hand is redesigned so its readings do not change state, the owner would move to the working engine definition in whole-Series architecture. That would be a different decision, and this design does not require it.
- If an accepted Book 13 already exists, the local option would be bound to that accepted artifact, and the decision would go through the revision-staleness contract (`docs/revision-staleness-contract.md`).

## 6. Under the counterfactual H1

H1 changes the Book 1–5 agency, which seems local. Its effects reach Books 8, 12 and 15, so it too needs a multi-Book repair. This is why the revision delta in [C](C-revision-delta.md) is applied at the arc level and not in Book 1 alone.
