Status: **working / noncanonical assessment by one agent.** No independent critic was run. Every judgment below, including "renewal supported", was made by the same agent that designed the architecture. That agent is Claude Haiku 5.5, working inside this exercise. Nothing here is human author validation or third-party review.

## 1. Answer to the feasibility question

**Partly feasible, at the abstract level.**

- **Supported by this run:** a 30-Book working architecture can be built with five macro arcs, three co-governing engines, explicit causal loops and five focal Books. It passes the Studio candidate's structural validation. One counterfactual can be applied as a reviewable working revision with the original preserved. The freshness guard holds.
- **Not supported:** a 30-Book horizon as an *accepted* series contract. Accepting it would need either 25 placeholder plans (which validate) or a schema change, which this evidence does not justify. Typed causal reasoning, quality judgment, and repeatable renewal were also not shown. This is one run with one agent.

## 3. Strongest weakness

**The late-middle and late series are under-derived.** Arc IV (Books 19–22) has one candidate conflict (R6), which risks reproducing the Hall as the Council. Books 9–11 and 13–14 have no designed conflict. As a result, Book 30's cost-bearing choice, which is the finale's promise of care without debt, is currently **asserted more than derived**. The reader is promised an earned ending that the architecture has not yet earned.

This is the weakness that matters most for a 30-Book design. A strong opening and a strong ending do not compensate for a middle that is only argued.

## 4. Strongest credible counterargument

**The layered alternative (Debt of Hands) may be the better product for an author.** Its one governing grammar is easier to hold in mind, easier for a host agent to keep consistent, and more renewable per unit of author effort. The ensemble's three loops are real, but the product cannot type them, and a three-engine design may be unholdable by an author or agent with only prose to carry it. If so, the synthesis's advantage on paper would not survive contact with a 30-Book revision cycle.

A second counterargument: the counterfactual's "reform from within" may reveal a bias toward institutional repair. If so, Arc I becomes soft, and the protagonist's adventure is weakened. This exercise cannot rule that out.

## 5. Where the exercise is uncertain

- Whether any of this is compelling fiction. This exercise did not write prose, and the brief did not ask for it. Reader response is unknown.
- Whether Arcs IV and V hold. They are argued, not derived in detail.
- Whether a fresh agent could reconstruct the architecture from the `resume` packet (about 43,000 characters). Not tested.
- Whether the browser apply route and the HTTP path behave the same as the Python call. Not tested.
- Whether the independence of Hesper's generation (R5) is real or authored. Not checked independently.

The architecture is a working, validated, reviewable proposal with one tested counterfactual. The middle and late series are under-derived. The product supports the working candidate and the revision flow, and does not support the 30-Book horizon as accepted canon. Nothing was accepted, and nothing in the canonical story records changed.
