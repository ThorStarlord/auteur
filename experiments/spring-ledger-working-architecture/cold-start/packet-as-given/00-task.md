# Task: develop Book 24's narrative function for The Spring Ledger

You are a creative coding agent continuing the development of a long-form fantasy Series. You start with no prior conversation. Everything you need is in this packet and in the working architecture, which you reach through Auteur's bridge (see 04-access.md).

## The creative responsibility

The author gave this responsibility, verbatim:

> Develop a meaningful architectural function for Book 24 of The Spring Ledger.
> Book 23 has transformed the next generation's understanding of the water commons.
> The later Series must still confront the Long Return and unresolved questions about shared responsibility.
> Construct a distinct Book 24 contribution that emerges from the inherited conditions without prematurely deciding the finale.
> The Book must offer a satisfying local narrative transformation while producing consequences relevant to later Series development.
> Preserve the established narrative identity, causal systems, character agency, and author-reserved choices.

Determine autonomously:
- What Book 24 is actually about.
- Which unresolved pressure warrants development now.
- Which established engines generate its conflict.
- Which characters or institutions have independent motivations.
- What meaningful choice creates the Book's transformation.
- What local resolution it offers.
- What changes in the wider Series.
- Which later possibilities it enables or closes.
- Which assumptions remain provisional.

Consider more than one plausible Book 24 function before you select a working direction. You do not need to enumerate exhaustively. Note the alternatives you considered briefly, and say why you chose the one you did.

## Constraints

- Write a function, not a manuscript. Do not write a chapter, a scene or prose.
- Do not create an arbitrary new antagonist.
- No finale alternative is accepted. Do not assume Alternative C, or any other, is the finale.
- Do not rewrite Arc IV merely to make Book 24 convenient.
- Do not postpone every consequential choice to Book 30.
- Do not treat a globally important concern as automatically requiring a global redesign.
- Keep the author's identity, intentions and author-reserved choices (02-author-intentions.md; section 8 of 01-series-architecture.md).
- Label every new element as agent-proposed or derived from a named source. Do not label anything author-declared. Use provenance fields only where the product requires them.

## Deliverables (write them to the output folder named in 04-access.md)

1. `book-24-function.md`, the Book 24 architectural function, of no more than about 2,500 words, with these sections:
   1. The Book's question and the pressure it develops.
   2. Why now: the inherited state it uses. Cite the candidate node or phase IDs you relied on.
   3. The engines and how they interact. Short causal chains where useful: condition, choice, consequence, changed state, new possibility.
   4. The characters and institutions with independent motivations.
   5. The meaningful choice and the transformation it produces.
   6. The local resolution.
   7. Changes to the wider Series.
   8. The later possibilities it enables and the ones it closes.
   9. The provisional assumptions, each labelled.
   10. The alternatives you considered, and why you did not choose them.
   11. Any information you needed and did not have.
2. `product-use.md`: which Auteur operations you ran, what each returned, what you could and could not express through them, and any error you met, quoted exactly.
3. Optionally, if you judge that the Book 24 function belongs in the working architecture, propose it through the product (`prepare --instruction` followed by `complete-file`), as 03-auteur-operations.md describes. Record the outcome in `product-use.md`. If you judge that the product cannot faithfully hold the function, say so and say why, rather than forcing it in.

You are not judged on the number of files, the length, or the number of product calls. Judge the quality of the function.
