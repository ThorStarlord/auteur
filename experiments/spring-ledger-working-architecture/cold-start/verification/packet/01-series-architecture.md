The candidate it agrees with is [02-working-candidate-W6.json](02-working-candidate-W6.json). ## 1. Story Vision and identity

**What the Series is.** A long-form fantasy Series about a shared water commons: a spring that answers how people keep it, a valley whose institutions govern it, and the meanings people attach to owing and giving. It follows one valley, Ammen, over about forty-five years. Each Book spans about a year and a half. [Fixed]

**The experience it promises.** Wonder at a physical order that responds to how people keep it; discovery of what the record and the water actually say; everyday belonging through shared labour; relationships whose obligations change meaning across generations; and an ultimately hopeful possibility that a shared practice can outlast the institution that began it, at a visible cost. [Fixed] (intentions, §3)

**The central question.** *Who gives back the water, and must giving be counted?* [Derived: `th_core_question`]

**The governing variable.** *Who gives return labour, and who is counted for draw.* Each arc changes the answer, and no arc restores an earlier one. [Derived: `th_hands_and_draw`]

**What must remain recognizable.**
- A physical commons whose balance is real and does not depend on any villain's intent.
- The principle that unkept water is dangerous, and that recorded water is power.
- The Ammen terraces as the place of belonging, and shared return labour as its source.
- Hope that people can keep faith with each other and the water, earned rather than announced.
- The genre: a quest to understand a commons that answers choices, not a war against a rival power. [Derived: `gn_epic_fantasy`]

**What can change radically.** Who keeps the accounts; whether a return is a debt, a gift or a contract; which institutions exist; who is credited and who is uncounted; how the valley reads its oldest script; its relation to the lowlands. [Derived: original §1]

**What the Series must not become.** A generic escalating-villain epic. The Series has no antagonist that the plot escalates against. Its conflicts come from the consequences of earlier successes and from the physical balance. No arbitrary new threats, and no revelations postponed past their partial readings. [Fixed]

**What the long-range transformations mean.** Victories change the world rather than restore the earlier situation. The Stone Hall's monopoly of the record ends and is never restored. The Turn changes from an institution into a practice that can be binding for a window, or can be voluntary. The finale's question is not whether to go back, but how the valley will count what it owes. [Derived]

## 2. The generative mechanisms

Three engines co-govern the Series. Each arc gives one of them the governing role, and the others keep acting. [Derived: `eng_ledger`, `eng_source`, `eng_old_hand`]

### 2.1 Ledger: records, shares, authority

*Existing condition:* shares are recorded by the Stone Hall, and the Hall decides which terrace is credited. Draw follows shares.
*Meaningful choice:* the Hall credits a favoured house and leaves a lower terrace's share unrecorded (Book 1).
*Consequence:* the lower channel runs wild in the thaw. The falsification can be proven against the Stones.
*Changed state:* the Hall's sealed custody is exposed. Records open to public access (Book 5). The Hall splits.
*New possibility:* shares can be traded (Book 6); credit can be re-read (Book 15); from Book 20 draw partly follows hands owed.

### 2.2 Source: balance, channels, the spring

*Existing condition:* the spring's balance is the gap between draw and return. Thinning (too much draw) dries the high channels and floods wild water below. Surfeit (too much return, or stored water with no draw) brings stagnant pools and fever. The Source responds to balance, not to anyone's intention.
*Meaningful choice:* shares are sold, so draw separates from return (Book 6). Later, a generation takes water as abundance (Book 23).
*Consequence:* the high cistern thins and the lower channels flood. The spring's deep-store pulse, the Long Return, is predicted by the Balance Stones from Book 23 and arrives in Book 30.
*Changed state:* the balance is visible as a public gauge.
*New pressure:* a share that cannot be honoured physically forces an institution to decide who returns labour. This pressure does not depend on anyone's villainy.

### 2.3 Old Hand: obligation, readings, legitimacy

*Existing condition:* the Stone Hall reads the Stones as a debt register.
*Meaningful choice:* scholars and the Council read the script publicly (partial readings in Books 8 and 12; full reading in Book 15).
*Consequence:* the first compact was a gift: "water given; hands given back; nothing counted." Three readings then claim binding force: debt (the Hall's remnant), gift (the Council), contract (the Compact).
*Changed state:* legitimacy is plural. The Reading Hall exists to publish readings. The uncounted gift practice is trialled in Lower Ammen.
*New pressure:* the gift is only physically safe if the return still happens. Gift without return thins the spring, and gift with unequal hands strands the poor.

### 2.4 The loops that must be exercised

- **L1, Ledger ↔ Source:** recorded shares set the draw, and the balance decides which shares can honestly be honoured.
- **L2, Old Hand → Ledger → Source:** a reading changes what a share is, which changes whether returns are recorded, which changes the balance.
- **L3, openness → trade → Source:** public records make shares tradable, trade separates draw from return, and separation thins the spring.

**Rule for development.** No new arc, Book or Chapter may be planned unless it exercises at least one loop and names the pressure it exports. [Derived: original §3.4]

### 3.1 Arc IV, the two conflicts

**Conflict A, the Counted Rota (Books 19–21).** *Existing condition:* the tally counts care, and few-hands households cannot meet their share. *Trigger (Book 19):* the Lowfen channel breaks in the thaw and needs about a rota's labour for a season. *Choice (Book 20):* the Council adopts lineage crews' repair under a Special Draw Right, and converts the draw of hand-light share-holders in proportion to their deficit. *Consequence (Book 21):* one household declines, forfeits its share, and its water runs wild down the lowest Lowfen terrace into a neighbour's orchard. *New possibility:* hands now set part of draw, and skill claims draw. [Agent: the Right, the conversion and the declined share are construction additions.]

**Conflict B, Two Practices, One Gate (Books 21–22).** *Existing condition:* the counted Turn in the high terraces and the gift practice in Lower Ammen both draw on the Hollow Gate, whose schedule the Council sets. *Trigger (Book 21):* a dry spring. Release at need draws the high cistern down, and the lower channels silt when the release stops. *Choice:* the valley forms a Gate Table with rotating seats (one for each practice, one by lot from households without lineage craft). It sets the schedule from the gauge and holds no record. *Consequence (Book 22):* a coordinating body exists that is neither the Council nor the Hall. The schedule is separated from the record. [Agent: the Gate and the Table.]

### 3.2 Book 23 and the next generation

Book 23 is the Brightwater festival. The next generation inherits three things that the construction keeps separate:
- **Genuine abundance, seasonal:** the Table holds water back in dry spells, so a real surplus sits in the high cistern.
- **Uneven abundance:** the surplus is drawn mostly by the best-handed houses and the lineage's Right. Hand-light houses have drawn less since Book 20.
- **A belief that abundance needs no work:** a cohort that came of age after Book 20 has never seen the labour that keeps the water. This is a belief about the water, not a fact about the spring.

Hesper Sallow, Idrun's granddaughter, is about fifteen and leads the festival. She takes an apprenticeship with the Council. The Balance Stones, cut by the Reading Hall, publish the Long Return's predicted date and limit Brightwater's draw. [Derived: original §5; W6 `ph_5_unmeasured`]

### 3.3 Books 24–26: deliberately open

Books 24–26 have no designed function. This is intentional. The architecture commits to the Long Return's arrival in Book 30 and to the finale's three alternatives, and it leaves the middle of Arc V open for future development. Books 9–11 and 13–14 likewise have no per-Book function, and their pressures are inferred from the arc. [Open: the design deliberately leaves the middle Books unspecified]

### 3.4 Book 30: the Long Return and the finale

The deep store sends a pulse. It needs two things the valley must supply before its date: channel labour, which is hands, and a Gate schedule that routes the surge. Which channel takes the surge, and whether any household floods, is an author choice. The finale chooses among three mechanisms (§6). None is chosen. [Derived: W6 `str_long_return`]

## 6. The finale alternatives

These are the three mechanisms for the Book 30 choice of who gives return labour and who is counted for it. **None is chosen. None is the author's preference. No alternative is declared the winner.** Each keeps its cost visible.

**A: Noncoercive coordination by mutual dependence.** Care is counted, and nothing is owed. Draw is decoupled from hands. Households depend on their neighbours' maintenance through shared channels. A care floor protects households that lack hands, funded by the Special Right and administered by the Gate Table with the Right's holders' consent. Costs: when every community is short at once, pair-hands fail; a surfeit trade needs the high terraces to consent, and they may not; the lowland draw carries no hands, and A has no lever on it; the tally can be read as surveillance; and labour is given but not owed, so a community's loss can fall on those who volunteered.

**B: Allotted, binding rota for a rebuild window.** Hands are owed in proportion to draw, with a floor for hand-light households. The Gate Table is seated by lot, its terms last one cycle, and the authority sunsets. The Table's schedule binds the water at the Gate for every practice. The Compact's grain-labour is owed. Costs: a lot-drawn panel must be seated and a rota published before labour can start, and that lead time can use up the window; a shortfall becomes a deficit that must be recorded, and under B the Reading Hall would keep that record, which re-admits a debt ledger for the window; refusal forfeits the window's draw, and its physical cost passes to others; the Table gains real power over the high cistern. Whether the gift practice's shares sit inside the rota is an open confirmation.

**C: Ratified trigger.** In normal seasons the valley follows A. A threshold is published in advance: a level on the Balance Stones' central estimate of the surge, read by the Reading Hall. When it is crossed, B's rota applies for a window with a pre-drawn panel of lot seats. Costs: the threshold becomes an authority, and its reading can be disputed; the ratifying body is not yet named; if the instrument is not ratified, or the threshold is misread, C reverts to A's outcome; the Balance Stones change their function from informing to triggering; and the rule may be stale when it is needed.

**Feasibility concerns shared by all three.**
- **The high cistern's storage.** B and C depend on the cistern absorbing surfeit beyond its tolerance. The architecture does not establish how much it can take. If it cannot, all three face a flood. This is the most important open physical fact. [Open: §8]
- **The lowland gate.** The Harrow Lowe gate, which the original places at the Compact, can pass the lowland overflow. No alternative binds it. [Provisional: §7]

**The stress test.** A frozen Book 30 crisis was run against all three alternatives in an earlier experiment. Under it, A left the overflow on the lowest terrace, and B and C kept it off by binding surfeit onto the high terraces. B and C differed mainly in lead time and in the size of the deficit. **That result depends on illustrative numbers that the experiment chose. None of them is canon.** It does not show that C is better. It shows what each mechanism would have to do.

## 7. Provisional working assumptions

Each of the following is **agent-proposed and reversible**. None is author-declared. Each can be replaced without changing the author's fixed commitments. They are recorded as provisional concepts in the Studio canvas.

- **The Hollow Gate** [Agent]. The sluice that divides the high cistern's release between the upper and lower channels. The original does not name it. It is the object of Conflict B.
- **The Gate Table** [Agent]. A rotating body that sets the schedule and holds no record. Its acceptance is an open author question.
- **The surfeit mechanism of the Book 15 trial** [Agent]. The original says the high cistern showed surfeit. The construction assumes that the trial's uncounted return exceeded the draw the Turn counted, so the surplus showed in the high cistern. The author may reject it.
- **The Special Draw Right** [Agent]. Conditional on training apprentices from outside the lineage, and revocable by the Council as a working design. Whether it is revocable or permanent is open.
- **The Harrow Lowe gate** [Inherited, not in the candidate]. The original places the Mill Compact "at the lower gate of Harrow Lowe". The candidate does not carry it. The lowland overflow path in the finale depends on it.
- **The Compact's labour contribution** [Agent]. Grain paid as hired lowland labour, capped, and owed only under B or C.

## 8. Open choices

**Author-reserved. Not to be decided by an agent.**
1. Whether the Hollow Gate and the Gate Table are accepted parts of the world.
2. Whether shared accounting of care binds, and in what form.
3. Which finale mechanism is preferred, A, B, C or a third.
4. The Compact's ultimate settlement, and whether it has a seat.
5. The moral stance toward institutional reform: whether reform from within is the Series' stance.
6. Whether the flood costs lives or lasting losses, and which channel takes the surge.
7. The final disposition of the Special Draw Right: revocable or permanent, and its training condition.
8. Whether the Old Hand needs states between Books 8 and 15.
9. Whether the spring intends, or responds only to measured balance.
10. Who carries the Turn when the maintenance lineages end.
11. Whether Hesper's motive is independent of the author's preference.
12. Whether any terrace keeps a measured ledger in Book 30.

**Unresolved fact, not a choice.** What the high cistern can physically store. Not established.

## 10. How to continue

Read this document, then [04-provenance.md](04-provenance.md) for what the candidate changed, and then the candidate through the product (see the package README). Before adding anything:

1. Identify the pressure you will develop, and name the loop it exercises (§2.4).
2. Keep the author-fixed intentions and §8's author-reserved choices exactly as they are.
3. Label any new element as agent-proposed, and say what would reverse it.
4. Prefer development inside the middle Books (24–26) to redesign of Arcs IV or V.
5. Keep every finale alternative open. Do not carry a preference forward from earlier experiments.
