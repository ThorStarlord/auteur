# C — One counterfactual revision and its focused delta

Status: **hypothetical, author-proposed, not accepted.** Nothing here amends the fixed brief, any accepted Identity, Structure, Series or Book, or the Series' canon. The original version is preserved intact (see §9).

## 1. The counterfactual (H1)

In Book 5 the Stone Hall's own head, **Keeper Anselm Corrow**, not the tenders, opens the sealed records. He does so to protect the Hall's standing when the spring thins, which the Hall's own falsified credit has helped cause. A lower-terrace household's evidence forces the timing and the proof, so the Hall is **reformed rather than destroyed**. The reform is partial: its committee keeps the debt reading to protect its own legitimacy. Dorran carries the reform as messenger instead of defecting.

H1 is a premise change to Arc I's **outcome and agency**. It is not a change to the Source, the Ledger's mechanism, the Arc II trade mechanism, the Book 15 gift reading, or the finale's choice.

**Distant payoffs needing reconsideration:**
- *The Stone that would not read* (Book 1 → Book 15): the reading is now an institution's act, not a protagonist's. The promise is still kept, but its agent is different.
- *Care without debt* (Book 30): the most exposed payoff. H1 supplies an early instance of institutional self-correction, so the finale must show why shared accounting of care is still costly and not already solved.

## 4. Preserved invariants

- The spring's physical balance and the loops L1–L3 (Ledger ↔ Source, Old Hand → Ledger → Source, openness → trade → Source).
- The Arc II trade mechanism and the thinning it causes.
- The Book 15 gift reading and the Unmeasured Return.
- The Series core question, the reader promise, and the hopeful flourishing.
- The fixed brief: victories change the world (the reform is a real change), several agents act independently (Idrun, Corrow, the Compact, the Council), no restoration, no escalating antagonist, no postponed revelation (the partial-reading schedule stays).

## 5. Local or broader repair

**Broader, at arc level.** Rewriting Book 1 alone would leave Books 6, 8, 12 and 15 with authors and motives that no longer match, and the finale's cost logic would be exposed. The repair therefore changes Arc I's agency, Arc II's responsibility, and Arc III's readings. It does not rebuild Arcs IV and V, and it does not change the engine set (see [WITHHELD reference]).

## 6. Repair alternatives (at least two genuinely different ones)

**R-A — Witness-led reform (applied in the revised candidate).** Idrun's household evidence forces the reform. Corrow's committee opens the records but keeps the debt reading. Dorran carries the reform. The committee owns the sale rules, so it pays for them in Arc II and III.
- *Keeps:* protagonist's adventure (proof, not force), institutional agency, the finale's cost, the Arc I structure.
- *Costs:* Corrow becomes an ambiguous character. His motives must be stated openly, not held back as a twist, or the series breaks its no-postponed-revelation rule.

**R-B — Institution-only Arc I (not applied).** The protagonists' Arc I victory is re-scoped as a terrace-level stewardship act: Idrun's tenders create the Turn at Quillmark in Book 4. The Hall's reform happens offstage, as a consequence Book 5 reports rather than shows.
- *Keeps:* protagonist agency through a different victory; avoids a reform "from above."
- *Costs:* the reform happens offstage, so the institution has no shown agency in Arc I. If its motives are withheld, the reform becomes a postponed revelation. It also converts Arc I's major victory into a smaller one.

**R-C — Restore the original premise through a hidden motive (rejected).** Make Corrow's reform a cover for theft, revealed in Book 12.
- *Rejected:* it adds a late revelation and an antagonist the brief forbids, and it would be an arbitrary new threat.

**Preference:** R-A. It repairs the affected dependencies with the smallest change to the Series' independent agencies and keeps the finale's cost. R-B is the fallback if the author wants Arc I's victory to stay with the tenders.

## 8. Effect on identity (working, not accepted)

- **Stays recognizable:** water that must be kept by people; the physical commons; the Ammen terraces; the hope that people can keep faith with each other and the water.
- **Changes:** the implicit assumption that liberation comes by seizure. The series' moral engine becomes *legitimate reform by evidence*, which raises a new risk: benevolent-institution drift. Books 15 and 30 must test it.
- **Open to author choice:** whether reform from within is the series' stance or a stage the finale must oppose.
