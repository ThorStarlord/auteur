## What the revision is

The revision is a hypothetical counterfactual, H1. It is not accepted and is not canon. Its repair, R-A, is the one applied in the revised candidate. Evaluate it as an architectural transformation.

## Tasks

1. **Trace the changed premises.** For each consequential changed causal assumption, state which later developments depend on it, which character or institutional motivations change, which emotional or thematic commitments may be affected, which long-range payoffs become stronger, weaker or impossible, and which existing elements remain genuinely unaffected. Separate direct dependencies from speculative interpretation.
2. **Challenge preserved meaning.** Examine whether unchanged elements remain semantically consistent under the revised architecture. A structurally preserved node or relationship is not automatically a valid preserved meaning. Report contradictions, unsupported inferences, stale references or newly impossible dependencies only where the evidence supports them. Do not invent a contradiction merely because a field was unchanged.
3. **Determine the repair level for each consequential weakness.** Name the owner: Local Book Structure; Multi-Book Structure; Whole-Series Architecture; Working Story Identity; Realization or Expression; Agent creative-reasoning method; or Representation or review capability in Auteur. A problem's location is not necessarily its owner.
4. **Evaluate R-A.** Does it preserve the governing Series identity where intended? Does it change all materially affected components? Does it avoid unnecessary changes? Does it preserve author authority? Does it create new contradictions or unintended consequences? Is it a coherent working alternative? If R-A is inadequate, you may propose a different repair. Do not apply it.
5. **Classify each consequential finding** as one of: creative architecture weakness; creative reasoning weakness; context-delivery weakness; representation weakness; review weakness; or no demonstrated product defect.
