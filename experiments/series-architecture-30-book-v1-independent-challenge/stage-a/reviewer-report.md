## 1. Dimension assessments

**A. Distinctiveness.** This is strong. The Series is distinctive because its mechanisms interact, not because of its setting or theme language. The commons has a real physical balance: "draw (recorded shares honoured) and return (labour returned)". Two failure modes pull in opposite directions: "Thinning… floods wild water below. Surfeit… stagnant pools and fever". The Old Hand changes what a share *is*: "debt, gift or contract". The genre contract also says what the Series is not: "not to defeat a rival power".
- Demonstrated: the identity comes from interacting mechanisms.
- Plausibly open: whether the wonder strand ("first chamber", "deepest chamber") is more than set dressing. Its link to the mechanics is only "visible responses make the spring feel alive".
- Gap: missing detail, not missing necessity.

**B. Causal renewal.** This is strong for Books 1–23. Each success creates a new kind of problem:
- Falsified custody leads to open records.
- Open records make shares tradable ("open shares can be bought and sold").
- Trade breaks the link between draw and return, which leads to the Turn.
- The Turn concentrates maintenance knowledge "in old lineages".
- The gift reading leads to the Unmeasured Return. Its "cost falls on the high cistern", which forces the stewardship tally.
- A generation raised under gift practice leads to Brightwater.

These conflicts really are different in kind: custody, then market, then labour distribution, then legitimacy, then generational inheritance. Engines can also end. The custody conflict closes for good ("never restored").
- Demonstrated: the Book 1–15 chain.
- Plausibly open: Book 23. It depends on "a generation takes water as abundance", and nothing yet sets up that abundance. A possible link exists in the Book 15 high-cistern surfeit, but the architecture never draws it.
- Weaker: renewal after Book 23. Arc V's pressure is mostly a scheduled "Long Return deadline" plus the Compact's "last bid".

**C. Multi-engine interaction.** This is strong. Loops L1–L3 are true causal connections, backed by candidate links such as `l_oldhand_redefines_share` and `l_trade_draws_without_return`. The candidate also states the planning rule: "no arc may be planned without exercising at least one loop". The best example of combined effect is Book 15. Legitimacy (the gift reading) collides with physics: "Gift without return thins the spring, and gift with unequal hands strands the poor". Neither engine alone produces the stewardship tally.
- Demonstrated: the interactions.
- Gap: the Old Hand engine after Book 15. Its readings are complete, so its continued pressure rests on the Reading Hall and plural readings. That is plausibly open but not shown.

**D. Long-range coordination.** This is mixed, and it is where the main weakness sits.
- Demonstrated: the readings are staged (Books 8, 12, 15). The Long Return has seeds: "the Source's cycle schedules a lower-valley flood" appears as early as Book 6, so the finale's flood is not arbitrary.
- Plausibly open: the intermediate state the finale's causal claim depends on. The architecture calls the finale "a consequence of Arc IV and V's uneven hands". Arc IV is the least specified arc. Its conflicts are "provisional" and it is "deliberately lighter".
- Contradicted (internally): the finale's options. The candidate gives Book 30 as "a choice between restored monopoly and shared accounting of care". The architecture lists three different options: a measured Turn, shared accounting, and a Compact settlement. It also lists "the Hall's monopoly" as a closed future.
- Contradicted (minor): Book 1's reading. The candidate says "Book 1 gives the first partial reading". The architecture says "the script is not solved", with partial readings in Books 8 and 12.
- Unsupported: "civilizational only at the finale". Book 30's content stays at valley and lowland scale.

**E. Book-level independence.** This is adequate. Books 1, 6, 15 and 23 each have real local closure: a corrected share, a sworn Turn, a tally, a limited fair. Each also exports a pressure. The 25 unspecified Books are intentional ("The brief forbade per-Book outlines"), and I do not count them as a defect.
- Plausibly open: Arc V's length. It runs eight Books (23–30), but only Brightwater, Books 27–29 and the finale have functions. Books 24–26 risk becoming a countdown to a "physical cycle the Balance Stones predicted since Book 23", which could stretch the arc artificially.
- Plausibly open: Arc IV's four Books. Their only engine is "small local disputes… from settled practice".

**F. Creative transformation.** This is strong. The architecture separates what stays recognisable ("Water that must be kept by people") from what may change: "who keeps the accounts; whether a return is a debt, a gift or a contract; the institutions". Institutions really do rise, fall and turn into something else. Corrow's remnant persists "as a legitimate institution rather than a villain". The protagonist ages from 22 to about 60, and the centre of gravity moves to Hesper. The narrative modes shift by arc too: mystery, then political, then pastoral, then epic.
- Demonstrated: identity is not confused with preserving the initial status quo.

**G. Authorial flexibility.** This is mostly sound. It commits to little ("Committed (sparse)"), and it puts real author choices in the open list: the Source's intent, flood casualties, the Compact's end state.
- The problem: some items listed as provisional are things the architecture needs, not free choices. "Arc IV's conflicts" is provisional, yet Arc IV must produce the "uneven hands" and the "abundance without memory" that Books 23 and 30 depend on.
- More upfront planning of Arc IV's *exported state* would add coherence. Planning Arc IV's Book-level plots would not.

## 2. Strongest demonstrated mechanisms

1. **A non-villain physical constraint.** The Source "constrains which recorded shares can honestly be honoured". Its pressure "does not depend on anyone's villainy". This meets the no-escalation directive structurally, not just by declaring it.
2. **A two-sided failure space.** Thinning and surfeit penalise opposite mistakes. So a fix aimed at one danger can trigger the other, as in Book 15.
3. **One governing variable.** "Who gives return labour, and who is counted for it", with the rule "Victories change this variable; it is not restored." This turns "victories change the world" into a variable the reader can track.
4. **Debt/gift/contract plurality.** Each institution's reading has its own logic, so the losers are given "reasons that are coherent from their side". That keeps agency independent.
5. **Promise discipline.** Readings are staged and the Source's ontology is "never promised as a reveal". This rules out indefinitely postponed revelations.

## 3. Judgment

**Achievement.** The best part is the causal chain from Book 1 to Book 15. Each local victory (open records, the Turn, the gift reading) produces a different kind of conflict through a named loop. Physics and legitimacy collide in Book 15 to produce something neither could alone.

**Weakness.** The finale's resolution is not mechanically distinct from the condition that caused the crisis.
- The flood is attributed to "uneven hands" in a valley that, since Book 23, has had a gauge that "informs but does not bind".
- The finale adopts "shared accounting of care, which informs but binds no one", and makes the Turn "no longer mandatory".
- So the stated cure for uneven maintenance is less binding than the regime that let unevenness build up. The architecture never says what shared accounting *does* to the governing variable, meaning who returns labour, beyond "rebuilds the channels with neighbours", which is a one-time response to the emergency.
- This is compounded by the arcs feeding the finale. The intermediate state the finale needs (Arc IV's uneven practice) is left "provisional". And the candidate describes the finale's choice differently, as "restored monopoly" versus care, contradicting the architecture's three-way choice.

This is a missing architectural necessity, not missing detail. Without it the finale's hope is "announced", which is exactly what the candidate's own aesthetic strategy forbids ("earned rather than announced").

**Counterargument.** The brief asks for "local closure plus long-range questions", and the architecture explicitly offers "a practice, not a formula". It can be argued that the crisis does the binding: the Long Return makes the shared labour happen, and a non-coercive practice that survives a shared emergency is the earned hope. Asking for a mechanism risks pushing the finale toward the "single measured Turn" the Series means to reject. On this reading the gap is detail that drafting Book 30 would supply. I find this partly persuasive. It still needs one stated difference between the Book 23 gauge and the Book 30 practice: for example, care becomes visible as reciprocal standing rather than as data. Without that, the governing variable is not shown to change at the climax.

**Owning level.** Whole-Series Architecture, for the finale's mechanism and the consistency between the two packet documents. Multi-Book Structure would own the secondary Arc IV export.

**Smallest revision.**
1. Add one causal sentence to Book 30 saying how shared accounting of care changes who returns labour, as distinct from the Balance Stones' "informs but does not bind".
2. Turn Arc IV's "Changed state exported" into a required export: uneven hands across terraces, plus apparent abundance from the surfeited high cistern.
3. Make the candidate's Book 30 choice match the architecture.

No new Book outlines are needed.

**Evidence that could change my view.** The withheld Arc IV sentences, if they establish the uneven-hands state. A drafted Book 30 showing a concrete difference in practice. A demonstration that Books 24–26 have independent functions beyond the deadline. Any reader-response evidence that the non-binding finale reads as earned.
