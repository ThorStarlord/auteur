# 07. Synthesis: the final architecture under pressure, and Auteur's capability

Status: **working / noncanonical.** Answers the eight questions in the mission. It keeps four levels apart:

| Level | What this exercise has | What it does not have |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanical** | Schema validation of W2, W3, W4, W5, H1 and its three corrected versions; the product's review objects for each change; a recomputation of the traces' arithmetic. | Any judgment of meaning, or any check that the product's checks are complete. |
| **Independent critique** | Five fresh-context reviews: Stage A (W2), Stage B (H1), and one finding-specific verification of each of three correction rounds (F, F2, F3). Each is verbatim in `review/`. | More than one reviewer per check, any reader's response, or any literary judgment. |
| **Coherent construction** | A frozen shared crisis, three consequence traces, and corrected candidates whose defects the reviews found and the next verifier checked. | Prose, or any reading of the chains on the page. |
| **Author acceptance** | None. Nothing here is approved, merged or canon. | Everything the author decides (§ 8). |

Who did what. The coordinating agent built the crisis, the traces, the comparison and every correction. The reviewers were fresh instances of the same model family as the earlier reviewers, and each judged only the packet it was given. That is independence of context, not of author. The limits are recorded in [00](00-freeze-and-provenance.md) § 3 and § 6, and in [06](06-adjudication.md) § F.

## 1. Are the final candidates semantically coherent?

**W2 (PR #553's final candidate): no, not everywhere.** It validates against the schema, but the Stage A review found contradictions of meaning in several places:

- **Book 15 physics (R8).** The surfeit was attributed to a release from the cistern, which draws it down, and the original defines surfeit as too much return or stored water with no draw.
- **The governing variable (R11).** The counted Turn was said to match draw to labour from Book 6, which contradicts the claim that hands first set draw in Book 20.
- **The Hall's fate (R12).** W2 said the Hall "dissolves" in Book 5, where the original says it "splits". The change was not declared.
- **The finale mapping (R15).** The original's adopted Book 30 outcome, roughly A, was not mapped.
- **The record's physical role (R17).** The tally was given a physical effect that only labour can have.
- **Prose contradictions (R3, R6, R10).** The gift practice's labour in B was left unstated, the threshold's reader was both the Reading Hall and a surveyor, and Book 23's abundance cited sealed high channels that Arc IV never repairs.

**W2 is coherent in the part the traces use.** The Hollow Gate's routing, the Book 19–22 exports (hands, the Right, the Table, the declined share, the silt) and the Book 21 drawdown are consistent with one another. The traces run only on that part.

**H1-from-W2: no, not as derived.** Stage B found that the revision changed a Book 30 commitment without declaring it (R1), its summary contradicted its own changes (R2), and it left the original's closed futures contradicted (R3). The corrected H1v4 declares those consequences. It leaves open what causes the Book 15 transfer, what Dorran does in Book 1, how share authority works in Books 5–15, and how the Book 8 and 12 readings are authorised.

**The corrected candidates (W5, H1v4).** The defects found in W2 and H1 were checked in three rounds. The residual items are listed in [06](06-adjudication.md) § F and are minor.

## 2. Did the previous corrections introduce new consequential defects?

**Yes, in three ways.**

1. **The PR #553 Book 21 correction exposed a contradiction and did not fix it.** The Book 15 claim (release causes surfeit) was already in W1. The correction that made the Book 21 physics explicit (release draws the cistern down) made the two claims contradict each other, and the earlier construction did not check the Book 15 claim against it.
2. **The PR #553 Alternative B correction left a gap.** "The binding is of water, not labour" left unstated whether the gift practice's labour was inside the rota. The crisis could not be run until the trace assumed an answer, and that assumption is now an erratum (E3).
3. **This mission's own corrections introduced new contradictions, three times.** Each was caught by the next verifier and fixed in the next round:
   - round 1: erratum E3 contradicted B's text that the gift practice's labour stays uncounted;
   - round 2: W4-1 (surfeit is too much return) and W4-2 (labour holds surfeit off) contradicted each other, and the H1 path reintroduced "upper cistern";
   - round 3: the mechanism 1 heading still reads "Gate dependence" (open).

   The schema also caught a fourth error, a narrative over its 1000-character cap, in the first draft of W3-4.

**What the pattern shows.** The wording most at risk is the statement of a shared mechanism. "Surfeit", "the record", "labour" and "release" each appear in several places, and a correction to one use can leave another use contradicting it. The correction process needs to list every use of a mechanism term before editing one of them.

## 3. Does Arc IV reliably supply the conditions Book 30 requires?

**Largely yes, for the labour and the routing decisions.** The crisis's conditions all come from Arc IV's exports: the hands in the window (Books 6 and 15), the lineage's Right and the four trained apprentices (Book 20), the Gate Table that sets the schedule (Book 22), the declined share's wild water on the orchard reach (Book 21), and the silted Lowfen (Book 21). Every trace uses them, and none has to be invented.

**Not reliably, for three conditions the architecture does not establish:**

- **The high cistern's storage.** B and C need the cistern to take 0.6 to 0.65 CU above its tolerance. Nothing in the architecture bounds that. If it cannot, B and C fail into an orchard flood as A does.
- **The Harrow Lowe gate.** The lowland overflow path depends on it. The original names it; W2 does not. The traces treat it as established, and the scenario flags it.
- **The Compact's clause and the ratifying body.** C's success rests on both, and the architecture names neither the assembly nor the Compact's obligation.

Book 15's physics (R8) is not needed for the Book 30 crisis, so its defect does not affect the traces.

## 4. Do Alternatives A, B and C behave differently under the same crisis?

**Yes, materially, in who bears the cost. Partly, in the water, and mainly in the process.**

| Frozen run (S = 1.7 CU) | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lowfen at the peak | 0.73 CU | 0.75 CU | 0.80 CU |
| High-cistern surfeit above tolerance | 0.2 CU (partial, by consent) | 0.65 CU (binding) | 0.61 CU (binding, ratified) |
| Overflow reaching the orchard | **0.47 CU** | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Deficit after the window | none | 19.8 hand-seasons | 6.6 hand-seasons |
| Who decides the sacrifice | high-terrace households, partly | a lot-drawn Table, in the crisis | the valley, at ratification |

- **A and B/C differ in distribution.** A leaves the flood on the orchard, which volunteered everything. B and C keep the orchard dry by binding the surfeit onto the high terraces.
- **B and C are close in the water.** Their physical outcomes differ by about 0.05 CU of capacity and 0.04 CU of surfeit. They differ in the deficit (a third as large under C), in who decided, and in when the legitimacy conflict arrives.
- **A's failure is coherent.** The high terraces' consent covers only part of the trade, the default schedule protects the cistern, the Compact's gate is voluntary, and pair-hands fail when every community is short together.
- **B's success is not labour's.** If the Compact refuses to pay, B still keeps the orchard dry by raising the surfeit. That is a finding about B, not a rescue.
- **C's failure is branch-shaped.** A misread threshold or a missing ratification returns C to A's outcome.

## 5. Which alternative has the strongest demonstrated causal structure, independent of author preference?

**C, conditionally.** It is the only alternative that meets the lead time (its panel is pre-drawn), pre-authorises the surfeit trade, and leaves a smaller deficit. Its strength depends on two things the architecture does not yet establish: a ratifying assembly, and the Compact's clause. Without them it is A.

**B second.** It succeeds physically, through the trade, and it makes the sacrifice explicit. Its costs are a lead time it cannot shorten and a deficit record it has not built.

**A third.** It fails coherently at the frozen surge, and its failure shows exactly what it lacks: an authority for the trade, and a lever on the lowland gate. At low surge (1.4 CU) A nearly succeeds if the high terraces accept about 0.37 CU above tolerance, so its failure depends on consent, not on structure alone.

Every alternative depends on a physical fact the architecture does not establish: the high cistern's storage. This is the strongest single open dependency in the exercise.

## 6. Which long-range promises are earned, weakened, contradicted or unresolved?

**Earned in the crisis:**

- *Consequential loss.* The declined share's cost returns in its original form, and it lands on its neighbours.
- *Who gives back the water.* Each alternative answers it with explicit bearers and costs, which the traces show.
- *The Turn, changed.* B and C make the Turn binding for a window, and the traces show what that costs.

**Weakened:**

- *Care without debt.* A keeps "nothing owed", but the orchard's labour and loss are owed by no one, which is a different failure. B re-admits debt as a deficit that needs a record. C reduces the deficit to a third of B's and keeps it inside a ratified rule.
- *Lowland obligations.* The Compact's 12 hands are owed under B and C, and voluntary under A. The Harrow Lowe gate is outside every binding clause, so the lowland lever is incomplete under binding too.
- *Hopeful flourishing.* A keeps the practice visible, but a community pays for it. B's flourishing needs one community's fever. C's depends on the ratification having happened.

**Contradicted by the revision:**

- *The plural record.* Under H1, the Reading Hall is the single public keeper from Book 15. That contradicts the original's Book 30 commitment, "no single institution holds the record". H1v4 declares this as a consequence and marks it for the author to confirm.

**Unresolved:**

- The Old Hand's readings between Books 8 and 15.
- Hesper's motive (is it independent of the author's preference for the Turn?).
- The Special Draw Right's revocability.
- The flood's lives.
- Whether the spring intends (the original answers "responds to balance").
- The ratifying assembly.

## 7. What did Auteur's operations support, and what relied on freeform reasoning?

**Supported by Auteur's own operations, or by checks the product provides:**

- **Schema validation** (`ArchitectureCandidate`, schema v2) of W2, the H1 derivations, and each corrected candidate. It caught a real overrun (a narrative over its 1000-character cap). It checks structure, types, lengths and identifiers. It does not check meaning.
- **The review builder** (`build_architecture_review`) recorded, for each change, which components were added, removed or changed, and which were preserved. It is the only check here that compares the candidates field by field, and it was used for every round.
- **The preserved check, as it actually works.** It compares whole entries for equality. An entry is preserved only if its content is unchanged. It does not check whether an unchanged entry still agrees with a changed one. This is a correction to an earlier claim in PR #553, which said it compared identifiers only. Running the check on W2 against H1 showed four unchanged entries (`eng_old_hand`, `ph_5_unmeasured`, `alt_b_allotted`, `alt_c_ratified_trigger`) that mention custody or Hall concepts H1 had changed. Whether they remained valid was for the reviewers to judge, and they did.
- **The H1 scripting method.** `make_h1_path.py` asserts each field it replaces, so a drifted base fails rather than silently changes. It is a repository script, not the Studio product.

**Not supported by Auteur's operations. These relied on freeform reasoning:**

- **Every semantic judgment.** The physics contradictions (R8), the governing variable (R11), the Hall's split (R12), the tally's physical role (R17), the finale mapping (R15), and every correction's wording were found and written by reading.
- **The simulated consequences.** The crisis design, its invented numbers (labelled INV-1 to INV-14), the labour and capacity rules, the routing rules, and the three traces. None of this is computed by a product operation. The arithmetic was rechecked by a script in the record, but the rules are the coordinator's.
- **The reviewer judgments.** The reviews were done by reading packets. The product did not sort, score or check them.

**What this session did not use.** It did not run a Studio canvas, revision or apply operation, and it did not use the browser. The H1 and correction candidates were derived and validated with the product's own schema and review code, outside the canvas. Canvas operations were used in PR #553 and are not repeated here.

**The product's capability gap, in one line.** The product can tell whether a candidate has the right shape and what changed. It cannot tell whether the changed things still agree with the unchanged things they depend on, and it cannot run a consequence through the rules it has.

## 8. Is the next warranted responsibility another construction, a product improvement, an author decision, or no further work?

**An author decision first.** The following decisions change what can be built next, and only the author can make them:

1. Whether the Hollow Gate and the Gate Table are accepted parts of the world.
2. Whether the Harrow Lowe gate exists and is operated by the Compact's factor.
3. What the high cistern can physically store. This is a fact to establish, and the architecture does not provide one.
4. Whether the Compact's grain-labour is owed (B, C) or voluntary (A), and whether the Compact has a seat.
5. Who ratifies the instrument (C), and whether the threshold is read by the Reading Hall alone.
6. Whether the pulse's size is uncertain, which C's threshold requires.
7. Whether the gift practice's shares are inside the window's rota (B).
8. Who records deficits, and whether a record that re-admits debt is accepted.
9. Who holds the Special Draw Right's authority over the floor (A).
10. H1 only: what causes the Book 15 transfer, what Dorran does in Book 1, and whether the revision's additions are sanctioned.

**Then, a bounded construction, if the author answers the first three.** The next construction step is to establish the high cistern's storage and to re-run the traces that depend on it. That is one trace set, not a new campaign.

**No product improvement is warranted now.** The observed failures are of meaning and propagation, which a structural check cannot detect, and the one capability that would be needed, a scenario-consistency check, would need its own design. The "repeated preserved-check trial" option that PR #553 nominated is less compelling than it was: this exercise shows that the preserved check's real limit is not identifiers but dependencies between entries, and a trial of the instruction would not test that.

**No further work on the verification loop.** Three correction rounds have been verified, and the stopping rule applies. Further rounds would cost more than their residual defects are worth, and the residual items are minor and recorded.
