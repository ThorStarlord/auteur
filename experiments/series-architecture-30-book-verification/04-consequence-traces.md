# 04. Consequence traces: Alternatives A, B and C against the frozen crisis

Status: **working / noncanonical.** Each trace runs the frozen scenario in [03](03-shared-crisis.md) (SHA-256 in [00](00-freeze-and-provenance.md)). The frozen run is S = 1.7 CU, w = 0.1 CU, with the labour and routing rules as written there.

**How to read the numbers.** Figures marked **[calc]** follow from the frozen rules. Figures marked **[inf]** are creative inference: the mechanisms are the alternative's own, but the way the actors use them is my reading. Lives are not decided anywhere; the traces report exposure and loss, not deaths.

Each trace follows the mission's chain: initial state → information → choices → labour → Gate routing → physical outcome → human cost → narrative transformation → remaining pressure.

---

## Alternative A: noncoercive coordination by mutual dependence

**1. Initial state.** As [03](03-shared-crisis.md) §4–§5. No binding. The care floor exists, funded from the Special Draw Right and administered by the Gate Table. Pair-hands are standing across the Gate. The Harrow Lowe gate is the Compact's.

**2. Information.** Everyone knows the range (1.4–1.9 CU), the central estimate (1.65) and the silt (0.6 CU) from week -10. Nobody knows the true surge until day D − 3. The tally records care, so each household can see who has been volunteering.

**3. Choices.**
- **Quillmark's Council (Idrun Sallow)** asks the high terraces for volunteers. Twelve of the sixteen free hands come forward. Four families keep their hands for their own Turn duty.
- **The lineage (Ysa Trant's line)** offers six of its ten free skilled hands. It refuses the other four, because the care floor is to be funded from the Right, and it will not carry that cost alone. It accepts a window-only cut in its own draw to fund the floor, which is the real authority behind the floor (A has no authority of its own to cut shares).
- **Lower Ammen** volunteers fourteen of its sixteen free hands, including all four trained apprentices.
- **The orchard terrace** volunteers all twelve of its free hands, because its channel is the one that breaks first.
- **The Mill Compact (Oona Brisk)** declines to supply grain-labour. A has no duty to compel it, and the Compact says the gate and the grain are part of its pending bid. The factor also declines to open the Harrow Lowe gate, which is a voluntary act for the Compact.
- **The Gate Table** sets the default schedule from the gauge: h = 0.4 CU, which protects the high cistern from surfeit. Going above 0.4 needs the high terraces' consent, and A has no mechanism that makes the high terraces consent.
- **Quillmark's Council** asks the high terraces to accept more fever in the pools. The high terraces agree to h = 0.6 CU. The low-lying houses are the ones that accept, and some refuse.

**4. Labour.** Volunteers total 44 hand-seasons. Delivered at the one-week lead factor: 44 × 0.9 = **39.6** [calc]. Capacity: c = 0.6 + 0.2 × 39.6/60 = **0.73 CU** [calc]. T2 is not started, because it is infeasible (§5 of the scenario). Pair-hands do not cover the shortfall: every community is short at once, so no pair has a spare partner on the day. The pairs are on paper only [inf].

**5. Gate routing.** h = 0.6 CU. Lower inflow = 1.7 − 0.6 + 0.1 = **1.2 CU** [calc]. Overflow = 1.2 − 0.73 = **0.47 CU** [calc]. The Harrow Lowe gate is closed, so the overflow reaches the orchard terrace.

**6. Physical outcome.**
- The Lowfen runs at 0.73 CU, which is 0.07 short of the 0.8 that full T1 would give.
- Overflow of 0.47 CU reaches the orchard terrace. It breaks first at the declined household's reach, which has been unrepaired since Book 21 [inf].
- High cistern: 0.2 CU above tolerance, so the low-lying high-terrace houses have pools and fever for a part of the window.

**7. Human cost (exposure, not deaths).**
- The orchard terrace floods over 0.47 CU. Stored harvest, the orchard, and the homes on the breach reach are exposed. The declined household's neighbours bear it first.
- Fever and pools in the low-lying high-terrace houses that accepted the trade. The households that refused were not exposed.
- Volunteers are exhausted. The labour was given without an obligation, so no one's exhaustion is compensated.
- Lives are not decided (author-reserved).

**8. Narrative transformation.** A's care is real, and it is visible in the tally: a lot of people came forward, the lineage gave what it chose to give, and the orchard gave everything it had. But the sacrifice has no assigned bearer. The high terraces' consent covers 0.2 CU of the trade. The rest of the overflow is left to the orchard, because the only lever that could move it, the Compact's gate, is a voluntary act. The Gate Table could set the schedule but could not make the sacrifice. The Book 21 cost returns in its original form: a declined household's exit lands on its neighbours.

**9. Remaining pressure.**
- The Compact's bid for lowland water rights now includes the gate, so the Compact has a bargaining chip that it did not have before.
- The Special Draw Right is seen to be a cost to be cut, not a claim, which weakens the lineage's agency and invites the craft withdrawal A already names.
- The orchard's losses force the question of who owes what to the lower terraces, and A has no answer except voluntary care.

**10. Mission items.**
- *Who volunteers:* most of the high-terrace, Lower Ammen and orchard free hands; the lineage gives six of ten.
- *Who can refuse:* every household and the Compact. Refusal costs nothing except the trade it would have made.
- *How shortages are redistributed:* they are not. The shortfall (20.4 hand-seasons below the 60 that T1 needs) falls on whoever is still working, and the orchard is still working.
- *Pair-hands in a correlated crisis:* they fail. No partner has spare hands.
- *The care floor:* it keeps the draw of hand-light households through the window, and it is funded by a cut the lineage accepted. The Table administers the floor, but only with the lineage's consent.
- *Scheduling versus voluntary cooperation:* the schedule protects the high cistern by default. The trade for the orchard depends on consent, and consent covered 0.2 CU.
- *Lower terraces and the Compact:* the orchard is included, and it bears the cost. The Compact is not included in the sacrifice, and it keeps its gate.

**11. Sensitivity.**
- **A at S = 1.4:** the default schedule (h = 0.4) gives overflow 0.37. With the same high-terrace consent (h = 0.6), overflow falls to **0.17**. A reaches zero overflow only if the high terraces accept h ≈ 0.77 or more, which is about 0.37 above tolerance [calc]. So at low surge, A fails or nearly succeeds depending entirely on consent.
- **A at S = 1.9:** with h = 0.6, overflow is **0.67**. The Compact's gate (up to 0.8 CU) would absorb it, but only if the factor opens it.

## Alternative B: allotted binding rota under a bounded authority

**1. Initial state.** As [03](03-shared-crisis.md). B is the chosen finale direction (a hypothetical), decided in Book 30. The Long Rota applies to the window. The Gate Table is redrawn by lot.

**2. Information.** The same as A. The lot-drawn panel is seated three weeks in, so the rota is not fully effective until then.

**3. Choices.**
- **The Gate Table** is seated by lot at week -7 (three weeks' lead). It applies the published rule: hands owed in proportion to draw, the floor protected, and the first repairs to the channels that serve hand-light households.
- **The Long Rota** assigns every free hand and the Compact's 12 hands. Quillmark's 16 free hands are owed in full, because they draw the most. The lineage's 10 free skilled hands are owed. Lower Ammen's 16 (including the four apprentices) are owed. The orchard's 12 are owed.
- **The Compact** owes its grain-labour under the binding clause. Oona Brisk pays it. The Harrow Lowe gate is not part of the binding clause, so the gate stays with the factor.
- **The declined household** refuses again. It forfeits its window draw, and its share (0.1 CU) runs wild.
- **The lineage** complies. Its refusal would have cost window draw, which is the price of its agency under B.
- **The Gate Table** sets h by its binding rule: the least h that keeps the lower channels within capacity, with the public priority applied. The high cistern takes the surplus.

**4. Labour.** Owed: 66 hand-seasons (54 free, 12 Compact). Delivered at the three-week lead factor 0.7: 46.2 [calc]. Capacity: c = 0.6 + 0.2 × 46.2/60 = **0.75 CU** [calc]. The deficit is 66 − 46.2 = **19.8 hand-seasons** [calc]. Under B each household's shortfall is a deficit, and deficits must be repaid.

**5. Gate routing.** To keep the lower channels within 0.75 CU, with the declined household's 0.1 CU of wild water, h must be at least 1.7 + 0.1 − 0.754 = 1.046. The Table sets **h = 1.05 CU** [calc]. Lower inflow = 1.7 − 1.05 + 0.1 = 0.75 CU. Overflow = **0.00 CU** [calc].

**6. Physical outcome.**
- The orchard is dry. The Lowfen runs at 0.75 CU.
- The high cistern takes 0.65 CU above its tolerance of 0.4 CU. That means pools and fever in the high terraces.
- The declined household's share runs wild on its own reach, and it is absorbed by the channel [calc].

**7. Human cost (exposure, not deaths).**
- **Quillmark's high terraces** bear the fever, in the low-lying houses first. They are the most-drawing households. The Turn's season is disrupted by the pools.
- **Hand-light households** in Lower Ammen carry deficits. Repaying 19.8 hand-seasons falls on them after the window.
- **The lot-seated Table members** carry the decision. They did not choose the job. The rule they applied is public, but the outcome is theirs.
- **The declined household** loses its window draw by refusing.
- Lives are not decided (author-reserved).

**8. Narrative transformation.** B makes the sacrifice explicit and assigns it, and that is its real achievement. The valley owes the labour, and the high terraces, who draw most, pay the surfeit. The gift practice's labour is counted for the window, which answers the question Stage A raised (R3): under B, yes, it is. The valley's first binding public duty since the Hall, and the Hall's echo, is what the Turn's families will hear in it [inf].

**9. Remaining pressure.**
- **The deficits need a record.** Under B the Table keeps no record. The Reading Hall keeps the public record, so the 19.8 hand-seasons of deficit must be entered there. That re-admits a debt ledger for one window, which is the cost B names [calc, inf].
- **The sunset** ends the Table's authority, but the fever and the deficits outlast it.
- **Quillmark's grievance** is now a precedent: the most-drawing households paid the trade.
- **The Compact's contribution** was delivered only because it was owed. Whether it accepts that owing is the open author question.

**10. Mission items.**
- *How the rota allocates labour:* by a published formula, proportional to draw, with the floor. Quillmark owes most, and it cannot provide more than its 16 free hands.
- *What happens when households cannot provide the assigned hands:* the shortfall becomes a deficit (19.8 hand-seasons). Refusal forfeits window draw.
- *Whether refusal is meaningfully possible:* yes, for the refuser, who loses window draw. But the physical cost of refusal (the declined household's 0.1 CU) is socialised: the Table absorbs it by raising h. The high cistern pays for a refusal it did not cause.
- *How the Gate Table exercises its temporary authority:* by setting h = 1.05 CU, which is binding on the high terraces' water. That is a far larger power than the Table had under Arc IV.
- *Whether the labour supply is sufficient before the pulse:* no. T1 is 13.8 hand-seasons short. B therefore relies on the surfeit trade, not on labour.
- *What limits institutional capture:* the lot seats and the sunset. They do not stop the Table from binding the high cistern for the window.
- *How the Compact's contribution is secured:* by binding it as grain-labour. The gate is outside the binding clause.
- *Which people bear the cost:* the high terraces (fever), hand-light households (deficits), the Table members (the decision), the declined household (window draw).

**11. Compact refusal branch.** If Oona Brisk refuses the grain-labour, delivered labour is 54 × 0.7 = 37.8 [calc], capacity c = 0.73, and h must be at least 1.07, which the Table sets at **1.08 CU**. Excess surfeit is 0.68 CU. The orchard still stays dry. So B's physical success does not depend on the Compact's labour. It depends on the high terraces' surfeit. That is a finding about B, not a rescue.

**12. Sensitivity.**
- **B at S = 1.4:** h ≥ 0.75, so the excess is **0.35 CU**. The trade is still needed at low surge.
- **B at S = 1.9:** h ≥ 1.25, so the excess is **0.85 CU**.

## Alternative C: ratified trigger

**1. Initial state.** As [03](03-shared-crisis.md). In Books 27–29 the valley ratified an instrument with four parts: normal seasons follow A; a threshold on the Balance Stones' central estimate of the surge at week -10, published by the Reading Hall; when crossed, B's rota applies for the window, with a pre-drawn panel of lot seats; and the Compact's grain-labour clause, which is included in the same instrument.

**Assumptions the trace must state.** Ratification is by an assembly the architecture does not yet name (Stage A R16). The Compact's clause is assumed ratified. The threshold level 1.5 CU is assumed. These are the trace's assumptions, not the scenario's, and they are the places where C is weakest.

**2. Information.** The Reading Hall publishes the central estimate (1.65 CU) and the range (1.4–1.9 CU) at week -10. Hesper's surveyed silt (0.6 CU) is published by the Council. Both are public from week -10.

**3. Choices.**
- **The threshold check.** The central estimate, 1.65 CU, is at or above 1.5 CU, so the threshold is crossed at week -10 [calc].
- **The dispute.** The Council disputes which figure should count. Hesper's silt measure and the range's lower bound (1.4 CU) are both below the threshold. The instrument says *central estimate*, so the activation stands. The dispute is recorded and not resolved. It is a legitimacy friction, delayed, not settled [inf].
- **Activation.** Automatic on publication. The pre-drawn panel is seated in one week.
- **The labour.** The Long Rota applies. Quillmark's, the lineage's, Lower Ammen's and the orchard's free hands are owed, and so is the Compact's grain-labour.
- **The Gate Table** (the pre-drawn panel) applies the binding rule.

**4. Labour.** Owed: 66 hand-seasons. Delivered at the one-week lead factor 0.9: (54 + 12) × 0.9 = **59.4** [calc]. Capacity: c = 0.6 + 0.2 × 59.4/60 = **0.80 CU** [calc]. The deficit is 66 − 59.4 = **6.6 hand-seasons** [calc]. T1 is 0.6 hand-seasons short of its 60, so the Lowfen reaches 0.80 CU only to within rounding. T2 is still infeasible.

**5. Gate routing.** To keep the lower channels within 0.80 CU, with the declined household's 0.1 CU, h must be at least 1.7 + 0.1 − 0.798 = 1.002. The Table sets **h = 1.01 CU** [calc]. Lower inflow = 1.7 − 1.01 + 0.1 = 0.79 CU. Overflow = **0.00 CU** [calc].

**6. Physical outcome.**
- The orchard is dry. The Lowfen runs at 0.80 CU, its best capacity in the window.
- The high cistern takes 0.61 CU above its tolerance.
- The declined household's share runs wild on its own reach, as in B.

**7. Human cost (exposure, not deaths).**
- **Quillmark's low-lying houses** bear fever in the pools. The excess (0.61 CU) is a little smaller than B's (0.65), because the labour arrived earlier.
- **Hand-light households** carry a deficit of 6.6 hand-seasons, a third of B's.
- **The declined household** loses its window draw.
- **The Reading Hall** is exposed to the dispute about which figure counts. The gauge is no longer only informing; it triggers (see §8).
- Lives are not decided (author-reserved).

**8. Narrative transformation.** C moves the drama earlier. The decisive choice, that the high terraces accept surfeit for the window, was made at ratification, and the Book 30 crisis tests it rather than making it. The reading dispute is the legitimacy conflict, and it is delayed until week -10. The gauge changes its Book 23 function from informing to triggering. That change is costed in the finale file and is an author decision.

**9. Remaining pressure.**
- **The threshold is now an authority.** The reading of the central estimate decides whether the sacrifice applies. The dispute proves this was a live risk.
- **The lead time was the advantage.** C's panel is pre-drawn, so it avoids B's three-week gap. That is the one thing ratification buys that the crisis cannot.
- **The Compact's clause** is still the weakest link: it was assumed ratified, and its grain counted as owed.
- **The rule's expiry** after the window is set in advance, but the fever and the deficits outlast it.

**10. What the dormant rule solves that A and B cannot.**
- A fails because no body can make the trade for the orchard, and its labour arrives late for no reason except that no one was ready.
- B succeeds physically, but its panel is seated three weeks in and its trade is decided in the crisis by a lot-chosen Table.
- C's advantage is narrow but real: the trade is authorised before the crisis, and the panel is ready at week -9. That is the causal difference: the sacrifice is decided by the valley and the crisis reveals it.

**11. Failure branch: the activation condition is not met.** The Reading Hall publishes the lower bound, 1.4 CU, by error, or the central estimate is below 1.5 CU (say, 1.45). The threshold is not crossed. The rule stays dormant. The valley runs A, and the outcome is **A's frozen trace**: overflow 0.47 CU reaching the orchard unless the Compact opens its gate. The dispute about the reading then arrives during the crisis, when there is no time to settle it. The dormant rule solved nothing in this branch.

**12. Ratification-failure branch.** If the instrument is never ratified (calm years), C reduces to A. The outcome is again A's trace.

**13. Compact clause branch.** If the Compact's clause is ratified but its factor refuses to pay, delivered labour is 54 × 0.9 = 48.6 [calc], capacity c = 0.76, and h must be at least 1.04. The excess is 0.64 CU. The orchard still stays dry.

**14. Sensitivity.**
- **C at S = 1.4:** h ≥ 0.71, so the excess is **0.31 CU**.
- **C at S = 1.9:** h ≥ 1.20, so the excess is **0.80 CU**.
