## 1. Inherited state at the end of Book 18

The state is taken from what Books 1–15 already established. Each row cites its source. One structure the original does not name is the Hollow Gate, which the table marks as a construction addition.

| Source | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Book 6 | A share draws water and owes hands. The Turn is the rota by which share-holders return labour. Compact-purchased draw carries no hands. Sales were capped, not reversed. |
| Books 9–11 | Channel-maintenance know-how sits with old lineages (Ysa Trant's). |
| Book 15 | The one-season Unmeasured Return in Lower Ammen gave labour uncounted. Its cost fell on the high cistern, which showed surfeit and fever. Families with few hands called the trial unfair to them. |
| Book 15 | The stewardship tally: a record of care, not a debt, kept by the Council so the high terraces do not surfeit. One high-terrace lineage left the Council over the cost. |
| Book 15 | The Reading Hall hosts public readings. Three readings (debt, gift, contract) claim force, and none binds. |
| Construction addition (not in the original) | The Hollow Gate is not named in the original. This construction adds it as the sluice that divides the high cistern's release between the upper and lower channels. The Council has operated it since the Turn (Arc II). The original names the Book 15 cost (surfeit on the high cistern) but not its mechanism. This construction reads the surfeit as an effect of the release the trial's schedule carried to the lower channels. That reading is an assumption the author may reject. |

At the end of Book 18 the valley has four properties that matter for what follows:

1. **Unequal hands.** Few-hands share-holders owe more labour than they can give. Lineage households carry the skill. Compact draw carries none.
2. **A record without a binding.** The tally counts care. It cannot yet say what anyone owes.
3. **Two proven practices.** The counted Turn (high terraces) keeps the high cistern from surfeit. The uncounted gift practice (Lower Ammen) keeps the lower channels working. Each practice's success depends on the other's constraint being absent.
4. **One outlet.** Both practices draw on the same Hollow Gate, whose schedule the Council sets.

## 2. Arc IV, Conflict A — The Counted Rota (Books 19–21) [Working]

Chain: inherited state → new possibility → meaningful choice → consequence → changed state → export.

**New possibility.** The tally can be netted against hands owed. Care given in the trial could be treated as hands already returned. Draw could also be cut in proportion to hands a household cannot return.

**Trigger (Book 19).** In the thaw the Lowfen channel, which carries Lower Ammen's wells, breaks. Repair needs roughly one rota's worth of labour for a season. Few-hands share-holders cannot meet their share. Lineage crews can do the work, but only by giving up their own rota time.

**Choice (Book 20, contested).** Three options:
- (a) Net the tally against hands owed. Care becomes a credit against debt, and the debt reading returns through the tally.
- (b) Lineage crews repair the Lowfen in exchange for a Special Draw Right, so skill claims draw.
- (c) Draw falls in proportion to the hands a share-holder cannot return. The household's rota shrinks along with its water.

**Working outcome (a construction choice, not an author decision).** The Council adopts (b) and (c) and rejects (a). The tally stays care-only in name. Lineage crews repair the Lowfen. Ysa's lineage receives a Special Draw Right, conditional on training apprentices from outside the lineage and revocable by the Council. Hand-light share-holders have their draw converted in proportion to their deficit.

**Consequence (Book 21).** One few-hands household refuses the conversion and forfeits its share. Its water goes wild and runs down the lowest Lowfen terrace into a neighbour's orchard. No one has acted as a villain here. The cost comes from a household's exit from the rota, and it lands on the terrace below.

**Changed state at the end of Arc IV.**
- Labour is concentrated. Lineage crews carry channel repair. Households that can meet the rota carry the rest. A household may decline, but declining forfeits its water and exposes the terrace below.
- Draw is no longer set by shares alone. For the first time in the series, a household's hands set part of its draw.
- The tally is care-only in name and a measurement in practice. Whether it is a record or a debt is now a live legitimacy question.

**Exports.**
1. A counting principle: draw set by hands for some households. The finale must keep it, reverse it, or floor it.
2. A skill claim on draw, with a training condition.
3. An ambiguous tally whose status the next generation inherits.
4. A declined share running wild at the lowest Lowfen terrace.

**Pressure type.** Distributional and feasibility. Engine: Ledger (what counts toward draw) acting through the Source (channel labour). This exercises loop L1.

**Reskin check.** Could it arise without Books 1–5? Yes, and it does not need the Hall. It traces to Book 6 (the rota and purchased draw) and Book 15 (the tally and the trial). The Special Draw Right is the nearest candidate for a new monopoly: power by skill (renewal test R2). The guard is the training condition plus revocability, which keeps the Right a live question rather than a settled hierarchy.

## 3. Arc IV, Conflict B — Two practices, one gate (Books 21–22) [Working]

**New possibility.** The Hollow Gate's schedule can be read from the spring's balance at the Gate, using a wooden gauge, rather than set by the Council's counted schedule alone.

**Trigger (Book 21).** A dry spring. The counted practice wants to hold water at the Gate to protect the high cistern. The gift practice needs release at need to keep the lower channels from running wild. Both are right for their own terraces.

**Choice (Book 21).** Three options:
- (a) The Council's counted schedule binds the lower valley's gift practice. This counts the lower valley's water, which repeats the Hall's pattern.
- (b) Release at need regardless. The high terraces absorb the surplus.
- (c) A Gate Table with rotating seats: one for each practice, and one drawn by lot from households without lineage craft. The table sets the schedule from the gauge reading. It has power over the schedule only, and no authority over the record, the shares, or either practice.

**Consequence.** Option (b) is tried for one season. Release at need in a dry spring draws the high cistern down. Its high channels dry for a season, one lineage household's spring-fed channel fails, and the lower channels flush once and then silt when the release stops. In Book 22 the valley forms option (c). The Reading Hall publishes the gauge reading. The lower valley accepts it because (a) is closed. The high terraces accept it because (b) cost them a season.

**Changed state.** A coordinating institution now exists between communities. It has a rule that separates schedule from record: the Table sets the schedule, and the Reading Hall keeps the record and the reading. Two practices sit at one instrument, and neither is abolished. The wooden gauge comes before the Balance Stones of Book 23.

**Exports.**
1. A coordinating body that is neither the Council nor the Hall.
2. A separation of schedule authority (the Table) from reading authority (the Reading Hall). This is the first capture guard in the construction.
3. A schedule that depends on a gauge reading. A number now decides who gets water, which is a risk.
4. A template for mutual dependence: each practice needs the other's gate schedule.

**Pressure type.** Coordination and legitimacy. Engines: Source (the physics at the Gate) and Old Hand (which practice's reading of the balance counts). This exercises loops L1 and L2.

**Reskin check.** The Table could become the Hall again if it absorbed record or share authority. The construction denies both explicitly. Its power is limited to a schedule the physics can check.

## 5. Book 23 — what the Brightwater generation inherits [Derived]

The Brightwater generation inherits three things that are easy to confuse. The construction keeps them separate.

- **Genuine abundance, but seasonal.** After two good thaws, a real surplus sits in the high cistern for two reasons. The Table holds water back in dry spells instead of releasing it, and lineage crews have sealed the leaks in the high channels that used to feed wild water (Book 20). The surplus is real and seasonal.
- **Abundance unevenly distributed.** The surplus sits in the high cistern. Draw there belongs mostly to the best-handed houses and to the lineage's Right. Hand-light houses have drawn less since Book 20. Brightwater shows the surplus for display and sale, and the water on show was drawn by the hands that already had it.
- **A belief that abundance needs no work.** Hesper, about fifteen, and her cohort came to adolescence after Book 20, when the cistern was full and the tally read "in credit". Hesper herself was born about Book 13. They cannot see lineage labour, and they experience the Turn as a tax. This is a belief about the water. It is not a fact about the spring.

**Preserved from the original.** Brightwater still goes ahead under the Balance Stones' limit. Festival labour is still counted as a visible gift. Hesper still takes an apprenticeship with the Council. Quillmark land still feeds Tarn's descendants.

**Causal chain for Book 23.** Surfeit (Book 15) → gate schedule (Book 21) → surplus (Books 22–23) → festival draw (Book 23). The original's chain runs across Books 15 and 23. This construction makes the middle links explicit.

## 6. Book 30 — the Long Return as a causal test [Derived, with the mechanisms in 02]

**The physical event.** The deep store sends a pulse. The Balance Stones, cut in Book 23, show that its date is approaching. The pulse arrives through the Hollow Gate and reaches the Lowfen and the lower channels.

**Two physical needs before the date.**
1. **Channel labour.** The Lowfen and the lower channels must be rebuilt in time. That takes a rota's worth of labour in a short window.
2. **Routing.** The Gate must split the pulse between the high cistern and the lower channels. Whichever channel takes the surge risks flooding. The routing is a physical fact. Who floods is the valley's choice. **[Open]** whether the flood costs lives.

**Causal inheritance.** The finale cannot be solved by Arc V's ideals alone. It needs the two exports Arc IV left. Need (1) requires hands that Conflict A converted or let go. Need (2) requires a schedule that two practices can agree, which is Conflict B's export.

**Why earlier forms were insufficient.** Each earlier form has a named failure.

| Earlier form | Named failure |
|---|---|
| The Hall's sealed record (Books 1–5) | Control without hands. The spring needed labour that the record did not count. |
| The mandatory Turn (Books 6–11) | The rota was bypassed by purchased draw, and skill concentrated. Draw came apart from hands. |
| The voluntary gift practice (Book 15) | It worked for the lower channels and cost the high cistern. It could not hold the upstream surplus. |
| The stewardship tally (Book 15, Arc IV) | A record without a binding. It cannot move hands. |
| The Gate Table (Book 22) | A schedule without labour. It cannot bring hands to the channels. |

The Book 30 mechanism must therefore do three things that no earlier form did. It must connect the Compact's unhanded draw, or settle it. It must make care reliable without a ledger that binds by default. It must bound any binding, so that binding does not turn into custody.

**The governing variable.** The brief's core question is *who gives return labour, and who is counted for it?* The three mechanisms answer it differently:
- **A** (noncoercive): draw no longer depends on hands. Care is counted, but nothing is owed.
- **B** (allotted, binding): hands are owed in proportion to draw. Inside the rebuild window a shortfall becomes a deficit to repay, not a cut in water, so Arc IV's conversion is suspended for the window and not repealed. The rule is floor-protected and time-bound.
- **C** (ratified trigger): the rule that counts hands is decided in advance and activated only when a published threshold is crossed.

The mechanisms are developed in [02](02-finale-alternatives.md).

**Constraints from the mission.** The pulse is physical and was predicted from Book 23. It is not a new antagonist and is not a convenient reason for a conclusion. The finale decision happens in Book 30 itself for A and B. For C it happens earlier, in Books 27–29.
