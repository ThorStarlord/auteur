Status: **working / noncanonical.** Reads the three traces in [04](04-consequence-traces.md) against the frozen scenario in [03](03-shared-crisis.md). It does not score literary quality, and it does not choose an alternative for the author.

## 2. Are the outcomes materially different?

**A and B/C differ materially, and the difference is who bears the cost.** Under A the orchard bears a flood of 0.47 CU, and the high terraces bear almost nothing. Under B and C the orchard is dry, and the high terraces bear fever in their pools. The same surge is absorbed in two different places.

**B and C differ less than their prose suggests, in the water.** Their physical outcomes differ by about 0.05 CU of Lowfen capacity and 0.04 CU of surfeit. What differs is process and timing:
- *Who decided.* B's trade was decided in the crisis by lot-chosen officials. C's trade was decided at ratification, and the crisis tested it.
- *How much was owed.* B leaves a deficit of 19.8 hand-seasons, which must be recorded and repaid. C leaves 6.6, a third as much.
- *When the legitimacy conflict arrives.* In B it arrives during the window, when the Table is binding the high cistern. In C it is a reading dispute at week -10, which is earlier and is still unresolved.

So the answer to the mission's question is: **materially different in distribution and in authority, not materially different in the water between B and C.** C's advantage is real but narrow, and it is an advantage of timing and authorisation, not of capacity.

## 3. Where each alternative fails

**A fails, and the failure is coherent.**
- *No body can make the trade.* The high terraces' consent covered 0.2 CU of the 0.6 CU shift. The default schedule protects the high cistern, so nothing moves the rest of the trade to the high terraces. A's failure is structural: the sacrifice has no bearer.
- *The lowland gate is voluntary.* Opening the Harrow Lowe gate would absorb all 0.47 CU, and the factor refuses. A has no lever on it.
- *Pair-hands fail on the day.* Every community is short together, so no pair has a spare partner.
- *The labour arrives late without a cause.* The volunteers are real, but nothing made them ready before the window opened.
- *The care floor is funded by the lineage's consent, not by the Table's authority.* The lineage accepted a window-only cut in its own draw. The floor's real authority is the lineage's agreement, which is exactly the kind of dependence A says it does not create.
- *Sensitivity.* At low surge (1.4 CU) A's failure depends on consent: the high terraces need to accept about 0.37 CU above tolerance for the orchard to be safe. A is not structurally doomed at low surge, but it fails at the frozen surge and at high surge.

**B fails in three places, and one of them is structural.**
- *Lead time.* The lot-drawn panel takes three weeks. Delivered labour is 46.2 of 66, so the Lowfen reaches only 0.75 CU, and T1 is 13.8 hand-seasons short. B cannot create labour that the lead time has lost. It succeeds only through the surfeit trade.
- *The surfeit trade is the load-bearing mechanism.* B's success does not depend on the Compact: if the Compact refuses, the Table sets h at 1.08 CU instead of 1.05 CU, and the orchard is still dry. A reader should therefore not credit B's physical success to its labour rules.
- *The deficit needs a record.* Under B the Table keeps no record, so the 19.8 hand-seasons of deficit must be entered by the Reading Hall, which keeps the public record. This is the debt ledger that Stage A (R4) predicted, and the trace shows the need for it.
- *Refusal is meaningful for the refuser, but its physical cost is socialised.* The declined household loses its window draw, and its 0.1 CU of wild water is absorbed by raising h. The high cistern pays for a refusal it did not cause.

**C fails in three places, and each is an unassumed element.**
- *Ratification.* C works only if the valley ratified the instrument, by an assembly the architecture does not yet name (Stage A R16). If ratification fails in calm years, C is A, and the outcome is A's flood.
- *The threshold's reading.* The rule depends on which figure counts, the central estimate or the range's lower bound. A misread (1.4 instead of 1.65 CU) gives the failure branch in which the rule stays dormant and the valley runs A. The dispute is then in the crisis, when no time is left to settle it.
- *The Compact's clause.* If the factor does not pay the grain-labour, delivered labour falls to 48.6 hand-seasons, and the surfeit rises to 0.64 CU. The orchard still stays dry, but the clause carries more than it should.
- *The high cistern's storage is unestablished.* All three alternatives rely on a surfeit trade of 0.6 to 0.65 CU above tolerance (for B and C), which the architecture never bounds. If the high cistern cannot physically hold that, B and C fail into an orchard flood as A does.

## 4. Distribution, agency, distinctiveness, promises

**Distribution of sacrifice.** A: the orchard, which volunteered everything. B: the high terraces' fever and the hand-light households' deficits. C: the high terraces' fever, with smaller deficits, and the sacrifice authorised in advance.

**Independence of characters and institutions.** A is strongest, because every household chose. C is middling, because agency is exercised at ratification and then reduced at the trigger. B is weakest in the window: the lineage complies to keep its Right, Lower Ammen's hand-light houses are bound to deficits, and the lot-drawn Table decides under a published rule it did not write.

**Distinctiveness of the conflict.**
- A: volunteers against the Compact's gate. The conflict is negotiation with a lowland actor.
- B: the high terraces against a binding Table. The conflict is about who pays, and it is the Hall's echo in the valley's own words.
- C: a ratification debate, then a reading dispute. The conflict is about the instrument, not the water.
These three are materially distinct as dramatic conflicts.

**Consistency with earlier victories.** A's volunteers echo Book 15's gift practice, and the orchard's flood echoes the Book 21 cost of a declined share. B echoes the Hall in its binding public duty. C's gauge changes from informing to triggering, which is the change the original's Book 23 function did not anticipate.

**Hopeful flourishing.** A keeps the practice visible, but a community pays for it. B's flourishing requires one community's fever. C's hope depends on the ratification having happened. The reader promise is kept in each case only by making the cost visible, which the traces do.

**Care without debt.** A keeps "nothing owed", but the orchard's labour and loss were owed by no one, which is a different failure. B re-admits debt as a deficit for one window, and its record is the proof. C reduces the deficit to a third of B's and keeps it inside a ratified rule. This is the same ranking the construction gave in PR #553, but the trace shows the mechanism behind it.

**Lowland obligations.** The Compact's 12 hands are owed under B and C, and voluntary under A. The Harrow Lowe gate is outside every binding clause, under all three. So the lowland lever is incomplete under binding too. The construction's earlier claim that B "reaches lowland draw" is true only for labour, not for the gate.

**Long-range payoffs.** A opens the Compact's gate as a bargaining chip for its pending bid. B opens the deficit record and the Reading Hall's role as keeper. C opens the threshold's authority. Each leaves a future arc that is credible and different.

## 5. What the crisis says about the architecture

- **Arc IV supplies the crisis's initial conditions.** The hands (Books 6 and 15), the lineage's Right and its apprentices (Book 20), the Table (Book 22), and the declined share (Book 21) are all used. In that sense Arc IV reliably supplies what Book 30 needs (PR #553's question 3).
- **The physical trade is the decisive mechanism for B and C.** Labour cannot close the gap on its own, so the sacrifice is what makes the outcome. That is a stronger claim than the construction made before.
- **A fails coherently.** Its failure is not a rescue by compulsion, and it is not an arbitrary loss. It shows which mechanisms A lacks: an authority for the trade, and a lever on the lowland gate.
- **Two construction additions are load-bearing and unestablished.** The Hollow Gate is used in every trace, and it is an addition to the original. The Harrow Lowe gate is inherited from the original but absent from W2. The high cistern's storage is in neither.

## 8. Verdict on the causal structure

- **Strongest demonstrated causal structure, independent of preference:** C, but only conditionally. It is the only alternative that meets the lead time and pre-authorises the trade. Its strength depends on a ratifying body and a Compact clause that the architecture does not yet establish.
- **Second:** B. It succeeds physically, through the surfeit trade, and it makes the sacrifice explicit. Its costs are a record it has not built and a lead time it cannot shorten.
- **Third:** A. It fails coherently at the frozen surge, and its failure teaches what it lacks.

The ranking is about causal structure in this scenario. It is not a recommendation. The author decides.
