## Evidence assessment

### Context isolation and reconstruction

**BOUNDED DEVIATION, not contamination.** Session B's path error was logged and
corrected before story construction; its initial output contained no story
facts. Session C froze the pre-source snapshot before raw artifact inspection
and did not read prior evidence files. Both later workers performed a declared
broad reconstruction after the required snapshot. That reconstruction was
necessary for detailed story creation and ground-truth comparison, so it is not
evidence that Auteur alone carried every creative decision. It does not invalidate
the category-A surface observations, but it bounds the stronger product claim.

The surfaces were adequate for the selected Book-3 probes after the worker had
already identified the targets. They were not adequate to plan responsibly
without the later directional lookup and broader reconstruction.

### Fixture quality

**BOUNDED WEAKNESS.** The trilogy has genuine cross-Book state changes and
causal pressure; it is not merely three isolated plots or a fully written master
plan. Book 3's detailed realization was not present in the SeriesIdentity.
However, the low-salience label, expected payoff markers, named commitments,
and probe questions disclose much of the intended benchmark. The probes therefore
show recoverability of selected, already-signposted meanings more strongly than
naturalistic relevance discovery.

### Representation and persistence

**Representation gap: not demonstrated. Persistence gap: not demonstrated.**
The tested meanings exist in accepted realization transitions, accepted Series
Direction, current-state/history projections, Global Map, repeated context,
Focus inputs, and continuity review. Accepted facts survive across Books and
the rebuild retains current and superseded values. This evidence does not prove
that every rich SeriesIdentity meaning is consumed by every review surface.

### Knowledge asymmetry death test

**NOT SATISFIED.** The artifacts use `knowledge` attributes and the prose says
“knowledge asymmetry,” but they do not encode incompatible character beliefs or
a character belief that differs from accepted world truth. Brann and Lio are
represented as evidence holders/witnesses, and Nera is protected, but custody,
protection, and public evidence are not epistemic positions. The Book-3 probe
asks how to protect and test evidence; it does not require a decision that
differs according to what distinct characters believe or know.

This is best classified as an experiment-instantiation failure for that
requirement, with insufficient evidence to attribute it to Auteur's product
representation. It is not evidence for a new ontology.

### Relationship path dependence

**PATH HISTORY HELPFUL BUT NOT NECESSARY.** The accepted path
`conditional alliance → costly mutual reliance` is present, and the Focus
rationale refers to it. But the chosen Book-3 question and recommendation could
plausibly be made from the current state “costly mutual reliance” plus the
worker-supplied premise of public exposure. The evidence does not include a
counterfactual showing that the earlier path changed the available decision.

### Low-salience to high-salience and delayed setup

**Partially supported / bounded.** Brann's fact is stored as an ordinary
accepted fact and later becomes corroborating evidence; the persistence chain is
real. Yet its low-salience status and Book-3 payoff were explicitly encoded, and
the worker deliberately selected it as a test target. This is evidence of
cross-Book recoverability under scaffolding, not a clean test of spontaneous
reactivation.

Nera's protection obligation and the bell-reef setup remain open across Books
and constrain Book 3. Delayed pressure is supported, but the same pre-labelled
fixture and targeted probe reduce the strength of the naturalistic claim.

### Selection and overload

**Selection gap not demonstrated; no overload at tested density.** The accepted
facts, 27-entry Global Map, three pressure groups, and default review outputs
were usable for this case. The successful selection was worker-directed around
three known targets, so it is not general selection success. Detailed output was
large but no material orientation failure was observed. Neither result supports
general scale success.

### Integration / projection

**Bounded INTEGRATION_PROJECTION seam demonstrated.** Rich canonical
`SeriesIdentity` diagnostics remain unaware of later accepted lived-history
payoff consequences, while the accepted-history/continuity path can project
those consequences. This is a real architectural separation in the repository,
not a demonstrated absence of the underlying concepts. It is not, by itself,
an implementation authorization.

### Revision stress

**Revision evidence is substantially supported.** Session R changed exactly one
semantic Book-1 transition, preserved revision 1, created revision 2 in the
same lineage, and left Book-2/Book-3 accepted payloads unchanged. Rebuild kept
Book 3's current accepted value while reporting the Book-2 before-state mismatch.
The raw impact output reports Book 1 fresh/contradictory and Books 2 and 3
stale/contradictory in review order; continuity review distinguishes affected
accepted artifacts from the reconciliation boundary and performs no repair.
Direct and transitive impact are therefore supported, as are authority
preservation and the affected-versus-review distinction.

## Strongest defensible claim

In this one synthetic, architecture-only trilogy, the tested Auteur surfaces
preserved accepted state and history across three Books and a post-freeze
semantic revision, and supported bounded reconstruction of several deliberately
selected older pressures without observed overload or authority leakage; the
evidence leaves bounded integration/projection and naturalistic-probe gaps and
does not establish knowledge asymmetry, human value, prose quality, or scale.

## Claims that must not be made

Do not claim general trilogy-product validation, natural workflow reachability,
human-author value, reader or prose quality, 50/100/300-Book scalability,
population robustness, spontaneous relevance selection, or successful
knowledge-asymmetry handling. Do not call the canonical/accepted-history seam a
new ontology requirement, and do not treat revision impact detection as
automatic downstream repair.
