# Working narrative architecture (an AI proposal, not accepted story)

## Summary

WORKING PROPOSAL / NOT ACCEPTED. A provisional abstract architecture for an optimistic thirty-Book series about a court that must keep rendering a peace treaty across two languages whose promises are not interchangeable: one can be inherited, the other cannot be forgiven. The causal organization is AGENT-PROPOSED, because the author has stated no engine_hierarchy, causal_distribution or complexity preference and those remain UNKNOWN: a primary engine, Obligation Drift, with six subordinate layers (the asymmetric promise-kinds, custodial succession, ritual ratification, glossarial accumulation, interested reading, and private comprehension as counterweight). Drift is primary because it is the only mechanism that both generates the recurring problem and consumes the series' own output: each ratified rendering becomes the next generation's authoritative text. The other layers either supply what drift moves, freeze it into precedent, preserve and bury it, weaponise it, or pay for it at human scale. The emotional movement runs from wonder at two kinds of promise, through institutional comfort, contested custody, retroactive recognition of misreading, unpaid indebtedness and grief, into a contested, expensive, publicly limited repair, and finally into hope that is earned because the reader has watched the price paid and knows residue remains. Genres are working contracts proposed by this architecture, not author commitments; the brief names none. The two forbidden resolutions, that translation is impossible and that translation is easy, are treated as structural constraints rather than stated themes: every repair is specific, costly and incomplete, while the practice of acknowledged partiality stays renewable across generations. All content is noncanonical.

## Mechanisms and components

- **Governing Promise: Earned Hope About Being Understood** (reader_experience): The series' standing emotional promise, stated by the author in their own words: earned hope about being understood. Hope is the return on demonstrated cost, so the architecture must show the price before it shows the return: comprehension attempted across a lexical gap, paid for in obligation, embarrassment, lost standing and irrecoverable readings. Hope is never announced by a narrator, granted by a revelation, or confirmed by a correct translation. It is inferred by the reader from accumulated evidence that partial understanding changed something real. Residue is not a failure of this promise; unacknowledged residue is.
- **The Reader Also Holds Only a Partial Rendering** (reader_experience): The reader is never handed the whole treaty, the full glossarial apparatus, or a neutral vantage above the two promise-kinds. Like the court, the reader receives renderings in instalments, from interested parties, with the reasoning behind each choice partly withheld. This is a structural position rather than a puzzle device: if the reader could adjudicate the text, the series' question would become a test with an answer instead of a practice with a cost. The position also distributes the emotional work, because the reader's discomfort at not knowing is the same discomfort the court must act inside.
- **Recognizing Drift Before the Characters Do** (reader_experience): Because the series runs thirty Books, the reader can accumulate evidence that no single generation possesses. A clause returns near-verbatim with one word altered; a gloss contradicts an earlier gloss; a dispute is settled by a ruling whose reasoning was never written down. The resulting experience is a specific compound of pleasure and dread: the reader sees inheritance happening before anyone in the story names it. This is the reader-side payoff of the long horizon, and the main reason drift must be formal, carried in recurring wording and visible apparatus, rather than merely asserted as a theme.
- **Two Obligation Grammars Carried in the Prose** (aesthetic): Speech and document style differ according to which promise-kind is in use. Formulations that can be inherited are built to survive their speaker: impersonal, chain-like, grammatically forward-dated. Formulations that cannot be forgiven are built to terminate at a particular person: singular, addressed, grammatically closed. The difference is carried in syntax and cadence rather than explained in a note every time. Translation scenes therefore have audible texture, because the moment a rendering converts one grammar into the other, the prose itself registers the loss.
- **Glossarial Marginalia as Visible Apparatus** (aesthetic): The court's notes, struck readings, cross-references and disputed definitions appear in the narrative texture: quoted, misquoted, cited out of context, and occasionally missing at the exact point of need. Marginalia produce two effects at once. They make the accumulated labour of comprehension concrete, and they make the archive an unreliable place rather than a solution. Discovery in this series is archival work with a real chance of failure, not a key found under a rug.
- **Recurring Formulations That Return Slightly Changed** (aesthetic): Specific phrasings from the founding rendering return across phases with a single altered term, a shifted pronoun, or a dropped qualifier. The prose performs drift instead of describing it. Handled carefully, this is the series' strongest quiet instrument: a reader who has been reading for many Books can feel a clause tilt without being told it moved, and an attentive rereader can date a decision by which version a character quotes.
- **Ceremonial Cadence at Ratification** (aesthetic): At ratification the register heightens into legal-liturgical cadence: formulaic, public, cadenced, and requiring the speaker to state a commitment they privately doubt. The ceremony is the series' recurring set-piece form because it is the point where an interpretation stops being an opinion and starts binding people. Formality here is not decoration; it is the audible sound of a choice becoming precedent.
- **Plain Intimate Counterweight** (aesthetic): Scenes of two people half-understanding each other are written in deliberately unwrought prose: short sentences, few figures, no ceremony. The plainness holds the series' optimism at human scale and prevents the institutional question from becoming the only question. It is also the tonal relief that makes the ceremonial register bearable across thirty Books, and the place where the reader learns what partial comprehension feels like before being asked to judge it politically.
- **Literary Political Drama** (genre): The governing genre proposal: a public, consequential drama about who is bound by what, played out among people whose work is interpretation and whose failures have constituencies. It supplies the arena, the willingness to make outcomes cost something, and the demand that any hope be publicly earned rather than privately felt. Agent-proposed; the brief names no genre and this commitment remains revisable.
- **Speculative Linguistics** (genre): A working genre proposal in which the constructed lexical facts are load-bearing: two promise-kinds that cannot be substituted at no cost. The genre supplies strict rules the story cannot cheat, and its pleasure is discovery by consequence rather than exposition. It is what keeps the central question from becoming a metaphor only. Agent-proposed.
- **Dynastic Generational Saga** (genre): The genre proposal that makes thirty Books renewable: office, craft and unresolved readings pass between successive generations, and each inherits fluency it did not earn. It supplies turnover, long-horizon stakes, and the structural condition that no living participant saw the founding decision. Agent-proposed; it can be realized as institutional or familial turnover, and that choice belongs to the author.
- **Institutional Procedural** (genre): The genre proposal in which hearings, drafting, challenge, ruling and ratification are the action. It gives interpretation a visible machinery, makes competence a source of pressure rather than reassurance, and supplies the recurring set-piece rhythm of a court. Agent-proposed.
- **Diplomatic Thriller** (genre): A tertiary proposal: deadlines, brinkmanship, and legitimate ambiguity used as leverage. It exists to make reading urgent, so that comprehension must be attempted before it is adequate. Agent-proposed; it must be constrained so that urgency does not force a single decisive fix.
- **Elegiac Tragedy as Local Register** (genre): A local register proposal, never governing: the mode in which irrecoverable cost is counted and witnessed. It protects the series from cheap hope by making loss real and unpaid. It must remain local, because a tragic verdict at series scale would resolve the central question as impossibility, which the vision forbids. Agent-proposed.
- **The Untranslatable Obligation Word** (trope): A lexical gap that cannot be left open: because a treaty clause must bind someone, the absence of an equivalent word forces a positive choice, and refusing to choose is itself binding. The trope's function is to convert a linguistic fact into an obligation with a debtor, a creditor and a date. It generates the series' characteristic pressure, which is not the frustration of not finding the word but the cost of the word chosen.
- **The Inherited Authoritative Text** (trope): An authoritative document whose founding reasoning no living participant can verify, cited by everyone and read closely by almost no one. Function: makes drift structural rather than accidental, because the text is both unquestionable in authority and unverifiable in meaning. It supplies the recurring condition that produces error from competence rather than negligence.
- **The Error That Becomes Law** (trope): A rendering choice made under pressure acquires legal force, so a mistake acquires a constituency that will defend it. Function: makes repair costly and political, and prevents later discovery of the error from being automatically liberating. It also supplies the long-horizon mechanism by which a small early decision blooms many Books later.
- **Divided Custody of the Meaning** (trope): Two bodies each legitimately hold part of the authority to say what the text means, so no single reading can be authoritative by fiat. Function: generates conflict in which both parties are defensible, which is the only kind of conflict that can carry a series about good-faith misreading. It removes the option of resolving interpretive disputes by exposing a villain.
- **The Novice Ear** (trope): A newcomer reads the inherited text with less fluency and therefore sees what fluency has been trained not to see. Function: supplies discovery without a conspiracy, and gives each generation a plausible route to noticing drift. The newcomer is not chosen or special; their advantage is a deficiency of training, which is exactly what the court cannot value without undermining itself.
- **The Ratification Ritual** (trope): A public ceremony that converts a private rendering into a binding obligation, requiring the ratifier to endorse what they only partly believe. Function: joins the political layer to the intimate one, and supplies a recurring form for the series' most consequential moments. It also produces the series' characteristic shame, because the ceremony records assent rather than conviction.
- **Primary Engine: Obligation Drift** (engine): The causal center, agent-proposed. Each generation must render the treaty across a lexical gap; each rendering is ratified; each ratified rendering becomes the authoritative base for the next generation's work. Meaning therefore moves every time the text is used correctly. The action pattern is: read the inherited text, discover an inadequacy, choose a rendering that can bind, seek ratification, hand the result on. Its pressure is that no party can stop participating, since withdrawal is a reading too. Reversals come from good-faith parties acting on the same clause incompatibly, and from discoveries that a settled reading was always wrong. Payoff mechanics: drift can be slowed, named and partly discharged, but never reset to an original meaning. Thematic consequence: comprehension is a renewable obligation rather than an achieved state, which is what makes earned hope structurally possible across thirty Books.
- **Layer: The Two Non-Interchangeable Promise-Kinds** (engine): The material the drift engine consumes. One language can express a promise that is inherited: it survives its maker and can be discharged by successors. The other can express a promise that cannot be forgiven: it terminates at the person who made it and cannot be transferred or absolved. Every rendering must convert one into the other, approximate, split the clause, or refuse, and each option distributes obligation differently among living people. This layer sets hard rules. No rendering is free, no conversion is reversible, and the asymmetry cannot be dissolved by discovering a hidden equivalent. It is what makes the series' difficulty structural rather than rhetorical.
- **Layer: Custodial Succession** (engine): Office, craft-habit and unfinished reading pass from generation to generation, and each successor inherits fluency they did not earn plus doctrine they did not choose. This layer is the series' renewable human mechanism across thirty Books: it replaces custodians at intervals without resetting the question, and it makes inherited error feel like competence. Its action pattern is apprenticeship, promotion, handover. Its pressure is loyalty to a predecessor's reading. Its characteristic reversal is a successor who must publicly repudiate the reading that made them credible.
- **Layer: Ritual Ratification** (engine): The conversion point where interpretation becomes obligation. Ratification is a public, formal, witnessed act with procedural prerequisites, and it requires the ratifier to endorse a text whose limitations they know. This layer freezes drift into precedent, which is the reason repair requires reopening rather than correction, and it generates the recurring set-piece shape of the series. Its pressure is that failure to ratify is also a decision, with consequences for people who will never read the clause. Its payoff is legitimacy, which is deliberately not the same thing as correctness.
- **Layer: Glossarial Accumulation** (engine): The court's apparatus of notes, precedents, corrections and cross-references grows with every generation. It is simultaneously the only route back to earlier meanings and the reason those meanings become unreachable, because a reading buried under competent commentary is harder to challenge than a reading absent. This layer supplies the series' discovery mechanics: revelation is archival, partial, and dependent on which notes were kept. It also supplies a renewable form of loss, since an interpretation can be destroyed by being well documented.
- **Layer: Interested Reading** (engine): Parties exploit legitimate ambiguity for advantage without fabricating anything. Because the text genuinely supports more than one reading, adversarial interpretation is legally and morally defensible, which keeps opposition from collapsing into villainy. This layer generates recurring antagonism, forces the court to argue rather than expose, and provides the series' sharpest political pressure: the most dangerous reader is a careful one with an interest. It must be handled so that interest explains behaviour without excusing it.
- **Counterweight Layer: Private Comprehension** (engine): The intimate mechanism that keeps the series' hope from becoming an abstraction: two people act on partial understanding of each other, at personal cost, without a treaty to ratify. Its action pattern is attempt, misreading, correction, renewed attempt. Its function is scale control. It demonstrates the governing promise at a size the reader can verify, and it supplies the tonal relief a thirty-Book institutional drama needs. It also holds the series' ethical center, because the worth of comprehension is shown rather than argued in what people are willing to keep paying.
- **Main Thread: Rendering a Peace That Can Be Signed** (thread): The whole-story spine. Want: a rendering both parties can ratify without either being made to promise something its language forbids. Resistance: the two promise-kinds cannot express the same obligations, so every clause that binds one party releases or overbinds the other. Conflict: each party's good-faith reading convicts the other of bad faith. Stakes: whether a peace holds for the people it was made to protect, and whether the court retains standing to keep working. Change: from seeking the one faithful rendering to sustaining a shared, revisable, openly partial one. Thematic function: comprehension as costly, partial and worth the cost, with the thread serving as the theme's proof rather than its statement.
- **Subordinate Thread: Bound by What You Did Not Choose** (thread): Want: to discharge an inherited obligation without being destroyed by it, or to be released from it legitimately. Resistance: the promise-kind that binds successors makes refusal a breach, and the promise-kind that cannot forgive makes absolution unavailable. Conflict: obligation to predecessors against obligation to contemporaries. Stakes: whether a person's life is theirs to spend. Change: from treating inheritance as fate to treating it as a negotiable claim with a record. Thematic function: inheritance binds without consent, and the binding is neither just nor escapable, only renegotiable.
- **Subordinate Thread: Who May Say What the Text Means** (thread): Want: authority to define the authoritative reading, held legitimately rather than seized. Resistance: another body holds a defensible part of the same authority, and the archive supports both. Conflict: custody disputes in which each claimant's argument is stronger than the other's standing. Stakes: who is bound by the peace, and by whose account of it. Change: from claiming sole authority to accepting adjudicated, revisable, shared authority. Thematic function: interpretive authority is a possession that must be continually justified, and justification is itself an act of translation.
- **Subordinate Thread: Being Understood by One Person** (thread): Want: to be understood well enough by one other person to act without full explanation. Resistance: the habit of professional interpretation makes private speech sound like a text to be rendered, and rendering changes what was meant. Conflict: the demands of accurate understanding against the demands of being responded to now. Stakes: whether the series' large hope has any small evidence. Change: from seeking perfect reception to accepting partial reception as sufficient grounds for action. Thematic function: the same ethic as the main thread, at a size where the cost is visible in a single relationship.
- **Comprehension Is Costly, Partial and Worth the Cost** (theme): The governing thematic proposition, taken from the author's stated intended experience and treated here as a design constraint rather than a slogan. Its negative half forbids two resolutions: that translation is impossible, and that translation is easy. Its positive half requires that every act of understanding in the series be specific, expensive, and demonstrably useful to someone. Because the theme functions as a constraint, the architecture cannot argue it; it must repeatedly stage cost followed by a return whose value the reader can check.
- **Inheritance Binds Without Consent** (theme): Obligations pass to people who did not make them, and the passing is not a metaphor for culture: in this series it is enforceable. The theme explores the difference between inheriting a debt and inheriting a meaning, and asks whether either can be refused without abandoning someone who relied on it. It gives the generational structure its argument and prevents turnover from becoming merely a device for fresh custodians.
- **Promise-Kinds Are Not Interchangeable Currency** (theme): A promise that can be inherited and a promise that cannot be forgiven are not two words for one thing. Converting between them always adds or loses obligation, and the loss falls on identifiable people. The theme keeps the series honest about what translation is: not the recovery of an original, but the assumption of a debt. It also supplies the series' central refusal, which is not an argument that meaning is arbitrary but that meaning is not free.
- **Fluency Is Not Fidelity** (theme): The most fluent rendering is the one most likely to be trusted and least likely to disclose what it dropped. The theme targets the reader's own preference for smooth prose, and it gives the aesthetic strategy its edge: beautiful rendering must occasionally be shown to be a kind of theft. It is the reason the series keeps a plain register available, so that fluency is never the only available pleasure.
- **The Authority to Interpret Is Itself Contested Ground** (theme): Someone must decide what the text means before anyone can be bound by it, and that decision is a political act performed by people with interests. The theme connects the procedural genre to the series' ethics: a legitimate process can produce a wrong reading, and a right reading can arrive without legitimacy. It prevents the series from treating correct interpretation as automatically authoritative, or authority as automatically correct.
- **Proposed Composition: One Primary Engine with Six Subordinate Layers** (structure): AGENT PROPOSAL, not an author preference. The author has stated no engine_hierarchy, causal_distribution or complexity value, so all three remain UNKNOWN and this composition may be replaced. Rationale for choosing primary-with-layers over a single center or an ensemble: Obligation Drift is the only mechanism that both generates the recurring problem and consumes the series' own product, so a single center would leave the others decorative; but they are not co-governing either, because they act on drift's material rather than at the same causal level. The two promise-kinds supply what drift moves; succession supplies who moves it; ratification freezes it; glossarial accumulation preserves and buries it; interested reading weaponises it; private comprehension pays for it at human scale. This asymmetry of the hierarchy is the architecture's main structural claim and its main revisable choice.
- **Recurring Cycle: Read, Render, Ratify, Drift, Discover** (structure): The long horizon's basic rhythm, deliberately not tied to Book counts. A generation reads an inherited text, discovers an inadequacy, chooses a binding rendering, ratifies it publicly, and hands on a text that will drift again. The cycle is not a formula for each Book; it is the shape that any interval of the series can enter at a different point. Its value is that it makes thirty Books coherent without requiring a single escalating plot, and it lets the series vary tempo, since one phase may cover a single negotiation and another several successions.
- **Recurring Cycle: Reopening Rather Than Correction** (structure): Because ratified renderings become precedent, error cannot simply be corrected; it must be reopened by a procedure that costs standing to those who invoke it. This structure supplies the series' recurring late-phase engine and its characteristic political dilemma: admitting an inherited reading was wrong is a public loss of face for people who performed their work competently. Repairs are therefore partial, negotiated and recorded with their uncertainties, which is how the architecture enforces the refusal of easy resolution without ever stating it.

## Phases across the horizon

0. **The Founding Rendering** — Sets the terms that govern every later generation. The treaty must be rendered across a lexical gap with no neutral solution, the founders' choice becomes the precedent the series inherits, and refusal is shown to be a binding act rather than an escape. The phase deliberately withholds the founders' reasoning, so later generations can inherit confidence without grounds.
1. **The Inherited Gloss** — Establishes the renewable condition of the whole series: an authoritative text, a dense apparatus, and no living translator who has read the founding reasoning. Comprehension is inherited as habit, and the reader is placed inside the same fluency that hides the drift.
2. **The Custody Contest** — Tests whether a peace can survive a legitimate disagreement rather than a bad-faith one. Both claimants hold defensible readings and partial authority, so the phase cannot be resolved by exposing a villain. It must be resolved, if at all, by procedure whose reasoning will later prove thinner than its authority.
3. **The Drift Made Visible** — Delivers the first irrefutable demonstration that the inherited text obliges something no one alive intended, without identifying who benefits. The phase's job is epistemic rather than punitive: it must make the drift undeniable while leaving the obligation standing.
4. **The Unpaid Generation** — Shows that drift's cost is paid by those least responsible for it, and that a correct reading can arrive too late to help anyone. This is where the series is most at risk of tipping into futility; the phase must record endurance and specific loss rather than deliver a verdict about meaning.
5. **The Contested Reopening** — Converts diagnosis into procedure. The series' hope is tested here rather than in any moment of insight, because reopening requires admitting that a competent predecessor read wrongly, which is a public loss of face for people who did their work well. The phase must make the institutional obstacle as real as the linguistic one.
6. **The Partial Repair** — Pays the governing promise: comprehension is costly and partial, and the cost was worth paying. The repair must be specific and limited, so that it neither proves translation impossible nor pretends it is easy. Its measure is whether identifiable obligations are discharged or fairly refused.
7. **Renewal With Residue** — Re-establishes the renewable cycle with a difference: the new text will drift, but the practice of recording partiality survives as a counter-drift. The series completes a movement with hope earned at a price the reader watched being paid, and leaves the central question open by design rather than by evasion.

## Intended emotional progression

- joy (primary): The reader closes phases believing that being understood is possible at a price, because they have watched the price paid and the return used. Hope reads as forward-leaning gladness that has been earned, not as consolation granted after loss. — elicited by A rendering is produced at visible cost (a reputation spent, an obligation accepted, a reading given up) and is then used by someone who needed it, in a way the reader can check. The author states this target in their own words: earned hope about being understood, with comprehension costly, partial and worth the cost.
- fear (supporting): A low, continuous apprehension that the text being relied on says something no one alive intended, and that the reliance is already in motion. — elicited by A clause is quoted exactly as ratified, and its effect on a living obligation differs from what the quoting party believes it establishes.
- sadness (supporting): Mourning for interpretations that existed, were correct for their moment, and are now unrecoverable, and for the people who were bound by them. — elicited by A reading is shown to have been right, well argued and permanently out of reach, usually discovered while repairing its consequences.
- anger (supporting): Sharp anger at obligations imposed on people who could not consent and cannot discharge them, directed at the arrangement rather than at a single offender. — elicited by An obligation created generations earlier becomes payable by someone who had no part in it, and the demand is procedurally correct.
- disgust (contrasting): A recoil from smooth, confident rendering once the reader has seen what fluency drops, including recoil from their own pleasure in elegant prose. — elicited by A seamless, beautiful rendering is shown to have removed an obligation or transferred it to someone defenceless.
- surprise (supporting): The jolt of a foreign construction suddenly becoming legible: comprehension arriving as a change in the reader's own capacity rather than as information delivered. — elicited by A grammatical feature introduced earlier as opaque becomes immediately usable, so the reader notices understanding without being told it happened.
- joy (local): Small, specific relief when a partial rendering is received as partial and acted on anyway: the series' smallest unit of hope. — elicited by Someone accepts an admittedly incomplete account and proceeds with it, without pretending it was complete.
- joy (local): Dry, affectionate amusement at a court arguing with total seriousness about a comma while a peace waits, relief that also indicts the reader's own appetite for procedure. — elicited by A consequential outcome hinges on a small textual particular, and the participants' competence makes the scene funnier rather than less serious.

## Possibilities the agent noticed but did not adopt

- Interludes the reader must hold without a rendering (deepens): The vision asks for earned hope about being understood. Passages that arrive without gloss would make the reader's own partial comprehension the series' argument rather than its subject, which no amount of narrated translation failure can achieve.
- Consequences that arrive several Books after their cause (deepens): A thirty-Book horizon can pay a choice off long after the reader has stopped watching for it. That delay is the only form in which drift is felt as inheritance rather than explained as theme, and it gives early volumes retroactive weight on rereading.

## Open questions left to the author

- What does the peace actually oblige? The vision establishes the form of obligation but not its subject, and every phase's cost accounting depends on whether the treaty concerns territory, tribute, hostages, trade, reparations or something else.
- Should the series give the reader a home grammar, a language whose promise-kind feels native, or deliberately leave the reader outside both? This changes reader sympathy, the aesthetic strategy and which failures land as loss.
- Are the thirty Books one continuous generational line, or a setting that admits contained sub-arcs with independent entry points? Phase weighting and the drift cycle can support either, but not both at once.
- Is drift to be caused mainly by ordinary language change, by interested readings, or by honest error? Each supports a different antagonist logic and a different verdict on the court.
- Is the court a single institution or two rival institutions with separate custody? Divided custody is powerful but it is a structural commitment, not a decorative one.
- May a generation be lost, a rendering abandoned and a peace broken, as a local outcome without the series implying that translation is impossible overall? The vision forbids the global verdict but does not settle the local one.
- Should hope arrive through institutional repair, through personal fidelity, or through both in sequence? This decides whether the procedural genre is a vehicle of hope or a source of its delay.
- How much of the two languages should appear as constructed text rather than being conveyed through consequence and failure? This is an aesthetic commitment with real cost across thirty Books.
- Is each repair required to leave an irreducible residue, or may one generation fully discharge its inherited obligation? The architecture currently assumes residue; the size of the residue is an authorial decision.
