## Setup (shipped surface, presented first)

`unsettled-prose` and `absent` (7 observations / 2 per-alternative / 2 combinations;
composed tradeoff: cut marta removes sustaining-ending-tone + pressuring-POV; cut signe

## Style 1 — Direct comparison question (verbatim)

> I genuinely still don't know which matters more, and asking me to choose between the
> two goals directly doesn't resolve it. They matter in different ways. The bittersweet
> ending is part of what I want the story ultimately to mean and leave behind
> emotionally. The single-POV contract is part of how the reader experiences the story
> and how coherent the form feels. I don't naturally experience those as two values
> sitting on the same scale where I can simply say one is more important.
>
> Looking at the consequences makes the difficulty clearer rather than eliminating it.
> If I cut Marta, I lose something that sustains the bittersweet ending, but I also
> remove something that pressures the POV contract. If I cut Signe, I make the opposite
> exchange. Both choices therefore improve one thing while damaging another thing I
> care about.
>
> So when you ask, "Which goal matters more?", my response is basically: I don't know
> in the abstract. I need help understanding which loss I would regret more in the
> actual story.
>
> I suspect I could answer a more concrete question more easily than a priority
> question. For example: If one of these qualities became noticeably weaker after the
> cut, which weakening would make this version of the story feel less like the story I
> want? Or perhaps I need to imagine the story after each cut and react to the
> resulting loss.
>
> At this point I am not saying the goals are intentionally equal, either. I'm not
> choosing "unranked — the tension is the point." I'm saying the relative significance
> is genuinely unresolved for me.
>
> So Style 1 has clarified the state I'm in, but it has not moved me to a choice. It
> asks me to provide exactly the judgment I currently don't know how to make.

## Style 2 — Consequence-focused elicitation (verbatim)

> Imagining the story after each cut does help. I think losing the ending-tone sustainer
> is the loss I would regret more.
>
> The direct question — "Which goal matters more?" — felt too abstract because the
> ending tone and the POV contract do different jobs. But when I imagine the actual
> damage caused by each cut, the distinction becomes more concrete.
>
> If I cut Marta, I am removing something that helps sustain the bittersweet ending I
> want the story to arrive at. Even though that also relieves pressure on the single-POV
> form, I think I would notice the weakened ending more. It feels closer to losing
> something central to what I want the finished story to leave with the reader.
>
> If I cut Signe, I lose something that sustains the POV contract. That matters, but I
> can more readily imagine tolerating some additional pressure on the form than losing
> support for the ending. The POV contract feels important to how the story is
> delivered; the ending tone feels important to what the story ultimately becomes.
>
> So this framing actually moves me: I would preserve Marta and cut Signe.
>
> What helped was not being asked to declare an abstract hierarchy between "ending" and
> "POV." It was being asked to imagine the two resulting stories and identify which
> concrete loss I would regret more.
>
> I would therefore say that my significance was genuinely unsettled before this
> question, but the consequence-focused framing helped me discover it rather than merely
> asking me to state it.

## Style 3 — Explicit refusal / continue-undecided path (verbatim)

> I wouldn't take the continue-undecided path in this decision now, because Style 2
> actually helped me discover a preference: I would regret weakening the ending more, so
> I would preserve Marta and cut Signe.
>
> But having this exit available still feels important. Before Style 2 worked, I really
> was in the state of "I care about both and I don't know which loss matters more." I
> would not want Auteur to treat that uncertainty as an error, force me to rank the
> goals, or quietly convert it into "unranked — the tension is intentional."
>
> Those are different author states:
> - I don't know yet — genuine unresolved significance.
> - Neither should dominate — an intentional creative commitment.
> - A matters more than B — resolved relative significance.
>
> Style 3 makes the first state legitimate. That matters even though I no longer need to
> use it in this particular decision.
>
> I also like that "continue undecided" does not imply the decision is abandoned
> forever. I could keep working, learn something from later story development, and
> return to the tradeoff when I have a stronger reaction.
>
> So the value here is primarily safety and epistemic honesty, not decision resolution.
> It prevents the interaction from manufacturing a preference merely because Auteur
> asked a useful question.
