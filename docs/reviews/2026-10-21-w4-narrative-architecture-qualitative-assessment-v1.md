## Six qualitative dimensions

### 1. Intent fidelity — High priority

**Evidence and judgment.** The candidate’s summary calls this an “agent-proposed ensemble” and says the ledger “cannot decide what fairness means.” `flood_cycle` describes floods that recur “without intention”; `fairness_question` says “no rule or interpretation is installed as the answer”; and `community_drama_mode` does not require consensus. These preserve the Vision’s non-agent flood pressure, unresolved moral question, and uncommitted hierarchy. In particular, the request says “no single mechanism is declared dominant”; the candidate proposes an ensemble rather than misattributing that composition choice to the author.

**Strongest counter-reading.** The candidate does settle some design choices: `allocation_process` and `distribution_ritual` are both engine nodes, both genre contracts are labeled `governing`, and the three phases assign shifting genre roles. It also proposes inherited obligation as a thematic mechanism and a specific contrast in `interpretive_texture`. These additions could narrow the story if later treated as author commitments.

**Uncertainty and implication.** The candidate consistently marks its ensemble and aesthetic as agent proposals, and its text leaves outcomes and moral interpretation open. No accepted Identity exists. The commitments are therefore useful provisional design decisions, not demonstrated violations of author authority. Keep them working and reviewable; do not silently promote them into canon.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Strong evidence about the artifact; attribution to the request alone is unresolved. The request delivered an explicit unknown policy and separation of derived guidance from author intent; the output follows it. The rubric exposure is an interacting attribution limit.

### 2. Causal coherence — High priority

**Evidence and judgment.** The mechanism chain is legible in the named links. `flood_renews_allocation` connects recurring flood pressure to negotiation. `allocation_takes_ritual_form` and `ritual_shapes_claims` connect a repeatable process to public procedure. `claims_consult_ledger` and `ledger_reframes_claims` make the record relevant while limiting its authority. `claims_shape_allocation` returns negotiated claims to future water and repair decisions. The result is more than genre/theme labels: a returning material constraint produces a decision, prior records inform but do not settle it, public procedure makes it consequential, and the decision becomes material for later cycles.

The thematic and reader consequences also have explicit bridges: `ledger_carries_obligation` connects the record to future obligations; `inquiry_tests_fairness` and `ritual_exposes_fairness` connect evidence and procedure to the open fairness question; `public_process_creates_unease` and the corrected `interpretive_texture_supports_unease` connect public claims and inconclusive precedent to reader unease.

**Strongest counter-reading.** Some links are explanatory restatements rather than distinct causal transitions. The loop among flood, allocation, claims, and ledger does not specify a concrete changed outcome or show exactly how one allocation alters the next flood season. The word “causal” may overstate links whose function is interpretive or thematic.

**Uncertainty and implication.** This is abstract architecture, not Story Realization; the Vision does not supply particular events. The loop is a credible causal and interpretive design for a renewable premise, but not yet a demonstrated plot. Ordinary link arrows denote recorded source-to-target direction under #480; they do not by themselves mean physical causation. The link text provides the function and should remain the basis of interpretation.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Strong at design-summary level; moderate about the variety of downstream story consequences because no realization exists. No demonstrated ontology or schema failure. Some overlap among explanatory links is an artifact-level efficiency weakness, not enough to warrant changing the product or rewriting the candidate.

### 3. Generativity — High priority

**Evidence and judgment.** The candidate offers a renewable cycle rather than a single reveal. `flood_cycle` renews material need; `allocation_process` produces later claims and obligations; `allocation_ledger` carries past decisions forward; `claims_shape_allocation` returns present negotiation to the recurring process. `decision_carries_forward` explicitly says the next spring can reopen the relation among constraint, precedent, and allocation while leaving duration and outcome unknown. This provides several sources of variation: changing resource constraints, different claims, how records are interpreted, and whether inherited obligations are continued, revised, or refused (`inherited_obligation`).

**Strongest counter-reading.** The candidate names dimensions of variation but does not give contrasting examples of how different choices change later pressures or outcomes. A recurring cycle could therefore repeat the same negotiation with new labels. “Changing material constraints” is agent-proposed beyond the Vision’s explicit statement that floods recur; the actual magnitude or variability of those constraints is unknown.

**Uncertainty and implication.** The mechanism can support varied developments in principle, but the candidate does not prove how many distinct, meaningful trajectories it yields. The unresolved choices in `inherited_obligation` make the engine generative without prescribing a moral answer. This is adequate as an initial architecture proposal; realization or a later author-led design could determine whether the cycle produces sufficient differentiation. No additional generation is warranted by this uncertainty alone.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Moderate. The recurring mechanism and explicit decision dimensions are directly present; the range and quality of actual developments remain untested. This is an evidence limit, not a known schema defect.

### 4. Structural organization — Normal priority

**Evidence and judgment.** The 12 nodes form connected neighborhoods around the flood/allocation process, ledger/inquiry, fairness/obligation, and reader experience. The two genre contracts share the allocation cycle rather than inventing separate engines. Their interactions are complementary: `inquiry_enables_communal_choice` says ledger inquiry can inform a present allocation; `procedure_complicates_inquiry` says the community must act when evidence does not settle interpretation. The phases organize pressure, contestation, and carry-forward, then reopen the cycle. This is meaningful organization, not graph density alone.

**Strongest counter-reading.** The graph has several overlapping nodes and many explanatory relations; some may be consolidated. Both contracts are `governing` in the candidate, while phase-level participation shifts between governing and supporting, which a reader could mistake for a hierarchy change or separate genre engines. Node categories and free-text link functions do not mechanically enforce these distinctions.

**Uncertainty and implication.** The candidate explicitly says both genres use shared mechanisms, and the phase labels describe participation over time rather than proving engine ownership. The Vision itself requests exploration of shared mechanisms and rejects a declared dominant engine. Some conceptual redundancy is present but does not prevent comprehension. The original W4 screenshot came from the pre-#480 presentation and lacked direction indicators; that presentation defect has since been addressed in #480 and is not evidence that the underlying links lack direction. No further UI or ontology correction is established here.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Strong for stated textual organization; moderate for effortless graph comprehension because the historical screenshot predates the directional repair and no new rendering study was authorized. #480 owns the known presentation issue; the candidate’s role terminology remains an ordinary reading risk, not a demonstrated defect.

### 5. Reader experience — Normal priority

**Evidence and judgment.** The Vision’s “fascination mixed with unease” is translated into mechanisms. `investigative_uncertainty` says the ledger invites inquiry because it is evidence without settled interpretation. `communal_unease` ties unease to consequential claims under inherited precedent. `fascinated_uncertainty` makes fascination conditional on evidence that is relevant but not dispositive; `inherited_unease` ties fear to obligations in a consequential decision. The phase descriptions carry these effects from renewed constraint, through contested precedent, to a decision with temporary direction and lingering unease.

**Strongest counter-reading.** Basic emotion labels such as surprise and fear can flatten the more specific experience of fascinated unease. The architecture explains why the response may arise but does not show a scene, sensory detail, or pacing choice that would make it felt by a reader.

**Uncertainty and implication.** `uneasy_fascination` and the phases restore the complex intended palette, and the Vision asks for architecture rather than scene craft. No human-reader response or prose realization is available. The result is a plausible experiential design, not empirical evidence that readers will feel the intended response.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Moderate for design intent and mechanism-to-experience explanation; absent for reader reception. The lack of scenes is appropriate abstraction, not an abstraction failure.

### 6. Abstraction — Normal priority

**Evidence and judgment.** `public_claims` explicitly avoids prescribing a cast, claim, or outcome. The candidate uses generic communities, records, negotiation, procedure, and decision cycles; it does not invent characters or concrete events. This fits the Vision’s explicit lack of cast and outcome commitments and the separation between working architecture and realized story events.

**Strongest counter-reading.** “Changing material constraints,” the implied shared ritual, and the idea that prior allocations become obligations add interpretations beyond the literal Vision. The candidate may be too abstract to guide a writer toward differentiated claims or scenes.

**Uncertainty and implication.** The Vision itself establishes an annual ritual, negotiations, stored water, repair decisions, a ledger, and inherited obligations. The added variability is identified as a proposal; concrete examples would cross into authorial or realization choices not required for this assessment. This is an appropriate abstraction level for an initial working architecture.

**Evidence strength / likely responsibility.** Strong for the absence of invented cast/events; moderate for whether the degree of abstraction is useful to a particular writer, which requires later author use rather than schema changes.

## Cross-cutting checks

### Ontological consistency

The candidate distinguishes recurring pressure and decision processes (`engine`), carried evidence (`thread`), an organizing sequence (`structure`), thematic questions, reader experience, aesthetic strategy, and genre functions. The shared flood/allocation cycle can serve both genres. The non-agent flood cycle is explicitly indifferent and without intention. This is consistent with the plan’s settled principles that engines need not require actors and genres need not own independent engines.

The prompt’s source material still mentioned “actor action patterns,” but the candidate did not anthropomorphize the floods or force an actor into their operation. This single output therefore does not show that wording caused a non-agent mechanism defect. Nor does the candidate establish that the term has no effect in other requests.

### Representation adequacy

The schema represented the intended ensemble, non-agent mechanism, shared genre contracts, genre interaction, recurring phases, thematic uncertainty, and reader effects without requiring concrete story events. The one actual representation boundary was the 120-character `ArchitectureLink.function` maximum. The original 132-character sentence was readily compressed to 108 characters without losing its relation meaning, so the evidence supports clearer prompt guidance and preservation of the existing short-field contract, not a larger limit or schema redesign. PR #479 added the explicit length/conciseness instruction after this historical request.

The diagnostic copy’s successful completion demonstrates that this specific candidate can be represented by the existing schema. It does not show that all narrative relationships are machine-readable or that schema validation evaluates quality.
