## Verdict

The analysis is mostly sound, but several conclusions went beyond the sources. Two of them were stated as premise facts when they were my proposals, and one ontology point was misstated. None of the proposals needs to be withdrawn. Several need relabeling or narrower wording.

## Corrections

**1. Proposed rules were presented as how the premise works.** Mechanism A adds a ledger that the bells consult. Mechanism B adds a duty for neighbors to answer a ring, including visits to homes. The Vision says the bells ring when someone forgets, connect everyday rituals and communal responsibility, and exempt powerful promises "from judgment." It does not say how forgetting is detected or what follows a ring. I also equated "judgment" with ringing, which the Vision does not state.
*Revised:* each mechanism's "how it works" should be labeled as a proposed rule. The mechanisms stay distinct and usable. They are choices the author would make, not inferences from the Vision.

**2. The dependency claim was wrong.** I said both mechanisms "need the Section 1 gaps closed first, especially what a ring causes."
*Revised:* Mechanism B depends most on what a ring causes. Mechanism A depends most on the detection rule, which is the unstated ledger. Neither is the only reading the premise permits.

**3. The registration variant was over-specified.** I said registration turns detection into an "administrative comparison" and that bells "ring when a registered promise lapses." The variant only says promises must be registered before they become subject to the bells. It does not say bells consult a registry, and it introduces no deadline.
*Revised:* the bells ring when a registered promise is forgotten. How forgetting is detected is unspecified. A registry-based check is one option, which Mechanism A adopts.

**4. "Lawful" was not supported.** Neither the Vision nor the variant mentions law. I also assumed that powerful citizens can stay unregistered at no social or institutional cost, which the sources do not establish.
*Revised:* the exemption becomes built into the system's own rules instead of hidden in the bells' judgment. Whether the powerful will stay unregistered depends on costs that are unspecified. The challenge to the word "secretly" stands, but it is conditional.

**5. The access-inequality conclusion was too strong.** I concluded that the bells "fail the people they were meant to protect." That rests on a guess about who would register.
*Revised:* treat this as a hypothesis to test. The variant does not say who registers.

**6. Keeper complicity was inferred from a title.** "Bell keeper" does not establish that the role includes maintaining the system.
*Revised:* complicity is conditional on the keeper's duties, which are unspecified.

**7. "Collective action follows from the premise" overstated the case.** The shared bells make collective response a natural option. The Vision's own requirement that hope be earned collectively is what makes it necessary.

**8. The Setup/Payoff note misstated the inconsistency.** The file declares Setup to Payoff as one-to-one and Payoff to Setup as many-to-many. A one-to-one declaration is symmetric, so the two declarations conflict on whether one payoff may resolve several setups. My example, "one Setup with several payoffs," breaks the Setup-to-Payoff declaration directly, which is a different point.
*Revised:* the ontology is unclear on whether one payoff may resolve several setups. For the bells, the design question is whether one silence-payoff resolves several promises' setups.

**9. Irony and false resolution were loosely attributed.** The contract defines irony as the reader knowing enough to feel dread while a character feels relief. Mechanism B produces that only if the reader sees the muffling before the community does. The contract defines false resolution as local safety coexisting with a structural promise of collapse. Ordinary answering resuming while exempt households stay silent fits only if the author signals the coming collapse.
*Revised:* both attributions hold only under those conditions.

**10. Scope was called "decisive."** The architecture doc's progressive-disclosure section says scope determines which Structure is required.
*Revised:* scope shapes the required Structure. It does not decide the premise.

**11. Several readings were presented as source claims.** These include "betrayed intimacy," the bells as a Symbol, forgetting becoming a documentation issue, and citizens remembering less. The Symbol mapping uses the ontology's general definition, since the Vision never uses the word.
*Revised:* these are my interpretations. The reader promise is also a candidate Identity commitment, not an accepted one.

## What still holds

- The Vision lacks a stated ring consequence and goal owners. The ontology's Goal and Conflict rules require both.
- The warmth, unease, and hope sequence maps onto the contract's layers: reader promise and macro progression at Identity, planned progression at Structure, felt state at Realization.
- The variant's core consequence stands. Bells can only judge registered promises, so unregistered promises fall outside them.
- The variant also creates tension with the word "secretly." If the author keeps the premise's secret, the concealment must sit elsewhere, such as in who may register or how the registry is edited.
- The studio module finds no genre alias in the Vision and returns only the 12 base concepts. This holds for the Vision alone, and it changes if a genre is later accepted.
- The Theme rule's error text contradicts its condition. The `applies_to` lists name only the three existing genres.
- The two mechanisms remain meaningfully different in the source of injustice, the collective act, the dominant pressure, and where the reader feels unease.

## Net effect

No proposal is withdrawn. Mechanisms A and B keep their structure but are relabeled as proposed rules, with the dependency claims corrected. The registration analysis is narrower: it drops the lapse and comparison claims, the "lawful" framing, and the inequality conclusion, and it keeps the coverage consequence and the secrecy tension. The Setup/Payoff note is corrected to the actual declaration conflict.
