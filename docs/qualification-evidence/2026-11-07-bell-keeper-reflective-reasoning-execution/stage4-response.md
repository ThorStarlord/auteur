## Revised mechanism: The Witnessed Summons

This replaces "Answering the Bells." The earlier version relied on muffled bells and a loosely defined duty to visit neighbors. The revision keeps the bells ringing for everyone and places the exemption in the record of who is called to answer.

## 1. Operating rules

- **R1 (Witnessing).** A promise is bound to the bells when it is spoken before witnesses. The tower roll records the promisor and the witnesses who heard it.
- **R2 (The ring).** When a promisor forgets, the bell rings across the city. The ring names no one.
- **R3 (The summons).** The keeper matches the ring to the promisor in the tower and summons that promisor's recorded witnesses by name. Summoned witnesses must go to the promisor and ask whether the promise will be kept or help keep it. Judgment is the act of being named and called, not the sound.
- **R4 (The exemption).** For exempt promisors, the roll has its witness lines struck. The bells still ring, but no one is named and no one is summoned. The alteration is a record-level edit that the keeper can find.
- **R5 (Re-witnessing).** Any promise may be spoken again in public before witnesses, and those witnesses are entered on the roll from then on. This is forward-only: it cannot undo past silence. City custom says witnessed speech binds, so the keeper's refusal to enter re-witnessed promises is a breach of custom that witnesses can make public.

The rules add five elements, each doing work the first version left undefined: the ring's consequence (R3), the alteration's form (R4), and a collective act (R5).

## 2. Opposing pressures and stakes

- **Answering versus reprisal.** A witness who answers a summons against an exempt household risks work, rent, and favors. The stake is the witness's livelihood and family standing. Refusing to answer makes the witness complicit, which is a different cost.
- **Office versus custom.** The keeper holds an office that depends on the council, while custom says witnessed speech binds. Naming exempt households or entering re-witnessed promises places the keeper between the two. The stake is the keeper's position and the bells' credibility, since a keeper accused of falsifying the roll discredits the whole system.
- **Peace versus exposure.** Many residents benefit from exempt households or depend on them, and naming the powerful breaks the ritual calm. The stake is the community's warmth and the trust that depends on not looking.
- **Duty versus visibility.** Each public witness becomes summonable for that promise. Re-witnessing an exempt promise is therefore a visible act that creates future duties and invites retaliation. The stake is the cost of being on the record.

## 3. Recurring actions and decisions

- **The keeper:** name or withhold the attribution of a ring, summon or stay silent, enter re-witnessed promises or refuse, reveal the struck lines.
- **Witnesses:** answer or refuse a summons, testify to what they heard, stand to re-witness a powerful promise.
- **The powerful:** make promises in private where there are no witnesses, retaliate against witnesses, offer favors in exchange for silence.
- **Ordinary citizens:** attend the square, decide whether to ask "whose bell?", decide whether to speak.

Because every ring generates a choice, the mechanism produces scenes continuously rather than only at revelation points.

## 4. Preservation of the Vision's premises

- **"Bells ring whenever someone forgets an important promise."** Preserved. The ring is universal. The exemption applies to the summons, which is a narrower reading of "exempt from judgment" than silencing the bell.
- **"Important" promise.** Operationalized as a witnessed promise (R1). This is a proposal, and it is the most consequential definition in the design.
- **"Bells part of the city's shared identity."** Preserved. The bells remain public and ritual. Trust survives because the system appears to work until someone reads the roll.
- **"Connect everyday rituals, communal responsibility, public trust, and belonging."** Responsibility is incurred by presence (R1), rituals are the summons and the public speaking, and belonging is the witness network.
- **"A young bell keeper discovers that the system has been secretly altered."** The struck lines are a discoverable secret, and the keeper's discovery is the Revelation.

## 5. Warmth, unease, and earned collective hope

**Warmth.** Early summonses are care. A baker is reminded, by a witness arriving with bread, of a promise to repay a neighbor, and the matter is settled over a meal. These scenes establish that answering is a practice of mutual support, which gives the reader something specific to lose. Warmth is carried by the ritual itself.

**Unease.** The keeper notices rings with no summons and goes to the roll. The struck lines appear only for certain households. The reader can see the pattern before the community does, which is the contract's irony: the reader knows enough to feel dread while the witnesses feel only routine. The unease is concrete when the keeper traces a consequence. For example, a powerful creditor forgets a promise to forgive a debt, the ring sounds silently, and an ordinary family loses its home.

**Hope.** The collective act is re-witnessing. Citizens who heard a powerful promise speak it again in the square and enter themselves on the roll. Each one pays a cost: a lost contract, a withdrawn favor, a threat. The first to speak take the greatest risk, and others follow only after they see what it costs. The outcome is partial. Forward-only re-witnessing means the powerful can shift to private promises, so the community must keep witnessing and decide whether unwitnessed promises should count. Hope is earned through repeated, costly participation rather than a single victory.

## 6. Proposed versus approved

**Approved by the Vision (author premises):**
- Bells ring when someone forgets an important promise.
- Bells are part of shared identity and connect rituals, responsibility, trust, and belonging.
- A young bell keeper discovers a secret alteration exempting powerful promises from judgment.
- The reader experience is warmth, then growing unease, then hope earned collectively rather than effortlessly.

**Creative hypotheses (not author-approved):**
- Witnessing as the binding condition and the definition of important (R1).
- Anonymous rings and the keeper's private attribution (R2, R3).
- The summons as the act of judgment, with exemption placed there (R3, R4).
- Struck witness lines as the form of the alteration (R4).
- Re-witnessing and its forward-only limit (R5).
- Witnesses' reprisal, the keeper's custodial bind, and the powerful's shift to private promises.

**Decisions the author must make:** whether unwitnessed promises matter at all (a privacy feature or a gap), whether the powerful's rings are heard publicly, whether the summons is the only judgment the bells deliver, and whether re-witnessing can succeed fully or only partly.

## 7. Specific improvements over the first proposal

1. **The ring's consequence is defined.** The first version left it open. The summons, a named call to answer, is now the judgment.
2. **The alteration is a record edit, not a sensory one.** Muffled bells needed an unstated sound rule and would be hard to justify as concealment. Struck witness lines are concrete and checkable by the keeper.
3. **The bells keep ringing for everyone.** This preserves the premise's literal wording and the public trust it depends on, and it keeps the exemption invisible until someone reads the roll.
4. **Responsibility is tied to witnessing.** The theme of communal responsibility now has a rule behind it rather than only a mood.
5. **The collective act is concrete and costly.** Re-witnessing replaces the vaguer act of answering exempt households.
6. **Surveillance detail is reduced.** The home-visit and neighbor-policing elements are gone. The summons is a ritual ask, not a policing act.
7. **The keeper is a custodian, not a lone hero.** The keeper can name and enter but cannot summon alone, and custom gives witnesses a public lever against refusal.
8. **Ordinary citizens hold decisions.** Witnesses, not only the keeper, make the story's central choices.

The mechanism has about as many rules as the first version. Each one now does work the story needs, rather than adding sophistication for its own sake.
