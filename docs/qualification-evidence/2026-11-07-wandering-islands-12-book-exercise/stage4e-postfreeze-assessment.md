## A. Physical continuity: what the original construction actually moved

**Completed first leg:** Daro, the provisional name of the Book 10 pilot, uses the already-funded small volunteer vessel and the near-side current observational conditions to reach **Oril's existing staffed first open station**. The original Book 10 service had actually authorized and initiated this leg. The waxed envelope originally entrusted by the woman at the council pier is physically delivered to Oril; the crew reserves water and does not continue into unknown water.

**Completed second leg:** Jessa, an ordinary independently working near-side craft ferry pilot [P4E], agrees at the station to carry that same envelope to **Rillturn repair dock [P4E]**. The proposal supplies an independent reason for her travel (paid freshwater-garden cloth and fittings), fresh local pilot/watch checks and actual observed shelter at the arrival. The transfer is **not** made possible by declaring that Daro's first leg proves a second one. It is made possible by a separately operating, already intelligible type of local trade service. Arrival and custody are actually constructed.

**Declined beyond-Rillturn leg:** A distant, unidentified sail is visible beyond a near-side outer cut. It is **not** a current sounding of the channel, a verified Nemi vessel, a pilot's offer or a promise of retreat. Jessa makes only a local checked approach to the final known-water mark, where the current and absent outbound return information prevent a warranted continuation. The boat returns **inside currently observed water**. Eda, a local dockkeeper [P4E], agrees to store the envelope for a **finite** period pending another independently warranted handoff or a bounded return. That is the terminal observed physical state.

**Finding:** The events conserve the identity of **one original letter**. Daro's boat does not transform into a fleet. Halen's two larger hulls remain withdrawn; Oril does not leave her promised station; Isha's clinic crews remain assigned; the same water cannot be spent both on a speculative crossing and a protected return. There is no magical navigation or invented global chart.

**Unproven:** The second near-side route and Rillturn are *new local story assumptions*, not validated facts in the Stage 3B or Stage 4D frozen outlines. The original text supplies recent observations and a known local pilot for its realization, which is credible within the world, but does not give a full independent sequence of soundings along every instant of that leg. It is valid as a plausible constructed **fictional** opportunity, not an engineering proof or a preexisting world commitment.

## B. Epistemic discipline: history, current observation and contact

The exercise properly keeps different information claims apart:

- Nemi's **Book 5 historical arrival** and authenticated Larkhome testimony establish that a journey occurred and that Larkhome was alive then. They do **not** validate a present passage from Rillturn, identify the distant sail or predict an island's future position.
- Daro's **Book 10 present observation** warrants a particular near-side departure, not unlimited onward travel.
- Jessa's **current local checks** justify an independently examined Rillturn landing, not a bridge to the outer gyre.
- The **visible distant sail** is an observed object; its affiliation, consent, navigational route and destination remain unobserved.
- Eda's **signed custody** establishes where the letter is, not delivery, receipt or agreement by Larkhome.

The prose makes this difference more than a formal label: Jessa can *see* a sail and wants to reach it, but her sounding line does not transform that desire into knowledge of the channel. The last reported event is a letter resting on a named shelf, explicitly marked **NOT DELIVERED**, not an optimistic declaration that it will be passed to Nemi.

**Caution:** A visual cue at the exact moment Jessa arrives at the edge is narratively convenient. It is not an epistemic contradiction, because the writer neither identifies it as Nemi nor grants an unseen safe passage. It would become a problem if later Books quietly reinterpreted it as actual outer contact.

## C. Human agency: real refusals without convenient antagonists

**Daro** fulfills the original limited pilot responsibility, stopping at the funded station. **Oril** records the envelope accurately and does not convert her original operational vote into a fresh emergency search. **Jessa** has an independent paid craft duty and chooses one limited custody transfer; at Rillturn she can genuinely refuse the unsounded detour, even though she might have been able to greet an outer mariner. **Eda** refuses indefinite free storage, then agrees to one bounded term because it can be recorded and eventually returned. The anonymous sender knowingly allowed conditional, not guaranteed delivery.

**The outer side:** Nemi is **not used** as a conveniently available pilot, messenger or cartographer. That protects their independent status, but also means their current motivations, travel resources and consent are **not actually demonstrated** by this probe. An unidentified sail does not count as an outer participant who chose anything. It would be incorrect to report the exercise as a test of an actually willing Nemi.

**Finding:** The human boundary is real: each person can say yes to what they can perform and no to what they cannot support. The local caretaker's limited willingness is a material outcome; outer willingness remains entirely undetermined.

## D. Resource and temporal conservation

The constructed chain uses (a) the **Book 10 funded boat/service** for first-station reception and staffing, and (b) **Jessa's separate paid repair ferry** for its ordinary, newly proposed near-side destination. The story does **not** spend Halen's withdrawn ships, double-book clinic transport, issue new public financing for a guaranteed outer route or assume unlimited maintenance at Rillturn.

Resource consequences appear inside the story: the station oil and first-leg water are used for the actual service while return stocks are preserved; Jessa fulfills contracted water-garden deliveries; Eda charges the story a bounded dry shelf and clerk labor rather than an abstract, free infinite relay. The characters explicitly accept that a later return leg would need fresh observations, too.

**Qualification:** The prose uses no absolute mileages, precise durations or artificial supply figures. That is appropriate to this research scope, but it means reserve **adequacy** for each leg is only plausibly stipulated and qualitatively shown, not mechanically simulated. The Rillturn custodian's deadline is a **conditional market-return event**, not a fixed appointment the unpredictable sea must honor.

## E. Causal sufficiency: what Book 10 actually enables

The key improvement over a purely speculative Book 10→11 arrow is that the Book 10 limited service now has a **specific recipient and state-changing action**. Because there is an authorized staffed first station with accountable records, the pier woman's envelope can be received there, assessed without false delivery claims and voluntarily offered to a near-side merchant who already has her own observed journey to make. The letter ends in another custody relationship with a publicly describable limit.

But the same construction also demonstrates the **limit of that causation**: a second near-side transfer cannot itself produce an *independently motivated outer mariner being physically present and willing to meet*. It would be circular to claim “the relay can continue to Nemi because Nemi once reached us.” Book 10 is materially useful as a first-stage service and honest packet handoff; it is **not sufficient for Book 11's planned outer encounter**.

**Counterfactual caution:** Jessa could conceivably have carried a letter along her ordinary ferry route *without* the council's new service if the sender had found her directly at a common market. Therefore the council's funded station is a **concrete enabling path in this attempted chain**, not proven a globally necessary prerequisite of all possible future correspondence. This is the same necessary/sufficient distinction the prior Stage 2 exercise emphasized.

## F. Dramatic quality and actual story movement

There is a distinct, bounded mini-story rather than only a ledger. The 1,057-word scene at Rillturn makes the crew's obligation and the false promise of the horizon palpable: the sail is visible, Jessa is tempted, Corren reminds her there may be no safe way home, and the sounding line cannot certify the outer cut. Her costly choice is to abandon the immediate possibility without claiming it was definitely a missed encounter. The repaired freshwater trough gives an independent positive reward to an ordinary act of sailing; she has delivered something essential even when she cannot deliver the treasured letter.

Eda's refusal of open-ended custody is a second ethical pressure, but it does not become a speech that magically solves the route. The resolution is one named shelf, a receipt and a limited promise. There is tenderness in the child who admires the repaired garden, and sadness in the fact that a living sister or neighbor still cannot simply be met. This continues the Series' warm fantasy identity without creating a new disaster.

**Risk:** Some emotional movement reprises Stage 4D's underlying pattern: a capable person refuses a tempting but unjustified risk and succeeds in a smaller, responsible act. The drama's distinguishing material question is **outward navigational reach**, not whether refusal is always virtuous. If used repeatedly in full Book 11, that pattern could become monotonous or reduce exploration to a string of prudent stops.

## G. Novel-length possibility versus actual qualification

The episode gives a credible **Book 11 opening or complication**: a limited service actually improves the reachable network but does not yet create an outer encounter. A reader could feel the scale of the living sea and the cost of long-distance communication without reviving the resolved Book 5 survival mystery.

It does **not** supply a complete Book 11. It does not dramatize an outer culture, reciprocal craft exchange, Nemi's independent economic struggle or the publication/privacy problem Stage 3B envisioned. Nor does it provide the Book 12 gathering's feasible transport, timing or social consent. The current episode could contribute to a longer series only if later genuinely different decisions become possible.

**Verdict at novel scale:** No Book 11, Book 12 or twelve-Book artistic sufficiency test has been passed.

## H. Supporting inventions: what was added to make this chain possible?

The most consequential [P4E] additions are **one named near-side trade dock (Rillturn), one existing-type repair-market ferry route with fresh local observations, a paid craft pilot Jessa and local custodian Eda**. Daro is a label for the already-existing Book 10 pilot, and Corren a minor crew voice. The repair goods connect naturally to Book 3's cooperative crafts and Book 6's water gardens. The existing Book 9 station and Book 10 funded service provide initial capacity. No new sea law, protagonist invention, centralized navigation authority, outer hidden route or opponent with unlimited influence appears.

These are **genuine construction choices**, not preexisting facts. Without Jessa/Rillturn, the known source evidence would warrant only the first station. The extra local leg therefore tests whether established kinds of institutions can be realized into specific opportunities at small cost. This is acceptable for a creative possibility probe but cannot be used to declare a real source-documented chain beyond the station.

**Strongest missing premise for the proposed positive Book 11 outcome:** A specific, currently reachable encounter opportunity involving **a present outer-community participant with their own purpose**, independently warranted conditions for every relevant movement/return, and voluntary permission to exchange. Stage 3B proposes the *type* of encounter, but neither its original outline nor the successful first leg supply that particular event. This is not evidence that Auteur lacks an ontology entity.

## I. Strongest adversarial reading of the preferred disposition

**Argument for \`CONTACT_UNDERDETERMINED\`:** The current sources have *no actual* contemporary outboard route. Even the second near-side leg to Rillturn is built from invented local geography and willing traders, not an accepted existing Story Structure. The speculative unknown sail appears precisely when the dramaturgy wants temptation. If all new contact-supporting facts are discounted, the probe demonstrates only a bookkeeping success at the first station. From that strict view, the causal gap to Nemi remains underdetermined, and any claim of contact feasibility would be inflated.

**Why \`CONTACT_PARTIAL_OR_DEFERRED\` remains better for the **actual constructed result**:** The task explicitly permits small, identified supporting characters and local customs and asks for one causal construction, not pure retrieval of existing canon. The local trade network, market ferries, station service, waters, patient duties and uncertainty-aware navigation are established *kinds* of worldly activity. The newly constructed Jessa leg specifies contemporaneous observations, a real paying purpose, participant agreement, conserved resources and actual arrival. It is more than a label on a missing route. The letter's physical custody changes at Rillturn; it is not merely assumed to get somewhere offstage. And the construction **does not claim outer contact**.

**What the objection correctly changes:** Any broad claim that this proves the Book 10→11 *outer connection* should be rejected. \`CONTACT_PARTIAL_OR_DEFERRED\` is a finding about real modeled **near-side progress under new plausible details**, not a backdoor positive feasibility verdict. If the decision requires actual Nemi contact now, the relevant state is **not established**.

**Adjudication:** Retain \`CONTACT_PARTIAL_OR_DEFERRED\`, with explicit notice that the disputed outer-contact transition remains a missing current-world event, not a resolved technical problem.

### One smallest warranted next responsibility (recommendation, not executed)

**Qualify the outer side of the meeting as a separate bounded creative source obligation:** Establish Nemi's **independent commercial/craft reason, current route observations, crew/resupply/return limits and willingness** to reach a specific *actually observable* near-side contact window. In a separate one-attempt, construction-first research task, follow the **outer participant's** real material circumstances rather than sending another near-side letter into unknown water. Stop if their route is unsupported; do not invent a universal chart or assume that the Rillturn unknown sail was Nemi. Only after such evidence may the narrative explore a genuinely reciprocal meeting.

This is more decision-relevant than another general series audit, schema work, rewriting Book 11 around an assumed success, or running the same near-side service with larger ships. It could substantiate an outer encounter, uncover a different real constraint or justify an author-level change to the planned Book 11 ending. Nothing here authorizes immediate implementation or new canon.
