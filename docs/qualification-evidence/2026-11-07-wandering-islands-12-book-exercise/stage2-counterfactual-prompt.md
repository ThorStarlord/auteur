## 3. The counterfactual author instruction

The following is a **new hypothetical author-directed change to the working Book 2 architecture**.

It is not a change to the six fixed Story Vision constraints.

### Changed premise: The third lantern was not an independent navigational reference

The original Book 2 architecture describes a successful rescue in which two shore watchkeepers and a moving volunteer vessel exchange observations.

The third lantern supposedly establishes an independently moving reference point. That information becomes the foundation for the three-position navigation technique, which is subsequently developed into the distributed passage network used in Book 12.

**Counterfactual change:**

During the critical portion of the Book 2 rescue, the third vessel's lantern could not provide reliable, independent positional observations.

A later examination of the rescue record establishes that its crucial bearings were unavailable during the crossing.

The third vessel was genuinely present, and the rescue still succeeded. However, the rescue **did not demonstrate a working, repeatable three-position navigation technique**.

The original explanation of why the rescue succeeded is therefore no longer valid in its existing form.

### What remains fixed

- All six author-provided Story Vision constraints.
- The moving-island premise.
- The missing island and uncertainty about its fate.
- The intended reader experience.
- The twelve-Book target.
- The fact that the Book 2 rescue succeeds.
- The third vessel's physical participation in the rescue.
- Previously proposed unrelated creative developments, unless the changed assumption has a defensible consequence for them.

The revised architecture may explain the rescue's actual success using plausible means compatible with existing world rules.

Do not introduce a convenient new magical capability, undisclosed ancient technology, or other unsupported mechanism merely to restore the old explanation.

### What is no longer established

The Book 2 rescue no longer proves:

- That the third lantern supplied an independent moving bearing.
- That a reliable three-position protocol was demonstrated.
- That the rescue's success depended on that protocol.
- That the original Book 2 event is sufficient technical groundwork for the Book 12 passage system.

The existence of the third lantern does not establish the effectiveness of its information.

A successful rescue does not prove that the original theory of its success was correct.

A future three-position system remains *possible*, but its feasibility and narrative development must now be justified independently.

## 4. First responsibility — establish the counterfactual's logical consequences

Before revising the architecture, identify exactly what changed.

Distinguish:

1. **Fixed author facts:** The six immutable Story Vision constraints.
2. **Preserved working proposals:** Stage 1 assumptions not affected by the counterfactual.
3. **Changed assumption:** The third lantern did not provide independent navigation observations during the critical crossing.
4. **Invalidated claims:** Propositions that actually relied on the original Book 2 demonstration.
5. **At-risk proposals:** Later developments that might depend on the original demonstration but could survive through other mechanisms.
6. **Unrelated proposals:** Elements that should not change merely because Book 2's technical explanation changed.
7. **New hypotheses:** Possible alternatives requiring further creative construction.

Pay special attention to **necessary versus sufficient conditions, enabling relationships, compatibility and uncertainty**.

Do not describe every later use of lanterns as invalid simply because the original Book 2 protocol is invalidated.

Equally, do not preserve the original technical dependency by redefining its terms after the fact.
