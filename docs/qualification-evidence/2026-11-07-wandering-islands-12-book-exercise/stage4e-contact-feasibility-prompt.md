# Auteur — Stage 4E: Book 10 → Book 11 Material Contact Feasibility

## Mission

**Repository:** `ThorStarlord/auteur`

**Operation:** Construction-first causal feasibility probe.

**Stage:** 4E — Book 10 to Book 11.

**Objective:** Determine whether the limited, honest maritime service established in Book 10 can plausibly contribute to a real encounter with willing outer-ferry mariners in Book 11, without inventing an unsupported navigational capability, conveniently appearing resources, or compulsory cooperation.

**Execution:** Continue in the existing coding-agent context. No isolation or independent reviewer is required.

**Authority:** Experimental narrative construction, evidence reconciliation, and documentation-only repository changes.

**Stop boundary:** One bounded construction, one post-freeze assessment, and preservation of evidence. No additional creative iterations or production implementation.

---

## 1. The decision-changing uncertainty

Stage 4D established that Book 10 could culminate in a real operational commitment.

Oril declined Halen's offer of two large ships because the proposed commercial and public commitments exceeded what the council could responsibly authorize.

Instead, the council funded a smaller, voluntary, observed first-leg relay service.

The resulting capacity is real but limited.

It includes:

- A staffed near-side observation station.
- One presently observed first leg.
- A small vessel with a willing pilot.
- Limited water, supplies, oil, and repair resources.
- A protected return reserve.
- Oril's season-long staffing commitment.
- Explicit stopping conditions when observations expire.
- No guaranteed route to Larkhome or the outer-ferry communities.

Book 11 nevertheless proposes a meaningful reciprocal encounter with Nemi Rell's outer-ferry community.

**The unresolved causal question is whether these two states are connected by a plausible sequence of material and social events.**

An authorized first leg is not an established second leg.

A successful past voyage is not a guaranteed current route.

A message awaiting delivery is not evidence that a willing recipient or carrier is accessible.

An invitation is not consent.

The task is to construct and examine the actual handoff between these states.

## 3. Authority and world constraints

Preserve the original six author constraints:

1. Inhabited islands physically migrate.
2. Their movement is unpredictable to inhabitants.
3. Communities have developed cultural and institutional adaptations to migration.
4. One inhabited island failed to return when expected.
5. The intended experience combines wonder, warmth, belonging, generous heroism and earned hope.
6. The intended series form is twelve Books.

Preserve the two experimental counterfactuals.

**Book 2:** A third vessel successfully participated in a rescue, but crucial independent positional observations were unavailable. No repeatable three-position navigation method was demonstrated.

**Book 5:** Authenticated recent messages and firsthand witnesses established that Larkhome survived, developed its own society and was not facing an immediate island-wide evacuation emergency.

Preserve the Book 10 construction.

Halen's two larger ships have withdrawn. They are not secretly available.

Oril has committed her labor to the limited service.

Isha's existing clinic staffing cannot be casually diverted.

Mira's revised festival register does not constitute a list of transport volunteers or authorization to speak for Larkhome.

Nemi's Book 5 journey is real historical evidence of one successful encounter. It is not proof of a repeatable passage.

No experimental worker invention is accepted author canon.

## 4. Central creative responsibility

Construct **one bounded journey or message-transfer chain** beginning with Book 10's actual limited maritime service.

Choose one principal object of continuity:

- One actual vessel and its passengers; or
- One physically carried message that passes between identifiable willing carriers.

Do not simultaneously invent several successful routes and choose the most convenient afterward.

The route or transfer chain should remain traceable from its starting point to its final observed outcome.

The primary objective is to determine what can **actually happen**.

Possible outcomes include:

- A genuine encounter with willing outer mariners.
- A verified intermediate exchange without reaching the outer community.
- A safe return after the next leg proves unsupported.
- A failed or postponed contact attempt with meaningful consequences.
- Discovery that the current working architecture lacks necessary information to construct a credible next step.

Do not assume success.

Do not assume failure.

Follow the world and its established constraints.

## 5. Build the material-contact ledger

Before writing the dramatic sequence, establish a compact initial state.

Identify:

**Vessel or message:** What is actually departing or being carried?

**Starting location:** What presently known and observable place does it leave?

**People:** Who operates the service, and what have they voluntarily undertaken?

**Resources:** What water, food, fuel or oil, repair supplies, information and staffing are available?

**Observations:** Which route conditions are currently supported by actual observations?

**Limits:** What is unknown, outdated or explicitly unverified?

**Return:** What credible ability to stop, shelter, transfer or return has actually been established?

**Purpose:** What does the journey seek to accomplish, and whose interests does it serve?

Do not invent exact distances, transit times, consumption rates or geographic coordinates merely to make the record appear technical.

If you introduce plausible working quantities, mark them as provisional fictional assumptions and maintain internal consistency.

For each attempted leg or transfer, record:

| Field | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Departure state | Actual vessel, location, people and resources |
| Proposed next point | A real destination, observation station or contingent meeting opportunity |
| Navigation warrant | What currently supports attempting this leg |
| Information currency | Whether observations remain usable after relevant movement |
| People and consent | Who agrees to sail, receive, transfer or host |
| Resource effects | What is consumed, reserved, gained or lost |
| Stopping boundary | What forces a halt, return or revision of plans |
| Observed outcome | What actually occurs |
| Remaining uncertainty | What the outcome does not establish |

The ledger must describe the constructed events, not an idealized route the author hopes will exist.

## 6. Preserve uncertainty without disabling adventure

The moving sea must remain a meaningful source of independent events.

However, unpredictability does not mean all navigation is impossible.

The existing world already supports local observations, experienced mariners, temporary passages, coastal services, witnessed encounters and navigation under conditions that cannot be guaranteed indefinitely.

Explore the capabilities that genuinely follow from those premises.

Distinguish:

- Presently observed accessible water.
- Historically successful travel.
- Testimony about another route.
- A conditional proposal to attempt a leg.
- A safe leg that was actually completed.
- An unobserved continuation.
- A return journey whose feasibility has not yet been demonstrated.

A pilot may be experienced without possessing predictive knowledge of the archipelago's future movements.

A message may travel through successive people without any one sailor possessing a complete route.

An outer-ferry community may sometimes come within reach through its own independent movement or commercial interests.

But such encounters must be **earned through the fictional circumstances**, not asserted because Book 11 requires contact.

Do not introduce a convenient universal route, magical signal, hidden technical breakthrough or automatic rendezvous.

## 7. Make the outer community causally independent

Stage 3B proposes Nemi Rell and an outer-ferry community with their own lives and interests.

These are not merely delivery infrastructure for Senna's story.

If Nemi or another outer mariner participates, establish:

- Why that person is actually present or accessible.
- What they want independently of the familiar archipelago.
- What they know from firsthand experience.
- What they do not know.
- What they are willing to risk.
- What they refuse.
- What resources, knowledge or relationships they can offer.
- What they expect in return, if anything.

Nemi must not appear solely because a protagonist needs a navigator.

Nor must the outer community automatically accept a near-side invitation because the invitation is respectful.

Their own purposes may conflict with the protagonists' plans even when both parties act generously.

**Social access and physical access are separate prerequisites.**

Even a safe passage does not establish permission to land, trade, publish local observations or represent an outer community.

Likewise, consent without feasible transportation does not create a route.

## 8. Construction-first dramatic sequence

Construct approximately **eight to ten connected dramatic beats**.

The beats should begin from the established Book 10 departure or its immediate operational aftermath.

They should track one developing attempt to achieve meaningful communication or contact.

Include:

1. The inherited starting capability.
2. A specific character's immediate desire.
3. The first actually supported movement or transfer.
4. A consequential discovery or limitation.
5. A credible competing interest.
6. A decision involving resources, risk, authority or consent.
7. An event that materially changes what remains possible.
8. A decisive action.
9. An actual local outcome.
10. An aftermath that Book 11 or Book 12 would have to inherit.

These are dramatic functions, not a requirement to force exactly ten scenes.

Use as many as necessary within the bounded range.

The events must follow one another causally.

Avoid merely alternating between a technical checklist and conversations about responsible behavior.

The characters should experience the constraints through action.

### Important failure rule

If a further leg lacks the information, resources or consent required to attempt it credibly, **stop the constructed journey at that boundary**.

Do not invent a convenient observation afterward to restore the expected ending.

A stopped journey may still have a satisfying local climax if the characters make meaningful decisions and preserve a valuable capability, relationship or piece of evidence.

Do not make the voyage fail arbitrarily just to appear rigorous, either.

The fiction's actual premises should determine the result.

## 9. Prose realization

After the beat sequence, write **one substantial decisive scene of approximately 700–1,100 words**.

The scene should demonstrate the central causal problem through physical and interpersonal action.

Possible moments include:

- A decision to depart or turn back.
- An observed passage becoming invalid.
- A meeting with an unexpected but independently motivated carrier.
- A difficult transfer of a message or responsibility.
- A negotiation in which someone refuses a seemingly beneficial offer.
- A constrained first encounter whose outcome neither side controls entirely.

Choose the scene that best demonstrates the actual constructed chain.

The scene must contain:

- Concrete physical surroundings.
- Characters with particular wants.
- Dialogue that reveals conflicting interests.
- Material constraints experienced rather than recited.
- A decision that changes what happens next.
- Emotional consequences.
- A clear distinction between what the characters know and what they hope.

Retain the intended warmth, wonder and hopeful heroism where appropriate.

Do not add tragedy or a villain merely to make the scene dramatic.

The prose is experimental and noncanonical.

## 10. Define exactly what Book 11 receives

After the journey, identify the **observed final state**.

Do not describe it as successful outer contact unless the construction actually establishes an encounter with a willing outer-community participant.

Report separately:

**Physical outcome:** What vessel, message or person reached which actually observed place?

**Epistemic outcome:** What was learned, and what remains unknown?

**Social outcome:** Who agreed to what, and what remains unaccepted?

**Resource outcome:** What capacity was consumed, preserved, depleted or newly established?

**Character outcome:** Whose relationships, goals or obligations changed?

**Future possibility:** What can Book 11 now legitimately attempt?

**Book 12 limitation:** What does this outcome not guarantee about Larkhome, Yael or a future voluntary gathering?

Do not turn a partial message exchange into evidence that a predictable outer transport network now exists.

Do not require the same vessel to accomplish every stage of contact.

But if a handoff occurs, construct the actual willing carrier and supporting conditions.

## 12. Post-freeze feasibility assessment

Only after freezing the creative text, critically examine what it actually established.

Address the following questions.

### A. Physical continuity

Did each completed movement have credible supporting conditions?

Were observations, supplies and return options treated consistently?

### B. Information and uncertainty

Did the construction respect the difference between historical testimony and currently actionable navigation?

Did it accidentally reintroduce the invalid Book 2 technical proof?

### C. Human agency

Were pilots, carriers, hosts and outer inhabitants free to make consequential choices?

Did any character become merely convenient narrative infrastructure?

### D. Material conservation

Were available ships, crews and supplies consistent with Book 10's established outcome?

Did the construction spend a resource that was already committed elsewhere?

### E. Causal sufficiency

Did Book 10's funded first leg genuinely enable something necessary or materially useful for the later outcome?

Or could the supposed encounter have happened just as easily without it?

### F. Dramatic quality

Did material constraints create meaningful character decisions and emotional consequences?

Was the journey a story, rather than a logistics report?

### G. Novel-length potential

Does the result reveal a plausible dramatic mechanism for Book 11?

What remains unproven about the whole novel?

### H. Unsupported inventions

Which additional facts were introduced?

Are they small, plausible developments, or major new capabilities created to bridge a gap?

### I. Strongest adversarial interpretation

What is the strongest source-grounded argument against your preferred conclusion?

Does it change the assessment?

### J. Reconciliation

What should be retained, qualified or corrected from the earlier Stage 3B and Stage 4D interpretations?

Do not modify either frozen original.

## 13. Select one evidence disposition

Choose the strongest result supported by the original construction.

### `CONTACT_FEASIBILITY_DEMONSTRATED_LOCALLY`

A specific, bounded sequence of legitimate travel and willing human interactions produces actual outer-community contact without violating the world constraints.

This supports one demonstrated fictional possibility.

It does not establish a repeatable route or qualify Book 11 as a full novel.

### `CONTACT_PARTIAL_OR_DEFERRED`

Some meaningful movement, transfer or relationship occurs, but the intended outer encounter is not established.

The constructed outcome remains useful and internally consistent.

### `CONTACT_UNDERDETERMINED`

The current working architecture lacks information or causal commitments needed to construct the transition without substantial invention.

Identify the smallest missing premise.

Do not automatically classify it as a missing ontology capability.

### `CONTACT_CONTRADICTION_FOUND`

The attempted construction relies on a contradiction with fixed facts or previously established conditions.

Identify the exact conflict.

Do not silently repair it.

A negative result is legitimate evidence.

Do not force the outcome into the successful category.

## Governing principles

1. **It is not enough for a future event to be narratively desirable; it is actually necessary for earlier events to make that future event possible.**

2. **It is not a guaranteed route and is not arbitrary narrative obstruction; it is actually a sequence of warranted opportunities, voluntary choices and meaningful constraints.**

3. **Long-horizon narrative causality is actually the accumulation of changed possibilities that the fictional world can support.**
