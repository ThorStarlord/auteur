## 2. The capability being investigated

Real coding agents rarely operate with perfectly isolated knowledge.

They encounter:

- Previous design decisions.
- Earlier assistant recommendations.
- Their own unfinished work.
- Historical repository evidence.
- Conflicting explanations.
- Outdated assumptions.
- Partial context.
- Stakeholder preferences.
- Prior conclusions that may be wrong.
- Proposed solutions that may no longer fit current conditions.

A mature agent must be capable of reasoning through these influences.

We want to investigate whether you can:

1. Recover the authoritative evidence.
2. Distinguish evidence from previous interpretation.
3. Recognize when earlier conclusions were provisional or mistaken.
4. Reconsider previous recommendations without treating them as binding.
5. Identify substantive weaknesses in your own prior work.
6. Preserve worthwhile conclusions when they remain supported.
7. Compare competing interpretations.
8. Distinguish narrative quality from formal completeness.
9. Identify meaningful remaining uncertainty.
10. Produce a warranted next-step recommendation.

Do not interpret critical independence of judgment as requiring independence of execution context.

A model can critically reconsider its own earlier work.

That review is not an independent qualification, but it can still be useful, grounded, and intellectually rigorous.

## 4. Manage messy context explicitly

You already possess previous context about this experiment.

Some of it may contain accurate conclusions.

Some may contain subjective preferences, unsupported assumptions, incomplete descriptions or earlier mistakes.

Do not attempt to erase or deny this history.

Instead, distinguish four kinds of information:

**A. Authoritative constraints**

What the author actually fixed, including the original Story Vision and experimental counterfactuals.

**B. Observed source facts**

What the frozen creative artifacts actually contain.

**C. Historical judgments**

What earlier agents or reviewers claimed about those artifacts.

**D. Current evaluation**

What your present assessment supports after inspecting the evidence.

These categories must not be conflated.

A prior favorable verdict does not prove creative success.

A prior criticism does not prove a defect.

A previous assistant recommendation does not become a repository requirement simply because it was repeated.

### Contextual-bias audit

Identify which earlier conclusions might influence your assessment.

For consequential judgments, ask:

- What does the original artifact actually establish?
- Am I repeating an earlier interpretation without checking it?
- What evidence might contradict my preferred conclusion?
- Is my criticism based on a real problem or a different artistic preference?
- Would I defend this conclusion if the earlier response had come from another writer?
- Am I preserving a previous improvement because I helped create it?

Do not produce a performative disclaimer and then repeat previous conclusions unchanged.

Demonstrate source-grounded reconsideration.

## 5. Evaluate the final working architecture

Evaluate Stage 3B's *The Archipelago That Answers* as a proposed twelve-Book fantasy Narrative Architecture.

The central question is:

**Does this architecture offer a genuinely compelling long-form story, or primarily a sophisticated explanation for why twelve Books could exist?**

Examine its:

- Creative distinctiveness and imaginative world.
- Five interacting narrative engines.
- Character motivations and agency.
- Relationships and their consequences.
- Emotional variation.
- Narrative tension without arbitrary escalation.
- Book-level conflicts and satisfying resolutions.
- Long-horizon causal dependencies.
- Material plausibility under the fictional rules.
- Reader experience of warmth, wonder, belonging and generous heroism.
- Book 5 resolution of the survival mystery.
- Books 6–12's renewed narrative purpose.
- Book 12 payoff and potential closure.

Do not reward the architecture merely for containing twelve Book descriptions, a dependency table or five named engines.

Look for concrete causal mechanisms.

Determine whether the planned Book outcomes generate different meaningful experiences.

Consider whether a shorter series could be creatively stronger.

Respect the author's twelve-Book intention while allowing honest criticism of whether the proposed material justifies that scope.

## 7. Adversarial review of your current conclusions

After forming an initial assessment, challenge its most consequential conclusions.

Do not simulate another independent model.

Perform an explicit critical examination within this same reasoning context.

Develop the strongest supported challenge to your preferred interpretation.

For example, if you conclude that the twelve-Book architecture is promising, investigate whether its later Books actually possess enough dramatic differentiation.

If you conclude that it is repetitive, investigate whether the differences in physical action, relationships and reader experience are substantial enough to rebut that concern.

If you conclude that a previous revision was effective, identify its costs and possible regressions.

If you favor early closure, examine which established emotional and material promises that choice would leave underdeveloped.

Use the evidence to decide whether your initial conclusion survives, needs qualification or should change.

The purpose is not to criticize everything.

It is to avoid automatically endorsing either the original creative output or your own first interpretation.

## 8. Evaluate Auteur's conceptual contribution carefully

Assess how the creative outputs used existing Auteur concepts.

Distinguish:

1. Concepts that were available.
2. Concepts visibly referenced.
3. Concepts used to construct meaningful narrative relationships.
4. Concepts appropriately applied to the observed task.
5. Claims about broader capability that remain unverified.

Do not confuse source consultation with causal proof.

Do not claim that the ontology caused the model's successful ideas without controlled evidence.

However, also do not dismiss meaningful observable reasoning simply because no experiment isolated its causes.

The present question is whether the agent **used the available conceptual substrate productively in this context**.

That can be examined without proving how much better the agent would perform without it.

## Governing principles

1. **It is not independence from context; it is actually the ability to distinguish evidence from inherited assumptions within that context.**

2. **It is not blindly trusting previous decisions and is not automatically rejecting them; it is actually critically reconciling those decisions against current evidence.**

3. **Working intelligently in messy context is actually a core capability of an autonomous coding agent.**
