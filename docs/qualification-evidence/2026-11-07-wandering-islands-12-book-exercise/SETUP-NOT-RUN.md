## 2. Frozen common author input — Stage 1 only

### Working title: The Wandering Islands

In a beautiful fantasy archipelago, entire inhabited islands sometimes change their positions overnight.

Their migrations are unpredictable, but generations of communities have learned to live with them. Families maintain friendships across changing seas. Traveling markets, seasonal celebrations, shared craftsmanship, and acts of generosity help people sustain relationships even when familiar places become distant.

Every inhabited island known to the communities has eventually reappeared within its expected migration cycle.

This year, one inhabited island does not return when expected.

The disappearance creates uncertainty, opportunities, competing interpretations, and consequences for people whose lives depend on the shifting geography.

The intended reader experience combines:

- Wonder, beauty, and a world that feels worth inhabiting.
- Warm everyday life, friendship, and belonging.
- Generous and courageous acts of heroism.
- Complicated choices whose consequences matter.
- Mystery, exploration, and changing understandings of the world.
- A growing sense of scale without abandoning intimate human concerns.
- Earned hope rather than predetermined victory.

The intended form is an ambitious twelve-Book fantasy series.

The series should be capable of meaningful changes in narrative emphasis, relationships, conflicts, and scope.

### Fixed creative constraints

Treat the following as author-supplied constraints:

1. The islands genuinely change geographical position.
2. The migrations are not currently predictable by the communities.
3. Communities have developed social and cultural practices around these migrations.
4. One inhabited island has failed to return when expected.
5. The intended emotional experience includes wonder, warmth, belonging, consequential heroism, and earned hope.
6. The target is a twelve-Book Series.

### Deliberately unspecified

The Story Vision does **not** establish:

- Why the islands migrate.
- What happened to the missing island.
- Whether anyone controls the migrations.
- Whether the disappearance was intentional.
- Whether the missing island is destroyed.
- Whether the underlying cause is magical, ecological, social, or something else.
- Who the protagonists or antagonists are.
- The eventual resolution.
- The narrative engine hierarchy.
- The governing thematic interpretation.
- The specific content of any future revelation.

These are opportunities for creative construction, not permissions to treat guesses as established facts.

The agent may develop provisional answers, including surprising ones, provided they are causally coherent and do not silently contradict the fixed Story Vision.
