## 4. Core conceptual distinction

Separate the following four propositions.

### A. Fictional occurrence

The original creative text says an event happened.

Example:

*The two pilots' currently inspected navigable reaches overlapped.*

That establishes what the worker wrote.

### B. Internal possibility

The event appears compatible with the world's established rules.

For instance, unpredictable island movement does not imply that all maritime travel is impossible.

### C. Causal warrant

The narrative supplies sufficient preceding observations, actions and material conditions to make the event credible rather than merely convenient.

### D. Generalized capability

The event demonstrates a repeatable technique or predictable future route.

Stage 4F does not claim D.

The main uncertainty concerns C.

Do not confuse A with C.

Do not confuse the absence of C with proof that B is false.

## 6. Second responsibility — Return and refuge adequacy

A completed outward passage does not automatically establish a credible return.

Reconstruct:

**Outbound refuge:** What was available if the near-side approach failed?

**Near-side shelter:** What could Rillturn actually provide if departure became temporarily impossible?

**Outer-side refuge:** What support was available at Lacebank?

**Homeward leg:** What current observations justified returning from Lacebank to *Kindling Tide*?

**Resources:** What water, supplies, crew capacity and maintenance margin were preserved?

**Temporal uncertainty:** What would happen if conditions changed after the trade?

**Human commitments:** Who had agreed to provide shelter, pilotage or custody, and under what limits?

Distinguish:

- Resources and refuge explicitly established.
- Plausible but unquantified reserves.
- Support newly assumed for the successful episode.
- Conditions genuinely missing from the text.

Do not demand real-world marine-engineering precision from an experimental fantasy outline.

However, do not treat a statement such as "there were enough reserves" as an independently verified calculation.

The appropriate standard is **fictional causal sufficiency**, not a real-world safety certificate.

## 8. Fourth responsibility — Adversarial contact-window stress test

After freezing the warrant reconstruction, perform one bounded assessment.

Use the exact Stage 4F journey.

Do not search for an alternate convenient harbor.

Do not change the destination.

Do not replace Tava or the near-side pilot with more capable characters.

Do not invent new observation equipment.

Do not add a previously undisclosed refuge.

### Test A — Temporal degradation

Suppose one side's observations become outdated before the entire passage is verified.

What can the pilots still justify?

Can they obtain a refreshed observation through already established capabilities?

Or must the vessel stop?

Do not assume observations expire automatically after a particular invented interval.

Use only timing relationships that can be grounded or explicitly stated as counterfactual changes.

### Test B — Incomplete spatial overlap

Suppose the observed reaches approach one another but do not establish a continuous navigable connection.

Is there a legitimate means to close the information gap?

Can incremental sounding proceed without crossing beyond the vessel's supported return capability?

Or would the pilot be relying on an unobserved middle section?

### Test C — Refuge or return deterioration

Suppose one intended refuge becomes unavailable after a movement or local condition change.

What alternative is already established?

If none exists, which decisions become unjustified?

Distinguish the ability to shelter at Rillturn from the ability to return to the outer ferry.

### Test D — Social withdrawal

Suppose the near-side pilot or Rillturn's dockkeeper declines participation.

Does the outer expedition retain a useful objective or safe return option?

Do not invent another suddenly cooperative pilot.

The right to refuse must have operational consequences.

### Test E — Resource margin

Suppose the journey consumes more time or resources than anticipated while remaining within plausible world conditions.

Does *Mothwake* retain the reserves needed for the relevant next action?

If the source does not specify enough information to answer, identify that limit rather than fabricating a calculation.

### Proportionality

These are bounded counterfactual checks, not five newly authored fictional journeys.

Explain what each changed condition does to the original warrant.

Classify each outcome as:

- Still supported.
- Supported only under an additional explicit condition.
- No longer supported.
- Underdetermined from available evidence.

Do not use arbitrary numerical confidence scores.

## 9. Fifth responsibility — Test narrative contingency

The stress test must not assume that any reliance on favorable circumstances is a defect.

Unpredictable geography is part of the creative premise.

A fortunate but locally credible encounter may be artistically valuable.

Investigate three distinct possibilities.

### Earned contingency

The characters' decisions, capabilities and observations make them ready to exploit an opportunity that arises independently of their wishes.

The encounter is contingent but adequately dramatized.

### Convenient contingency

The opportunity arrives at exactly the right place and time, and the story does not supply enough preceding conditions to make the connection feel earned.

The event remains possible but comparatively weak as a necessary Series dependency.

### Contradictory contingency

The opportunity requires something prohibited by established facts, such as predictable migration or retroactively valid Book 2 observations.

This is a genuine conflict, not merely an underdeveloped explanation.

Determine which description best fits Stage 4F.

Do not automatically favor the harshest criticism.

## 10. Sixth responsibility — Assess causal necessity

The prior experiments established several different contributions.

Stage 4D made Book 10's limited relay operationally meaningful.

Stage 4E moved the letter to Rillturn.

Stage 4F gave Nemi an independent reason to approach near-side trade.

The resulting encounter enabled an additional letter handoff.

Examine the complete causal relationship:

**Book 10 relay → Rillturn custody → independent Nemi voyage → contingent encounter → letter handoff.**

For each transition, distinguish:

- Necessary condition for this particular chain.
- Sufficient condition for the next event.
- Enabling contribution.
- Mere chronological predecessor.
- New supporting invention.
- Unresolved assumption.

Do not require Book 10 to be the necessary cause of Nemi's commercial voyage.

Its contribution may consist precisely in making the existing letter available when a separately motivated encounter occurs.

That is a potentially valid form of long-horizon causality.

## Governing principles

1. **It is not enough for an event to be possible; it is actually necessary to understand what makes that particular event credible.**

2. **It is not guaranteed success and is not inevitable failure; it is actually a contingent outcome whose supporting conditions must be distinguished from its uncertainties.**

3. **Research maturity is actually knowing when evidence supports further inquiry and when construction itself becomes the better next step.**
