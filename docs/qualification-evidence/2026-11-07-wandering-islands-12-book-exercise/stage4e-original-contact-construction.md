## One waxed letter, two witnessed near-side legs, and a boundary nobody can sing away

**Status:** Experimental first contextual GPT-6 construction for the Book 10 → Book 11 material-contact question. **No post-freeze feasibility judgment is included in this file.** It is provisional story research, not canonical fiction or an independent reviewer output. It deliberately follows **one actual physical object** rather than inventing several rival journeys and choosing a successful one afterward.

## III. First connected dramatic construction: nine beats

1. **A letter leaves on the boat that actually departed in Book 10.** The anonymous pier woman gives Daro the waxed envelope, clearly accepting his promise to reach the first station only. Its outer inscription is not a rescue plea; it asks for contact if any willing person on Larkhome can receive it. Senna watches from shore with her **own** letter still unsent. The larger Halen ships depart toward known trade, not to help. The small boat's watch starts a dated travel record.
2. **The first leg succeeds under a narrow warrant.** Daro checks the near-side mark and contemporaneous soundings and brings the boat into the actual staffed first station. He uses some supplies but does not raid the protected return water. Oril, now committed to the season's station work, writes **“received at station”** rather than **“on its way to Larkhome.”** Daro learns the next outward depth report has expired, so he stops instead of making the authorized leg quietly grow into a speculative expedition.
3. **A useful next carrier exists—but only for another kind of work.** Jessa's repair-market ferry pulls into the station with independently paid cargo meant for families repairing rain catchments at Rillturn. Her own job and local observations give her a reason to sail to that *near-side* dock now. Oril offers the envelope and asks for one honest additional handoff, not a rescue duty. Jessa agrees because her route is actually being undertaken for her own customers, but names **Rillturn only** as her obligation.
4. **The second leg is made possible by fresh local knowledge, not the distant letter's emotional force.** Jessa's crew carries the letter and freshwater-garden parts together. A present watch and local sounding let her approach Rillturn's landing; an island's recent position on an old chart is not used as a guarantee. Jessa is pleased when the ordered repair cloth and fittings actually reach people whose gardens are failing. A family invites her crew to eat; this is the work that pays for the ferry, not a distraction from its more glamorous possible mission.
5. **A new opportunity appears, but it is only half an opportunity.** From Rillturn's low dock, a single unfamiliar sail is visible beyond the unsounded outer cut. Its cloth could belong to any one of several visitors. People remember that Nemi once brought news from Larkhome and some immediately call the unknown sail an answer. Jessa has sight of an object, not the sailor's identity, not the depths to reach it and not consent to board or trade.
6. **A sincere conflict has a cost in the present.** Eda, the dock's keeper, says the letter cannot be housed on her dry shelf forever; she already shelters repair notes, seed orders and patient ferry notices with finite space and labor. Jessa wants desperately to complete what she promised to the stranger on the council pier. Her mate points out that chasing an unsounded sail could cost the crew, the return water and next market work without delivering anything. A nearby family offers to watch the letter, but cannot promise an outward carrier. An offered hand is not an offered sea route.
7. **Decisive action: Jessa declines to turn a sighting into a passage.** She takes her own sounding line to the very last usable known-water mark, where the current at the exposed cut defeats a justified retreat. She does not sail out past it. The distant sail has moved farther away; no readable reply ever came. She signals **“unknown / stop”** to the near-side watch rather than waving the envelope as if the unseen ship could somehow read it. She loses the one moment in which she might have been close to an outer visitor, and accepts that she cannot prove it was an opportunity at all.
8. **The local outcome is a real, small custody relationship.** Jessa returns to the safe Rillturn landing and asks Eda to hold the envelope **until the next ordinary market return opportunity**, with a specific return instruction if no independently identified outer carrier is available. Eda agrees for this limited period because Jessa and the station will shoulder the record/return costs; she refuses an unlimited promise. They inscribe the date, custodians and **“not delivered”** on a slip outside the wax. The original letter remains sealed. Rillturn's freshwater-garden families receive the goods Jessa came to deliver.
9. **Book 11 must inherit the actual smaller result.** The message has traveled through two presently warranted near-side passages and two genuine willing transfers. It is now at Rillturn, farther through the *near-side human network* than it began, under a finite handoff instead of a fictional guarantee. Oril will have no receipt until it is physically carried back. Nemi has not appeared, the distant ship is unidentified, and no Larkhome contact exists. Jessa returns to known trade when warranted, with a new understanding of what a truthful promise costs. A future Book 11 outer exchange will require an independently motivated, **actually present and willing** outer person, plus contemporary observations for any leg beyond Rillturn. Nothing in this episode supplies that missing encounter automatically.

## IV. Decisive prose realization — the shelf at Rillturn

The sail beyond Rillturn's outer reef was no larger than Jessa's thumbnail when she first saw it. It was pale in the morning glare, then briefly dark as a cloud crossed the water. She could make out one square of cloth and the suggestion of another.

“Outer boat,” said a boy who had come down with the water-garden baskets.

“Boat,” Jessa corrected.

“It's just like the one that brought the news.”

“You weren't here when that boat came.”

“My grandmother was.”

He said it triumphantly, as though a grandmother's memory could sound an unfamiliar passage. Jessa couldn't blame him. All across the known islands people had kept lamps lit for Larkhome after they learned its people were alive. Hope was not something one simply put away because it had become less useful as evidence.

The waxed envelope sat beneath a wooden weight on Eda's workbench. It had crossed from the council landing to the first station on the small public boat, then come aboard Jessa's ferry with a bundle of fresh-water cloth and copper fittings. The wax had held. So had the two boats' promises, such as they were.

“Can you take it?” Eda asked.

Jessa watched the distant sail. “I can take it to someone who might be able to take it. That isn't the same.”

Eda shut the little drawer in her desk. “I meant, can you take it farther *today*? I have a shelf, not a service to the edge of the world.”

Behind them the rain-garden makers were unpacking Jessa's cargo. A small girl held up a repaired catching trough to the sunlight, delighted to see water collect instead of slipping through a seam. Her mother called Jessa to come look. Jessa smiled and said she would.

The boy ran to the end of the dock. “It's turning! The sail is turning toward us.”

It might have been. The sea was full of angles that appeared to change when the islands moved. Jessa fetched her lead line and stepped into the ferry. Her mate, Corren [P4E, minor crew name], looked up from the water barrels.

“Are we going?”

“We're going to the last mark.”

“Not beyond it.”

“I know.”

They pushed along the currently observed approach where the dock's soundings had been refreshed that morning. Jessa counted the line into the water. The bottom came up, then fell away. A ribbon of pale foam curved across the outer cut, showing the current she had seen in older notes, but not the depth beneath it today. The last known safe turning mark lay behind her; the next stretch had no return-soundings current enough to stake her crew on.

The distant sail drifted behind the reef. Whether it had been heading inward or outward was suddenly impossible to tell.

Corren set a hand against the tiller. “If we cross that line, we may have to find a new way home.”

Jessa thought of the woman at the council pier. She had pressed the envelope into Daro's hands as though he were taking hold of her pulse. *Tell the next person the same thing,* she had said: a first station, then another willing hand if conditions allowed. No destination had been promised. Yet people would remember the whole undertaking as a promise to carry letters. They always did, once an envelope had left the hand that wrote it.

“Give me the line again,” Jessa said.

Corren passed it. The lead swung and vanished. Jessa listened for the pull and the changes in the vessel's movement. She could see the pale sail. She could not see a bottom, a safe passage or anyone offering to receive the letter.

“Stop,” she said.

Corren had been waiting. He backed the sail, and the ferry turned within the water they had actually checked. The distant boat slipped past the opening. It sent no signal Jessa could read. She would not know whether she had refused a meeting or saved two people from a reef.

They returned to Rillturn with their spare water untouched and the envelope no farther outward than it had been.

Eda was sorting thin slips of paper beneath an awning. She had drawn a line through an old name and written a new one at the top of a list of ferry collections.

“There,” she said without looking up. “You've seen how little space I have.”

Jessa held out the envelope.

“I didn't go,” she said. “I could have sailed toward it. I couldn't tell you what I was sailing into.”

Eda took the parcel but did not lay it on the shelf. “How long?”

“Until your next repair-market return. If no willing outer carrier appears with an actual route and a safe way to leave, send it back toward Oril on the first checked near-side leg.”

“Don't make me promise that leg will exist on market day.”

“I won't. Only that you'll give it to someone when the water and the person both allow.”

The girl with the catching trough came over, carrying a cup of water. “You brought our cloth,” she said to Jessa. “It works. Look.”

She poured the cup into the trough. Water gathered, trembling silver in the repaired seam. Her mother watched, one hand around the girl's shoulders.

Jessa felt foolishly near tears. She had crossed a living sea to bring people what they knew they needed, and it had been enough for one afternoon. The same sea would not give her the people she had hoped to meet.

Eda found a clean receipt. She wrote **RECEIVED AT RILLTURN**, then looked up. “What do I put under delivered?”

“Not delivered.”

“Even if it stays with me?”

“Especially if it stays with you.”

Eda added a date and her own name. Jessa wrote hers beneath it. The letter remained sealed, its blue wax a little chipped from two boat decks and a station shelf.

Outside the dock, the outer sail was gone. The boy had stopped watching for it. He was helping the garden makers carry their repaired troughs home and asking how the catching cloth changed the sound of rain.

Jessa watched them and saw a voyage she had actually completed. That did not make the unfinished one a lie. It meant that the letter, for now, had a place to stay—and someone willing to tell the truth about where it was.

## V. Observed final state and the exact Book 11 inheritance

**Physical outcome:** The single original waxed envelope traveled (1) on Book 10's funded near-side boat **from the council shore to the existing staffed first station**, and (2) by a separately willing repair-market ferry **from that station to Rillturn near-side dock**, under fresh local observations at each actual departure/landing. Jessa made a short checked approach to the last known-water mark near Rillturn, then **turned back within observed water**. The envelope **never crossed the outer cut** and remains physically sealed on Eda's dry receiving shelf at Rillturn pending a limited, explicitly revocable next handoff. No return relay receipt has reached Oril yet.

**Epistemic outcome:** People now know that this particular small near-side relay chain can be carried out under the specific observed conditions. They know a sail was seen beyond the reef, not who occupied it. **No currently safe outer passage, Nemi sighting, distant pilot, Larkhome route or repeatable service has been discovered**. The pilot's observation categories retain different meanings: current sounding, witnessed sail, uncertain identification, expired report, and delivered custody are not interchangeable.

**Social outcome:** The sender agreed to conditional onward offers; Daro, Oril, Jessa and Eda accepted **specific bounded duties** and documented where each ended. Rillturn's residents got their paid water-garden supplies. Neither the distant sail nor Nemi nor the recipient was contacted or consented to anything. Eda is a temporary custodian, **not a new government or a representative of the recipient**.

**Resource outcome:** Book 10's limited operating allowance supplied the first leg and station reception; Jessa's second leg used **her independent paid near-side service** without appropriating the council's protected return water or Isha's clinic labor. Dock shelf space and clerk work have costs, with explicit limited duration. Halen's two large ships remain elsewhere. Neither the first-leg crew nor the second ferry has infinite availability.

**Character outcome:** Oril's refusal to overclaim now becomes an operational practice whose actual state is recordable. Jessa loses an emotionally attractive opportunity—**perhaps** an outer encounter—but does not forfeit crew, return stores or paid repair work chasing a stranger across unsounded water. Eda chooses bounded hospitality rather than perpetual unpaid caretaking. The sender may be disappointed by the delay, but nobody has told her the letter was delivered; a return receipt must still actually travel back.

**Future possible Book 11 move:** A willing outer mariner could eventually enter an actually observed, safe, accessible near-side landing, or a separately qualified escort chain could connect from Rillturn onward. **Either requires a new real event and new current evidence**; Book 10's station makes someone able to receive, hold and truthfully pass a letter at the near-side margin, not summon the next person. If no such event occurs, the letter must be returned under the agreed bounded terms when travel permits.

**Book 12 limit:** No inference follows about a future mutually chosen market gathering, Yael's wishes, Larkhome's future course, repeatable routes or consent to contact. None of these is bought by two successful near-side legs. The story can remain hopeful because people have performed what they promised and preserved the possibility of a future honest reply, not because the sea has secretly yielded its unknown stretches.

**Frozen construction boundary:** This is **one** selected message, one linear account of actual movements and the point where the remaining route stopped. No alternate successful Nemi encounter, creative repair of the outcome, general architecture rewrite or comparative feasibility judgment is supplied in this original text.

### Governing principles

1. It is not a route merely because a letter moved; it is actually a sequence of individually warranted legs and limited custody commitments.
2. It is not failure to love the distant and is not proof that contact is impossible; it is actually care constrained by what people can safely observe and willingly offer.
3. A credible partial journey is actually an earned change in what the next story can attempt.
