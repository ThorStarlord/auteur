# Auteur — Stage 4D Post-Freeze Dramatic Differentiation Assessment

**Disposition: DIFFERENTIATION_DEMONSTRATED_LOCALLY — bounded, provisional.** The two first-attempt dramatic sequences exhibit different principal agents, acts, losses, emotional paths and local completions. This does **not** qualify two whole novels, the twelve-Book series or the independent Stage 4 review.

**Chronology:** This assessment was begun **only after** the complete initial creative artifact [stage4d-original-construction.md](stage4d-original-construction.md) had been created **once** in GitHub commit 9dfeedf29a57c8318399081086ba6827f14dd335 and read back with verified Git blob **0ba1cd7ea285d7631e275cb8409ad5b82644bf49**. The creative file was not subsequently rewritten. The construction contains **seven connected beats for Book 7 and seven for Book 10**, followed by **889-word and 946-word** prose samples respectively. This document evaluates *that* attempt, not an imagined improved version.

**Evidence hierarchy:** Story Vision and the Book 2/Book 5 counterfactuals are fixed experimental constraints; [Stage 3B](stage3b-original-worker-response.md) is provisional working fiction (original blob e268a11dec7536874c9afa6dd8077e5b0da8e3ec); [Stage 4C](stage4-contextual-review-original-response.md), particularly C1–C3 and L, is a contextual hypothesis to test (original blob f57dfaf0407b5c5905edfa66369f8046c3ab72f3); the new frozen first creative text is the direct object of evaluation. None is accepted author canon.

## A. Sources of dramatic pressure: not merely festival versus council

**Book 7:** A culturally inherited **one-piece blue runner and one-home procession** cannot accommodate the child Pela's actual two-home life. This is a household-level conflict before the moving geography becomes urgent. When the island begins to drift at the opening celebration, actual safe crossing ends, so the old ritual cannot physically achieve its promised togetherness. Iren's choice is whether to keep an ancestral object intact or sacrifice its indivisibility so both her daughter Tir and granddaughter Pela can participate without pretending their shores have joined. The consequence is in cloth, voice, separation, regret and a new performance. The critical event cannot be resolved by an abstract assertion about representation.

**Book 10:** Halen's **two financed ships** can make a larger outward attempt, but the attached Return Charter would bind two seasons of public station oil, priority landing access and an inadequately evidenced travel promise. Mira's Book 7-authored register exposes one dubious claim of representation; importantly, Halen **corrects his preamble** and the negotiation continues. The remaining pressure is debt, work, finite public resources, current soundings, and the difference between authorizing a first locally observed leg and promising an outer service. Oril must choose an actual public operational undertaking, losing her planned maker's voyage and the availability of Halen's faster hulls.

**Judgment:** These are different causes of obstruction. Book 7's old ritual fails a particular living family **when geography separates bodies**; Book 10's attractive charter fails a financially and physically verifiable commitment **even after its language is corrected**. The distinction is present in the constructed actions, not merely their declared genres.

## B. Agency: who actually drives the sequence?

| Function | Book 7 | Book 10 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central initiator | Pela demands a place in both family songs; Mira attempts to classify; Iren owns the decisive artifact | Halen offers ships; Mira challenges a claim; Oril owns the technical/public operational decision |
| Credible opposing good | Iren protects generations of stitched memory and the integrity of a shared communal event; Tir must keep her real ferry service | Halen needs paying voyages and enforceable terms; families need messages to travel; Isha must protect already staffed medical coverage |
| Irreversible local choice | Iren herself cuts an heirloom at the blank seam; Pela does not risk an unsafe plank | Oril commits her season and public first-leg capacity; Halen takes his two ships away |
| Senna's role | She contributes reliable public life news but is **not** the decisive ethical voice or rescuer of the scene | She frankly wants Halen's fast ships, remains disappointed and holds her own letter back; she does not win the hearing by reciting an autonomy principle |

**Finding:** Moving decisive agency from the repeatedly well-informed Senna to Iren/Pela and Oril/Halen addresses part of Stage 4C's concern about one heroine executing the same “correct” choice four times. It does so without character assassination: Iren has a defensible love of the intact runner, Halen changes the inaccurate preamble but still cannot finance Oril's conditions, and Senna remains emotionally divided.

**Limit:** Tir's independent inner desire is thinner than Iren's and Pela's, and Halen's lender requirements are asserted rather than dramatized from the lenders' own viewpoint. These are viable characters in bounded sequences, not yet deep ensembles.

## C. Costs and material changes

**Book 7's loss** is specifically domestic and ritual: Iren will never have the original unbroken first procession *that evening*, loses the inherited intact runner, cannot hold mother and granddaughter together and remains hurt. A repair could later reunite the material, but would not undo that opening night's lost form or establish a universal new custom. Pela gains a public role in a festival shared across two actually separate shores, not the impossible physical reunion she wanted. The cut carries an emotional debt: her grandmother had to surrender something precious for her welcome.

**Book 10's loss** is the withdrawal of two large private hulls for that season, real near-side public supplies committed elsewhere, and Oril's own season of maker travel. Halen's financial livelihood is preserved at the cost of not carrying the waiting families' letters; some letters cannot be accepted for delivery to Larkhome. The new smaller craft actually departs a **locally observed, return-capable** first leg. It does not solve the wider passage. Book 9's clinic crews are neither confiscated nor the excuse for another abandoned emergency expedition.

**Material comparison:** Cutting a runner does not authorize ships. Voting to authorize a bounded service does not repair a familial missed dance. Both have costs that persist beyond saying “consent matters.” The vividness of the losses, though, is still a quality hypothesis about written scenes.

## D. Emotional trajectories and evidence from the prose

**Book 7:** Preparation and granddaughter pride become an embarrassing classification, then fear as the plank is removed. The emotionally decisive passage is Iren's own choice to cut the **unnamed joining seam**, and Pela's song being answered across widening water. Tir cannot be held beside Iren; the little delayed chorus and a baker's mistimed singing turn disappointment into genuine laughter without canceling loss. The final scene does not label the loss beautiful; Iren is allowed to grieve her altered inheritance. Source: original construction, Book 7 beats 1–7 and its 889-word prose realization.

**Book 10:** The reader sees splendid ships, paid crews and families waiting with letters. Halen corrects the register misuse but offers a substantive commercial objection to a purely voluntary, indefinitely uncertain service. The central emotional question becomes whether to take two ready hulls on an overstated public commitment or genuinely fund a smaller one. The large ships leave visibly; a tiny patched-sail craft departs between their wakes; Senna and Oril admit they partly wanted the rejected choice. Source: original construction, Book 10 beats 1–7 and its 946-word prose realization.

**Finding:** The prose samples give distinguishable sensory experiences—bread, cloth, family and unharmonious music versus contracts, draft cards, water barrels, waiting letters and departing hulls. They contain different forms of generosity: an elder releases an owned heritage object; a navigator accepts accountable service with personal opportunity cost.

**Caution:** Both scenes resolve through an admirable, self-denying offer and end on a light/response image. The shared moral grammar remains conspicuous. Their separate material mechanisms make it **cumulative thematic variation in this local test**, not proof that the same pattern can carry seven more Books without wear.

## E. Causal continuity: does Book 7 *actually* change Book 10?

There is an **observable, limited** dependency. Mira's revised festival register—created after Pela's two-home request and Iren's runner sacrifice—has individually authored statements rather than a single-origin roll that can be treated as unquestioned public identity. In Book 10, Halen cannot convert it into a vote to speak for Larkhome. He **changes the charter's opening** after seeing this record. The construction therefore demonstrates that the prior Book affects what can truthfully be argued and signed later.

The effect is **not** the direct cause of Book 10's final resource outcome. Once Halen revises the preamble, the high-stakes conflict remains: the council cannot truthfully obligate two seasons of stations to unobserved passage or grant exclusive priority on that basis; Halen's financial terms still exceed what Oril will certify or council members will fund. The rejection and small near-side undertaking could largely happen even without Book 7's festival change.

**Critical correction to the first construction's own wording:** Book 7 depicts **self-described names and publication permissions**. It does **not** depict an actual person pledging a ferry or a labor shift. Thus the construction's statement that the register later helps identify *near-side volunteers who have actually offered service* is **not established by the illustrated Book 7 events**. Book 10 would need fresh voluntary service commitments, which it does show through Oril and the pilot; those commitments must not be silently attributed to Pela's festival attendance. This is a specific weakness in the proposed B7→B10 service link, not a reason to revise the already frozen text.

**Confidence:** Differentiation of the local dramatic events is well supported by the constructed beats and samples. Their cross-Book necessity is only **partial**. The Book 7 record is a consequential constraint on a misused argument, not yet the material prerequisite of Book 10's positive solution.

## F. Local satisfaction and possible novel-length runway

Each sequence provides a recognizably dramatic local completion: **the first festival genuinely happens in a new way** and **a smaller boat actually leaves under an accountable authorization**. Both are better than a chapter-ending charter declaration or a table labeling Books as different genres.

But the Book 7 episode alone could be an excellent *chapter* or extended festival climax rather than a complete Book 7. To support a full novel, the source would have to realize more than one affected family, the social history of the old runner, how a lasting practice spreads, and what Mira's record excludes or enables. The original file does not invent these additional arcs.

Likewise Book 10's departure is a potentially satisfying climax to a political-adventure Book but needs the larger buildup of port relationships, conflict over financing, earlier crew contracts, measurable navigation/return risks and the lasting consequences of the lost hulls. Halen's new financing detail is a helpful seed, not a ready-made novel.

**Judgment:** Local **event-level dramatic generativity** is demonstrated. **Book-length sufficiency** and the **twelve-Book target** remain unqualified.

## G. Invented support: small, usable, but decisive

The construction requires **no new magic, antagonist faction, new vanished island, catastrophe, secret travel technology or sixth major engine**. Book 7 uses only three related new supporting characters (Iren/Tir/Pela) plus an heirloom runner and a festival practice. Book 10 adds one shipowner (Halen), two offered hulls, financier terms, a limited station budget and explicit observed/expired records; those fit Stage 3B's previously specified Return Charter, Book 3 repair economy, Book 6 water supplies, Book 9 station and Stage 2 evidence norms.

This is a **strength with a measurement limit**. The exact cuttable banner, dual-home family and financier deadline were **created by the Stage 4D worker** to supply the missing dramatic pressure. They were not already richly defined in Stage 3B. The probe shows the working world can **accommodate** credible localized construction, not that the prior outline **already contained** these two excellent scenes ready to unfold automatically.

Book 10's newly detailed commercial terms especially carry its differentiation: without Halen's exclusive fees and finite funding condition, the council might revert to a simpler refusal of unauthorized representation. This is an epistemically important addition, not an illegitimate invention under the task's bounds.

## H. Regression and consistency risks

1. **The geography as cue rather than autonomous world:** The host island starts moving at the maximally dramatic festival moment, just as the child is torn between homes. This is compatible with unpredictable overnight island movement but **conveniently timed**. A Book realization must make the shift's contingency credible rather than a device rewarding correct values.
2. **Artifact sacrifice as too-neat symbolism:** Iren's cut affects a beloved object at a blank seam conveniently located to spare every stitched name. That allows a humane choice without the graver cost of erasing any individual memorial. It is apt but perhaps conspicuously engineered; the scene retains the unbroken-dance loss, yet does not prove the broader society will preserve the change.
3. **Financial disagreement as easy characterization:** Halen's terms are plausible, but the two ships' withdrawal and the council vote settle quickly in a single scene. His crew and lenders have no direct voices. A novel would need to explore those obligations rather than reduce him to the keeper of a conditional gift.
4. **Stage 2 epistemic constraints held:** Book 10 treats one present local leg as observed, the outer passage as unknown and lanterns as communication only. It does not infer future island positions from a ship's safe arrival. The small boat has water and return reserve; its actual next station is observed, not a magically discovered outer destination.
5. **Book 5 proof held:** The life messages and personal note remain true; no new rescue emergency or contrary hidden message appears. Senna's grief is about distance, not uncertainty whether Yael's whole community lives.
6. **Book 8/9 continuity mostly held:** Published qualified stillmoor impact and the partial retrofit explain current landing constraints; the hospital's already promised staffing remains protected. The new station funds are a separate near-side budget, not a retcon of the canceled Stage 3A expedition.
7. **Book 9-to-10 novelty risk:** Stage 3B had already established volunteer stations. The Stage 4D council's contribution must be understood as a newly **funded and operationally bounded service**, not as invention of the first observation station. A reader must see the difference to find the Book 10 climax an actual advancement.

No violation of the six author facts or either counterfactual is visible in the constructed scenes. These are **bounded consistency observations**, not schema/model or reader-tested validation.

## I. Strongest alternative interpretation, and adjudication

**Objection to the positive local disposition:** Both scenes could be the same moral fable. A person is excluded by a one-size-fits-all communal practice; an authority or elder recognizes difference; a generous individual surrenders something; a new arrangement respects consent; the final light signals hope. The differences in object—runner versus ship allotment—may only decorate a repeated trajectory. Moreover, the new family, cuttable seam, ships and loans were consciously invented to make the Books appear different. The Book 7 change only edits Halen's preamble, so the causal bridge is weak; a stricter reviewer could select **DIFFERENTIATION_PARTIAL**.

**Best rebuttal grounded in the first samples:** Book 7's decisive loss is **Iren's own inherited object and foregone physical family dance**, caused by a child with multiple lived homes and geography that makes a single circle impossible. Book 10's decisive loss is **two withdrawn private vessels and Oril's forfeited season**, caused by bounded public authority, unverifiable continuation of route evidence and real financial obligations. The two central actors are not Senna. Halen corrects the representational language and still refuses an unfundable bargain, so the climax cannot be explained solely as “learn that other people have rights.” Both samples show concrete choices that directly alter who can sing and who can sail.

**Adjudication:** Select **DIFFERENTIATION_DEMONSTRATED_LOCALLY**, not because the two Books are now independently compelling novels, but because the *observed bounded constructions* meet the stricter event-level criterion of **different causes, choices, costs and emotional realizations**. The objection survives as a warning about repeated self-sacrifice tone and a *partial* B7→B10 long-horizon dependency, and must not be misrepresented as refuted at series scale.

## J. Reconciliation with Stage 4C and next warranted responsibility

**Stage 4C C1 (Books 7/10 may repeat the same representation choice):** The risk was real in the Stage 3B synopsis, but **this first construction demonstrates that it is not an unavoidable consequence** of the five-engine architecture. Book 7 can be driven by a family-held ritual under moving-island pressure; Book 10 can be driven by an operational/financial public promise that persists after a record's political ambiguity is corrected. Stage 4C's diagnosis should be narrowed to an **unrealized/underdeveloped differentiating mechanism in the outline**, not a proof of irreparable repetition.

**Stage 4C C2 (Senna always performs the correct ethical decision):** Local risk is reduced by giving Iren and Oril costly decisions. Senna remains present with genuine, unfulfilled longing instead of serving as the universal adjudicator. That does not eliminate the broader twelve-Book concern.

**Stage 4C C3 (distant voices remain mediated):** **Unchanged** in this probe. Neither scene gives Yael direct present agency; nor is it supposed to. The Book 5 note remains an optional source-established proposal.

**Stage 4C C4 (material routes and contingent mariners):** **Unresolved, and more decision-relevant now.** Book 10 finances only a local observed leg, yet Book 11 seeks meaningful encounter with Nemi's outer-ferry community. The gap between a near-side service and an actually reachable outward human network cannot be filled by a council authorization alone.

**Stage 4C overall twelve-Book sufficiency concern:** **Somewhat strengthened**, narrowly. Two adjacent-ethic Books can support distinct local drama without changing the larger series. No author-scope change is warranted. Finished novel lengths, scene variety across each Book, actual world trajectories and Book 12's emotional density remain untested.
