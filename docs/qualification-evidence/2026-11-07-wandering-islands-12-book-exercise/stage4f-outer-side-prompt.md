## Task identity

**Repository:** `ThorStarlord/auteur`

**Operation:** Construction-first narrative causality and autonomous character-agency probe.

**Stage:** 4F — Following Stage 4E's partial Book 10 → Book 11 contact exercise.

**Guiding question:** Can an independently motivated outer-ferry community develop a materially credible opportunity for reciprocal contact with the familiar archipelago, without existing merely to satisfy the protagonists' narrative requirements?

**Expected outcome:** One bounded original outer-side dramatic construction, a material and social feasibility record, a frozen original response, and a subsequent critical assessment.

## 1. Research objective

The Wandering Islands experiment has established several useful results.

Stage 4D demonstrated locally differentiated drama in Books 7 and 10.

Stage 4E then tested the proposed causal transition between Book 10's limited maritime service and Book 11's reciprocal outer-community encounter.

That attempt produced a meaningful but incomplete result.

The original wax-sealed letter traveled through two credible near-side movements:

1. The council landing to Oril's staffed station.
2. Oril's station to Rillturn aboard Jessa's independently operating repair-market ferry.

It remained sealed under Eda's temporary custody at Rillturn.

An unidentified sail appeared beyond an unsounded passage, but no safe continuation or outer-community contact was established.

The result was:

`CONTACT_PARTIAL_OR_DEFERRED`

The next uncertainty is not simply how to move that letter farther.

It is whether the **outer community possesses its own causes that could bring it into an actual, voluntary relationship with the near-side communities**.

### Central research question

Can the narrative construct an outer-side sequence in which Nemi or another outer-community participant acts for reasons arising from their own circumstances—and those actions create a materially plausible opportunity for reciprocal encounter?

The answer may be yes, partially, or not yet supported.

Do not predetermine the outcome.

---

## 2. What this experiment investigates

The experiment has three interrelated objectives.

### A. Autonomous motivation

Nemi's community must have its own interests, commitments, relationships, limitations and reasons to act.

Those reasons must remain intelligible even if the near-side protagonists had never sent their letter.

### B. Material feasibility

The outer-side journey must respect the setting's movement uncertainty, actual observations, finite provisions, willing crews and conditional opportunities for shelter or return.

A voyage completed years earlier cannot establish present accessibility.

### C. Reciprocal causality

If the outer and near-side storylines intersect, determine what actually produces that intersection.

Distinguish a contact that happens independently of Book 10's relay from one that is materially enabled or transformed by it.

**The objective is not to force two storylines to meet. It is to discover whether their independently developed causes can support a meaningful meeting.**

Preserve all six original Story Vision constraints:

1. Entire inhabited islands physically migrate.
2. Their movements are unpredictable to inhabitants.
3. Communities have developed cultural and social adaptations.
4. One inhabited island failed to return when expected.
5. The intended emotional experience combines wonder, warmth, belonging, generous heroism and earned hope.

But that single journey does not grant Nemi a presently usable route to Rillturn or another near-side destination.

## 6. First responsibility — Construct an autonomous outer-community situation

Before considering near-side contact, establish a plausible current situation for Nemi and the outer-ferry community.

Treat them as an actual society within the fictional world.

Identify:

**Community:** What kind of community is involved, and how does it sustain everyday life?

**Material conditions:** What resources, repair needs, trade obligations, shelter arrangements and movement constraints currently matter?

**Principal characters:** Who can make consequential choices?

**Independent purpose:** What does Nemi or another participant currently want to accomplish?

**Competing interest:** Who disagrees, and for what legitimate reason?

**Existing commitments:** What work, relationships or obligations would be affected by a new journey?

**Available knowledge:** What contemporary observations are actually accessible?

**Uncertainty:** What does the community not know?

**Potential gain:** What worthwhile outcome could justify the contemplated action?

**Potential loss:** What might be sacrificed even if the voyage is locally successful?

Choose one coherent situation rather than inventing an exhaustive society.

### Independence test

Explicitly answer:

**Would this outer-community situation still generate a meaningful choice if the Rillturn letter did not exist?**

The preferred construction should pass this test.

A willingness to exchange crafts, seek medical knowledge, fulfill a commercial obligation or maintain existing relationships may be plausible, provided those motivations arise from actual circumstances.

Do not select a motivation merely because it produces the desired destination.

The community's own objective must have dramatic significance.

## 7. Second responsibility — Select one bounded outer-side undertaking

Construct one specific undertaking by Nemi or another outer-community participant.

The undertaking might involve trade, maintenance, social obligations, craft exchange, exploration, care or another established kind of activity.

Do not require that its original destination be Rillturn.

Do not create several alternative journeys and choose whichever successfully intersects the letter.

The undertaking must have:

- A concrete initial purpose.
- An identifiable vessel or message chain.
- Willing participants.
- Actually available resources.
- Current and historical observations distinguished clearly.
- Specific opportunities for shelter, transfer or retreat.
- A credible decision point.
- Consequences if the undertaking is deferred or abandoned.

Choose the starting circumstances creatively but record which details are new experimental inventions.

A new local port, ordinary trade connection or supporting character may be reasonable.

A newly invented permanent route across unpredictable migrating seas is not a small supporting detail.

## 8. Third responsibility — Material feasibility ledger

Create a concise ledger covering the actual outer-side undertaking.

For each attempted movement or transfer, record:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Starting state | Actual vessel, people, location and resources |
| Independent purpose | Why this movement is undertaken |
| Destination or opportunity | What is being approached and why |
| Navigation warrant | Current evidence supporting this leg |
| Information currency | What observations remain valid |
| Crew and consent | Who agrees, refuses or sets conditions |
| Resource consequences | Supplies, labor, money or opportunity spent |
| Shelter/return capability | What actual fallback is currently supported |
| Stopping boundary | When the attempt must halt or change |
| Observed outcome | What actually happened |
| Unresolved uncertainty | What cannot be inferred afterward |

Do not invent exact travel times, distances or consumption figures for decorative precision.

Use provisional quantities only where they help establish meaningful constraints, and maintain consistency.

The point is not to mechanically simulate an ocean.

It is to ensure the fictional events obey their own causal rules.

### Separate four different things

1. An independently motivated reason to travel.
2. A physically feasible opportunity to travel.
3. Willingness from other people to receive or negotiate.
4. An encounter that actually occurs.

One does not automatically imply the others.

## 9. Fourth responsibility — Construct the dramatic sequence

Produce approximately **eight to ten connected dramatic beats** from the outer community's perspective.

The sequence must show:

1. An ordinary but consequential outer-community situation.
2. A specific desire that predates any new near-side invitation.
3. An opposing interest or genuine constraint.
4. A decision to attempt, postpone, redirect or refuse an undertaking.
5. A materially warranted movement or transfer, if one occurs.
6. A changing condition that affects what remains possible.
7. An interpersonal decision with consequences.
8. A local climax.
9. An observed final state.
10. An aftermath that may influence Book 11.

These are dramatic functions, not mandatory formulaic beats.

### Character agency

Do not make Nemi automatically the most competent person in every situation.

Nemi may have incomplete information, responsibilities that limit travel, or a legitimate conflict with other participants.

Crew members, traders, craftspeople and hosts should have their own motives and rights to refuse.

Do not make the entire community speak with one voice.

### Emotional variation

Stage 4E already demonstrated a prudent stopping decision.

Stage 4F should seek a genuinely distinctive emotional experience when the material supports it.

Possible experiences include discovery, adventurous exhilaration, conflict between generations, professional pride, generosity between strangers, curiosity, nostalgia, surprise, affection or an unexpectedly difficult trade-off.

Do not artificially require optimism, danger or failure.

A safe decision is not automatically the right dramatic answer simply because earlier probes emphasized restraint.

A bold undertaking can be warranted.

A generous risk can be justified.

A successful exchange can be earned.

The standard is causal and emotional credibility, not universal caution.

## 10. Fifth responsibility — Test possible intersection with the near-side network

Only after the outer-side motivation and initial undertaking are established should you evaluate whether its development creates a meaningful intersection with the familiar archipelago.

Possible outcomes include:

- Nemi or another willing participant reaches a currently supported near-side location.
- An outer participant encounters a genuinely available near-side carrier.
- Reciprocal communication occurs without physical meeting between the principal characters.
- The outer crew obtains a possible approach but lacks the observations or resources to complete it.
- Contact remains socially unwelcome despite physical access.
- The outer undertaking serves its own purpose without intersecting the near-side network.

All are legitimate.

### If contact occurs

Show what each party actually knows and agrees to.

Distinguish:

- A visual sighting.
- Identified presence.
- Safe physical reachability.
- Permission to approach.
- Actual conversation or exchange.
- Acceptance of a message.
- Agreement to carry something farther.
- Proof of delivery to the ultimate recipient.

Do not collapse these into one event.

Contact with an outer-ferry participant is not automatically contact with Larkhome.

Contact with Larkhome is not automatically Yael's consent to travel.

A first successful meeting does not establish repeatable annual access.

### If contact does not occur

Do not force a late-stage rescue of the plot.

Preserve the actual outcome.

Explain what the outer-side undertaking accomplished independently and what remains missing for reciprocal contact.

## 11. Counterfactual causal checks

The resulting construction must be examined against three counterfactual questions.

### A. Remove the Rillturn letter

Would Nemi's undertaking still take place?

If not, the purported independent motivation may be derivative of the near-side storyline.

If yes, identify what changes when the letter becomes available.

### B. Remove Book 10's funded relay

Would the same outer-community encounter still be possible?

If yes, Book 10's relay may be useful but not causally necessary.

If no, identify the exact resource, observation, custody mechanism or communication opportunity that makes the difference.

Do not assume every earlier Book must be a strict prerequisite for every later event.

### C. Remove the conveniently timed encounter

Does the outer-side sequence still possess a meaningful local dramatic outcome?

If it collapses without meeting the near-side protagonists, the outer community may still be functioning as plot infrastructure rather than a genuine narrative agent.

These checks belong to the **post-freeze assessment**, not to an iterative rewrite of the original creative construction.

## 12. Sixth responsibility — Prose realization

Write one substantial scene of approximately **700–1,100 words**.

Select the scene with the strongest actual dramatic event.

The scene should be primarily experienced through the outer-community characters.

Demonstrate:

- A concrete lived environment.
- An identifiable participant's desire.
- Material and social pressures.
- Distinct character voices.
- A consequential choice.
- Visible or felt effects of the migrating geography, where relevant.
- The limits of what characters know.
- Emotional movement that cannot be reduced to a moral statement.
- An outcome that changes what a character can do next.

Avoid dialogue that merely explains maritime governance.

Avoid a scene whose only purpose is to celebrate a clever logistical solution.

If contact occurs, make the encounter two-sided.

If contact is postponed, let the scene still achieve a meaningful local resolution.

Respect the warm, wondrous and hopeful tonal orientation without removing legitimate costs.

## 14. Seventh responsibility — Post-freeze assessment

After verifying the original construction's identity, assess it in a separate document.

### A. Autonomous agency

Did the outer-community characters demonstrate independent purposes, choices and costs?

Would their story remain meaningful without the near-side letter?

### B. Material continuity

Were every completed movement's observations, resources and stopping conditions supported?

Did the construction introduce any unearned navigation capacity?

### C. Social reciprocity

Did any contact involve actual willingness from both sides?

Were conflicting interests preserved?

### D. Dramatic quality

Did the outer-community storyline produce meaningful events and emotional consequences, or primarily a logistical account?

### E. Difference from Stage 4E

Did the new construction create a different kind of story from Jessa's cautious stop at Rillturn?

Or did it repeat the same narrative pattern?

### F. Causal integration

If an encounter occurred, did Book 10's relay materially influence it?

What would have happened without the relay?

### G. Supporting inventions

What new characters, locations, routes, purposes or capabilities were introduced?

Which are modest realizations of existing world concepts, and which are substantive new architectural commitments?

### H. Remaining Book 11 obligations

What still needs to happen to produce the reciprocal exchange proposed in Stage 3B?

### I. Remaining Book 12 obligations

Does anything now support later communication with Larkhome, Yael's independent decision or a mutually chosen gathering?

What remains entirely conditional?

### J. Adversarial interpretation

Present the strongest credible challenge to your preferred finding.

Do not treat either success or failure as automatically more rigorous.

### K. Reconciliation

Which Stage 3B, Stage 4C, Stage 4D or Stage 4E claims should be retained, qualified or revised?

### L. Next responsibility

Identify one smallest decision-changing next research responsibility.

Do not execute it in this Stage.

## Governing principles

1. **It is not independence from the protagonists in name; it is actually possessing causes, interests and choices that remain meaningful without them.**

2. **It is not a convenient encounter and is not an endless sequence of prudent refusals; it is actually a materially warranted intersection between independently developing lives.**

3. **Reciprocal narrative causality is actually the meeting of autonomous storylines whose interaction changes what each can become.**
