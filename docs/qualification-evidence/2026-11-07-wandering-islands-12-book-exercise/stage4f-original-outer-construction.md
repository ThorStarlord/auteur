An unfamiliar sail Jessa observed **remains unidentified**; this experiment does not retroactively identify it as Nemi or change Jessa's journey. Senna's separate private letter to Yael remains unsent.

**New [P4F] inventions, not prior canon.** The inhabited outer-ferry vessel *Kindling Tide*, its small work cutter *Mothwake*, the locally observed outer-side refuge Lacebank, maker Ivo, pilot Tava, a replaceable copper collar used on the ferry's rain-catching troughs and one particular outer-edge trade window are created here. ## II. Independent outer-community situation

*Kindling Tide* is an inhabited ferry where families live, tend herbs in sailcloth troughs, mend boats, teach children textile work and bargain over which labor and water to spend. Salt and repeated handling have loosened the metal collars that keep several garden troughs useful. The problem is not a new catastrophe or pretext for rescuing Larkhome: without replacements, the keepers can **dismantle one productive herb bed** to recover binding material, losing familiar food, work and pleasure.

**Nemi's preexisting purpose:** use craft and firsthand knowledge from the genuine Book 5 near-side visit to seek a small barter for workable metal collars from **any currently accessible, consenting near-side repair maker**. The offer is an adjustable, woven water-catching sleeve. The objective exists before Nemi has heard of Rillturn or its letter.

**Genuine opposing interests:** Ivo made the prototype and has promised the original family pattern to the home gardeners. Ivo refuses Nemi's initial suggestion to trade it without permission, and agrees only to bring a **second, personally authored prototype**. Tava controls the cutter's sailing and refuses to spend the home ferry's communal travel reserve or cross an unsounded gap. Nemi wants a repair outcome; Ivo wants fair recognition and protection against surrendering every local craft secret; Tava wants all three to survive and return. The community is not unanimous, and Nemi is not its infallible navigator.

**What happens without any letter:** the herb-bed sacrifice or a separate trade attempt still has to be decided. A limited outing could still win one collar, fail for lack of willing traders or produce useful craft observations. The letter is neither an initiating cause nor a concealed destination.

**Bounded available resources:** One small cutter, three willing people, a personally owned sleeve sample, ordinary trade goods, water and provisions with a **protected return share**, a sounding line, local repair tools and an outer-side shelter whose current approach Tava has recently checked. The populous home ferry remains at its **presently observed mooring** with its own crew, protected stores and unpromised future movements.

## IV. First dramatic construction: nine connected beats

1. **A valuable ordinary life is wearing through.** On *Kindling Tide* several garden troughs lose water along worn fittings. Children still harvest herbs, but the caretakers want to dismantle one favorite bed for repair cloth. Nemi remembers the metalcraft seen during an earlier independent Book 5 visit and proposes a limited search for an accessible craft trader—not another Larkhome expedition.
2. **A smaller person's refusal changes the plan.** Ivo will not let Nemi trade away the one sleeve design already promised to the family's gardeners. Nemi recognizes Ivo's ownership and offers their own labor to help make a separate tradable prototype. Tava agrees to captain *Mothwake*, **not Kindling Tide**, on a scout with actual reserve supplies and a refusal right.
3. **An outer undertaking begins without near-side information.** The cutter reaches Lacebank using Tava's present soundings. Its purpose is barter for collars. No one knows of the waxed letter. If no safe cross-water opening appears, Ivo will have to help dismantle a garden bed on return.
4. **A possible trade site is earned through observations, not a remembered address.** A repair pennant is visibly flying above an inhabited near-side dock. Tava sounds progressively toward it, keeping retreat water checked. The near-side watch signals willingness to examine a trade request; a local pilot confirms depths from the other side. This time their **current observations overlap**. Tava elects to cross with the cutter; they later learn the dock is Rillturn. This new favorable state is not the earlier mystery sail and not an established future route.
5. **The attempted bargain exposes different obligations.** Eda cannot sell several collars when near-side families also await repairs. The local maker offers **one** available collar in return for a working sleeve demonstration. Nemi's immediate instinct is to demand more. Ivo refuses to disclose the outer community's original weave or sheltered locations merely to get a better price.
6. **The local climax belongs to both makers.** Ivo fits their prototype around a leaking trough; the repair maker tests water flow. They jointly alter the replaceable loop and see a modest working result. Ivo agrees to trade the *sample and its taught adjustment*, not the family's inherited design. Eda releases one collar with a local cost. It is a bold, mutual craft success that does not repair all outer gardens.
7. **The unforeseen letter is a second independent negotiation.** Only after the trade does Eda ask whether anyone on the cutter would **voluntarily accept conditional custody** of the one sealed letter. She describes exactly how Jessa and Oril transferred it; nobody claims it is delivered. Tava refuses a Larkhome voyage and Ivo asks who will bear failed-carriage work. Nemi accepts **only custody to Kindling Tide**, recorded as such, subject to a safe return and future willing handoffs.
8. **Leaving takes fresh evidence too.** The pilots sound the entrance again; conditions permit the cutter's return to Lacebank and the outer ferry in the observed window. The larger inhabited boat never traveled. Tava retains protected water; the crew does not substitute the letter's importance for knowledge of a new safe leg.
9. **Two communities change without becoming one.** Ivo uses the single new collar to repair one trough on *Kindling Tide*, leaving other gardens' needs still contested. Near-side makers keep the new sleeve adjustment and have parted with one scarce collar. Eda knows Nemi as an actual, self-described, willing outer carrier, but cannot promise a regular connection. The original letter is sealed aboard *Kindling Tide*; its intended addressee remains unreached. The unknown sail from Stage 4E remains unknown.

## V. Decisive prose scene: what a sleeve is worth

Nemi had promised Ivo that the sleeve would not leave their hands without a price they chose. That promise was harder to keep at Rillturn than it had been at home.

The repair dock smelled of hot metal and wet baskets. Tava had moored *Mothwake* where the near-side pilot directed, with enough line to respond if the entrance changed. They had reached it by sounding one part of the water while another pilot sounded from the opposite side. The crossing had been real. No one could make it remain real tomorrow.

Eda laid a copper collar on her workbench, its rim warm from straightening.

“One,” she said. “I can spare one. Other families are waiting.”

“I asked for four,” Nemi replied.

“And I wish I had four to offer.”

Ivo held their woven sleeve beneath a coat. The pattern had taken the evenings they were supposed to spend repairing the family's own garden. It was a second prototype, not the original the elder gardeners had already claimed.

“You promised not to sell it for me,” Ivo said.

“I haven't.” Nemi lowered their voice. “I only asked what the work might bring.”

The near-side repair maker put a leaking trough on the bench. Her wrist bore a pale burn from an old forge.

“If your sleeve holds water, I'll show you how to set the collar. If it doesn't, we can still share lunch.”

Ivo spread the cloth over the torn seam. Its loops caught the light like a net made for catching rain. “Hold this corner,” they said to Nemi.

Nemi obeyed. Years ago, when their ferry had carried the first trustworthy word that Larkhome lived, people across the familiar sea had praised Nemi as a great navigator. Now they were holding damp fabric while a younger maker corrected their grip.

The woman tipped a cup of freshwater into the sleeve. A bright bead swelled beneath the fabric and fell inside the trough. Another followed it. Ivo pulled a loop tight and the water stopped escaping at the torn edge.

Eda leaned forward. “You can change that part without emptying the whole bed?”

“If your hands can reach the knot,” Ivo said. “That's what our old weave couldn't do.”

“Would you teach us the knot?”

Ivo watched the collar on the bench. “That knot, yes. Not the pattern my family keeps, and not where our boats sleep.”

“I mend copper,” the woman said. “I have no wish to buy your sea.”

“Other people have,” said Ivo.

The woman nodded once. “Then they've asked too much. One collar for this replaceable sleeve and a lesson?”

Nemi almost interrupted. The outer ferry needed several collars. But Tava stood by the gangplank watching the angle of the mooring, waiting for a fresh report before they could go home. Asking for everything might mean leaving with nothing.

Ivo considered. “One,” they said at last. “And you may tell other makers how this loop works, but not claim you invented it.”

“Then tell me your name,” the woman said.

“Ivo.”

She wrote it beside the sketch Eda was keeping. Ivo corrected the line for the loop before the ink dried. The maker slid the collar across the bench.

The weight of it changed Ivo's expression. It would repair one trough. It would not save the entire herb bed the caretakers wanted to cut apart. Still, when Ivo turned the metal, its curved edge made a small bright sun in their palm.

Eda waited until the bargain had been made. Then she opened a drawer and took out a waxed envelope, its surface scuffed by careful handling.

“This arrived with a repair ferry,” she said. “A woman wishes it carried toward Larkhome, if anyone can truly take it farther. I promised not to leave it here forever.”

Nemi did not touch the envelope. “I didn't come for a letter.”

“I know. That's why I can ask rather than appoint you.”

“Do you know who it's for?” Ivo asked.

“Not by opening it. The outside asks for a willing hand. The sender knows the route may stop.”

Tava came closer. “We have water to return if the current entrance is still as observed. We do not have a voyage to Larkhome.”

“Nor do I,” Nemi said.

Ivo held up the collar. “If we take it and find nobody else, whose work does it become?”

“Ours, until an honestly agreed return or onward handoff,” said Nemi. “No secret delivery promise. We write exactly where it is.”

Eda looked troubled. She had watched enough people bring letters to know the difference between a promise that comforted them and one that could be kept.

“I can write *received by an outer carrier*,” she said. “And *not delivered to Larkhome*.”

“Write the cutter, too,” Nemi said. “If the return water closes, it may wait here rather than go anywhere today.”

They signed the custody record. Nobody opened the wax.

The near-side pilot called from the entrance: he had sounded the usable approach again. Tava checked the marks and decided the cutter could depart under those conditions, keeping its reserve for the known outer shelter. Ivo tucked the collar beneath a coat and raised a hand to the maker, who raised a blackened hand in answer.

As *Mothwake* left, Nemi watched Tava take the water one sounding at a time. Lacebank's lee appeared first, and only then the inhabited ferry beyond it, gardens shining where the sun reached the cloth troughs. They could not predict where that home would travel next.

“One won't repair everything,” Ivo said.

“No,” Nemi replied. “But it repairs something we came to repair.”

“And the letter?”

“It has come farther,” said Nemi. “We mustn't call it anything more.”

Back at *Kindling Tide*, Ivo went to the gardeners with the collar. Nemi put the sealed envelope in the crew's dry cabinet and wrote its custody terms before accepting supper. The voyage had given both communities work they could actually use—and left other work, especially the distant letter, unfinished.

## VI. Observed final state, inherited possibilities and strict stop boundary

Ivo retained the inherited outer weave and gave a prototype/lesson by choice; Tava retained the authority to stop the cutter. A meaningful local trade story would have occurred without the letter.

**Completed travel and evidence.** The small cutter, not the whole home ferry, completed a locally sounded outbound approach to Lacebank, a separately verified overlap with the **presently welcoming** Rillturn pilot, an actual Rillturn berth and a return **rechecked before departure** to the current outer shelter and home vessel. Its success is a *new event [P4F]* contingent on presently observed conditions. It is neither proof of an old hidden channel nor a guarantee that a future island movement will permit the same leg. The earlier unidentified Stage 4E sail remains unidentified.

**Actual reciprocal contact.** Nemi, Ivo and Tava meet Eda and a willing repair maker. Each side has separate needs and can refuse. They perform a real, small, two-way craft exchange with specific material costs and change in knowledge. The outer team did not travel at the invitation of near-side protagonists; Rillturn's local makers did not have to accept the trade. Nemi's historical Book 5 role is background knowledge of a craft *possibility*, not today's navigation warrant.

**Book 10's influence and the intact letter.** The original waxed envelope exists at Rillturn because Book 10's funded first leg and Stage 4E's separately willing repair ferry actually transported it. Eda voluntarily transfers **the same still-sealed letter** to Nemi under a conditional custody contract *after* the independent craft barter. The letter is now physically on *Kindling Tide* in a dry cabinet, **not delivered to Larkhome or its intended recipient**. Senna's separate note remains unsent. With no Book 10 funded relay, this specific letter would not be available for the exchange; the **outer-side trade meeting itself could still have occurred**.

Any later Nemi journey, Larkhome contact, route disclosure dispute, continued supply exchange or repeated meeting requires independent present observations, consent and available resources. The named outer shelter, Rillturn crossing and favorable custody-period timing are substantial **new story contingencies**; their credibility and effect on the broader architecture must be assessed after freeze.

**Book 12 limits.** Larkhome's inhabitants are known alive but have not received this letter. Yael has not consented to a journey or meeting, and no neutral market gathering, reliable interbasin passage or guaranteed return has been established.
