# The Wandering Islands — Stage 2 Counterfactual Architecture
## Original working response to the lost third-lantern bearings

**Status:** First-response, agent-proposed, noncanonical Stage 2 counterfactual revision of the original twelve-Book working architecture. No author acceptance, product change, certified Series file, independent review or later-stage challenge is implied.

**Immutable source of comparison:** Stage 1 original architecture in PR #528, head `ab74e9c270b44404c8ed2f3cc2d632c443e5e3fe`, Git blob `fe482137f119234c573d3331223d3ed689989ec2`. This response proposes an alternative to some of that original text; it does not update, reinterpret as canon, or overwrite the frozen original.

## 1. Exact change: the rescue happened; its technical proof did not

**Counterfactual instruction [CF]:** During the decisive Book 2 crossing, the third vessel was physically present, and the apprentices and medicine were rescued, but its lantern provided **no reliable independent positional observations**. The critical bearings were unavailable; a later examination of the surviving record establishes this. Consequently, the rescue cannot be cited as a successful demonstration of repeatable three-position navigation. The original account of *why* the rescue succeeded is wrong in that respect.

That creates a precise epistemic difference:

- **Observed in the story and held fixed:** There was a successful rescue; a third vessel helped; two inhabited islands had changed position; Oril, Senna and the rescue crews made consequential choices; the displaced cargo and subsequent generosity mattered.
- **The new negative observation:** There was an absence of usable independent third-vessel bearing data at the decisive time. The *existence* or visibility of a lit lantern is not evidence that it supplied reliable measurements.
- **Invalidated technical explanation:** “Two shore reports plus a trustworthy moving third reference demonstrated a repeatable three-position navigational protocol” is false as a description of this rescue. The original Book 2 claim that such a rule was learned from the event must be withdrawn.
- **Not entailed by the counterfactual:** That three-position navigation is impossible in principle; that all lantern signals are untrustworthy; that the shore lamps failed; that no pilot can navigate a short visible stretch; that Larkhome must be impossible to contact; or that the harbor structures caused the data failure. None of these follows.
- **New causal burden:** Identify an ordinary, bounded mechanism for this particular successful rescue; and provide any later long-distance access capability with evidence **other than** the invalid Book 2 “successful protocol.”

Let R be the observed rescue, P the availability of the purported third positional measurement, and D the claim that the incident demonstrated a general repeatable method. Under the counterfactual, R is true, P is false in the critical interval, and D is not supported. **R does not imply D**; nor is P necessarily a prerequisite for every rescue. A new reconstruction H may explain R without claiming that H has become a universally validated method. This distinction matters even if the author eventually chooses a Book 12 route that uses genuinely validated three-point bearings from some later date.

### Preserved author facts [F1–F6]

1. Inhabited islands genuinely move geographically.
2. Their migrations are not currently predictable by the communities.
3. Communities have established social and cultural practices around the movement.
4. One inhabited island does not return in the expected interval.
5. The reader promise combines wonder, warmth, belonging, consequential courage and earned hope.
6. The form remains twelve Books.

None of these author facts contains a third lantern, any navigation protocol, a known cause of island movement, the island's ultimate fate, or a predetermined finale.

### Preserved working proposals [P], not author facts

The Reciprocal Archipelago's five co-governing engines, Larkhome and the Harrow sisters, Stillhaven's stillmoor teeth, Isha's clinic, Mira's market ledger, Tovin's technical and moral conflict, Book 3's cooperative repair market, Book 4's independently monitored inlet, Book 5's partial exploration, Book 6's rain gardens, Book 7's traveling festival, Book 8's contested soundings, Book 9's distributed compact, Book 10's representation question, Book 11's outer-gyre possibility, Book 12's proposed contact and consent, and the recurring empty place at a table **all remain available working proposals** unless a specific dependency below requires alteration.

The original **technical** Book 2-to-12 chain is invalidated. The original **human** Book 2-to-12 association—someone stayed to help, and later someone must willingly answer—can survive as a changed thematic promise only if later events do the actual technical work. One cannot silently reclassify the disproved technical claim as a technical fact with different words.

## 2. What actually got the Book 2 ferry home: a bounded reconstruction

The counterfactual does not dictate the rescue's actual means, so the following is a **new proposed local explanation [H-RESCUE], not something proved by the mere absence of bearings**.

After the two islands' overnight migration, the ferry and the nearby rescue vessel are close enough for ordinary signaling, shouted instructions at short range and a cautious boat-to-boat approach. The crossing takes place through a **short, locally observable** stretch of changing water. The two shore lamps are usable as indications of each shore's *present* whereabouts and as stop/hold signals, but not as a reliable pair of coordinates on a fixed chart.

Oril attempts the proposed three-position calculation. The third vessel's crucial independent observations are not received; no reliable third bearing is available for the dangerous interval. **The rescuers do not navigate by the missing calculation.** Instead a shallow-draft crew advances by existing seamanship: moving slowly, checking depths beside the hull with a sounding line, observing immediate currents and keeping a short retreat route in sight. The third vessel provides people, a deck and tow/transfer capacity; it stays near the endangered ferry as a physical escort and emergency refuge, not as a verified reference point. When the channel becomes too uncertain, the crews stop. They move the apprentices and medicine to the third vessel in a limited window when observed depth and local visibility permit. Some freight is lost and the rescue vessel is damaged. The episode does **not** establish a reproducible open-water route or an ability to predict either moving island's next location.

**Necessary conditions for this proposed local rescue:** an accessible short-range approach existed at that moment; the third boat could physically assist; crews had ordinary local sounding and steering skill; a safe-enough observation window and time to transfer people existed; and Senna, Oril and others chose to persist. No new magic, forgotten technology, clairvoyance, stable global coordinates or infallible chart is introduced. **Sufficient conditions are not proved:** even those factors would not make every comparable rescue safe.

**Existing Stage 1 details protected:** Senna delays her personal search for Yael to help the children; the visiting community's cargo loss and its replacement by others remain; Oril declines a private ownership claim over rescue practices; Isha records an initial rescue procedure; Mira records missing intervals and a near-failure. Those last two facts become important because the initial report overstates what was observed, while the ledger contains enough omission to uncover the error. The counterfactual specifies that *later examination* establishes the absence. It does not require every character to know this during the rescue.

**What Book 2 truly establishes:** a striking **unverified technique claim**, a successful collective rescue under imperfect information, an incomplete log, a voluntary craft-and-care obligation, and a consequential choice by Senna. It **does not establish a valid long-range navigation method**. The title *The Third Lantern* now has deliberate double meaning: a light may show that somebody stayed, but it need not show where the vessel is.

### Cross-Book constraints that are easily confused

- **Book 4 independent measurements remain evidence about harbor works**, not about the efficiency of a Book 2 technique.
- **Book 6's supply network is necessary to this selected slow relay expedition's endurance**, but not sufficient for the existence of a navigable water route.
- **Book 9's stations can be useful without any triangulation**, and the fact they exist does not establish a continuous offshore passage.
- **Book 10's consent and epistemic standards are justified by who is exposed to rescue risk**, even when the navigator is using only ordinary local soundings.
- **Book 11's arrival at an outer community is conditional on finding safe enough observational stages**. It cannot be treated as an inevitable consequence of wanting to travel there.
- **Book 12's far-side answer is not sufficient for safe crossing**: it is a required act of communication/permission for this proposed ending; actual passage still requires local observation, capable crews, supplies and safe conditions.
- The two sisters' reunion, if earned, **does not retroactively prove** the rescue technique that failed in Book 2.

## 4. Two substantive revision alternatives

### Alternative A — Rebuild the technical proof through an explicit research arc

**Mechanism:** In Book 4, the rescue-log audit disproves Oril's first account. Oril and Isha publicly retract the “demonstrated three-position method.” A later research program uses *actually timed and independently recorded* moving-vessel observations; starts in safe sight of two shores; compares calculated bearings to contemporary soundings; repeats the trials after shifts in relative position; and certifies a method **only if it can distinguish its failures from its successes**. This is ordinary testing of proposed navigation rather than a hidden magical instrument.

**Earliest honest technical setup:** Book 5, or later, after a genuine trial. Book 2's blank log motivates the research but provides no positive proof. If repeated experiments fail, the method is not restored simply because the narrative needs it.

**Book progression and cross-engine effects:**

- Book 4 adds an audit and a public methodological embarrassment, without changing Tovin's independent stillmoor test.
- Book 5 tests a limited three-observation protocol near a safe shore. The experiment may yield a narrowly qualified procedure rather than long-distance ability.
- Book 6 supplies observers and calibration crews, but remains about wells.
- Books 8–9 create reproducible records and staffed observatories; arguments over access to trials become a civic conflict.
- Book 10 publishes rigorously limited protocols with criteria for withholding a “safe route” signal.
- Book 11 must still verify connected relay legs and undertake discovery where stations cannot see; three reference points do not conjure a station beyond the last known horizon.
- Book 12 can return to a *proven later* version of three-position navigation, with Larkhome's voluntary far-side watch supplying an observation essential to the last short-range leg.

**Required new assumptions:** Oril's later equipment and crew can provide independent, timely observations; repeated trials can validate usefulness under specified conditions; stations can be maintained at appropriate moving positions; relevant local geography sometimes permits a connected chain. These are *provisional future developments*, never justified by Book 2's lost bearings.

**Merits:** Pays off the apparent technical theme in a satisfying altered form: a failed explanation leads to disciplined innovation. Makes empirical error, correction, and communal public science dramatically useful. Preserves much of the original lamp geometry and technical suspense.

**Costs and risks:** A substantial research program absorbs narrative space from the original ensemble; the world may begin to feel governed by an increasingly universal navigation algorithm. A writer might secretly smooth the validation difficulties, invent perfect timing or conveniently position an observer whenever needed. Most importantly, if a certified method returns only to reproduce the original climax, the counterfactual may be narratively neutralized. This alternative is not rejected as impossible; it requires actual later scenes of method construction and must pay for them.

**Effect on emotion:** Wonder shifts toward the excitement of public invention. The risk is replacing earned generosity with an engineer's eventual victory, but a disciplined version could instead make trust in fallible cooperation the source of triumph.

### Alternative B — The honest gap and the chain of witnessed passages (selected)

**Mechanism:** The rescue is explained through *physical participation*, short-range sounding, local pilots, hazard signals and decisions to pause; **no generalized three-position solution emerges from it**. The technical development over the series is not “better geometry of moving lights” but **accountable local access**, stitched together by people who continually report what they can actually see, escort travelers through stretches they presently know, and return when the next leg is not justified.

The decisive new institutional practice is an **open passage record** distinguishing three report types:

1. **OBSERVED NOW:** a sighted shoreline or vessel, immediate depth/current measurement and the time and observer responsible; valid only within a stated local observation window.
2. **REPORTED / NOT YET CONFIRMED:** another crew's testimony or an older sounding, useful for choosing where to investigate, **not** sufficient to send families into an unknown channel.
3. **UNOBSERVED / EXPIRED:** missing bearings, interrupted signals, stale shore positions; these are preserved as data gaps, not silently completed by a map.

The record is an ordinary ledger and shared signal vocabulary. It is **not** an invented sensor, long-range radio, supernatural network or algorithm for forecasting island migration. Messages can be relayed by existing short-range lights, ferry crews and physical transport; beyond sight or practical sailing distance, someone still has to sail and look. A report can guide *the next cautious attempt* but never guarantee safety across a changing sea.

**Book 2-to-12 transformed trajectory:** The third vessel saved lives by remaining present when its data were absent. Later, the archipelago learns to make responsible offers of passage with explicit unknowns. In Book 12, a messenger/pilot from the outer side and a voluntarily returned signal allow a bounded final passage to be attempted, but only after contemporary depth checks and consent. The climax pays off the **difference between witnessing and claiming to know**. The physical lantern returns as a signal of participation, not a geometric solution.

**Causal prerequisites:** Book 3's reciprocal craft/fuel economy; Book 4's willingness to correct records; Book 5's actual short observed passages; Book 6's potable water and resupply; Book 8's public raw-data norm; Book 9's willing maintenance sites; Book 10's open, veto-capable signaling rights; Book 11's incremental route scouting and a contact with an outward-facing community; Book 12's far-side pilot and up-to-date local observations. If the Book 11 access conditions are absent, the expedition cannot truthfully report success: it must pause or return. The possibility of reaching Larkhome is a provisional outcome, not a guarantee conferred by the method.

**Merits:** The mistake changes the meaning of the whole Series without rewriting unrelated Books. It strengthens the relationship between the epistemic and care engines: a safety report has moral significance precisely because others act on it. Oril learns a genuinely hard limit rather than simply inventing a better lamp. Isha's refusal to endanger patients becomes a way to define what evidence is enough to travel. Larkhome's reply is not reduced to a missing technical input. The rescue's loss of proof **remains consequential at Book 12**.

**Costs and risks:** Less geometric or gadget-like spectacle; more dependence on credible logistics and on the author making sightlines, return supplies, fatigue, and pilot knowledge legible. There is a danger of turning the epic finale into a bureaucratic chain of permissions. The positive spectacle should come from tangible shared action—an unfamiliar harbor opening, hundreds of ordinary watchkeepers passing a message, blue cloth arriving on a small boat—and from a genuine possibility of refusal, not a council delivering a speech. The far-side pilot cannot arrive conveniently without narrative groundwork.

**Effect on emotion:** The reader's delight at a clever rescue matures into respect for people who admit they have not seen. Suspense remains in the water's physical uncertainty and in an absent person's freedom to answer or refuse. Earned hope means a passage can be attempted responsibly **without** certainty of return, and sometimes must wait.

**Selection:** **B** is the preferred *working* revision. It does not assert that A is impossible or inferior under every authorial preference. B better preserves the original multi-engine balance while allowing the failed explanation to produce a materially new creative insight. The choice is an agent proposal, not accepted Identity, and remains open to author preference.

## 5. Selected revised causal foundation and knowledge statuses

A credible replacement must distinguish **material means**, **information** and **permission**.

**Physical movement [preserved P]:** islands move on an uncertain ocean; no island's next location is predictable or controllable by the communities. Vessels can sail; a vessel cannot teleport to a future island position. Nearby shores and ships sometimes become visible. Short-range sounds, lamps, watchkeepers and material depth measurements are already part of the Stage 1 world. An individual passage is possible where *present* depth, weather, visibility and available craft justify it; it is not thereby repeatable across unobserved future conditions.

**What a lantern can truthfully do [revised P]:** a lamp can communicate presence, a request, current observations, an agreed signal code, a stop warning or consent. If its location is independently observed and its bearings are reliable it *may* function as a reference, but Book 2 did not establish that. **Do not infer position from illumination**.

**Book 2 local rescue [new H]:** short-range sounding-guided transfer and escort as specified above. The third vessel provides a physical platform and people, not missing independent coordinates. The details of exactly which crew sounded which shoal remain open to Book-level realization; no hidden new capability is required.

**Correcting a record [new P]:** Book 4's case review, motivated by Mira's recorded gaps and Isha's medical-safety requirement, publicly withdraws Book 2's “proven rescue procedure.” This is a formal correction to the working world history, **not** a retrospective assertion that the characters knew it in Book 2. The author might later choose Book 5 for this disclosure instead, without altering the physics or the later dependency findings.

**Navigation for later voyages [new P with conditional use]:** Book 5 demonstrates a limited, re-surveyed local passage; Book 9 supports known short routes and physical message movement; Book 10 publishes explicit uncertainty statuses and permit/refusal procedures; Book 11 scouts genuinely observable stages toward the outer region, turning back when none can be justified. The record may be carried across multiple legs, but any crossing is authorized only with appropriately recent and locally sufficient evidence. The network connects *people and observations*, not distant destinations on a timeless fixed map.

**The outer-gyre approach [conditional P]:** Book 11's exploration can meet experienced navigators from the outer circulation at a reachable boundary, following actual sightings and soundings, not a guaranteed route. The cloth and unusual cadence establish **a clue**, not certain identification of Larkhome. A carried request for contact can travel further by ordinary trading crews. Book 12's possible return invitation must be carried by an actual person or short-range signal chain with intermediate participants—not by an instant transoceanic lantern. The last leg is undertaken only after a far-side local pilot or watch reports current conditions. If it cannot do so, the fleet waits. The Series can plan an eventual successful contact without pretending it was mechanically inevitable.

**What remains uncertain:** how often a temporary safe route survives a migration; exact range and reliability of local signals; the stillmoor works' share of Larkhome's altered route; whether Yael wants sustained visitors; how long an outward search might reasonably take. These uncertainties should be managed by story decisions, not filled by invented global laws.

## 6. What happens to the five generative engines

The architecture is **not rebuilt** around the counterfactual. It keeps the original Reciprocal Archipelago ensemble.

- **E1 — migrating sea (preserved):** genuinely mobile, ecologically interactive and unpredictable. By refusing a universal three-lamp solution, the revision strengthens the sea's independent causal role. Sailors must work with locally observed conditions; “unseen route” and “impossible route” remain different claims.
- **E2 — commons of keeping faith (preserved, sharpened):** aid now includes the cost of maintaining observations and the right to withhold a misleading safety report. The labor of a volunteer station is not heroic only when it emits light; sometimes it is heroic because it reports “unsafe/unknown” and turns families back.
- **E3 — stillmoor economy (substantively preserved):** clinical safety, engineering externalities and independent inlet measurements retain their sources. The Book 2 error demonstrates a general habit that must be avoided—mistaking benefit for causal vindication—but does not cause the sediment changes or erase the benefits of harbor engineering.
- **E4 — maps and knowledge as power (stronger role):** a new **epistemic distinction within existing narrative reasoning**, not a new ontology entity: an observation can be missing even if an outcome is successful, and a trusted record must mark that absence. Book 4's disclosure, Book 8's disputed evidence and Book 10's open standards now form a more explicit transformation of how communities justify rescue decisions.
- **E5 — kinship across distances (preserved, newly resonant):** Senna's Book 2 choice matters because she physically stays, not because she masters navigation. By Book 12, Senna must also learn to stay *at the edge of another person's decision*: Yael's home is not a bearing Senna is entitled to demand. The analogy should arise through action rather than a speech equating people with instruments.

### Particular character-state changes

**Oril:** In Book 2 she is a courageous improviser who *believes* her third observation helped. The Book 4 audit gives her a choice: protect the story that made her famous or publicly acknowledge that an indispensable observation is missing. She retracts the claimed technique and commits to shared field notes and bounded signaling. She does not become an incompetent navigator; she becomes a capable one willing to separate rescue success from method validity. Her technical arc is now partly about the discipline to say “I do not know.”

**Isha:** Her initial Book 2 rescue procedure was responsibly recorded given apparent success but is unsafe as an unqualified standard once the data gap becomes known. In Book 4 she adds an explicit warning to clinic boats and helps commission safe local pilot procedures; she does not gamble patient transport on a nice story. This changes the practical basis of Book 10's safety standards, while her central arc—from fixed-harbor medicine to accessible mobile care—remains.

**Mira:** The original missing intervals in her ledger finally have consequences. Her insistence on recording them makes the later correction possible, even if she initially trusted Oril's summary. The Book 7 amendable register is consequently more than a sentimental gesture: it generalizes a principle that a trusted record must be correctable by people it affects. This should not turn Mira into an all-knowing judge; her original failing was incomplete representation.

**Senna:** No intervention in her Book 2 ethical act is needed. The vessel stayed; people survived because rescuers chose to help, not because the scene taught a reliable algorithm. In Book 12 she can wait for her sister's independent reply while continuing to provide care; this is a costly choice rather than a clever thematic parallel.

**Tovin and Yael:** Neither becomes implicated in the Book 2 bearing failure. Tovin's responsibility still derives from testimony, stillmoor design and independent measurements. Yael's agency derives from her own lived history and choices, not the technical path used to reach her. Do not recruit either character as a device to “prove” Oril's lost observation.

## 7. Revised twelve-Book working progression

The following deliberately preserves most of Stage 1's local victories and changes only the consequences of the mistaken Book 2 explanation. “Preserved” here means preserved as a **Stage 1 proposal**, not accepted author canon.

### Book 1 — *The Empty Place* [preserved]

Larkhome misses its expected return. Mira must fund a bounded search without cancelling medicine deliveries to present islands, and an ordinary ferry rescue proves that mutual aid can work under uncertain geography. The extra cup remains the symbol of trust in expected return. **No third-lantern event exists yet; no new revision.** The emotional achievement is a modest rescue while the great absence remains unsolved.

### Book 2 — *The Third Lantern* [revised locally and consequentially]

Two islands move and trap an apprentice-and-medicine ferry between disturbed channels. Oril's proposed three-position strategy lacks crucial third-vessel observations during the critical crossing. She and Senna instead help carry out **cautious, shallow, local sounding-led transfer and escort** while the two shore lights remain imperfect indicators of current shore positions and of warnings. The third vessel is present and physically indispensable to taking children aboard; its bearings are not. The apprentices and medicine are saved, the rescue craft is damaged, and the lost market freight is replaced by generous contributors. Senna loses a night of searching for Yael in order to help others.

Isha records a provisional rescue procedure; Oril refuses a proprietary claim on what she thinks may be a useful signaling technique; Mira records interrupted measurements and near-failure. Neither Isha nor Oril has yet received the later proof of missing critical bearings. The Book closes in warranted triumph at the rescue and **unwarranted but narratively productive confidence about its technical explanation**. A battered third lantern remains as an object, but not a certified navigation instrument.

**Book 2's actual new Setup, S2-GAP:** the physical third lantern alongside a contemporaneous interval with **no trustworthy third positional data**. Its near-term payoff is a correction of the account; its distant payoff can be the practice of allowing the far shore to speak without fabricating what the near shore cannot know. This is a new proposed setup/payoff interpretation, not an erasure of the frozen S2-LANTERN text.

### Book 3 — *The Market That Arrived Twice* [preserved]

A relief-guild's prepaid access rule excludes communities unable to guarantee when they will be encountered. Mira and craftspeople build a time-and-skill repair commons; a child's hospital device is repaired by makers from three islands. The story centers on economic membership, not navigation method. Spare craft, food and fuel become available to later pilots, but **this capability already follows from Book 3's own exchange**, not Book 2 geometry. Local satisfaction and delight are retained.

### Book 4 — *The Harbor That Stayed* [core preserved; one bounded record correction]

Isha fights to protect a clinic from an unsafe wholesale demolition of Stillhaven's stillmoor teeth; Tovin faces testimony from small ports and a measurable question about altered local water flow. The independently monitored inlet, medical contingency route and costly partial retrofit still form the Book's principal conflict and local outcome. Mira and Tovin's disagreement remains about Tovin's dismissal of vulnerable shores, not about Oril's lantern.

A **short, separate safety-record inquiry** compares the rescue log's timings against the later witness accounts and discovers that the supposed third independent bearings were unavailable at the decisive moment. No sabotage is inferred. Isha corrects the written procedure before authorizing its use as an approved route; Oril admits the rescue succeeded **despite**, not because of, the unproven geometry. This disclosure costs Oril some reputation and prevents a false protocol from being exported. It is not “evidence” of harbor-sediment causation. The old harbor study and the new procedure correction share a theme—accountability—but retain distinct causal sources.

### Book 5 — *The Sounding Garden* [local passage substantiation, same discovery]

The expedition finds luminous reef gardens and currents extending beyond the recorded basin; it does not find Larkhome. Rather than using a three-position rule, navigators open one **temporary observed passage** using local depth checks, close-ranging escort, recognizable contemporary shore markers and a documented decision to turn back when its next section cannot be examined. Their report states where observation stopped. The successful passage is useful to two isolated inhabited islands under present conditions; no one calls it a chart guaranteed to survive their next migration.

A small, truthful success displaces a seductive rumor of Larkhome. The absence of a grand find remains a legitimate emotional disappointment for Senna. Book 5's method also demonstrates that *particular* routes can be explored without demonstrating a scalable *universal* method.

### Book 6 — *Wells After the Tide* [preserved; logistical propagation only]

A revised harbor barrier and changed water circulation disturb two gardens' freshwater lenses. Farmers, craftspeople and Isha develop portable rain gardens and a shared maintenance service. Neither the environmental problem nor the repair needs Book 2's lantern data. The new water commons can support longer conservative voyages later, but it cannot make an unobserved channel navigable. The Book retains its tactile, intimate victory and changed maintenance obligations.

### Book 7 — *A Festival for the Unreturned* [preserved; provenance resonance]

Senna refuses to represent all the missing islanders; the return festival admits people whose relationship to home no longer fits expected cycles. Mira's register becomes amendable by those it describes. The Book's conflict and generous resolution remain interpersonal and civic. A revised log standard provides a secondary analogy—the right to say what was not witnessed—but the festival is not made to depend on a navigation audit. The place kept at the table means welcome, not enforced return.

### Book 8 — *The Quiet Sounding* [preserved evidence and costly disclosure]

The independent inlet measurements, reef observations and freshwater changes support a contested hypothesis: cumulative stillmoor works may have reduced some inner return paths while a deep-water pulse also mattered. Tovin and Oril publish the evidence with intervals and uncertainty rather than an accusation of deliberate abduction or an infallible chart. Oril has already faced the Book 2 gap and is now personally invested in honest reporting, but **her earlier error is not a measurement of the sea**. The revision makes the public disclosure's ethical weight clearer while preserving what is and is not established about Larkhome.

### Book 9 — *The Ports That Chose* [purpose preserved, station function specified]

Ports decide separately about retrofit, compensation, clinic access and maintenance. A voluntary compact forms; refusal remains legitimate and visible. The first open-relay station is a staffed **local service**: fuel, observation reports with timestamps and known limits, requests for escorts, weather/depth signals, a place to wait, and a route to an actual pilot when one exists. It is **not** an operationally validated triangulation hub. This narrower capability still gives E2 a renewable institutional form and gives E4 material work. The station is worthwhile even if Larkhome is never found.

### Book 10 — *The Unmoored Council* [rights preserved, standards revised]

The council must decide whose voice counts when an island is absent and who is permitted to issue a rescue order using shared resources. Oril's published open standards now distinguish *sighted*, *reported*, *stale*, *not measured*, *danger/stop*, *pilot available*, *request made*, *consent received* and *authorization granted*. These are communicable symbols and recordkeeping rights, not a new worldwide navigation sensor. A report's age, source and uncertainty must be visible to anyone bearing its risk; a recorded signal cannot be taken as permission to coerce an island into return.

Tovin undergoes independent engineering audit for different reasons; Mira and he can collaborate on the charter without forced personal reconciliation. An institution previously threatened by monopoly now learns to share authority **and uncertainty**. The council cannot make the sea predictable by passing a rule.

### Book 11 — *Beyond the Written Sea* [revised operational constraint; discovery still provisional]

The expedition may sail outward **only as far as it can actually justify**: a chain of directly surveyed short legs where practicable, fresh reports from known ships, usable crew/fuel/water reserves, and a viable retreat plan that is recalculated as moving islands alter the map. There will be stretches for which no station has an observation. The group cannot claim those stretches are safe because a diagram connects two dots. Its first attempt may have to turn back, creating a local test of courage defined by refusal to gamble with others' lives rather than by reckless persistence.

On a later bounded attempt, the crew reaches a *currently encountered* outer-gyre ferry community by navigating observed water, not by anticipating where a missing island will be. Blue-dyed, salt-resistant craft cloth and an unfamiliar signaling cadence suggest people connected to Larkhome are alive; they do not independently identify every maker or prove Yael survives. Outer-gyre navigators maintain their own local knowledge and may or may not offer to carry a request for contact farther. The expedition sends a message **with the assistance of a willing physical messenger** rather than transmitting a lantern across an ocean. It ends with a conditional lead and a working return route over actually observed legs, not guaranteed contact.

**Stop condition within the fiction:** if contemporary observations, provisions, cooperative carriers or a credible return corridor fail, they stop or return. The Book 11 outcome is an achieved but bounded contact with an outer community, not full dominion over the unknown.

### Book 12 — *The Shore That Answers* [far-side consent retained; causal payoff replaced]

The outer-gyre contact from Book 11 carries a question to people who may know Larkhome: Is a visit wanted? Can anyone show a *presently safe, bounded* approach? A reply is **not guaranteed**. In the selected working storyline, a willing outer navigator returns by ordinary vessel to the near-side rendezvous, bringing provisional information and a proposal for an escorted exchange. This navigator has local experience, not magical knowledge of all future island movement. The far-side arrangement is negotiated with people who actually live beyond the old charts, including Larkhome inhabitants when contact has been confirmed.

The fleet does not “follow” a distant lamp across unobserved water. It travels in stages through presently checked short routes, with physical pilot handoffs, safe waiting places, water and fuel reserves, and explicit conditions that require a stop. Once the final near side of Larkhome is in practical visual/signaling range, a current far-side watch on the inhabited island reports the *last local approach* and communicates a willingness to receive the visitors. The report **enables** a final leg only together with observation, timing and seamanship; it is not an independent third bearing in a triangle. If the shoal or current is unsafe, the expedition must delay, even after permission is given. There is no promise that the island's next overnight migration is known.

**Payoff P12-ANSWER (replacing P12-LANTERN as the technical dependency):** An early rescue involved a third lantern whose presence mattered but whose missing measurements were falsely credited with certainty. At this final crossing, the near side explicitly admits its missing knowledge; a far-side participant **freely answers**, and both sides verify enough present conditions to allow an actual, limited exchange. The battered Book 2 lantern may stand on the near-side escort vessel, now emitting a simple *we are here / we will wait* sign rather than a claimed positional fix. Its light is part of a conversation whose missing reply cannot be invented. The original technical chain is **retired**, not disguised as a new one.

Larkhome's garden and loom communities have not frozen in the past. Yael has social obligations of her own. Senna's reunion becomes a negotiation among people with different actual homes; the fleet cannot claim a right to move the island or its inhabitants back. The provisional mixed-cause ecological account—rare deep-water pulse and possibly altered inner routes—remains uncertain and does not acquire a single guilty inventor. Mira's accountable records, Oril's corrected signals, Isha's mobile care, Tovin's reviewable engineering, and the voluntary port compact make continued **contact with limits** possible.

**Local satisfaction:** one safe first exchange, not mastery of an entire unknown sea; living persons choose to visit, care and part again. The original warm final image of a table keeping an extra place can survive, because it has changed from certainty that somebody will return to a shared willingness to answer when possible.

**The Book 12 contingency remains real:** This is the favored *planned* outcome of an unaccepted working series. No given author fact guarantees the pilot will appear, Yael will agree, Larkhome will be found, or an observed route will exist at the needed moment. The fiction would have to earn those circumstances in Books 11–12. The storyteller cannot simply announce them as automatic consequences of Book 9's compact.

- **Disposition:** The first is false in the counterfactual; the second cannot follow from it. A future real three-position method is only a separate, conditionally possible innovation, not inherited evidence.

**Dependency types, not an invented graph result:** These lines use the existing Series relationship vocabulary—setup, payoff, depends on, pressures, transforms—and ADR 013's **source affects target** convention as an interpretive aid. A real `SeriesIdentity` and derived dependency graph have **not** been compiled. Story causation is not mechanical reachability: for example, Book 2's gap can *pressure* Book 10's standards without logically necessitating the particular council design.

### Necessary, sufficient, compatible: the controlling distinctions

1. **Book 2 missing reliable bearings → invalid technical proof:** necessary correction given the counterfactual.
2. **Third vessel present → rescue possible:** true as an enabling material contribution in the proposed local explanation, but neither necessary to every conceivable rescue nor sufficient without capable crews and a workable local approach.
3. **Book 5 observed short route → all twelve Books navigable:** invalid. A locally observed passage can close after a migration.
4. **Book 9 staffed observation stations → Book 11 possible:** support is conditional; stations need a route, provisions and honest reports. They cannot create unseen ocean floor or summon an outer pilot.
5. **Book 10 truthful signals → Book 12 feasible:** helpful, often necessary to safe coordination in this *selected plan*, but not sufficient to locate Larkhome.
6. **Larkhome's voluntary reply → moral authorization to contact:** a required and meaningful choice for the proposed climax. It does **not** imply a traversable channel; the local navigational conditions must be met separately.
7. **A final safe passage → proof that all future passages are safe:** false; success is bounded and maintenance remains the ending's enduring work.

## 9. What has and has not been transformed

**Architecture preserved:** same six author constraints; same twelve-Book horizon; same Reciprocal Archipelago's five engines; same loved, visually beautiful world; same disappeared inhabited island as inciting problem; same substantive port-economy, care, environmental, legitimacy and relationship arcs; no introduced omniscient map or controlling antagonist; no automatic author acceptance.

**Architecture materially transformed:** the title *The Third Lantern* becomes a critique of a mistaken explanation rather than a technical discovery; Oril and Isha acquire an early accountability transition; E4 now directly affects which routes other people can responsibly attempt; the Book 2-to-12 chain changes from technical scaling to an evidence-and-consent relationship backed by later independent physical navigation; the Book 11 access constraint is sharpened; Book 12's far-side signal changes from a requisite geometric point to an actual far-shore person's bounded invitation.

**Creative gain:** A generous rescue's success does not confer epistemic innocence. The archipelago can learn to care *better* because it can admit an incomplete explanation. This is stronger than merely increasing the stakes of the search: it creates a repeated conflict between socially gratifying certainty and the burden of making a claim that other people can safely act upon.

**Creative loss:** The elegant early invention / distant technical payoff is replaced by a more distributed and less instantly legible mechanism. The dramatic design must make the eventual handoff chain visible, not drown it in paperwork. A reader may prefer the cleaner technological arc of Alternative A. That preference remains legitimate, and Stage 2 does not need to “win” an imaginary contest between tastes.

**Explicit conditional weak point, not concealed:** The selected finale requires enough *presently discoverable* ocean passages and one willing chain of outer navigators. No document reviewed proves those conditions must occur in this fictional world. They are prospective Story Structure hypotheses to be earned, and Book 11/12 must show the actual crossings, return alternatives and choices rather than declare the network universally capable. This is **not** a newly invented physical law; it is an unverified story circumstance. If it cannot be justified in Realization, this proposed Book 12 outcome must change. Recognizing that limit is part of Stage 2's local consistency check, not a new reflective Stage 3 exercise.

## 11. Unresolved decisions intentionally left provisional

1. Is Book 4 the best place for the record correction, or should it come during Book 5's expedition? The correction must occur *after* the original rescue and before the purported method is used as certified infrastructure.
2. What made the third vessel's observations unusable: broken time stamps, unobserved motion, visibility, incomplete communication, or conflicting memories? The counterfactual **fixes the absence of reliable data**, not its exact explanation. Do not select sabotage merely for drama.
3. How long is the physical sounding-led transfer in Book 2, how damaged is the rescue craft, and which cargo is sacrificed? The story must keep the immediate physical conditions plausible without claiming a universal method.
4. What precisely counts as enough fresh route information for a pilot to say “go”? The current sketch requires local observation and a stop rule, not a detailed universal maritime safety algorithm.
5. Is the eventual outer-gyre partner a moving island, a ferry community, or several cooperating crews? None grants free prediction. The trade-off is between intimate human faces and the complexity of the contact network.
6. How can the Series make the tedious cost of safe waiting dramatically felt—someone relieved by a delayed voyage, someone harmed by a delay, and a community willing to carry that cost?
7. Does Yael respond to Senna's request, to the broader care network, or to a practical need that arises on Larkhome? Her response should belong to her own dramatic situation rather than satisfying the protagonist on cue.
8. What kind of ending would honor the author promise if Larkhome cannot safely be approached by Book 12? The selected narrative has a conditional contact ending, but another good structure might make a truthful uncompleted search an earned hopeful outcome instead.
9. Should a later legitimate three-position method ever emerge as a *minor* tool? It may, but it requires separate evidence and must not retroactively vindicate Book 2.
10. How much continuing uncertainty about human harbor works' causal contribution is artistically desired? The lantern correction should not be used to replace ecological investigation with generalized distrust of all expertise.

## 12. Final Stage 2 working disposition

The counterfactual **does not destroy** the twelve-Book Reciprocal Archipelago, and it **does** invalidate a consequential cross-horizon dependency. The Book 2 rescue remains generous and dangerous, while the rule supposedly demonstrated by its lantern is no longer established. The preferred transformation replaces the defective **S2-LANTERN → P12-LANTERN** technical proof with an honest-gap chain: **S2-GAP → P4-RETRACTION → B5 locally observed passage → B9 staffed access → B10 uncertainty-aware communication → B11 bounded outer contact → B12 invited, piloted, actually observed crossing**.

The far-side answer can remain as a major emotional climax only because *later independent choices and capabilities* support it. It is neither a license to claim omniscient navigation nor a guarantee of reunion. The cost of revising this single provisional premise is real, concentrated, and creative: the series becomes less about mastering a miraculous lamp and more about what people owe one another when an illuminated shore still cannot tell them what lies beyond it.

**Stop at Stage 2.** This is one unreviewed initial counterfactual construction, not Stage 3 reflective critique, a Book 5 renewal experiment, an independent literary assessment, a fresh-context recovery, a schema qualification or author acceptance.

### Governing principles

1. It is not saving the Book 2 formula; it is actually saving the story's causes and consequences when the formula fails.
2. It is not erasing the twelve Books and is not pretending the other eleven were unchanged; it is actually tracing the effects that matter while respecting the ones that do not.
3. A far shore's answer is actually a choice that must meet the material conditions for being heard.
