## Most serious findings

1. **p1.effect: overreaching.** The answer says: "Inferred from E1: early disclosure leaves fewer partial answers to reopen assumptions later." E1 says: "Renewable because each partial answer reopens what else communities assumed was stable." This brings back the confusion from c1(a). It treats the author's disclosure to the reader (d3) as if it consumed the characters' in-world partial answers. It also partly restores the constraint that r2 now says no item records ("No item records a constraint").

2. **p1.ordering: overreaching.** The answer says: "an early lean toward E3's fully explicable or partially understood answers". E3 says: "How far should the migrations be explained by the end". E3's three answers are end states. Mapping early timing onto them goes against the answer's own acceptance of c3: "E3 concerns the end state".

3. **r7: overreaching.** The answer gives this relation as kind "prerequisite" with dependence "causal", while its own reason says: "This is a craft condition of the proposal, not a recorded dependence ... otherwise it will feel unearned." The quoted E4 words, "generations of communities have learned to live with them", carry no planting obligation. The answer labels a craft preference as a causal necessity, which is the same kind of point c1 raised about the earlier weakening of the explicability-first order.

4. **o2: overreaching.** The answer says: "communities differ in whether they keep faith, reach out or repair, and the bonds become the field where rival readings of the absence meet." E2 says: "producing repeated action families of reaching out, keeping faith and repairing". E2 presents these as shared repeated actions, not as rival stances. The phrase "the field where rival readings ... meet" is a milder version of the unsupported thesis c1 named ("the bonds become 'the answer'"), and the c1 assessment does not mention that point.

5. **s6 / o1 / c2 assessment: grounding weak.** The proposal is correctly labelled ("The record does not state that the practices carry such clues; this assumes it"). Its cited premise is E4: "Their migrations are unpredictable, but generations of communities have learned to live with them." The word "unpredictable" weighs against practices encoding the migrations' order. E4's "expected migration cycle" partly offsets it. The answer weighs neither phrase. The c2 reason ("E4's generations who learned to live with the migrations make it a fair proposal") treats living with the migrations as if it were knowing their order.

6. **r2: defensible, but the kind is mislabelled.** The answer uses "complicates" while its reason says "No item records a constraint" and "as a tendency". The system prompt reserves constraint-type relations for "where the record shows one development constraining another". Either the kind or the reason should change.

7. **r3 against reader_experience: residual tension.** r3 says: "independent ... dependence: none ... the bonds can be tested under any of E3's three answers." reader_experience says: "d3 decides what kind of world the wonder belongs to ... where understanding honours the practices, or ... where the practices are how people live well". In the reader experience, d3 changes what d2 means. That is not a contradiction about whether the bonds are tested, but it gives the author a thematic coupling the relation denies.

8. **r6: defensible, but "prerequisite" is strong.** The answer says: "partial answers arise from the attempts to explain the failure that E1 names". E1 says: "every attempt to explain, exploit, mourn or reverse that failure generates pressure, action and revelation". E1 does not say that partial answers can arise only from attempts to explain.

## Did the reflection repair the earlier misreadings?

Mostly yes. s4 states plainly that d3 is the author's disclosure question, not an in-world drive. The constraint in r2 is withdrawn into a bounded tendency that names its own lack of a record. The d2/d3 contradiction is resolved by making the coupling depend on whether the author adopts the clue proposal. E4's authorship is attributed, and the unreachability assumption is removed. The c2 clue-bearing reading enters properly labelled as proposed. Three new or residual weaknesses remain:

- The in-world/d3 confusion survives in the explicability-first path. Its effect treats early authorial disclosure as using up in-world partial answers, and its ordering maps timing onto E3's end-state answers.
- The new proposal brings a craft obligation labelled as a causal prerequisite, which repeats the c1 pattern of presenting craft preference as dependence.
- The clue proposal's E4 grounding leaves out the word "unpredictable".

None of these is a misread of recorded text. They are overreaches in labelling and inference.
