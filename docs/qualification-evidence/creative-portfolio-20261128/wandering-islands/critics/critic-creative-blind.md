# Creative-substance critique: W1 to W4

## Summary

All four works agree on the basics. The broken pattern (d1) drives the series, the bond practices (d2) carry its warmth, and bonds-first is the better sequence. They differ in how much each one imagines past that agreement. W4 has the most generative idea. W2 has the most fully caused arc and the bravest culmination. W1 is competent, but its culmination is creatively hollow. W3 is careful and occasionally sharp, but it avoids the culmination question entirely.

## 1. Compelling or only functional?

- **d2 (bonds)** is compelling in every work, because the Story Vision gives it concrete texture: markets, festivals, craft, generosity.
- **d1 (the unreturned island)** is mostly treated as an engine. Only W2 and W3 give it emotional form of its own. W2 shows the practices as "a bet that the map would come back". W3 asks what it means to keep faith with a place that cannot answer.
- **d3 (explicability)** is where the works separate:
  - In W1, W2 and W3, d3 is mainly functional. It is a valve that keeps d1 renewable: partial answers good, full answers bad. That is plot mechanics, not creative substance.
  - Only W4 ties d3 to the reader experience the Vision promises. It names the risk that explanation disenchants wonder, and it treats d3 as a dial on what kind of world this is. That makes d3 a matter of meaning rather than throughput.

## 2. Creative or descriptive interactions

**Genuinely creative:**

- **W4: "customs as encoded knowledge."** The migration-era practices may preserve fragments of the world's order. This fuses d2 and d3 without making either depend on the other succeeding.
- **W4: "faithfulness without explanation."** This is the deliberate counter-hypothesis to the one above.
- **W2: different responses divide communities.** Communities choosing different responses (explain, exploit, mourn, reverse) strain the bonds through division, not just distance. This uses the Vision's "competing interpretations" well.
- **W3: rival explanations travel the bond networks.** The same markets and celebrations that hold people together also spread competing accounts, so keeping faith may mean keeping it across disagreement.
- **W1: care and inquiry as one action.** Reaching out to the missing island might itself produce revelation. This is a lighter version of W4's idea.

**Merely descriptive:**

- Every work's "d1 tests d2" relation.
- The repeated "d3 complicates d1 via renewability" relation, which appears almost word for word in W1, W2 and W3.
- The d2/d3 independence statements, which are correct but inert.

## 3. Causation or ordering

- **W2** explains causation best. Each stage changes how earlier stages read: practices become conditional bets, reversal attempts become mourning in retrospect, and repairs stop being stopgaps and become the new form of the custom. That is development, not sequence.
- **W1** is causal in its first three stages, then turns into announcement at the return.
- **W3** is causal through the middle but ends on a stage that only says the d3 question gets settled late.
- **W4** has no trajectory. Its causal thinking is real but sits in its hypotheses ("a partial explanation can change which practices are needed") rather than in a staged arc.

## 4. Reader experience, and how different the sequences really are

All four describe the same contrast:

- **Bonds-first:** attachment, then grief, then curiosity born of care.
- **Explicability-first:** a puzzle first, with relationships as its cost.

The sequences are less different than the works claim. W1 to W3 dismiss explicability-first as "weakened" with near-identical reasoning, and their interleaved third options are interchangeable.

Only W4 gives explicability-first its real advantages: fair-play clue planting, early outward voyages, and giving rival interpretations material sooner. Only W4 names what bonds-first actually costs the author: clues must be hidden in domestic texture from Book 1, or a late explanation will feel unearned. That single observation turns the sequence choice from a preference into a craft obligation. It is the most useful sequence insight in the bundle.

## 5. Earned, predetermined or announced culminations

**W1: the island returns, altered, and the communities "decide how to share a map they had believed fixed."**

- This is announced. Nothing in the trajectory says what the islanders learned or why the island returns, so the return is something that happens to the story, not something the story earns.
- "A map they had believed fixed" also sits badly with the Vision. These communities have always lived with unpredictable migrations, so they never believed the map was fixed. W1 notices the tension in its uncertainty list but does not resolve it.

**W2: no restoration, bonds re-formed across a changed map, explanation left partial.**

- This is the most earned ending. Every necessary condition is grown by earlier stages.
- It risks drifting toward elegy, with less wonder and less heroism than the Vision asks for. Still, its hope comes from adaptation, which fits "earned rather than predetermined."

**W3** gives no culmination. That is cautious, not wise.

**W4** properly keeps the island's fate separate from the question. It offers two thematic endpoints rather than an event, which helps the author but is not a culmination.

**Standalone value:**

- W2 handles it best and most concretely: each Book resolves a particular bond across a particular distance through a particular act of repair, plus one partial answer.
- W1's standalone note is sound but generic.
- W3 touches it in one sentence.
- W4 notes that bonds-first depends on Book-level questions to carry early momentum, but does not spell out what each Book keeps if the later Books never arrive.

## 6. Most useful for the author's next decision

**W4.** It shows that the d3 question cannot wait until late Books if the author wants a payoff that reveals the customs' hidden meaning. Whether the customs secretly encode the migrations' order has to be decided before Book 1 is written, because the clues must be planted in its markets and festivals. W4 frames the decision as a real fork: encoded knowledge, or faithfulness without understanding. It also shows what each branch requires.

**W2** is the runner-up. It helps the author decide whether the series' hope rests on recovery or on adaptation, and shows what the adaptation version demands.

## 7. Most creatively valuable possibility

**The customs themselves carry knowledge of the migrations.** On a second reading, the reader would see that the warm everyday practices of the early Books were holding clues all along. A late reframing would then deepen the earlier warmth instead of replacing it.

- This joins wonder, belonging, mystery and changing understanding in one move.
- It is compatible with the Vision's "not *currently* predictable".
- It is fully developed in **W4**, with the hypothesis plus the clue-planting obligation.
- It appears in embryo in **W1**: bond practices as an instrument of discovery.
- **W3** gestures nearby, with networks that carry explanations, but does not reach it.

## 8. Weakest element

**W1's culmination.** The altered island's return has no stated cause and no stated content: what was learned, by whom, and at what cost. It rests on a "fixed map" premise the Vision contradicts. It also undercuts the "earned hope rather than predetermined victory" goal: a lost island coming back at the series' close is the most predictable victory available, and W1 supplies nothing that earns it.

W3's null culmination is a close second. It is careful, but it leaves the author with less than they started with.
