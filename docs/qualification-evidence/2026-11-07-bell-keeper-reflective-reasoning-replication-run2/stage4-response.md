## Proposed mechanism: The Virtue of Silence

This is a revision of the first proposal, the sealed ledger. The keeper finds that the town reads the bells' silence for the powerful as moral virtue, and that her own routine maintenance has been the instrument of that silence. Tags mark what comes from the author's Vision and what is my proposal.

### 1. Operating rules

- **R1. Trigger [Vision, C1].** A citizen who forgets an important promise has their household's tone struck. The Vision does not say how forgetting is detected, and this proposal leaves that unspecified.
- **R2. Household tones [Proposal].** Each household has its own tone, which the town knows by ear.
- **R3. Pinned cylinder [Proposal].** Strikes are produced by a pinned cylinder in the tower. A tone whose pin is lifted cannot strike. The keeper maintains the cylinder.
- **R4. Exemption [Vision, C3, via R3].** The powerful households' pins have been lifted for generations. Their promises are never struck, so they are exempt from judgment.
- **R5. Judgment and consequence [adopted reading of C3; consequences are a proposal].** A struck tone is the system's only judgment. Its consequence is social: the household is named in the morning rite and the town responds.
- **R6. The cover [Proposal].** Each morning, the town's rite names the "still houses" and praises them as those whose bells do not fail. The praise is sincere, and it depends on the silence.
- **R7. Hand strike [Proposal].** A keeper can strike a tone by hand. The sound is audible, but it is not judgment unless the town accepts it as judgment.

### 2. Opposing pressures and stakes

- **Belonging vs. examination.** The morning praise is one of the town's warmest rituals. Questioning it means examining the town's own pride. *Stake:* the town's self-image as a place where promises are kept.
- **Stability vs. justice.** The predecessor and the council argue that the still houses hold up the city's bridges, granaries, and peace. *Stake:* material security against equal judgment.
- **Authority vs. truth.** The bells are the town's only shared judge. Exposing their corruption threatens the trust that C2 depends on. Repair requires the town to trust a hand strike. *Stake:* whether the bells remain the town's judgment at all.
- **Access vs. accusation.** The keeper handles the cylinder, so she is the obvious suspect. *Stake:* her name, her role, and her freedom.
- **Individual act vs. collective answer.** A hand strike is only a claim until the town answers it. *Stake:* whether anyone will act with her.

### 3. Repeated actions and decisions

- **Maintenance sessions.** Each time she lifts, restores, or leaves a pin, she chooses. The council's orders reach the cylinder through her hands.
- **Morning rite.** Each morning, citizens choose whether to repeat the praise of the still houses or to fall silent. Each repetition sustains the cover.
- **Testimony.** Townspeople choose whether to say aloud the promises they have seen the powerful make.
- **Council response.** Accusation, reward, and reassignment are decisions that can be revised.
- **The town's answer.** After a hand strike, each morning the town decides whether to treat it as judgment. The answer is repeated until it holds or breaks.

### 4. How it preserves the Vision's premises

- **C1.** The trigger is unchanged. Ordinary households still ring when they forget.
- **C2.** The bells remain the town's shared identity. The rites are both the site of the cover and the site of the answer. Belonging is examined, not destroyed.
- **C3.** The system is altered in its tower, not in its concept. The still houses' promises are never struck, which is exemption from judgment under the adopted reading. The alteration is secret: the town believes the silence is virtue.
- **C4.** Warmth lives in the rites and the town's pride. Unease lives in the silences and the discovery. Hope depends on the town answering the strike, so the keeper's act alone changes nothing.
- **Working premises.** The keeper stays young and central, and the bells remain the judging instrument. No premise is changed.

### 5. Warmth, unease, and earned collective hope

- **Warmth.** The opening material is the keeper's craft: tuning tones, knowing households by sound, and the morning rite. It is beautiful because the town believes it is good.
- **Unease.** The keeper witnesses a councillor break a publicly made promise, and she expects a tone that never sounds. Her first unease is professional: something is wrong with a tone. The morning praise begins to sound wrong. Dramatic irony is available if the reader notices lifted pins in the maintenance scenes before she does.
- **Reversal.** The town's belief that "they never forget" becomes "they are never struck." This reinterprets the whole rite. It is the kind of revelation the ontology describes: one that changes how the narrative is understood.
- **Accusation.** The council points at the keeper. She must decide whether to obey, bargain, or act.
- **Earned hope.** She hand-strikes a still tone at the morning rite. The sound is clear and unauthorized. The town's silence in response is the story's crisis. Hope comes only if the town answers, and the answer must be repeated over several mornings until it is firm. The ending depends on the town, not on her.
- **Ending hypothesis.** When the cylinder is publicly reset, the first struck tone is a still house. The next is the keeper's own, because she has also forgotten a promise. The bell rings for her too. That is equality, paid for, not triumph.

### 6. Creative hypotheses vs. author-approved facts

**Author-approved constraints (treat as fixed):** C1 through C4; a young bell keeper; the system secretly altered so powerful citizens' promises are exempt from judgment; the bells as shared identity; hope earned collectively.

**Creative hypotheses (not approved):**
- Household tones and the identification of who rang (R2).
- The pinned cylinder as the mechanism (R3).
- The morning praise of the still houses (R6).
- The hand strike as non-judgment until accepted (R7).
- The social consequences of a ring (R5).
- The council's stability rationale.
- The predecessor as the one who lifted the pins under orders.
- The keeper's own forgotten promise and the ending image.
- Promises made publicly enough for the town to witness their breach. The reversal needs this.
- Left open: how forgetting is detected, and whether some powerful people know of the alteration.

### 7. Improvements over the first proposal (sealed ledger)

1. **It fixes a premise conflict.** The first version rang for exempt citizens, which conflicts with C3. In this version the still houses are never struck.
2. **One evidence object instead of two.** The sealed ledger was a second record. The cylinder is itself the evidence, which is simpler.
3. **Secrecy and complicity share one mechanism.** The first version's secrecy depended on a hidden book. Here the town's belief that silence is virtue keeps the secret, and the same morning rite carries both C2 and C4.
4. **A stronger revelation.** The first version's discovery was a found document. Here it is a reversal of the town's praise, which changes how the whole story reads.
5. **Access creates suspicion.** The keeper is both investigator and suspect. The first version lacked this pressure.
6. **Recurring decisions.** The first version offered one discovery. This version repeats choices in maintenance, rites, testimony, and the town's answer.
7. **Collective hope is structural.** A hand strike becomes judgment only through the town's answer. C4 is built into the mechanism rather than appended as an ending.
8. **A moral antagonist with a rationale.** The predecessor defends exemption as stability and carries his own complicity, which gives the keeper a personal stake.

**What this does not claim.** It is not more sophisticated for its own sake. It adds a physical cylinder and a praise rite, and both serve the mechanism directly. If the author wants less, the morning praise alone can carry the cover, and the cylinder can be a simple pinned list.
