- **C2:** bells are part of shared identity, tied to rituals, responsibility, trust, and belonging.
- **C3:** the system "has been secretly altered so that powerful citizens' promises are exempt from judgment."
- **C4:** reader experience of warmth, growing unease, and hope "earned through collective action rather than an effortless individual victory."

**1. Bells that still ring for exempt citizens (earlier A and B): incompatible under the closest reading of C3.**
- *Source:* C1 and C3. The ring is the only judging act the Vision describes, and the alteration is to the bell system.
- *Establishes:* if "judgment" is the ring, a ring for an exempt promise contradicts C3.
- *Uncertain:* the Vision never defines "judgment." If judgment means social consequence, the earlier versions would be permitted.

## Revised creative proposals

Each revision keeps C1 through C4. All remain noncanonical.

**Mechanism A (revised): The Silent Tuning.** Faithful to C3.
- *Mechanism:* the exemption is a tuning record in the tower's maintenance book. Exempt households' bells are tuned to stay silent. The keeper maintains the bells and has been carrying out the tuning without knowing what it does.
- *Evidence is absence:* a promise the town witnessed was never rung. This needs the assumption that promises are publicly witnessed.
- *Engine:* one silence leads to the tuning book, then to exempt names, including the council.
- *Pressure:* only she reads the tuning book. If she is seen as a forgetter, the bell rings for her. C1 stays intact because she is not exempt.
- *Hope:* exposure fails alone; the council calls the silence a fault in the bronze. Hope comes when residents count the silences themselves, which gives the ending to the collective as C4 requires.

**Mechanism B' (new, faithful): The Morning Count.** Communal ritual and collective knowledge.
- *Mechanism:* the bells stay silent for exempt names. A silence makes no sound, so the keeper cannot detect it alone. The town's rituals include speaking promises aloud in the square (C2), which creates a public record. The gap appears only when spoken promises are compared with rings.
- *Pressure:* the powerful argue that the gap is a lapse in speaking, not in the bells. The Morning Count is the town's shared comfort, so questioning it threatens belonging.
- *Engine:* unease grows through ordinary speaking. The reader sees the ritual's honesty erode without anyone announcing it.
- *Hope:* the town chooses to speak every promise, including the powerful's. Collective action is structurally necessary because only the collective holds the full comparison.
- *Relation to A:* A keeps the evidence in a hidden record. B' keeps it in public ritual.
- *Needs:* public speaking of promises, which is an assumption to be confirmed.

**Premise-changing alternative (labelled, not faithful to C3): The Public Naming.**
- *Premise changed:* the bells ring for everyone. The exemption lies in the public naming rite, which officials withhold for the powerful. The alteration moves out of the bell system, which is why it changes C3.
- *What it keeps:* the bells stay public, so the irony of hearing judgment and seeing no consequence is strong.
- *Requirement:* the author must accept this as an explicit premise change and state that "judgment" means social naming.

**Variation (revised).** This is the author's own hypothetical, so exploring it is permitted.
- *Ungated base (entailed):* the exemption moves to where registration begins: the first register, the registrar, or the act of registering.
- *Withdrawal (conditional):* if withdrawal is allowed, powerful citizens can register and withdraw, reopening the exemption under a legal appearance.
- *Public versus filed register (compatible possibility):* if the register is spoken aloud, the Morning Count survives. If it is filed quietly, the town loses its common comparison.
- *Retained as creative options (compatible possibilities):* ritual becoming filing, unease about a readable register, access costs, and apparent fairness as a false resolution (an analogy to the contract's definition, lines 100-101).
- *Collective act:* registering together or refusing together fits C4.
