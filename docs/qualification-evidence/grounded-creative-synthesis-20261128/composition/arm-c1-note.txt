Working note: the Special Draw Right, its training condition, and the finale mechanism

Scope. This note reads the two open developments against the author's vision (E3), the coverage record (E4) and the accepted reconsideration (E5). E5 is a reconsidered judgement with current standing. It is advisory, not established record. Books 24 and 25 are the only existing Books read. Anything later is proposed.

What the record carries. E1 asks what training condition the Right carries. The question presupposes one but does not state it. E2 names options A, B, C or a third, but the items read do not describe them. E4 counts no citing element for either development among the 182 elements read in Books 24 and 25. Whether earlier Books carry the Right in other wording is not recorded. E5 is the only item that links the two developments. It reports that the training condition may be the hinge, that under alternative A "the training condition must be dropped or recast as counted care" (interpretive, per E5), and that whether the change reaches later development through Book 24's review is unresolved.

Possibility 1: a revocable Right with training as a withdrawable condition. The Right is granted through training and can be taken back. The reader feels it first as a gift and then as a leash. The finale then counts whoever training has bound, and withdrawal makes counting an act of power. This fits E3's concern with institutional transformation. It depends on revocability being the author's intent, which E1 leaves open.

Possibility 2: a permanent Right with training as inherited obligation. The Right persists and the training condition becomes a standing duty handed down. Belonging is earned through training, which fits E3's "everyday belonging." The risk is that the finale becomes a confirmation of something already settled rather than a choice.

Possibility 3: finale first, with training recast as counted care. If alternative A is taken, the condition is recast, and "who gives return labour" (E2) and "who carries the Turn" (E1) may be answered by one decision. The reader then discovers that labour read as extraction was counted all along. This is the most discovery-rich option, and it serves E3's line "Major victories change the world rather than restore the initial situation." It rests on the interpretive reading that E5 flags and the record does not confirm.

Possibility 4: deferral. The Right's status stays open into later Books and the finale carries the hinge. This keeps the question open, but E3 asks for an "ultimately hopeful possibility of flourishing," so deferral needs a resolution the record does not yet supply.

Reinforcement and competition. Revocability reinforces the finale, because who is counted matters most when the count can change. Permanence competes with the finale's choice, since a settled Right may already fix who is counted. The two developments may also collapse into one. If the training condition is recast, deciding one settles the other, and the Books should not treat them as separate threads. The finale can undercut a permanent Right if counting makes the training redundant. That is an inference, not a recorded consequence.

Premises and gaps. Every possibility depends on whether training is causally, not merely thematically, tied to the finale. E5 leaves this open. E5 also says that the counts it relies on list no element identifiers, so it is not confirmed that Book 24's review carries the condition. E5 reports that the outside-apprentice reading rests on a qualifier absent from the cited item. Treat that reading as unestablished. E3 is an excerpt, so anything after its cut-off is unknown here.

Most worth the author's consideration: Possibility 3, but only after the author decides whether training is a condition the Right carries or a count the finale produces. That decision would show whether E1 and E2 are really two questions, and it tests E5's reach claim directly. It also leaves Books 24 and 25 meaningful in their own right, because no read element yet depends on the Right.
