Agent interpretation — noncanonical

My reading: the question of what the tally is (the hours column itself, or a separate count that feeds it) can stay in Book 24. The Series does not need the tally to be either one. Book 24's need is also weaker than both readings. It only requires that a hand missing from the tally has no hours in the column, a one-way rule that both readings satisfy. I agree with you on that. One thing is not local, and it is a check, not a change. The Architecture records the tally as a record of care that 'does not hold the water' (The counted Turn), and from Book 15 it records care, not debt. Separately, the revised Ledger engine now records each hand's hours beside the mark, 'so a count of the draw can be read straight from it', and from Book 20 draw is partly set by hands owed. Put these together with Book 24's rule: if the hours column has any weight in setting draw, then leaving a hand out of the care tally costs that household water. The care record would then hold water after all. That contradicts the counted Turn as recorded, and it would quietly settle open question 4 (does the shared accounting of care bind?) and lean the finale away from Alternative A, where care is counted and nothing is owed. If the hours column is descriptive only, a record that can be read but sets no draw, Book 24's rule costs the hand standing and belonging, not water, and nothing at the Series level changes. So the decision that belongs to you, at Architecture level, is narrow: does the Ledger's hours column feed draw, or only describe it? The Book 24 question needs no Architecture change either way. What it does is expose this one ambiguity in the Ledger revision. The alternatives below set out three ways to settle it. They are interpretations, not graph proof. Creative alternatives (noncanonical)
h1 · Descriptive column: the tally stays Book 24's matter (varies structure): What changes: the hours column is fixed as descriptive. It records hours beside the mark, so the draw can be checked against them, but it does not set draw. Hands owed under the Book 20 conversion are reckoned elsewhere. How it operates: Book 24 is free to have the tally be the column or feed it. A hand left out of the tally has no hours in the column, and that costs them recognition and standing in the Turn's record of care, not water. No Architecture change is needed beyond, at most, clarifying the ledger revision. Decisive choice: the omitted hand and their household face being unseen in a shared record of care. The cost is belonging and dignity, not draw. The reader weighs whether being counted as having cared matters when nothing is owed. This keeps Alternative A and open question 4 untouched for the finale.Use h1 as revision basis
h2 · Identity: the tally is the hours column, and care becomes reckonable (varies causal engine): What changes: the Council's stewardship tally is the ledger's hours column, and that column carries weight in draw after Book 20. How it operates: a hand the Council leaves out of the tally loses hours, and because hours partly set draw, their household's water is cut. The care record becomes a debt record by another route. Making this the Series' reading would need an Architecture change: The counted Turn's 'does not hold the water' would have to be revised, the Ledger engine would gain an incoming link from the Council's tally, and open question 4 would be answered 'yes, it binds', leaning the finale toward Alternative B. Decisive choice: the Council faces it. Keeping the tally is now gatekeeping water. The cost is the Council's claim to keep care rather than debt, and the omitted household's draw. The reader weighs whether the care of Book 15 was always going to be turned back into reckoning. This is a strong engine, but it decides things you have left open.Use h2 as revision basis
h3 · Feed with a hand at the gap: the transfer is someone's discretion (varies character agency): What changes: the tally is a separate count, and a named person or office copies it into the ledger's hours column, which bears on draw. How it operates: Book 24's rule holds, since nothing missing from the tally reaches the column. But each omission is now an act by someone with discretion over what is carried across, echoing the Stone Hall's favoured credit of Book 1 at a smaller scale. At Series level this would add a tally-to-ledger link and raise a custody question: who holds the feed, and does that body come to hold part of the record? This touches the Book 30 promise that no single body holds both the record and the Gate schedule. Book 24 alone could keep it as one local event. Decisive choice: the keeper of the feed faces whether to carry a disputed hand across, and the omitted household faces whether to contest a gap. The cost falls on trust in the record rather than on the record's design. The reader weighs negligence against favour. This is the only reading that gives Book 24 an agent to blame, and also the one most likely to grow into a Series-level custody thread.Use h3 as revision basis
Uncertainties
The Ledger revision's phrase 'so a count of the draw can be read straight from it' could mean the hours column sets draw or only lets draw be checked. My central conditional rests on that reading, and it is the author's to settle.
The Architecture does not say whether 'hands owed' under the Book 20 conversion is measured in the hours column, in the rota, or in some other count.
The Book 24 element is agent-proposed and its commitment authority is undecided. I have treated its stated need as Book 24's working requirement, not as the author's commitment.
I have not read Book 24's other elements (assume-hours-column, eng-old-hand-ledger), which the element cites. They may already settle whether the column bears on draw in that Book.
Whether the 'ambiguous tally' carried into the next generation is meant to be resolved, or left deliberately ambiguous across Books 23-30, is not stated.

Developing none of these alternatives leaves the Architecture as it is; the Book keeps its own record either way.
