# Adaptive Problem Discovery and Responsibility Selection — Divergent Exploration

**Date:** 2026-11-10  
**Repository and inspected baseline:** `ThorStarlord/auteur`, current `main@20d19cd01d54c855e884fc4c6d510bd520a4a36c` (PR #543 merge).  
**Status:** **DIVERGENT RESEARCH / CANDIDATES ONLY**. Source-grounded investigation, not an approved ontology, architecture, behavioral qualification, implementation roadmap, or authorization to change either repository.  
**Question:** How might an autonomous agent notice the consequential problem, generate real alternatives, bring relevant knowledge into judgment, compare outcomes and opportunity cost, choose the right bounded responsibility, and reconsider that choice as evidence changes?  
**Scope of change:** This research document only. No changes to narrative canon, Layer 0, Reasoning code, PR #543, Studio, Series, tests or the draft Wandering Islands PR stack. No behavioral experiment performed.

## 1. Problem and governing authority: two different things to optimize

The immediate *Auteur* owner-endorsed product priority is generative, ontology-grounded **long-form Narrative Architecture**—distinctive renewable engines, coherent Book functions and cross-Book transformations, reflective revision and durable authoring—not merely fluent prose. See the [long-form strategy](2026-11-07-generative-long-form-narrative-architecture-strategy.md) and [coverage assessment](2026-11-07-generative-long-form-narrative-architecture-coverage-assessment.md). Realization and Expression can probe Architecture but must not silently substitute for the original capability claim. The [canonical semantic layers](../narrative-architecture.md) still separate narrative concepts, authorial Identity, Structure, Realization and Expression. The [Architecture Constitution](../architecture-constitution.md) keeps recommendations derived and acceptance under the owning artifact's authority.

The broader *research question* is different: **what reasoning capacity lets an agent discover and select the right problem or responsibility?** This can be relevant to an Auteur agent developing a twelve-Book architecture and to a general-purpose coding agent choosing between a local patch, strategic reorientation, a probe, an owner decision, or stopping. Its generality does not by itself confer ownership on Auteur or Sensemaking Skills. Nor does a local-next-action bias automatically mean poor judgment: in many states a small reversible build is the highest-value way to advance a global objective.

For this exploration distinguish five targets, none reducible to another: (1) **problem identification**—which uncertainty/need matters; (2) **alternative discovery**—which materially different actions exist; (3) **decision warrant**—what the available source/consequences justify; (4) **authority and execution**—who may turn a recommendation into work; (5) **learning and reentry**—whether results should change the problem or strategy. “It was documented” is not equivalent to “it was available to the agent,” “it was available” is not “it was used,” and “it was used” is not “the choice was good.”

### 2.2 Who decided what? Do not collapse four decision surfaces

1. **Original owner/product direction**: whole-Series generative Architecture and author authority remained the higher-level capability target. It was source-available. Its exact use by the Stage 4G executor is unknown.
2. **Stage 4G task author/coordinator**: chose a legitimate **bounded** contact-window stress question and offered A/B/C/D. The alternative set itself omitted an explicit mission-level Architecture reentry; the design of the menu could constrain what is easiest to compare.
3. **Stage 4G executor**: within this frame, selected A and gave a stated value argument comparing prose with more local checking and closing. This is visible decision reasoning, not mere silence; it need not have been the wrong decision.
4. **Stage 4H author/coordinator**: separately authorized execution of the proposed local research responsibility. Recommendation-to-task promotion was not an automatically executed agent action.

The [retrospective's decision audit](2026-11-10-wandering-islands-creative-scope-drift-reconciliation.md#focused-stage-4g--4h-decision-frame-audit) supports **incomplete visible mission-level comparative warrant at promotion**, not failure to know the definitions, missing source text, irrational choice, or a proven defect in an autonomous coding agent. Stage 4H's scene outcome could indeed have provided high information value. Only an appropriately matched comparison could test whether returning earlier to Series Architecture would have been better.

## 3. Divergent causal hypothesis landscape: different failure owners

The hypotheses below deliberately mix competing, combinable, and **non-failure** explanations. “Support” concerns *what the traces allow us to suspect*, not a causal attribution. Each has a discriminating observation rather than a proposed permanent repair.

| Candidate causal hypothesis and mechanism | Supporting / limiting case evidence | Rival, uncertainty, or discriminating observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 — Task-menu narrowing (coordination).** An agent optimizes sensibly among options its task author supplies, but important mission alternatives never become candidates. | Stage 4G menu A/B/C/D omitted explicit Series-Architecture reentry; A was compared to local options. | Can a separate executor with identical sources and a broader open option-generation request spontaneously name reentry? If yes, framing was important. If not, another capability may be weak. |
| **H2 — Objective not reinstated at decision time (context transport).** Long-form strategy exists but is not placed among decision inputs at the mission handoff. | Strategy existed; task did not require it, and recorded source reads did not show retrieval. | Give exact strategy to otherwise matched agents, keeping option-generation and choice instructions unchanged; inspect *actual citations and option changes*, not declarations of having read it. |
| **H3 — Objective understood but locally overridden (objective substitution).** The immediate goal of credible contact takes precedence without explicit notice, shifting the success criterion toward scenes. | Stages 4E–4H increasingly evaluate one contact and then prose; later task explicitly says “write the story.” | In a fresh task that visibly contains **both** objectives, does the agent correctly distinguish temporary method from governing outcome and preserve that distinction in subsequent selections? |
| **H4 — Provisional commitment inertia.** Vivid [P4E–P4H] fictional choices are repeatedly used as fixed research inputs, making competing architectures costly to reopen despite being noncanonical. | Retrospective documents accumulated characters, relays and a successful Rillturn visit, still unaccepted. | Compare decision behavior when provisional commitments are tagged as revisable versus presented as fixed; check if the agent can generate *structurally distinct* plans without erasing owner-fixed facts. |
| **H5 — Retrieval occurred but semantic uptake failed.** The agent sees strategy/ontology terms yet compares only the latest artifact. | Existing ontology and reasoning documents provide language; no historical attention trace. | Use a context-delivery receipt and a specific prediction about what a source changes in the option set; compare retrieval, accurate paraphrase, operational use and actual decision separately. |
| **H6 — Semantic misapplication of “decision value.”** The agent treats “a scene will tell us whether it works” as sufficient without identifying which **mission-level** judgment would change. | Stage 4G describes local dramatic proof; retrospective challenges whole-Series evidence relevance. | Hold alternatives fixed; require concrete possible results and resulting mission decision for each; check whether supposed high-value tests would actually alter Architecture assessment. |
| **H7 — Search-space myopia / premature convergence.** Repeated local refinements decrease the chance of discovering new architectural questions; alternatives become reworded versions of the same contact plot. | Consecutive 4E–4G probes pursue one meeting; 4H/4J deepen that same thread. | Ask for causally orthogonal candidates at different units (scene, Book, Series, stop), and test whether agent can reject cosmetic variants without being forced toward global work. |
| **H8 — Poor comparative forecast.** Agent generates relevant options but misjudges information gain, direct value, downstream effects, or omission cost. | No matched evaluation of A against whole-Series reentry exists. | Supply the same complete option set and source state; obtain explicit falsifiable consequence predictions, then observe outcomes of cheap matched tasks. Good documentation alone cannot establish forecasting quality. |
| **H9 — Short-horizon salience/recency bias.** Recent successful artifacts and emotionally vivid open ends become more salient than strategic work with delayed payoff. | Stage 4G just finished a crossing warrant; Stage 4H proposes an immediately interesting scene. | Keep evidence equal but vary presentation order and recency emphasis; do choices/forecasts change absent a real difference in value? |
| **H10 — Exploration/exploitation policy mismatch.** Agent may wrongly continue exploring local detail, or wrongly broaden when a bounded build is the cheapest useful evidence. | Repeated local tasks create diminishing architectural returns, yet one prose sample may be genuinely decision-relevant. | Include **both** a local-action-favorable and a reentry-favorable case. Ability is adaptation to state, not a constant preference for the broadest option. |
| **H11 — Evidence freshness or authority conflation.** Provisional working material is treated as accepted, or a later conclusion is silently used as if available at an earlier decision. | Research labels were careful, but provisional world details accumulated in later tasks. | Give the agent conflicting accepted/provisional versions and revision timestamps; test whether its choice respects source hash, authority and historical cutoff. There is no demonstrated integrity defect in Stage 4G. |
| **H12 — Handoff promotion ambiguity.** A subordinate review's recommendation is turned into a new mission by another actor without a mission-level comparison. | Separate Stage 4G recommendation and Stage 4H user task; promotion lacks recorded wider alternative comparison. | Record actor and authority at recommendation, selection, authorization and execution separately; if the executor made the right recommendation under a narrow envelope, a *coordinator-level* change may be more relevant. |
| **H13 — Observability gap rather than judgment gap.** Agent may have compared global reentry privately but only narrated B/C/D; retrospective cannot see its reasons. | No hidden trace; PR #543 responds by checking declared decision records. | Capture source inputs, candidate generation and short structured outcome predictions at the decision point, not chain-of-thought. Look for actual changed choices or merely fuller forms. |
| **H14 — Checker can be gamed / explanation optimization.** Presence-checking incentivizes ritualized mission comparisons or superficial “global was less valuable” rationales. | [Checker code](../../src/auteur/reasoning/responsibility_frame.py#L113-L175) only inspects strings/declared categories; [tests](../../tests/test_reasoning_responsibility_frame.py#L89-L98) explicitly acknowledge a truthful-exclusion gap. | Adversarially compare substantive versus empty-but-formally-compliant frames under the same checker. Observe whether actions improve even when records pass equally. |
| **H15 — Learning/reentry trigger absent.** After useful local evidence, the controller lacks a reason to revisit a now-obsolete question or close an exhausted line. | Repeated iterations from scene/reading/correction are visible; retrospective written later. | Provide a deliberately resolved or diminishing-value target and a materially changed Series objective; observe whether a new question arises without a prescriptive prompt. |
| **H16 — Resource/delegation constraints favor local work.** Limited time, available tools, lack of independent reviewer or active authority scope make one bounded scene the best feasible next action. | Stage 4G was an explicitly bounded task, Stage 4H had separate authorization, and subsequent independent-reading work encountered external limits. | Compare feasible/authorized option sets with actual costs and available actors; do not label an agent defective for correctly refusing out-of-scope global work. |
| **H17 — Local action was globally appropriate (non-failure).** Direct substantial prose is a cheap reversible probe that reveals otherwise inaccessible dramatic weaknesses. | Stage 4H did produce textured, extended fiction and exposed weaknesses in chart debate and interpersonal aftershocks; retrospective recognizes its value. | Compare subsequent Architecture revisions induced by the scene against an equally scoped global alternative study; consider retained prose value and information gained. |
| **H18 — Existing concepts are enough; integration is the problem (non-ontology cause).** Objective, sources, hypotheses, comparisons and authority already have owners, but no consequential-point consumer invokes them. | Existing Reasoning, Studio Inquiry, strategic guidance and PR #543 cover vocabulary; checker is optional. | Demonstrate changed selections from a minimal prompt/context/consumer arrangement before proposing new ontology entities. |
| **H19 — Incentives reward visible artifacts, not the right target.** Sequential prompts and handoff expectations can favor producing another PR/scene/report over rethinking the mission. | Many narrow research outputs accumulated, but no evidence of an optimizing reward function or production scheduler. | Change reward/reporting expectations in a controlled task and observe whether agents stop, escalate or reopen strategically without losing valid local work. |

**Correlations are not root causes.** H1 and H12 can coexist: the coordinator supplies a narrow menu, the executor reasons well inside it, and the next assignment faithfully implements its suggestion. H2 and H5 are different: one concerns access, the other use. H7/H9/H15 may produce similar visible patterns. H13 can coexist with excellent judgment. No single hypothesis is proven by the Stage 4G artifact.

## 4. Ontological landscape: what needs to be distinguished, not what must be added

### 4.1 The three proposed dimensions are valuable but not a complete operational model

| Analytical dimension | Correct question | Existing coverage and remaining distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Knowledge Integrity** | Is a record attributable to the right source/revision, current and correct within its warranted authority? | Canonical [Reasoning Architecture](../reasoning-architecture.md#validity-invariants) and [Architecture Constitution](../architecture-constitution.md) already demand provenance, claim/evidence separation and stale-source safety; the Stage 4G source availability question is not settled by these invariants alone. |
| **Semantic Coherence** | Are the same concepts/relations interpreted consistently between contexts and operations? | [Narrative Architecture](../narrative-architecture.md), [base ontology](../../data/ontology/base_ontology.yaml) and [Reasoning](../reasoning-architecture.md) distinguish identity, structure, evidence and recommendation. A coherent concept can still be ignored at choice time. |
| **Decision Usefulness** | Does knowledge help select worthwhile responsibilities under the present objective, alternatives and constraints? | PR #543 introduces explicit `ObjectiveContribution`, `DecisionValue` and `ComparativeWarrant` fields, but **recorded relevance is not measured decision improvement**. |

**Answer:** These are three complementary **diagnostic dimensions**, not sufficient primitive types for a controller. They do not specify **which objective governs now, which problem is decision-changing, which alternatives were generated, what counterfactual outcomes matter, which actor controls the transition, how much opportunity cost exists, or when fresh evidence should reopen the question**. A richer operational model might represent these relationships—but a capability-first agent may reason over them transiently without extending any ontology. The choice between models is still open.

### 4.2 Candidate conceptual objects and relationships (research vocabulary only)

**Objective domain.** `GoverningObjective`; `SubordinateObjective`; `ObjectiveRevision`; `PriorityRelation`; `SuccessCriterion`; `OwnerReservedConstraint`. Possible relations: “subordinate-to,” “tests,” “temporarily suspends,” “conflicts-with,” “authorized-by.” Needed operation: **objective-continuity/reentry check**, not an extra authorial Story Identity field. PR #543 already represents a *single* source-bound GoverningObjective.

**Problem and epistemic domain.** `Situation`; `ProblemCandidate`; `CurrentQuestion`; `Hypothesis`; `Uncertainty`; `Observation`; `SourceReceipt`; `ContextUptake`; `EvidenceGap`; `Interpretation`. Possible relations: “observation supports/contradicts hypothesis,” “question operationalizes objective,” “knowledge supplied-to decision,” “claim used-in choice.” Needed operations: **problem discovery**, **evidence relevance** and **transport/uptake diagnosis**. `ContextUptake` is not equivalent to storing a retrieval event or claiming private thought was inspected.

**Alternatives and control domain.** `ResponsibilityCandidate`; `ActionOption`; `AlternativeFamily`; `DecisionBoundary`; `Warrant`; `Selection`; `DecisionValue`; `OpportunityCost`; `Reversibility`; `Dependency`; `SearchMode`; `StopCondition`. Candidate relations: “candidate addresses problem,” “action tests hypothesis,” “option displaces alternative,” “requires prerequisite,” “changes future option set,” “selection belongs-to boundary.” Needed operations: **meaningfully divergent option generation**, **bounded comparison**, **stop/reentry control**. PR #543 already has a narrower ResponsibilityOption/ComparativeWarrant vocabulary.

**Authority and continuity domain.** `ProvisionalCommitment`; `AcceptedDecision`; `Recommendation`; `AuthorizedMission`; `DecisionOwner`; `ExecutionReceipt`; `PredictedOutcome`; `ObservedOutcome`; `ReassessmentTrigger`. Possible relations: “recommended-by executor,” “promoted-by coordinator,” “authorized-by owner,” “observed-outcome-of action,” “invalidates assumption,” “reopens objective.” Needed operations: **responsibility handoff**, **learning** and **claim reconciliation**. Narrative canon and research authorization remain different authority owners.

**Material caution:** These are candidate **roles**, not a proposed persistent schema. Some should be transient reasoning steps, some already exist as report fields, and some are already source-backed in another repository. A “Goal” in the narrative Layer-0 ontology is a character's fictional goal; it is **not automatically** the coding agent's governing product objective. Likewise an Arc's Book dependency is not a dependency between engineering responsibilities.

### 4.3 Alternative ontology/representation architectures (unranked)

| Architecture family | Core approach / real consumer to demand | Opportunity and downside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **O1 — Reuse Auteur Reasoning with no new types.** | Use existing Observation→Hypothesis→Evaluation→Recommendation and PR #543 `ResponsibilityFrame` at consequential handoffs; add **no** data-model fields until a choice operation requires them. | Lowest semantic duplication; may rely on free-text and strong agent judgment, with poor automatic alternative discovery. |
| **O2 — Enrich Auteur's operational Reasoning ontology.** | Typed objective lineage, question–responsibility link, source-use receipt, consequence and reassessment trigger **only if** a live selector/diagnostic would actually consume them. | Better local traceability but risks conflating general agent governance with story-specific creative judgment and increasing form completion without real reasoning. |
| **O3 — A separate reusable agent-control ontology.** | Distinct non-narrative objects for problem, warrant, action, resources and learning; a generic coding-agent controller consumes them. | Clear generality and autonomy target, but parallel policy/authority graphs may duplicate Sensemaking Skills and create another controller. |
| **O4 — Sensemaking reuse.** | Reuse the already-described inquiry, metareasoning, exploration, warrant/choice, strategic reentry and learning concepts from [Sensemaking's adaptive semantic-control crosswalk](https://github.com/ThorStarlord/sensemaking-skills/blob/da223b37fd706e8700636d465c6263c9ab028362/docs/adaptive-semantic-control-architecture-v0.md). | Avoids rebuilding general concepts; actual host integration, versioning, data boundaries and Auteur-specific creative evaluation remain unproven. Its descriptive architecture is **not** a guaranteed executable runtime. |
| **O5 — Domain-independent core + narrative extensions.** | General objective/uncertainty/choice machinery decides when to invoke Auteur's Series engine, Premise Fitness, Studio inquiry and author-acceptance flows. | A plausible separation of general control from narrative craft; risks extra integration surface and unclear responsibility owner if duplicated in both repositories. |
| **O6 — Capability-first/minimal ontology.** | Improve one live behavior via explicit source exposure, orthogonal alternative elicitation, counterfactual reasoning, or a lightweight reversible task. Store only needed evidence. | May improve actual choice fastest; without durable records can be hard to debug cross-session drift or evaluate claim attribution. |
| **O7 — Decision-event log rather than a rich ontology.** | Persist sparse objective revision, alternatives *actually considered*, actor, chosen action, observed consequence and next trigger; derive richer concepts for audit. | Evidence of handoffs may become much clearer; logging does not invent missing alternatives or verify the value model. |

**Ontology admission test for every novel role:** Name (1) the operation that reads it, (2) the input evidence it consumes, (3) the observable decision/behavior it changes, (4) why present free-text/typed fields fail, and (5) the authority and persistence boundary. If this cannot be answered, leave it exploratory. More concepts are not presumed to cause more intelligence.

## 5. Divergent capability landscape: behavior-changing routes

Each capability below is an *alternative or composable candidate*, not a proposed unified implementation plan. Status (“available,” “partial,” “unproven”) is based on inspected sources and does not mean deployed in a production task controller.

| Candidate capability / mechanism | Input → observable output or action | Existing coverage, possible deficit, and introduced risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C1 — Mission-goal continuity/reentry.** Detect when local success ceases to inform the governing goal. | Source-bound objective + current question + previous outcomes → a **reentry question or no-change** with consequences; not automatic global selection. | [Strategy](2026-11-07-generative-long-form-narrative-architecture-strategy.md) and [AGENTS](../../AGENTS.md) supply guidance; not proven automatically applied. Can become ritualized re-checking at every small task. |
| **C2 — Problem discovery from anomalous evidence.** Generate *candidate problems* rather than only answers to a supplied one. | Product objectives + friction, omissions, contradictory outcomes → independent problem hypotheses with source anchors and discriminating tests. | Auteur diagnostics find story defects; general engineering problem discovery is not shown as an operational PR #543 function. Risk: invented defects and endless exploratory backlog. |
| **C3 — Evidence transport and semantic uptake.** Make the relevant source available at the actual choice point and distinguish access from use. | Pinned source receipts + task context + candidate assertions → “supplied / accurately interpreted / actually invoked in warrant / unknown.” | [Studio ontology context](../../src/auteur/beginner/studio_ontology_context.py) projects meanings, but does not prove usage. Risk: false “read” assurances or excessive context stuffing. |
| **C4 — Causally orthogonal option generation.** Discover different *responsibilities*, not cosmetic action variants. | Governing question + resource/authority constraints → local, mission, probe, stop, owner-decision candidates with distinctly different possible outcomes. | [Studio Inquiry explore](../../src/auteur/beginner/studio_architecture_inquiry.py) already requires distinct **narrative** causal alternatives, not general task choices. Risk: huge lists, spurious global options, weak execution. |
| **C5 — Objective-relative value comparison.** Evaluate consequences and omitted opportunities without fabricated numerical scores. | Alternative set + evidence + direct value + information gain + reversibility + opportunity cost → conditional comparison and explicit uncertainty. | PR #543 checks for stated decision value, not its truth. Risk: subjective value forecasts and rhetoric masquerading as optimality. |
| **C6 — Cross-horizon consequence exploration.** Trace a local action's effects on later structures and decisions. | Local action / anticipated outcome + Series Book functions or dependency graph → alternative future pressures and falsifiable cross-horizon deltas. | [Series graph](../../src/auteur/series/graph.py) models source-affects-target links; [Premise Fitness](../premise-fitness.md) discusses runway. | **C7 — Problem reformulation / unit-of-analysis switch.** Notice the question is wrong or too small. | Repeated local inconclusive outcomes + global target + rivals → new problem statement, explicit scope change, or permission escalation. | [Long-form strategy](2026-11-07-generative-long-form-narrative-architecture-strategy.md) describes reflective regulation; automatic selection remains unproven. Risk: disruptive strategy churn. |
| **C8 — Adaptive exploration/exploitation controller.** Choose inquiry vs reversible construction vs challenge vs exit when useful. | Current warrant gap + existing evidence + costs → bounded next control move, sometimes **STOP**. | Sensemaking provides these *normative* policy distinctions; PR #543 is not such a controller. Risk: verbose procedural overhead and over-search. |
| **C9 — Semantic adversarial challenge of a decision.** Test source use and sufficiency instead of only field presence. | Selected option + strongest alternative + objective → counter-reading with falsifiers and unexamined omissions. | Studio has `challenge` and PR #543 checks references; neither proves a general decision's substantive quality. Risk: forced negativity and expensive review loops. |
| **C10 — Context-independent multi-perspective deliberation.** Distinguish framing, source use and rival valuation across agents. | Same pinned case, separated investigative roles and actual isolation → compared evidence-bound accounts, not majority vote. | No direct subagent-spawn facility is exposed in this ChatGPT execution; not attempted. Risk: correlated models, role-play masquerading as independent evidence, latency and cost. |
| **C11 — Decision-event provenance and observability.** Explain **where** a choice came from without claiming internal thoughts. | Task origin, objective revisions, actually supplied material, options, actor, output, executed work → compact event chain. | Historical research has strong Git hashes, but not full choice-point provenance; PR #543 is caller-declared. Risk: detailed paperwork becomes the success metric. |
| **C12 — Authority-aware recommendation promotion.** Separate suggested work from selected/authorized next mission. | Reviewer suggestion + new task owner + explicit scope comparison → accepted next responsibility or blocked/owner decision. | [Architecture Constitution](../architecture-constitution.md) separates derived/candidate/acceptance; [AGENTS](../../AGENTS.md) has a handoff cue. Risk: imposing a new central permission gate or shifting author authority. |
| **C13 — Consequence feedback and learning.** Reconsider an objective or problem after a bounded outcome. | Predicted value + actual outcome + changed source state → confirm, revise, retire, or reopen a responsibility hypothesis. | [Reasoning](../reasoning-architecture.md) and the retrospective support this concept; not proof of autonomous cross-episode learning. | **C14 — Reversible construction as evidence.** Build a scene/probe/tool when it creates lasting value and a discriminating observation. | Specific unresolved claim + cheap bounded artifact → real outcome and decision update, not merely extra analysis. | Stage 4H shows that direct writing can reveal distinct defects; it did not establish that every extra scene should be preferred. | **C15 — Minimal “do nothing” comparator.** Let stopping, waiting for authority or leaving a capability unchanged compete seriously. | Current evidence, residual uncertainty, opportunity and omission cost → STOP/DEFER/ASK rationale when warranted. | PR #543 supports `close` and `author_decision` options, but compares them only if recorded; risk of passive avoidance of good opportunities. |

**Important counterposition:** Better use of the existing local Reasoning tools, a small change in what a coordinator supplies, or a short source-bound alternative comparison could solve a useful part of the problem **without any new ontology or workflow engine**. Conversely, better decision records without a better way to *discover* alternatives may solve observability only. No candidate above is selected for implementation.

## 8. Local versus global without a built-in winner

| Scenario | Locally best may be… | More global or other action may be… | Distinguishing fact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A scene exposes a precise causal gap that can be repaired quickly | Write a short realized scene with a falsifiable architectural prediction | Rebuild the whole Series prematurely | Does the scene's outcome actually change a Book function or system-wide hypothesis? |
| After several scenes, the same unresolved Series-engine question remains | Stop polishing another scene | Reopen rival organization/engine exploration | Is remaining scene uncertainty orthogonal to architectural discrimination? |
| An external reviewer is unavailable but text is frozen | Preserve a neutral handoff and wait, or continue independent authorized work | Forge a review or multiply same-context self-critiques | Does an independent response change the next decision, and is it obtainable? |
| A repository has a broken low-level authority check with broad potential effects | Correct the localized boundary and run focused tests | Launch speculative strategy reopening | Is the system objective already clear, and will the bounded repair preserve it? |
| The governing objective itself may be obsolete | Refuse further local optimization | Reassess objective/seek reserved decision | Has new evidence invalidated the original purpose, or is it only a new local obstacle? |
| Several candidate designs look equally plausible and consequential | One cheap counterfactual/probe | Larger implementation, or author escalation | Which evidence would reverse the choice at acceptable cost? |
| No remaining option is warranted or authorized | STOP or request owner direction | Forced backlog work, new ontology, or imagined success | Which missing premise or authority actually blocks action? |

Optimize **relevant total value**—direct retained product value plus information worth acquiring, reversibility, downstream opportunity cost, and omitted alternatives—*qualitatively*. Do not force a numeric score, and do not fetishize zooming out. Effective autonomy can mean exploiting a known local opportunity, exploring a wider alternative set, challenging a premise, or ceasing work.

## 11. Coverage Assessment handoff: questions to test, not decisions already made

The subsequent **Coverage Assessment** should determine whether this divergent space is broad *enough for meaningful discrimination*, not count ideas or converge prematurely. In particular:

1. **Causal separation:** Can it distinguish task framing, objective availability, semantic uptake, option generation, comparison, promotion, recency bias, feedback and genuine local appropriateness? Which hypotheses share observations and need different evidence?
2. **Genuine alternatives:** Are there real local, global, inquiry, reversible construction, stop and author-reserved cases? Are “creative problem discovery” and “engineering task selection” meaningfully distinguished?
3. **Ontology admission:** Which proposed relations are already represented by Auteur Reasoning, narrative ontology, Sensemaking policies or ordinary source-bound prose? Which *consequential live operation* would truly require a new type?
4. **Capability sufficiency:** Can an existing source/handoff change, alternative-generation operation, or advisory checker usage obtain the same improved decision without a new architecture? What actual consumer and return path would be needed?
5. **Historical epistemic limits:** Has any new record been found showing Stage 4G actually read/used the higher-level strategy, or that a broader comparison was made? Do not convert absence of visible evidence into proof of absence.
6. **Authority and ownership:** Does the observed selection gap belong to the executor, prompt author/coordinator, general control system, Auteur creative judgment, or a combination? What cross-repo interface would have to be authorized before implementation?
7. **Consequences and value:** What concrete future decision would change if a capability worked? What would count as a regression: over-exploration, forcing strategic reentry, laundering fake rationale, consuming excessive context, or blocking useful local construction?
8. **Evidence economy:** Which cheapest bounded observations can distinguish the strongest remaining causes, and which questions must remain open until a fresh-context agent can be observed?
9. **Adversarial sufficiency:** Do proposed approaches hold up when (a) existing local work is clearly best, (b) the governing objective changes, (c) the correct move is stop/escalate, and (d) a coherent explanation is disconnected from actual action?
10. **Readiness to converge:** Which real distinctions have surviving competing models and consumers? Which attractive ontology additions should be rejected or deferred for lack of operational use? A result of **“existing capabilities are adequate, but unqualified in use”** is admissible.

**Coverage Assessment output should be** a grounded gap/redundancy matrix, hypotheses that remain observationally indistinguishable, a capability-to-consumer mapping, and an evidence-readiness judgment. It should **not** produce a chosen architecture, canonical ontology revision, implementation queue, or actual behavioral test without a subsequent explicit decision.

## 12. Breadth synthesis and stopping boundary

This exploration preserves **multiple causally distinct families**—goal continuity, knowledge access/uptake, hypothesis and option search, comparative forecast, consequence/value, authority handoff, decision observability, adaptation/learning, environmental constraints and **non-failure** cases. They admit both narrative-specific and general-agent interpretations. The historical Stage 4G pattern is informative but too narrow to isolate which family dominates.

**Notable discoveries from the exact sources:** (i) the owner-endorsed strategy was source-available before Stage 4G, whereas PR #543's explicit agent-facing reentry cue was not; (ii) the executor compared locally supplied choices and did not visibly compare whole-Series reentry, yet another actor authorized the next mission; (iii) the current checker detects omissions in a **caller-supplied** frame and explicitly permits superficial exclusion rationales; (iv) existing Studio `explore` and Reasoning Runtime have “selection” capabilities for *narrative hypotheses* and *critics*, respectively, not an agent's strategic next responsibility; (v) Sensemaking already contains a descriptive general-control vocabulary, so any new general ontology would have to justify avoiding duplication.

**Bounded finding:** There is a **decision-selection research problem worth exploring**, not yet a demonstrated missing ontology, a proven wrong Stage 4G choice, a defect in PR #543, or a reason to prefer global to local decisions categorically. Preserve the current five-layer story model and author authority. Work stops after documenting this divergent landscape and opening/verifying the documentation-only PR. **No convergence, architecture synthesis, new test, code change or Stage 4H rewrite follows automatically.**

### Governing distinctions

1. It is not merely failure to choose the best answer; it may be failure to discover the decision-changing question.
2. It is not possession of sound knowledge and is not semantic fluency; it is actually warranted use of knowledge in comparing consequences and choosing actions.
3. Adaptive autonomy is actually the capacity to notice when the problem, evidence or objective has changed—and to select local action, strategic reentry or stopping accordingly.
