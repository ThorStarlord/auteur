**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The controlling constraint is the series commitment `contested-history` — every Book must expose the conflict between official history and lived memory, which means the monastery testimony has to surface, not stay buried. But Book 3 established that the treaty protects the archive as the *only evidentiary chain*, and that protection was the direct answer to the council's retraction of its own admission (council.archive_position = retracted admission). That retraction is exactly the kind of institutional erasure this series punishes — the open question asks whether truth can survive without becoming an instrument of power, and the series pressure warns that every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness.

Given that pressure, exposing testimony *by cracking open the protected archive itself* would hand the archivists precisely the opening the series' own pressure clause warns about. publish-verified-testimony authenticates the testimony independently and releases it while the archive stays sealed under treaty protection — it exposes the conflict (satisfying `contested-history`) without staking the evidentiary chain on the same act. The founding record being forged and the original falsifier already being identified (Book 2) means the testimony's credibility doesn't depend on opening the archive — it can stand on its own authentication.

**Principal tradeoff:** Release is delayed — verification takes time the council's retraction and the ongoing pressure to erase witnesses don't necessarily grant. The monastery testimony sits authenticated but unpublished for a stretch, which is real narrative risk (a delay is itself an opportunity for erasure), but it's a bounded, chosen risk rather than a structural one against the chain itself.

**What I excluded:** stage-protected-hearing — presenting testimony beside "selected archive evidence under treaty" necessarily draws on the very archive the treaty was built to keep as the sole, undisturbed evidentiary chain (Book 3 Realization). Doing that under the current pressure clause (corrections invite erasure) risks compromising the chain's integrity for immediate impact — a tradeoff the option itself admits ("reveals strongest records"). I did not treat the repaired lantern as decision-relevant here; it's a continuity/state fact (archive_lantern.condition = repaired) with no bearing on which publication mechanism protects the evidentiary chain. I also did not invent any new archivist antagonist actions or assume the retraction was reversed — only the stated facts above were used.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
