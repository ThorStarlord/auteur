**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The series commitment `contested-history` requires every Book to expose the conflict between official history and lived memory — the monastery testimony (preserved since Book 1) is exactly that lived-memory material, and returning it to public view is the right move. But the current state is fragile: the council's admission was already retracted once (Book 2), and Book 3's whole Direction was protecting the archive as "the only evidentiary chain" via treaty — precisely because a prior public exposure (the retraction) put the record at risk. Given the series pressure ("every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness"), reintroducing the testimony through a controlled, authenticated publication keeps the treaty-protected archive intact as the anchor evidence while still surfacing the suppressed witness. This path respects Book 3's Realization (the treaty protects the archive as sole evidentiary chain) without requiring that protection to be pierced or renegotiated.

**Principal tradeoff:** Verification takes time, so public correction is delayed — the testimony doesn't reach public memory immediately, and the series pressure (archivists erasing witnesses in response to correction) has a longer window in which the unverified testimony could be attacked or the witness targeted before publication.

**What I excluded and why:** I did not recommend stage-protected-hearing. Presenting the testimony beside "selected" archive evidence necessarily discloses which records carry the strongest evidence — that directly cuts against the reason the treaty exists (protecting the archive as a single, undifferentiated evidentiary chain) and against Book 3's Direction, which was a response to the council's retraction and the resulting need to shield the archive from further exposure. That option's "immediate public pressure" benefit isn't supported by any accepted fact establishing urgency for Book 4 — no accepted material indicates the testimony is time-critical or that delay itself causes harm, so I did not weight that tradeoff as decisive. I also did not treat the repaired lantern as relevant to this question — it's a physical/continuity fact carried forward from Books 1 and 3, not something bearing on evidentiary-chain or testimony-publication decisions, so I left it out of the analysis.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
