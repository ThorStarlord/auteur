**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The active continuity requirement (contested-history) obligates Book 4 to expose a consequential conflict between official history and lived memory, and the planning intent is explicit — return the monastery testimony to public memory *without breaking* the protected archive. Two accepted facts bear directly on this: monastery testimony remains preserved and available to bring forward, and the archive is currently treaty protected — not merely a background condition but a live constraint on how that testimony can be surfaced. Authenticate-and-publish is the only option that keeps the archive's evidentiary chain fully intact while still discharging the contested-history obligation, since the original stays secured under the existing treaty protection rather than being exposed for the sake of dramatic immediacy.

**Principal tradeoff:** Preserving the chain of custody costs narrative urgency — verification and authentication take time, so the corrective disclosure lands later rather than as an immediate confrontation.
