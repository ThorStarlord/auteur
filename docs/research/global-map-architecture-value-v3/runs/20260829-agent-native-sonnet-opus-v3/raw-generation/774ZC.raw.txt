Recommendation: **publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** Two accepted, currently-active constraints govern this decision as a grouped pressure cluster — monastery-testimony (the testimony is preserved and Book 4 planning intent explicitly wants it back in public memory) and archive-protected (the archive currently sits under treaty protection). The planning intent is explicit: bring the testimony back *without breaking the protected archive*. Authenticate-and-publish is the option built to keep the original archive secure inside its treaty protection while still surfacing the testimony — it satisfies both halves of the grouped commitment simultaneously rather than trading one against the other.

The derived incompatibility (archive-protected ruling out any archive-burning move) doesn't directly touch either presented option — neither proposes destroying the archive — but it confirms the boundary condition both options must respect, and publish-verified-testimony respects it with the widest margin, since it never exposes the underlying records at all.

**Principal tradeoff:** Preserving the evidentiary chain costs time — the option delays release in exchange for authentication and continued treaty protection.

**Against stage-protected-hearing:** it satisfies the treaty technically ("under treaty") but achieves urgency by exhibiting selected archive evidence alongside the testimony. That's a closer approach to the strongest records than the grouped commitment calls for — the intent is to protect the archive's evidentiary chain, not merely its formal custody status, and immediate pressure isn't a stated need at this horizon. It's a viable option, not an excluded one, but it carries more exposure risk than the situation demands.
