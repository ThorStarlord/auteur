Recommendation: **publish-verified-testimony**

Why: The series commitment `contested-history` requires every Book to expose conflict between official history and lived memory — but the monastery testimony is already preserved (Book 1) and the falsifier already identified (Book 2); what Book 4 needs is to move that testimony into public memory, not to re-litigate the archive's evidentiary basis. The council's admission was retracted (Book 2), which means public trust in the archive record is already fragile — introducing the testimony through a process that visibly ranks or exposes which archive records are strongest (stage-protected-hearing) compounds that fragility right when the treaty (Book 3) has just settled the archive as the sole protected evidentiary chain. Publishing the testimony as its own authenticated document, with the treaty-protected archive left untouched as the secured original evidence, keeps the two lines of proof separate: the testimony re-enters public memory on its own authority, and the archive's status as evidentiary chain is not reopened for public comparison.

Principal tradeoff: Verification and publication takes time, delaying the testimony's public impact — but that delay is the cost of not exposing comparative archive evidence while the retracted admission and the series' pressure clause ("every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness") are both live.

Excluded as not relevant: The forged founding record and the identity of the falsifier (Book 1–2) are settled history, not decision points for this choice. The lantern repair (Book 3) is a physical/continuity fact with no bearing on evidentiary or publication strategy. I did not treat the series' open question ("can truth survive without becoming instrument of power") as a fact to resolve — it's framing, not something this recommendation can settle.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
