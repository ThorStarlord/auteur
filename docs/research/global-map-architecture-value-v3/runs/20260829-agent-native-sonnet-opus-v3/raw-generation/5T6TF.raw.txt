**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The series commitment "contested-history" requires every Book to expose the conflict between official history and lived memory — the monastery testimony is exactly that lived-memory material, and returning it to public memory is the right move. But Book 3's treaty explicitly protects the archive as "the only evidentiary chain," and the current state confirms archive.protection = treaty protected. That protection was won at real cost — Book 3's whole Direction was "The Protected Archive," adopted specifically because the council had retracted its earlier admission of forgery (council.archive_position = retracted admission). A retracted council is a hostile or unreliable institutional actor: this is exactly the environment where the evidentiary chain is most fragile and least able to absorb a second rupture. Authenticating and publishing the testimony while the treaty keeps the original archive secure honors both live commitments — it exposes the contested history (satisfying contested-history) without asking the treaty's protection to bend. The founding record being forged and the testimony being preserved (established since Book 1) are exactly the kind of buried material this option is designed to surface — through publication rather than through opening the archive itself.

**Principal tradeoff:** Preserving the evidentiary chain intact means delaying the testimony's public release — a real cost given the series pressure that "every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness." Delay is a defensible price for chain integrity here; the alternative risks the chain itself.

**Why not stage-protected-hearing:** it presents testimony beside "selected archive evidence," which necessarily surfaces some of the archive's strongest records under present pressure. Given a retracted council and the series pressure toward erasing witnesses, exposing more archive material now — rather than through the treaty's protective custody — raises the exact exposure risk Book 3 was fought to prevent. Immediate pressure release doesn't outweigh that risk absent any accepted fact suggesting the treaty or council posture has changed since Book 3.

**Deliberately excluded as not relevant:** the repaired lantern (a physical/continuity detail, not evidentiary-chain or memory-politics relevant), and the identity of the record-falsifier from Book 2 (already resolved, not in tension with either option here). No unaccepted material was used — the analysis relies only on the contested-history commitment, the Book 3 treaty/protection facts, the retracted council admission, and the preserved testimony.

This is a planning recommendation only — it creates no Book Direction and modifies no canonical state.
