Recommendation: force-council-hearing

Why: The series commitment "contested-history" requires every Book to expose conflict between official history and lived memory — a public hearing that puts the named falsifier and the council's own retraction on record does this directly, staging the conflict rather than routing around it. The council already admitted the archive record was falsified before retracting; that retraction is itself the live conflict Book 3 must dramatize, and only a hearing forces the council to answer for the retraction specifically, engaging the series open question (can truth survive without becoming instrument of power?) on the terrain where power already showed its hand once. Book 2's Direction "The Council's Retraction" established that the council's pattern is to admit, then retract — a hearing tests whether that pattern repeats under public pressure, which is the sharpest available test of the series question.

Principal tradeoff: The council controls the forum and timing of any hearing it's compelled into, meaning it can shape, delay, or stage-manage its own accountability — the same institutional power the series is scrutinizing. This risks a second retraction or a hollow, controlled admission rather than genuine reckoning.

What I excluded: publish-witness-account. It has real merit (an unretractable public record directly answers "erasure" pressure and protects witness authority structurally), but it centers the witness's individual exposure to retaliation rather than the council's institutional accountability — and the series pressure ("every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness") suggests that publishing without addressing the council directly may just trigger the next erasure cycle without resolving who profits from control of the record. I did not treat this exclusion as ruling the option out entirely, only as less aligned with the immediate planning intent of responding to the retraction itself.

I did not invent any facts beyond the accepted history and commitments listed above. This is a non-authoritative planning recommendation — it creates no Book Direction and modifies no Canonical State.
