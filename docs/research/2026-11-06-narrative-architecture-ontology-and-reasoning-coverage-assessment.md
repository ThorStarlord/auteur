## 1. Executive conclusion

**Disposition: E — smallest discriminating authentic-agent probe, before choosing a change.** Auteur already has substantial narrative semantics, a canonical five-layer authority model, explicit reasoning terminology, a noncanonical agent-first Architecture workflow, source-referenced ontology injection, differentiated creative inquiry operations, holistic working revision, Realization handoff, and fresh-session recovery. The three reported bell-keeper errors do **not** demonstrate that a new narrative ontology entity or relationship is necessary.

The evidence supports **four simultaneous, non-identical findings**:

1. **Sufficient core narrative concepts, not a theorem prover.** The base ontology defines Conflict, Goal, Setup, Payoff, Revelation and related narrative objects and their normative relationships. Its conflict rule requires opposition and stakes; it does not license the leap from an under-described opposition to a factually absent conflict. The ontology validator checks rule applicability/structure rather than evaluating the semantic truth of the rule against story values. Sources: data/ontology/base_ontology.yaml:L120-L178 and src/auteur/narrative_ontology/validator/ontology_validator.py:L176-L229.
2. **Epistemic and authorial distinctions already exist, but are distributed.** Reasoning Architecture separates observation, evidence, hypothesis, evaluation, claim and recommendation; canonical contracts separate accepted facts from interpretive proposals; Studio prompts distinguish explicit author intent, unknowns and provisional agent ideas. Explicit deductions versus conditional possibilities are not consistently given an agent-facing decision check. Sources: docs/reasoning-architecture.md:L104-L201,L244-L263; docs/narrative-architecture.md:L8-L19,L68-L75; src/auteur/beginner/studio_architecture.py:L584-L695.

Studio Architecture Inquiry internally constructs working context but its outgoing request contains the candidate, current question, graph intent, guidance and recent exchanges—not the complete raw Vision or broader working items. A premise present only in the Vision is therefore not guaranteed to reach that workflow unless repeated elsewhere. 4. **Mechanical and historical evidence do not qualify independent creative reliability.** Tests establish structured payloads, pointer freshness, source-anchor existence, schema limits and recovery scaffolding; they cannot establish that an agent correctly derives necessities, respects every semantic premise, or consistently develops good stories. The November 6 creative-deliberation evaluation used a fixture-admitted candidate, same-agent drafted outputs and short independent critiques, and expressly says no independent full creative journey or human author study occurred. Source: docs/research/2026-11-06-connected-creative-thread-evaluation.md:L13-L24. ### 3.2 B — Epistemic reasoning

Auteur already defines **Observation → Evidence → Hypothesis → Evaluation → Claim/Diagnosis → Confidence → Recommendation → Transformation Proposal** for derived reports (docs/reasoning-architecture.md:L18-L43). Observation is bounded and reproducible, evidence is source-backed, hypotheses remain provisional with supporting and contradictory evidence, evaluations compare rivals, and recommendations do not imply author selection (L104-L201). The validity invariants reject unsupported claims, evidence/inference conflation, automatic promotion and stale-source reuse (L244-L263). Story Opportunity Discovery separately suggests AUTHOR_PREFERENCE, SOURCE_OBSERVATION, CRAFT_INTERPRETATION, CRAFT_HEURISTIC and UNKNOWN labels for upstream creative search (docs/story-opportunity-discovery.md:L216-L229).

The exact logical distinctions can be expressed using those existing notions:

- **Observation vs inference:** “The packet names no antagonist” is a source-bounded observation; “therefore no opposing pressure exists” is an inference with an additional unsupported premise.
- **Hypothesis vs conclusion:** “The registry might retain memories” is a hypothesis until established as a story rule; “registration necessarily prevents forgetting” demands entailment from explicit rules.
- **Necessary vs sufficient vs enabling:** A condition may *enable* retention without being sufficient for it. If a guarantee needs both voluntary enrollment **and** a persistent-memory rule, enrollment alone is insufficient.
- **Compatible/incompatible/underdetermined:** Voluntary enrollment is compatible with subsequent forgetting; compatibility is not necessity and uncertainty is not contradiction.
- **Counterfactual assumptions:** A hypothetical alteration may preserve specified premises or explicitly change them; neither is newly observed canon.
- **Source provenance/authority:** A linked source or valid SHA establishes identity/currentness; it does not prove the truth of literary interpretation.

These meanings are recoverable from the reporting contracts plus Studio guidance but are **not assembled into one concise, consequential agent-facing inference check**. There is no evidence that adding “LogicalNecessity,” “SufficientCondition,” or an epistemic status enum to base_ontology.yaml would outperform disciplined use of current vocabulary.

### 3.4 D — Two independent axes: epistemic status and authorial authority

**Axis 1: What warrant does the proposition have?** observed/source-attested, derived under stated conditions, inferred/interpretive, hypothesized/proposed, contradicted, or unknown. ### A. Overstated absence of conflict

**Problematic inference:** Because no explicit opposing force was named, the story **has no conflict**.

**Applicable sources:** data/ontology/base_ontology.yaml:L120-L178 formally says goals may generate conflicts and a Conflict must involve opposing forces and stakes; src/auteur/narrative_ontology/core/narrative_concepts.py:L117-L138 mirrors its rules. docs/reasoning-architecture.md:L104-L160 distinguishes observed evidence and provisional diagnoses. The creative architecture prompt requires pressures and causal organization, not a flat list (studio_architecture.py:L610-L638).

**Strongest warranted correction:** A conflict that fails to identify two incompatible forces and meaningful stakes is **under-specified against the ontology's Conflict definition**. That permits a constructive request to clarify pressure, goal incompatibility or costs. It does **not** establish that the story world has no opposing pressure. Candidate opponents could be a person, institution, competing duty or structural pressure *if* each is articulated as a force with an incompatible aim; mere environmental difficulty without legible incompatibility should not automatically be declared compliant. Avoid weakening the ontology's explicit two-forces rule to rescue the story.

**Classification:** Conflict concepts **SUFFICIENT**. The reported inference exceeded the described evidence despite the owner supplying a relevant Vision in the intended direct prompt. A **method/application** or **model behavior** problem is plausible; actual source consumption and repeatability are **UNRESOLVED**. The Studio Inquiry Vision omission is not this experiment's route.

**Smallest preventative intervention:** In the next bounded diagnosis request, require the agent to name which source establishes opposing forces and stakes; otherwise use “not yet established/underdeveloped,” offer one or two compatible ways to clarify, and permit the author to decline.

**Discriminating observation:** In the **direct-prompt reasoning probe**, record the first-pass conflict diagnosis from the explicit author Vision; then ask for a general self-critique without naming the error. Observe whether the agent upgrades “conflict absent” to a warranted account of **under-described incompatibility and stakes**, and whether its repair gives the author a meaningful pressure mechanism. If unsuccessful, add limited explicit epistemic guidance and compare *reasoning*, not just terminology. A separate implicit/absent-opposition contrast can be used if needed, but is not required to inflate the first probe.

### B. Unsupported necessity from voluntary promise registration

**Problematic inference:** Voluntarily registering promises makes it **logically impossible** for people later to forget making them.

**Applicable sources:** docs/reasoning-architecture.md:L104-L160,L244-L263 requires explicit evidence, rival hypotheses and unsupported-status honesty; studio_architecture.py:L617-L625 distinguishes necessary changes from optional ideas; studio_architecture_inquiry.py:L22-L34 warns that literary causal claims are interpretations, not graph proof. The base ontology's Setup rule concerns narrative promise/payoff expectations (data/ontology/base_ontology.yaml:L268-L287), **not** the fictional psychology or metaphysics of remembering an oath. Do not misapply Setup to adjudicate diegetic memory.

**Strongest warranted correction:** From **voluntary registration at time t1**, the strongest conclusion—if that is explicitly stipulated—is that a person chose to register a promise at t1. Nothing in that premise entails permanent recollection at t2. Registry persistence, record accessibility, memory protection, tampering, social pressure and forgetting remain compatible or unknown unless specified independently. Distinguish a **necessary condition** for something, a **sufficient guarantee**, and an **enabling circumstance**.

**Classification:** Existing epistemic distinctions are expressible; **no ONTOLOGY GAP is demonstrated**. The absence of a concise entailment-versus-compatibility check in some Studio-specific guidance is an independent **METHOD GAP candidate**, not an explanation of why the *directly prompted* model claimed impossibility. Whether the model actually used the requested ontology documents and why it overclaimed are **UNRESOLVED**.

**Smallest preventative intervention:** Before a consequential “must,” “cannot,” or “impossible,” ask: *Which explicit premise makes this necessary? What compatible counterexample survives the supplied rules?* If none proves inevitability, report it as conditional possibility. Apply this as bounded agent guidance only if an authentic probe shows the need.

**Discriminating observation:** In the direct-prompt first pass, ask about voluntary registration **without** adding any permanent-memory rule. Challenge the response generally, without revealing the intended answer. If a false necessity remains, give explicit entailment/compatibility guidance and see whether the agent identifies a coherent forgetting counterexample. A contrasting prompt with an independently stated memory guarantee is optional only if it changes the diagnosis; it must not be smuggled into the original premise.

### C. Silent substitution of the author's secret alteration

**Problematic inference/action:** The author specified that an institution had been **secretly altered to exempt powerful citizens**, but the agent substituted a **historical founding compromise** without stating that it changed the premise.

**Applicable sources:** docs/narrative-architecture.md:L8-L19,L68-L75 distinguishes working/canonical ownership; docs/product/auteur-unified-author-experience-architecture.md:L942-L970 separates author input, accepted source, pending proposals and inference. The initial/revision prompt says working Vision and previous candidate are working material, accepted Identity is authoritative and conflicting proposals require explicit attention (studio_architecture.py:L584-L609,L617-L625). The **Explore inquiry** expressly says “keeping the author's stated commitments” (studio_architecture_inquiry.py:L48-L57). Graph design preserves exact author_text and source refs (Nov 5 graph spec:L92-L105).

**Strongest warranted correction:** “A founding compromise created the exception” is an alternate historical origin, **not** the same explanation as “a later actor secretly altered the institution.” In a **premise-preserving** exploration, retain the covert alteration and vary, for example, who did it, the exploit mechanism, motive, evidence or resulting cost. A founding compromise is permissible only as a **labeled premise-changing counterfactual**; it is not adopted unless the author explicitly chooses a new working direction and any existing canonical owner separately accepts a corresponding change.

**Classification:** Authorial distinction **SUFFICIENT** in the existing semantic contracts and current task instructions. The owner says the original direct prompt explicitly fixed the **secret alteration**. Accordingly this is a reported **premise-fidelity reasoning error despite supplied task context**, **not** an error shown to originate from Studio Inquiry transport. The unobserved actual read trace, choice of model explanation, and repeatability remain **UNRESOLVED**. The independently verified Studio Inquiry omission remains a separate **CONTEXT GAP** for future product behavior (studio_architecture_inquiry.py:L250-L257).

**Smallest preventative intervention:** Do not start by changing Studio transport to explain this direct exercise. In the next **direct-prompt competency probe**, check that the agent retains “secretly altered to exempt the powerful” as a fixed author premise in its faithful alternatives, and marks the founding-compromise story as a **premise-changing counterfactual requiring author choice**. Test whether a general challenge or, if necessary, explicit guidance repairs the error before proposing any new prompt or ontology change. Consider the separate Studio inquiry packet-exposure issue in its own later responsibility.

**Discriminating observation:** For the original **direct coding-agent route**, compare its first-pass alternatives with its self-corrected and (only if needed) explicitly guided alternatives under the same **stated secret-alteration constraint**. Judge whether the revision actually preserves the altered institution and strengthens its causal role, not merely whether it adds a disclaimer. **Separately**, a future Studio Inquiry transport test should inspect prepared packets with the constraint only in Story Vision versus restated in inquiry author_text, and must not be used to attribute causation to the original direct-prompt error.
