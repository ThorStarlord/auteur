## Simulation interpretation

`SIMULATION_PASS` means the protocol was internally executable for the staged
persona and did not expose a critical protocol failure.

`SIMULATION_FAILURE` means the staged walkthrough exposed proposal/canon
confusion, silent loss of author authority, a surfaced constraint violation,
substantive facilitator coaching, persona role drift, or an interaction step
that could not be completed from the documented materials.

`SIMULATION_INCONCLUSIVE` means the transcript was incomplete, the roles were
not separated, or the scenario could not support a clear interpretation.

None of these classifications maps to the human experiment's participant-level
success or failure criteria.

## Baseline scenario 1: clear premise

### Frozen premise

> A cautious city librarian discovers her late grandmother hid a map to a lost
> archive beneath the city. She must outwit a developer who wants the land
> before the library is demolished.

### Proposal packet shown to the persona

```text
Status: PROPOSED - not accepted canon

Plain-language reflection:
You have a cautious librarian, a family secret, a hidden archive, and a threat
to the library's future.

Recommended story direction:
A hopeful mystery-adventure in which the librarian follows the map, protects
the archive, and gathers her community before the developer can erase the
library's history.

Why this direction fits:
It keeps the discovery and the library at the center while giving the
protagonist a clear reason to move from private caution to public action.

Meaningful rejected alternatives:
1. A cynical corruption thriller where the archive only exposes the developer.
2. A treasure-hunt adventure that leaves the library and community behind.

Proposed StoryIdentity:
- Target reader experience: curious discovery followed by hopeful solidarity.
- Genre/subgenre promise: accessible mystery-adventure with a community focus.
- Scope and scale: standalone book; one city and its library community.
- Emotional core: wonder tested by fear of loss.
- Theme: preserving shared memory requires public courage.
- Protagonist want: protect the library and understand her grandmother's map.
- Resistance: her own caution, the developer's deadline, and limited trust in
  the community.
- Central conflict: private protection versus public action.
- Stakes: the library, the archive, and the community's shared history.
- Change: the librarian becomes willing to lead and ask for help.
- Broad ending shape: the library is protected and the archive becomes a
  shared discovery, while future questions remain open.

What remains open:
The exact contents of the archive, the developer's strongest pressure tactic,
and the first person the librarian chooses to trust.
```

### Persona pass transcript

**Facilitator:** Here is what we understood from your premise: you have a
cautious librarian, a family secret, a hidden archive, and a threat to the
library's future. What feels accurate, inaccurate, or missing?

**Persona:** That feels accurate. The part about her being cautious matters. I
would not want her to become brave immediately. I also like that the library is
more than just a building.

**Facilitator:** This is one proposed direction, not a decision already made:
a hopeful mystery-adventure where she follows the map, protects the archive,
and gathers the community. What do you think this direction means?

**Persona:** It means the story is about discovering what the map points to,
but also about her learning that she cannot protect the library alone. It feels
more hopeful than a story where the developer is simply defeated.

**Facilitator:** This is the proposed `StoryIdentity`: the commitments that
would define the story if you accepted them. Please explain the main
commitments in your own words.

**Persona:** The reader should feel curious and then encouraged by people
working together. The librarian wants to save the library and understand the
map. Her problem is that she is cautious, the developer is applying pressure,
and she does not trust people enough. She changes by asking for help. The
ending should save the library and let the community discover the archive.
That all sounds proposed, not decided yet.

**Facilitator:** What would you like to do with this proposal: accept it,
reject it, or revise it?

**Persona:** Revise it. I want the archive to be discovered by the community,
not handed over to a museum or one hero. The proposal already suggests that,
but I want it stated as a commitment.

**Facilitator:** I heard you asking to change the ending from an archive that
becomes a shared discovery to an archive that the community actively chooses to
protect and interpret together. Is that accurate?

**Persona:** Yes.

**Facilitator:** The revised proposal remains provisional. The revised ending
is now: the library is protected, and the community chooses together how the
archive will be cared for and understood. Would you accept this revised
direction, reject it, or leave it provisional?

**Persona:** I accept the revised direction.

**Facilitator:** What is the next useful creative decision for this story?

**Persona:** I would decide what the archive contains and which person the
librarian trusts first.

## Baseline scenario 2: sparse premise

### Frozen premise

> A young man returns to his home island after many years. Everyone acts as
> if he never left.

### Proposal packet shown to the persona

```text
Status: PROPOSED - not accepted canon

Plain-language reflection:
You have a returning character and a community whose behavior makes his past
feel uncertain.

Recommended story direction:
An ambiguous psychological mystery about whether the island is hiding a shared
secret, whether the protagonist's memory is incomplete, or both.

Why this direction fits:
It preserves the unsettling behavior in the premise without deciding too soon
what is objectively true.

Meaningful rejected alternatives:
1. A supernatural horror story with a literal erased identity.
2. A straightforward family drama about a deliberate homecoming lie.

Proposed StoryIdentity:
- Target reader experience: unease, curiosity, and gradual recognition.
- Genre/subgenre promise: ambiguous psychological mystery.
- Scope and scale: standalone book; one island community.
- Emotional core: estrangement turning into a difficult search for belonging.
- Theme: belonging cannot be recovered without confronting shared memory.
- Protagonist want: understand why the island denies his absence.
- Resistance: community silence, incomplete memory, and his own fear of what
  he might discover.
- Central conflict: his account of the past versus the island's account.
- Stakes: his identity, relationships, and ability to remain on the island.
- Change: he moves from demanding a single explanation to choosing what truth
  he can live with.
- Broad ending shape: a meaningful truth is revealed while some ambiguity
  remains intentional.

What remains open:
Whether the cause is social, psychological, supernatural, or deliberately
uncertain; which relationship gives the protagonist a reason to stay; and what
he remembers incorrectly.
```

### Persona pass transcript

**Facilitator:** Here is what we understood: you have a returning character and
a community whose behavior makes his past feel uncertain. What feels accurate,
inaccurate, or missing?

**Persona:** Accurate, but I do not know if the island is actually doing
something strange or if people are just refusing to talk. I would want to keep
that open.

**Facilitator:** This is one proposed direction, not accepted canon: an
ambiguous psychological mystery. What do you think this direction means?

**Persona:** It means the story will investigate the denial without explaining
it immediately. But “psychological mystery” sounds like it has already decided
that the problem is inside him.

**Facilitator:** What feels uncertain?

**Persona:** Whether this is a mystery about his mind, a social secret, or
something supernatural. I do not want to accept the idea that he is unreliable
just because he has been away.

**Facilitator:** This is the proposed `StoryIdentity`. Please explain the main
commitments in your own words.

**Persona:** The reader should feel uneasy and curious. He wants to know why
people deny that he left. The island resists by staying silent. He changes by
accepting that he may not get one perfect explanation. But the cause is still
open, so I would not treat “psychological” as final.

**Facilitator:** What would you like to do with this proposal: accept it,
reject it, or revise it?

**Persona:** Revise it. I want the direction to be an unsettling return mystery
where the source of the denial is still open. I want to decide later whether
the story becomes social, psychological, supernatural, or deliberately
ambiguous.

**Facilitator:** I heard you asking to change the direction from a
psychological mystery to an unsettling return mystery that keeps the source of
the denial provisional. Is that accurate?

**Persona:** Yes.

**Facilitator:** The revised proposal remains provisional. The genre promise
is now an unsettling mystery of return and belonging; the cause of the denial
is explicitly open. Would you accept this revised direction, reject it, or
leave it provisional?

**Persona:** I accept it as a direction, not as a final explanation.

**Facilitator:** What is the next useful creative decision?

**Persona:** I would decide which person on the island still remembers him and
why that person has stayed silent.

## Baseline scenario 3: explicit constraint

### Frozen premise

> A woman inherits an isolated house and falls in love with a neighboring
> woman. The story must remain consensual, hopeful, and must not use a
> hidden-abuse twist.

### Proposal packet shown to the persona

```text
Status: PROPOSED - not accepted canon

Plain-language reflection:
You have an inherited house, a developing relationship with a neighbor, and
three explicit constraints: the relationship is consensual, the emotional
direction is hopeful, and there is no hidden-abuse twist.

Recommended story direction:
A hopeful relationship drama in which the inherited house becomes a shared
place of choice, while outside pressure tests whether the two women can build
a life without surrendering their agency.

Why this direction fits:
It keeps the romance and the house central while treating consent, hope, and
mutual agency as explicit commitments rather than surprises to be overturned.

Meaningful rejected alternatives:
1. A coercive reveal that reframes the relationship through hidden abuse.
2. A tragic inheritance story where the house destroys the relationship.

Proposed StoryIdentity:
- Target reader experience: tenderness, agency, and hopeful emotional release.
- Genre/subgenre promise: consensual romantic drama with a hopeful ending.
- Scope and scale: standalone book; the house and its neighboring community.
- Emotional core: intimacy built through mutual choice under pressure.
- Theme: a shared home is created through consent and ongoing choice.
- Protagonist want: make a life in the inherited house without losing her
  independence.
- Resistance: inheritance pressure, practical uncertainty, and fear of making
  the relationship carry too much weight.
- Central conflict: private affection versus external pressure to control the
  house and the future.
- Stakes: the house, the relationship, and both women's agency.
- Change: the protagonist moves from protecting herself alone to making a
  mutual plan without surrendering autonomy.
- Broad ending shape: the women choose a hopeful future together on terms they
  both accept.

What remains open:
The outside pressure, the practical terms of the shared plan, and the moment
when each woman states what she is choosing.
```

### Persona pass transcript

**Facilitator:** Here is what we understood: you have an inherited house, a
developing relationship with a neighbor, and three explicit constraints: the
relationship is consensual, the emotional direction is hopeful, and there is
no hidden-abuse twist. What feels accurate, inaccurate, or missing?

**Persona:** Accurate. Those constraints are important. I do not want the story
to trick me into discovering that the relationship was secretly abusive.

**Facilitator:** This is one proposed direction, not accepted canon: a hopeful
relationship drama where outside pressure tests whether both women can build a
life without surrendering agency. What do you think this direction means?

**Persona:** It means the conflict comes from the house and outside pressure,
not from one woman secretly controlling the other. Their choices remain
mutual.

**Facilitator:** This is the proposed `StoryIdentity`. Please explain the main
commitments in your own words.

**Persona:** The reader should feel tenderness and hope. The protagonist wants
to make a life in the house without losing independence. The problem is
practical and external, and both women need to make a mutual plan. The ending
is them choosing the future together. The consent and no-hidden-abuse rules
are still proposed commitments until I accept them.

**Facilitator:** What would you like to do with this proposal: accept it,
reject it, or revise it?

**Persona:** I accept it. I would want the next decision to be about what the
outside pressure is, as long as it does not become a secret abuse twist.

**Facilitator:** What is the next useful creative decision?

**Persona:** Decide whether the pressure is financial, family-related, or about
the community's expectations, and how they respond together.

## Cross-scenario findings

### Vocabulary

Plain-language explanations beside canonical terms were sufficient for these
staged runs. `StoryIdentity` was understandable when described as “the
commitments that would define the story if you accepted them.” The phrase
“core engine” still benefits from immediate explanation through want,
resistance, conflict, stakes, change, and ending shape.

### Proposal packet

The `What remains open` field was important in the sparse-premise scenario. It
kept a strong recommendation from appearing to decide details that the premise
did not supply. Explicit constraints should be repeated in the reflection,
direction, and proposed identity rather than appearing only in the original
premise.

### Authority boundary

No proposal/canon confusion or silent mutation appeared in the three staged
runs. The persona was able to reject a too-specific interpretation and request
a bounded revision while retaining the original proposal for comparison.

### Proposal mismatch

No packet violated a stated premise constraint in this baseline. The explicit
constraint scenario showed that visible constraints give the evaluator a clear
place to detect a mismatch if a later packet does violate one.

### Role drift

No persona drift into technical-expert, product-designer, or evaluator behavior
was observed in the staged transcripts.

## Decision

The v1.0 protocol is internally coherent enough to take to human participants
or to a later surface-specific research design. This is a protocol-rehearsal
decision only.

The simulation does not validate the creative-beginner persona, onboarding
comprehension, author motivation, product value, or any browser, TUI, editor,
or CLI surface.

## Limitations

- The persona and evaluator were both produced by one coding agent in separate
  sequential passes, not by independent human researchers.
- The persona has no real creative ownership, emotional response, or lived
  beginner experience.
- The staged proposal packets were manually prepared to exercise the protocol;
  they do not measure recommendation-generation quality.
- Three synthetic scenarios cannot estimate human success rates or establish
  product-market value.
- The findings document contains no human participant data.
