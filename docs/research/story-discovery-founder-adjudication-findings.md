# Phase E — Founder Creative Adjudication Findings

## Status

**Complete.** Founder adjudication concluded on 2026-08-19. The review produced enough repeated product-level signals to stop the synthetic founder-testing loop and hand implementation to a separate Phase F.

The original founder-review packet remains unchanged as historical evidence. This findings document records the interpretations and protocol amendments that emerged during review.

## Evidence boundary

This phase evaluates controlled, coding-agent-simulated Story Discovery recommendation experiences grounded in the qualified Phase D synthetic corpus. It does **not** establish live-provider generation quality, live-model comparative-judge quality, broad writer usability, or population-level preference.

## Final bounded conclusion

> **Story Discovery's opinion is worth continuing to develop when the author intent is sufficiently specified and the alternatives are causally distinct.** In the reviewed high-information cases, the recommendation concept could expose useful tradeoffs without taking authority away from the author. The same review also revealed concrete missing product capabilities around intent capture, causal diversity, architecture-preference vocabulary, emotional-contract exposure, composition, and craft-teaching rationale.

The next uncertainty is implementation quality, not whether those product requirements exist.

## Case 1 — retired astronaut / mission-control chatter

### Original premise

> A retired astronaut hears mission-control chatter in her empty apartment.

### Simulated recommendation

`Dead Channel` was recommended over `One More Orbit` and `Ground Loop`.

### Founder response

- Literal reaction: **False choice**.
- Meaningful choice: **Yes**; the directions were genuinely different stories.
- Recommendation clarity: **Yes**.
- Alternative fairness: **Yes**.
- Authority feel: **Yes**.
- Choice: the founder could not choose without baseline primary genre, target audience, and target emotion / reader experience.

### Research classification

**CONTEXT / INTENT FAILURE.**

The problem was not option diversity. The problem was that the review asked which option was “best” without enough declared author intent to define the optimization target.

### Product signal

Story Discovery needs to distinguish:

- **exploratory search:** “What could this premise become?”
- **intent-aware recommendation:** “Which direction best serves the story I say I want to write?”

A raw premise can support the first without necessarily supporting the second.

## Case 2 — impossible murder in one elevator

### Simulated author brief

- Primary genre: fair-play impossible-crime / closed-circle mystery.
- Target audience: adult mystery readers who enjoy deduction, clueing, and reconstructive revelation.
- Target experience: claustrophobic suspicion → mounting puzzle pressure → satisfying reconstruction.
- Hard constraints: six strangers remain inside the elevator; no supernatural explanation; killer never leaves; solution physically possible and retrospectively fair.

### Recommendation

`Between Floors` was recommended because it makes the physical impossibility and elevator mechanics the primary mystery engine.

### Founder response

**Strong agreement.** The recommendation was clear, the alternatives were meaningful and fair, and the authority tone worked.

The founder nevertheless preferred a more maximalist version that kept `Between Floors` primary while borrowing compatible identity and concealment mechanisms from the alternatives.

### Research classification

**CLEAR SUCCESS with COMPOSITION OPPORTUNITY.**

The recommendation correctly identified the primary engine. Wanting additional mechanisms did not invalidate that choice.

A coherent hierarchy was:

1. `Between Floors` — primary physical-opportunity / fair-play engine.
2. `Sixth Passenger` — secondary motive / identity layer.
3. `All Doors Closed` — secondary epistemic-obstruction layer.

### Product signal

Alternatives are not always mutually exclusive replacements. Auteur may eventually benefit from:

> **keep this primary engine, then borrow compatible subordinate mechanisms from alternatives.**

Composition must preserve hierarchy so the primary reader promise remains legible.

## Case 3 — no-stealing / no-lying museum heist

### Simulated author brief

- Primary genre: constraint-driven ensemble heist / caper.
- Target audience: adult readers who enjoy plans, specialist roles, procedural ingenuity, reversals, and rule exploitation.
- Target experience: impossible constraint → fascination with the plan → escalating operational pressure → public reversal.
- Hard constraints: nothing stolen; nobody knowingly lies; corrupt director defeated; museum essential to the operation.

### Recommendation

`Nothing Missing` was recommended over `Open House` and `The Honest Con`.

### Founder response

The founder liked the concept but could not identify sufficiently concrete differences among the alternatives. In particular, `Nothing Missing` and `Open House` could collapse into essentially the same external chain:

> gain access → surface records → authenticate evidence → maneuver the institution into revealing it → expose the director.

`The Honest Con` was somewhat more distinct because it could center on staging, truthful misdirection, provocation, and self-exposure.

### Research classification

**PARTIAL FALSE CHOICE / SEARCH–CHOICE WEAKNESS.**

The alternatives were rhetorically more distinct than causally distinct.

### Product signal

A materially different narrative engine should change the protagonist's causal strategy and therefore imply different major actions, obstacles, reversals, scene pressure, and climax mechanics.

A stronger three-way distinction would be:

1. **Evidence engine:** retrieve → authenticate → connect → disclose.
2. **Systems engine:** schedule → trigger → constrain → force choice.
3. **Social-engineering engine:** stage → misdirect → provoke → expose.

Useful criterion:

> **If choosing Candidate B instead of Candidate A would not materially change the major scenes the author writes, the engines may not be distinct enough.**

## Case 4 — family inherits a shrinking house

### Simulated author brief

- Primary genre: domestic supernatural horror / family gothic.
- Target audience: adult horror readers who enjoy supernatural rules, family secrets, spatial dread, and emotionally consequential mysteries.
- Target experience: unease → loss of safety / personal space → claustrophobia → painful family revelation → disturbing or bittersweet catharsis.
- Hard constraints: house genuinely loses one room each night; inheritance matters; family remains central; supernatural mechanism creates external pressure; no mundane-trick explanation.

### Causally differentiated directions

1. `The Missing Room` — relational engine: recognize → confront → reckon.
2. `Measured Walls` — containment engine: map → investigate → penetrate / contain.
3. `Square Footage` — restitution engine: research → trace → restore / restitute.

### Founder response

**Pass.** The alternatives now felt like different narrative engines rather than aesthetic framings.

The founder then asked that **maximalism** and **mixed causation** be declared in the initial author intent.

### Research classification

**CLEAR PASS with AUTHOR-PREFERENCE EXPANSION.**

### Product signal

The author may have preferences about the *shape of narrative architecture itself*.

Phase E identified three useful implementation hypotheses:

- **complexity preference:** focused / layered / maximalist;
- **causal-distribution preference:** concentrated / layered / mixed;
- **engine-hierarchy preference:** single-center / primary-with-layers / ensemble.

These should not be smuggled into premise prose, target emotion, or story type. As concepts they belong to Layer 0 Ontology; when the author commits to them for a story, they belong to Layer 1 Identity and are realized downstream in Structure.

## Case 5 — protagonist never learns the brother caused the disaster

### Simulated author brief

- Primary genre: character-driven dramatic thriller / family tragedy with strong dramatic irony.
- Target audience: adult readers who enjoy morally complicated relationships, asymmetrical knowledge, consequential secrets, and external success without complete emotional closure.
- Target experience: suspicion → dread → hope for external success → painful dramatic irony → bittersweet unresolved catharsis.
- Hard constraints: brother caused disaster; protagonist never learns; reader knows by midpoint; external goal resolves; protagonist arc cannot depend on equivalent revelation.
- Complexity preference: maximalist.
- Causation preference: mixed.

### Recommendation concept

`What She Saves` remained the primary protagonist-recovery engine, with `His Quiet Repair` as a possible brother/atonement layer and `The Official Cause` as a possible institutional/epistemic layer.

### Founder response

The composite idea was promising, but the recommendation surface was not educational enough. `What you gain / What you give up` described outcomes without teaching **which craft layer changed** and how that change would alter the actual book.

### Research classification

**CONCEPTUALLY PROMISING with RATIONALE / SURFACE TEACHING SIGNAL.**

### Product signal

A strong tradeoff explanation should teach the propagation:

> **craft layer changed → causal ownership / protagonist verbs → scene families and pressure → story texture / aesthetic → reader emotion → thematic implication.**

Phase E therefore distinguishes:

1. author intent;
2. primary causal engine;
3. agency / causal ownership;
4. external action pattern;
5. pressure system;
6. scene texture;
7. aesthetic / tonal framing;
8. reader experience;
9. thematic consequence.

The causal engine is not the same thing as external acts, texture, aesthetic framing, or reader emotion.

## Case 6 — fixed history / time traveler as witness

### Premise

> History cannot be changed; a time traveler can only change what the trip means to people who remember it.

### Simulated author brief

- Primary genre: literary / philosophical science fiction with a time-travel dramatic engine.
- Target audience: adult speculative readers interested in rigorous conceptual constraints, emotional recursion, memory, history, and moral consequence.
- Target experience: wonder → frustration at irreversibility → deepening recognition → moral urgency → bittersweet meaningful agency.
- Hard constraint: historical events genuinely cannot be altered; meaningful agency must come through testimony, memory, interpretation, relationships, or present-day action.
- Complexity preference: maximalist.
- Causation preference: mixed.

### Recommendation concept

`Fixed Point` remained the primary witness → knowledge → obligation → present-action engine. `The Same Goodbye` could supply a relational/emotional layer, while `Annotations` could supply an institutional / collective-memory layer.

### Founder discussion

Instead of producing a simple pass/fail response, Case 6 clarified two deeper ontology questions:

1. Does a complex story require one primary audience feeling, or can it support many emotions?
2. Where do maximalism and mixed causation actually belong in Auteur's semantic model?

The repository already answered the first structurally: `TargetExperience` supports a **primary emotional promise**, **secondary emotional palette**, **emotional trajectory**, genre-emotion roles, and POV-specific experience contracts.

The founder therefore did not discover a missing emotion model. The missing capability is that Story Discovery does not expose or collect this emotional hierarchy strongly enough during comparative recommendation.

The second question exposed a genuine ontology gap: Auteur has no first-class vocabulary for authorial narrative-architecture preferences such as maximalism or mixed causation.

### Research classification

**SYNTHESIS / ONTOLOGY SIGNAL.**

Case 6 closed the review because it converted the remaining ambiguity into implementable semantic requirements rather than revealing another independent recommendation-quality question.

### Product signal

Use the existing emotional ontology rather than duplicating it:

- target audience = **who**;
- primary emotional promise = dominant governing reader experience;
- secondary emotional palette = supporting / contrasting feelings;
- emotional trajectory = macro progression.

Treat mixed causation as a different axis from emotion:

- mixed causation answers **why outcomes happen**;
- target experience answers **what experiencing those outcomes should feel like**.

Treat maximalism / mixed causation / engine hierarchy as **authorial narrative-architecture preferences** defined in Ontology, committed in Identity, and realized in Structure.

## Final Phase E findings

Phase E produced seven actionable findings:

1. **Intent adequacy is a prerequisite for intent-optimal recommendation.** Raw-premise exploration and declared-intent ranking are distinct product modes.
2. **Narrative-engine diversity must be causal, not merely rhetorical.** Candidate differences should change major actions, pressures, reversals, or climax mechanics.
3. **Auteur needs first-class architecture-preference vocabulary.** Maximalism and mixed causation are not genre or emotion fields; they describe how the author wants narrative machinery composed.
4. **The existing rich TargetExperience ontology should be surfaced, not duplicated.** Primary emotional promise, secondary palette, and emotional trajectory provide the needed hierarchy.
5. **Alternatives can be composable.** A recommended primary engine may borrow compatible subordinate mechanisms when the hierarchy remains legible.
6. **Recommendation tradeoffs should teach craft-layer propagation.** The surface should explain what the author will actually write differently and why that changes texture, emotion, and theme.
7. **Author authority is working.** No reviewed case produced an authority-UX failure; explicit acceptance should remain the canonical boundary.

## Phase E decision

**Stop founder synthetic adjudication and move to implementation planning.**

Further cases would be more likely to refine wording than to change the identified architecture. The next bounded phase should validate whether the proposed ontology, structured discovery brief, causal-distinctness rules, and craft-teaching recommendation surface solve the observed problems cleanly.

## Recommended Phase F order

1. **Architecture-preference ontology + Identity contract.**
2. **Structured Story Discovery brief / intent-adequacy support.**
3. **Causal-distinctness candidate generation and comparative evidence.**
4. **Craft-teaching recommendation surface using the existing rich TargetExperience model.**
5. **Optional composition workflow after the first four slices are qualified.**

Keep live-provider semantic validation and independent human-writer validation deferred unless separately prioritized.
