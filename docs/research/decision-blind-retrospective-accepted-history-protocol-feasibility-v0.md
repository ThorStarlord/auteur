## Status

The protocol is not admissible for the intended later same-author long-horizon
reconstruction value test. Honest retrospective acceptance requires the author
to revisit the history that the later test is meant to determine whether Auteur
can reconstruct for them. No reviewed control restores the pre-ratification
working-memory baseline.

The result does not say that a frozen external corpus is useless for every
possible system measurement. It separates a narrower, source-scored
system-output question from this responsibility. That narrower question gives
up the same-author product-value claim and is not authorized here.

## Research question

> Can a provenance-preserving protocol retrospectively establish Auteur
> accepted history from an existing corpus while remaining blind to the future
> decision being tested and without introducing enough conversion or
> ratification contamination to invalidate the later independent reasoning
> experiment?

For this campaign, the relevant later experiment includes whether Auteur adds
long-horizon reconstruction value for the author whose narrative history is at
issue. A system-only score from a different evaluator is a different, narrower
question.

## Protocol goal

A hypothetical later protocol would need to show all of the following before a
separate owner could consider a fixture:

1. Freeze the corpus SHA and a complete source-unit inventory before a future
   decision is selected or disclosed.
2. Freeze one mapping and coverage rule before processing.
3. Keep converter, researcher, and ratifier blind to the later decision.
4. Preserve a claim-level source crosswalk, omissions, unmappable material,
   correction history, and acceptance history.
5. Distinguish literal source statements, interpretation, author correction,
   and accepted present-time author declaration.
6. Keep later system reasoning in a fresh context containing only the frozen
   accepted artifacts and the decision packet.
7. Preserve an unprimed author baseline if the later claim concerns the
   author's long-horizon reconstruction value.

The seventh condition fails. The first six describe controls that might make a
different system-only study auditable. They do not make this intended
author-value study admissible.

## Decision-shaped conversion risk

Conversion can leak a later decision before the system ever reasons over the
fixture:

```text
converter sees decision
-> selects source material, fact boundaries, relationships, or omissions
-> accepted fixture already carries answer-shaped history
```

| Risk | Necessary future control | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The exact future decision reaches a converter, researcher, or ratifier. | Select the decision only after fixture freeze; use separate roles, immutable access logs, and a frozen decision-selection record. | The author's broad future concerns can still affect present corrections even when the exact decision is unknown. |
| Source selection makes relevance look neutral. | Freeze corpus SHA, source-unit inventory, and one mechanically enumerable census before mapping. | A census can be complete within its boundary while the boundary itself remains a research choice. |
| Mapping rules privilege useful facts or relations. | Freeze rules and versions before any source processing; prohibit rule changes during a case. | Frozen interpretation is still interpretation. |
| Omission hides material that would weaken a conclusion. | Account for every source unit as mapped, omitted with reason, or unmappable. | An omission ledger exposes a choice. It does not prove fact segmentation was faithful. |
| A later reasoning agent receives raw corpus or conversion notes. | Run later reasoning in a fresh execution context containing only the accepted fixture and decision packet. | This protects system-context isolation, not author-memory contamination. |

Decision blindness is potentially controllable for a frozen system-only test.
It does not repair author-ratification priming, and it cannot turn semantic
interpretation into neutral transcription.

## Author-ratification priming risk

The author must explicitly accept canonical history. A retrospective review
therefore places old narrative material back into the author's working context
immediately before the later test. That changes the thing the test means to
measure. The author already has historical knowledge, but a reconstruction test
depends on the difference between diffuse memory and a fresh, organized review
of the history.

| Scenario | Authority and honest accepted history | Memory and relevance effect | Owner baseline and native equivalence | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Author reviews every reconstructed fact | Preserves authority and can honestly accept the reviewed claims. | Maximum refresh of facts, chronology, and possible connections. | Removes an unprimed same-author baseline. Not equivalent to naturally accumulated native history. | Fails the intended test. |
| Author reviews only disputed facts | Preserves authority only for disputed claims. Unreviewed claims cannot honestly become fully accepted. | Disputed items still focus attention; silent acceptance launders unreviewed semantics. | Leaves both incomplete authority and priming. | Fails. |
| Author reviews source-linked batches | Can preserve acceptance if all claims are reviewed. | Batching improves audit but refreshes history batch by batch. | Changes working context before the later decision. | Fails. |
| Author approves structural mappings instead of individual semantics | May accept a coarse organization, not the detailed fact semantics. | Reveals grouping and interpretation even when prose detail is reduced. | No honest full accepted-history claim and no unprimed baseline. | Fails. |
| Independent reviewer prepares the fixture before author ratification | Reviewer has no author authority. | Preparation alone does not prime the author, but the later required review does. | Does not solve the author baseline. | Does not solve the failure. |
| Delayed decision selection | Blocks knowledge of a named decision during mapping. | Does not undo broad history refresh. | The later decision may be unknown, yet the author is already reoriented. | Controls only decision leakage. |
| Cooling-off after ratification | Acceptance remains honest. | No evidence shows a delay restores the prior memory state. | A washout assertion would itself be an unsupported research claim. | Not a credible control. |
| Different evaluator | The author may still accept the fixture. | The evaluator can be unprimed. | It tests another person's system-output judgment, not the author's product-value baseline. | Different question only. |
| Partial acceptance | Honest only for the reviewed subset. | Reviewed material remains priming; unreviewed coverage weakens. | Cannot stand in for whole-story accepted history. | Fails for this objective. |
| Candidate history without canonical ratification | Avoids false claims of canonical authority. | Can avoid author review. | Cannot honestly test accepted-history behavior and is not equivalent to native canon. | Fails the accepted-history premise. |

No scenario preserves both explicit author authority and a credible unprimed
same-author baseline. This is a fatal limitation for the stated product-value
question, not a minor caveat.

## Source and provenance controls

A future research ledger would need a claim-level crosswalk. It would not be a
new Auteur artifact schema or ontology. At minimum it would record:

| Ledger element | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Frozen corpus SHA and stable source-unit ID | Makes the input and inventory repeatable. |
| Exact source range | Lets a reviewer inspect the basis for a mapped claim. |
| Destination artifact, revision, field or fact pointer | Connects a source claim to the accepted result. |
| Mapping actor and frozen rule version | Separates a conversion choice from source material. |
| Literal-source versus interpretive claim class | Prevents interpretation from posing as direct text. |
| One-to-many and many-to-one mapping record | Makes segmentation and aggregation inspectable. |
| Author correction delta and explicit acceptance record | Shows what the author changed and accepted at present time. |
| Omitted or unmappable outcome and reason | Keeps missing coverage visible. |
| Reconciliation totals | Shows whether every frozen source unit has an account. |

Existing Auteur provenance records accepted artifact IDs, revisions, hashes,
acceptance actor, rationale, and dependencies. Its current reference models do
not make arbitrary external prose spans a native accepted-artifact field. A
separate research ledger could improve auditability, but this study did not and
cannot demonstrate that it produces faithful conversion. It cannot resolve
ratification priming.

## Coverage controls

Fixture size is not a quality metric. Too little material can encode relevance
through omission. Too much selectively organized material can make the answer
easier before the test begins.

| Inclusion rule class | Decision-blindness assessment | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete predefined artifact categories | Weak unless category membership is mechanically enumerable from the frozen corpus. | Classifying a source as a category can itself require semantic judgment. |
| Chronological coverage | Potentially blind if both boundaries are frozen before the decision. | Boundary choices can exclude formative or later relevant material. |
| Scope-bounded coverage | Potentially blind when scope is literally enumerable. | Narrative scope often relies on interpretation. |
| Structural coverage | Not safely neutral. | Structural selection can privilege the shape likely to matter. |
| Random sampling | Avoids targeted selection. | Supports only a sampled-history claim and may omit the history needed for a decision. |

The critic's necessary tightening is adopted: a future system-only protocol
would need one fixed, mechanically enumerable census within frozen source
boundaries. It must not try several inclusion rules against the same case after
learning what looks useful. This is a necessary condition for a different
study, not an authorization to create a census or a fixture.

## Authority model

The only honest authority sequence considered was:

```text
frozen source corpus
-> source-linked candidate records
-> author review and correction
-> explicit acceptance at an existing owning artifact
-> retrospective accepted-history fixture
```

| Stage | Authority | Required distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source corpus | External source evidence | Source date and prose content do not create Auteur canon. |
| Candidate record | Candidate only | Mapping actor, source evidence, and interpretation remain visible. |
| Author correction | Present-time author action | A correction is not proof that the reconstructed claim existed as a prior accepted commitment. |
| Explicit acceptance | Canonical only at the named owning artifact and revision | Acceptance can make a present record canonical. It cannot recreate contemporaneous historical acceptance. |
| Derived Map or Focus | Derived | It cannot promote a mapped row or an interpretation to canon. |

Disputed material must remain candidate, rejected, or explicitly unresolved.
Partial or structural approval cannot silently canonize detailed semantics.
Confidence can rank an interpretation. It cannot change its authority.

## Interpretation boundary

Retrospective prose conversion is not semantically neutral. It chooses units,
identity, events, state, scope, relationships, omission boundaries, and
interpretive claims. The required distinction is:

| Claim kind | Permitted treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Literal source statement | Source-linked evidence, never mistaken for present canon until explicitly accepted. |
| Mapping or segmentation choice | Candidate conversion record with source basis and rule version. |
| Interpretive relation or narrative meaning | Candidate or derived evidence with its origin visible. It never becomes canon through confidence. |
| Author correction or declaration | Present-time author action through an existing acceptance boundary. |

No new relation type, lifecycle state, or ontology concept is proposed. If the
existing model cannot express material without inventing semantics, the record
must say `UNMAPPABLE` rather than solve the gap.

## Independent methodology critic

One fresh methodology critic received the research question, current authority
model, proposed decision-blind controls, coverage proposal, ratification
scenarios, claim ceiling, and retrospective-versus-native comparison. It was
not asked for a particular outcome.

**Critic disposition:** `REQUIRES_REVISION`

The critic agreed that ratification priming is fatal for same-author value
evidence. It also found that the provisional statement was too broad if read as
rejecting every possible system-only measurement. Its material objections were:

1. An exact future decision must be selected only after fixture freeze, but the
   author's broad future concerns remain a residual source of hindsight.
2. One mechanically enumerable census is stronger than trying complete
   categories, scope, structural coverage, and random sampling in one case.
3. The provenance ledger needs stable source IDs, destination pointers, rule
   version, revision and hash, one-to-many accounting, correction deltas, and
   reconciled totals.
4. Source-linked interpretation cannot pose as literal fact. Semantic
   acceptance must be explicit, present-time, and claim-specific.
5. A fresh reasoning context, independent post-freeze decision selection,
   source-grounded scoring, decision-swapped controls, abstention cases,
   irrelevant accepted distractors, and a flat-history control are necessary
   for any separate system-only question.
6. Converter quality can dominate the result. Holding the frozen fixture
   constant can limit, but not erase, that confound.
7. Narrative time, source time, mapping time, and acceptance time must remain
   separately labelled.
8. No retrospective control reaches prospective native lifecycle evidence.

## Final admissibility disposition

`RETROSPECTIVE_FIXTURE_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`

The route fails because the author must either review enough reconstructed
history to make acceptance honest, which primes the later same-author value
test, or review too little to create honest accepted history. Delaying the
decision, changing the evaluator, or waiting for a cooling-off period either
does not remove the priming or changes the claim into a different study.

Decision-shaped conversion can be constrained, and provenance/coverage can be
made more auditable. Those controls do not repair the author-memory
counterfactual. The fatal objection remains unresolved.

## Claim ceiling

This negative result authorizes no research fixture and no later experiment
under this protocol. The only bounded analytic conclusion is:

> A retrospective external corpus cannot support the campaign's intended
> same-author long-horizon reconstruction value claim after author ratification.

The conditional system-only cells in the matrix are not an exception. They
describe a different question that would require material new evidence and a
separate owner decision. They establish neither product value nor native
history equivalence.

## Campaign implications

Retire retrospective external-corpus fixture work from this campaign unless
materially new evidence establishes a credible non-priming method for the
intended author-value claim. Do not spend another cycle trying to make a
retrospective fixture look native through stricter conversion paperwork alone.

If independent product-value validation remains important at the next campaign
reassessment, the clean route is a prospectively accumulated Auteur-native case:
the author accepts material when it arises, revisions accrue in normal use, and
the later decision is unknown at the time the relevant history is accepted.
This record does not authorize or begin that work.
