## Results

### D1 — Intent adequacy: retired astronaut

Criterion: raw premise remains exploratory; an under-specified structured brief is not rankable as declared-intent recommendation; an adequate brief carries genre, audience, and target experience as prior author intent.

**Result: PASS.**

The controlled regression distinguishes an under-specified `DiscoveryBrief` from an adequate one. This preserves the Phase E distinction:

- exploratory: **What could this premise become?**
- intent-aware: **Which direction best serves the story I say I want to write?**

The implementation does not need to invent candidate-generated taste to make an inadequate brief appear rankable.

### D2 — Causal false choice: constrained museum heist

Criterion: evidence, systems, and truthful social-engineering engines imply materially different action chains, pressures, reversals, climax mechanics, and scene families; rhetorically different but causally collapsed alternatives do not receive a fake winner.

**Result: PASS.**

Controlled profiles for:

1. retrieve → authenticate → connect → disclose;
2. schedule → trigger → constrain → force choice;
3. stage → frame truthfully → provoke → expose;

qualify when the bounded semantic assessor classifies their causal mechanics as `distinct`. The same candidate set becomes `not_adjudicable_near_duplicate` when the pairwise evidence says the major causal machinery remains substantially the same.

This directly addresses the Phase E Case 3 false-choice failure. F3 remains categorical and uncertain rather than presenting a numeric creativity/diversity score as truth.

### D3 — Maximalist mixed causation: shrinking inherited house

Criterion: maximalism and mixed causation remain authorial architecture preferences; one primary engine stays legible while compatible subordinate mechanisms may enrich it under a `primary_with_layers` hierarchy.

**Result: PASS.**

The F4 evidence request keeps:

- `complexity=maximalist`;
- `causal_distribution=mixed`;
- `engine_hierarchy=primary_with_layers`;

inside declared architecture intent. The craft explainer explicitly treats architecture preferences as distinct from emotions and retains the requirement that `primary_with_layers` still have one governing engine.

The result supports a dense interacting architecture without turning maximalism into “combine everything.”

### D4 — Craft teaching: protagonist never learns brother caused disaster

Criterion: alternative explanation identifies the craft layer touched, causal ownership shift, actions/scenes added, pressure/texture change, reader-experience change, thematic consequence, gain, narrative-weight give-up, composability, and primary displacement risk.

**Result: PASS.**

The controlled `His Quiet Repair` comparison makes the consequences concrete:

- more `conceal → intervene → compensate → sacrifice` activity;
- causal weight moves toward the brother;
- hidden-intervention / near-discovery scene families increase;
- dramatic-ironic pressure intensifies;
- the governing reader promise can remain intact while the supporting emotional palette is reweighted;
- the gain is stronger hidden causal pressure / moral complexity;
- the give-up is some protagonist causal ownership;
- composability can be `compatible_as_secondary`;
- the explicit risk is that the brother becomes the effective protagonist.

This addresses the Phase E Case 5 surface-teaching signal: `What you gain / What you give up` is now tied to an actual craft-layer propagation rather than generic product-comparison prose.

### D5 — Emotional hierarchy: fixed history / time-traveler witness

Criterion: target audience, governing emotional promise, supporting emotional palette, emotional trajectory, causal architecture, and architecture preferences remain distinct concepts. Multiple emotions are not treated as confusion merely because several are present.

**Result: PASS.**

The controlled surface preserves:

- governing reader promise: **bittersweet meaningful agency**;
- supporting palette: **wonder, grief, moral urgency**;
- trajectory: **wonder → frustration → recognition → bittersweet agency**.

An institutional/public-memory alternative can reweight the secondary palette and pressure system while preserving the governing promise. This demonstrates the intended rule:

> Many emotions can coexist when their hierarchy and trajectory remain legible; mixed causation does not require mixed or ungoverned emotional promises.

## Cross-case result

Under controlled Phase F regression:

- intent adequacy remains explicit rather than inferred;
- false causal choices can block recommendation instead of being cosmetically ranked;
- maximalism/mixed causation remain architecture preferences rather than target emotions;
- `primary_with_layers` preserves a legible governing engine;
- F4 can teach downstream action, scene, texture, emotion, and thematic consequences;
- rich emotional palettes remain subordinate to a governing reader promise rather than being treated as automatic confusion;
- no tested behavior changes canonical Story Identity before explicit acceptance.

The controlled gate therefore supports the first-wave Phase F claim:

> **Auteur can use declared author intent to compare causally distinct Story Discovery architectures, refuse false-choice recommendation when alternatives collapse into the same causal strategy, and explain how a selected engine changes external action, scene pressure, reader experience, and thematic emphasis without taking canonical authority away from the author.**

This is still a controlled product-mechanics claim, not a live-provider or population-level usability claim.

## F5 composition decision

**GO — implement the bounded candidate-only composition workflow.**

The GO is based on two separate evidence sources that should not be conflated:

### Prior human signal from Phase E

Phase E Case 2 was a **clear success with composition opportunity**: the founder preferred `Between Floors` as the primary fair-play engine while borrowing compatible identity/motive and concealment mechanisms from the alternatives.

Phase E Case 4 then explicitly added **maximalism** and **mixed causation** to author intent, with one primary engine and compatible secondary causal mechanisms.

Phase E Case 5 again found the composite architecture promising, while identifying the missing craft-teaching surface that F4 now supplies.

This is the human preference evidence. No new human preference was manufactured in this Phase F gate.

### Phase F mechanical qualification

F1–F4 now provide the machinery composition needs to fail closed:

- explicit architecture preferences;
- adequate prior author intent when the author supplies it;
- F3 causal profiles and a causally qualified source set;
- F4 categorical composability and displacement-risk evidence;
- a legible primary engine / subordinate-layer hierarchy;
- explicit author-facing craft consequences.

D3 and D4 specifically show that `maximalist + mixed + primary_with_layers` and `compatible_as_secondary` can be represented without flattening all engines into equal weight.

## F5 implementation boundary

The GO authorizes only the bounded operation:

> **Keep this recommended primary engine and borrow these explicitly selected compatible subordinate mechanisms.**

F5 must:

1. operate only on an existing F3-qualified Story Discovery run;
2. require complete F4 craft analysis;
3. accept only explicitly selected `compatible_as_secondary` mechanisms in v1;
4. preserve primary story type, governing target-experience promise, architecture preferences, and hard constraints;
5. generate a **new candidate**, never canonical state;
6. re-profile the composed candidate through F3 causality;
7. separately assess whether the primary engine remains governing;
8. fail closed on `primary_displaced` or `uncertain` hierarchy;
9. leave source recommendation and source candidate files unchanged;
10. require ordinary explicit `story-discovery accept` for canonical promotion.

F5 does **not** authorize automatic “combine everything,” automatic acceptance, or a general-purpose story-merging operation.
