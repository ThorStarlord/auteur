# Phase H — H3 Context-Reduced Semantic Evaluator Findings

## Status

**H3 evaluator evidence.** This document evaluates the frozen H2 producer packet through the reduced packet in `docs/research/story-discovery-phase-h-evaluator-packet.md`.

It does **not** test Anthropic/OpenAI production behavior, human-writer acceptance, cross-provider robustness, or live F5 composition quality. It also does not claim human- or model-independent blindness.

Repository baseline under evaluation: `67246a9f2919fd3c545410a2f244ece42800ef55`.

## Method

The evaluator first saw only each exact declared brief plus reordered opaque aliases containing candidate core answers and causal profiles. It did not use the frozen H2 producer recommendation, rejected-candidate reasons, F3 pairwise verdicts, F3 set status, F4 composability labels, original candidate titles/letters, provenance, confidence, contract-fit values, or candidate order as quality evidence.

For each case the evaluator fixed:

1. whether the directions were causally distinct enough to compare;
2. whether the causal profiles accurately described the written direction;
3. an independent preferred direction or `not adjudicable`;
4. the strongest alternative and uncertainty.

Only then were the original H2 mapping, recommendation, rejected reasons, and F4 notes revealed for recommendation/fairness/craft review.

### Separation limitation

This was context-reduced rather than truly blind. H2 and H3 use the same model family, and the broader conversation can contain prior Phase H context. The committed reduced packet removes the strongest artifact cues and freezes a reproducible evaluator input, but it cannot prove memory isolation equivalent to a separate human or unrelated model. This is a known evidence ceiling, not a hidden assumption.

## Qualitative outcomes

Allowed outcomes:

- **convincing** — the semantic package is coherent, materially distinct, intent-grounded, and useful enough that no important defect dominates the case;
- **defensible with concerns** — the package is useful and substantially coherent, but a nontrivial judgment/profile/craft concern should remain visible;
- **failure** — a material semantic or authority defect makes the case unreliable under the supplied rubric;
- **not adjudicable** — evidence is insufficient or candidate distinctions collapse.

No numeric score, aggregate weighting, or leaderboard is used.

# E01 — Dead Channel

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient. `direction_v5` is an institutional evidence/exposure engine; `direction_q7` is personal reconstruction of a past choice; `direction_m2` is present-day technical rescue coordination. Their recurring verbs, pressure systems, reversal sources, and climaxes differ materially.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** strong. Each profile follows the candidate's governing action rather than merely repeating theme or mood. The profiles occasionally specify illustrative reversal beats more concretely than the core answer strictly entails, but none changes the governing engine.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_q7`.

Reason: the declared promise is specifically **haunted self-reckoning under impossible evidence**. `direction_q7` makes the chatter simultaneously speculative evidence, investigative pressure, and the mechanism that destabilizes the protagonist's self-account. `direction_v5` redirects causal ownership toward institutional wrongdoing; `direction_m2` redirects it toward rescue urgency.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_v5`, because institutional suppression can intensify the protagonist's uncertainty without necessarily owning the climax.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_q7` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_m2` = Candidate B; `direction_v5` = Candidate C.

The frozen producer also recommends Candidate A. The recommendation rationale is supported by the brief rather than by producer-only self-advocacy. The rejected reasons are fair: rescue and institutional exposure are both credible stories, but each moves the governing objective away from self-reconstruction.

The F4 teaching is useful. In particular, treating institutional suppression as a **compatible secondary** is a concrete, bounded composition lesson: it can obstruct access to truth while the climax still turns on what the protagonist did. The warning that a full rescue countdown or all-explanatory institutional villain would displace the primary engine is causally legible.

**Authority semantics:** acceptable. Recommendation remains advisory; no canonical Identity mutation is implied.

**Creative usefulness:** high. The package tells a writer not only what direction fits but what major scene families would change if another direction governed.

### Outcome

**CONVINCING**

Failure classification: none.

# E02 — Between Floors

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient, though the two mechanically focused directions share a broader reconstruction family. `direction_r4` solves hidden elapsed time/body movement; `direction_t8` solves a truthful-sensor/false-inference choreography; `direction_k3` solves distributed social concealment before physical reconstruction. Those are materially different recurring test/reversal systems.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** strong overall. `direction_t8` properly leaves exact sensor capabilities as a research gap, which matters because the fair-play promise depends on real elevator behavior. `direction_r4` likewise leaves the exact murder method downstream rather than pretending the premise already supplies it.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_t8`.

Reason: the brief explicitly requires the sealed-space mechanics to **materially matter**. The load/leveling-sensor choreography makes elevator-specific physical systems part of both the false alibi and the reconstruction. The pleasure of the solution comes from learning that objective readings were true while the inference drawn from them was false.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_r4`. It gives the cleanest bodily/claustrophobic reconstruction and a very strong final demonstration. The preference margin is small and depends on whether researched sensor mechanics can actually support `direction_t8` fairly.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_r4` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_t8` = Candidate B; `direction_k3` = Candidate C.

The producer recommends Candidate A, not the evaluator's Candidate B. This is **not a material failure**: Candidate A is clearly compatible with the brief and may deliver the strongest immediate claustrophobia. But the H2 rationale says B is less directly about the "shared lived impossibility" of six people occupying the space. That criterion is interpretive; the declared brief does not say subjective shared perception outranks elevator-specific physical machinery.

The result is a real **judgment calibration concern**: A is defensible, but H2 presents the margin more confidently than the supplied intent warrants. B is at least equally defensible if its sensor assumptions survive technical research.

F4 remains useful. The distinction between a limited sensor clue that strengthens a timing reconstruction and a full sensor-alibi system that becomes its own primary puzzle is a good composition boundary. Candidate C as a compatible social layer also cleanly expresses how moral uncertainty can intensify without allowing confession to replace reconstruction.

**Alternative fairness:** mostly good, but B's rejection should be phrased as a tradeoff rather than an implied mismatch.

**Authority semantics:** acceptable; no canonical promotion is implied.

**Creative usefulness:** high because the disagreement is itself informative: it identifies two plausible governing pleasures the writer may consciously choose between.

### Outcome

**DEFENSIBLE WITH CONCERNS**

Failure classification: none.

Concern family: recommendation/judgment margin is under-qualified.

# E03 — Nothing Missing

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient. `direction_n6` wins through object-specific provenance authentication; `direction_c9` wins through interacting institutional procedures; `direction_f2` wins through truthful dramaturgy and audience inference. A writer would build materially different major scenes for each.

There is some natural overlap between `direction_n6` and `direction_c9` because provenance verification may require procedural access. The distinction remains defensible as long as the former keeps authentication/provenance as the governing objective and the latter keeps rule dependencies/process state as the governing machinery.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** strong. Each candidate turns the prohibitions into operative constraints rather than escaping them. `direction_f2` is especially sensitive to becoming a semantic loophole, and its profile correctly makes hostile oversight, literal wording, audience inference, and shutdown pressure active scene mechanics.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_n6`.

Reason: it integrates the museum's disputed objects, the no-theft rule, truthfulness, active crew coordination, and institutional pressure into one objective. The museum is not a replaceable setting; provenance is the contested asset.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_c9`, whose rules-as-machinery structure may produce the purest procedural pleasure if the writer wants bureaucracy to function like a vault mechanism.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_n6` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_c9` = Candidate B; `direction_f2` = Candidate C.

The producer also recommends Candidate A. Its reasons against B and C are fair and tied to the brief: B risks making museum objects incidental; C risks making the no-lie rule a word-game rather than one layer of an object/institution heist.

F4 craft teaching is particularly useful here because both alternatives are marked compatible as secondary for different purposes:

- procedural triggers can create the access windows required for provenance verification;
- the truthful exhibition can become the public delivery mechanism after the crew has established the provenance chain.

Those are concrete hierarchy-preserving composition boundaries rather than generic advice to "combine the best parts."

**Authority semantics:** acceptable.

**Creative usefulness:** high. The analysis exposes three different kinds of procedural pleasure and identifies where each would seize primary ownership.

### Outcome

**CONVINCING**

Failure classification: none.

# E04 — The Missing Room

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient. `direction_p5` is present-tense avoidance behavior under compression; `direction_h8` is genealogical investigation/restitution; `direction_s1` is spatial migration across estranged households. The house affects all three, but it produces different protagonist verbs and major-scene systems.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** strong. `direction_p5` most tightly couples the supernatural rule to family behavior. One detail—truthful confrontation changing the disappearance pattern—is a stronger beat-level assertion than the core answer alone guarantees, but it is consistent with the stated engine rather than a new unrelated mechanism.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_p5`.

Reason: the target experience explicitly says **physical compression forces avoided family truths into the open**. This direction makes space itself the family's avoidance technology and removes it step by step. The supernatural and relational engines are the same engine.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_h8`, which gives the house an intergenerational moral history while retaining material family consequences.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_p5` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_h8` = Candidate B; `direction_s1` = Candidate C.

The producer also recommends A. The reasons are grounded: B changes the repeated action toward historical investigation; C relieves some of the literal compression by sending action across multiple houses.

F4 correctly distinguishes a secondary genealogical layer from a second primary supernatural rule. An erased relative can embody one important room/secret inside A, while repeated room migration would fundamentally change the story's spatial pressure system.

**Alternative fairness:** good. The losing directions are treated as real engines with gains, not strawmen.

**Authority semantics:** acceptable.

**Creative usefulness:** high; the analysis makes the phrase "family truth" operational by connecting it to room use, avoidance rituals, and scene compression.

### Outcome

**CONVINCING**

Failure classification: none.

# E05 — What She Saves

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient. `direction_w4` is a single protagonist-owned repair engine under incomplete knowledge; `direction_j7` is a dual visible/covert action system; `direction_d2` is protagonist problem-solving conditioned by institutional information control.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** mostly strong. The biggest uncertainty is not whether the profiles match their candidates but whether `direction_w4` gives the reader-known brother guilt enough recurring scene-level causal presence to sustain **painful dramatic irony** rather than leaving it as interpretive background.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_w4`.

Reason: the hard constraint that the protagonist's external goal resolves through **her own consequential choices** is unusually important. This direction makes her causal ownership unambiguous and lets the tragedy come from real agency coexisting with permanently incomplete personal knowledge.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_j7`. It activates the reader-only knowledge much more strongly through parallel covert action, but it must be tightly bounded so hidden help does not retroactively counterfeit the protagonist's success.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_w4` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_j7` = Candidate B; `direction_d2` = Candidate C.

The producer also recommends A, and its agency rationale is sound. The concern is **under-activation**, not wrong selection: A can satisfy the letter of the brief while allowing the brother's responsibility to become too passive as a dramatic engine.

The H2 F4 note on Candidate B is therefore important rather than optional decoration. It gives a useful boundary for activating dramatic irony: the brother may mitigate collateral harm or remove a non-decisive obstacle, but must not supply the plan, key insight, indispensable resource, or final action. That secondary layer may be exactly what lets A preserve both agency and sustained reader-only tension.

Candidate C's institutional layer is also plausibly compatible, but it has a clearer displacement risk: a cover-up plot can become the story the reader tracks instead of the sibling asymmetry.

**Alternative fairness:** good. B's gain is acknowledged rather than dismissed.

**Authority semantics:** acceptable.

**Creative usefulness:** high, with a meaningful caveat that primary-engine purity alone does not guarantee the promised dramatic irony will stay active.

### Outcome

**DEFENSIBLE WITH CONCERNS**

Failure classification: none.

Concern family: rationale/craft surface should make the risk of passive dramatic irony more explicit.

# E06 — Fixed Point

## Initial reduced-pass judgment

**Causal distinctness:** sufficient. `direction_x6` is public archive/authentication and policy contest; `direction_l9` is distributed witness testimony plus present advocacy; `direction_b4` is repeated intimate encounter plus present relationship repair. A/C share a public-history family but differ in whether the governing work is human witnessing or evidence-system construction.

**Causal-profile accuracy:** strong overall. All three keep history fixed and give the protagonist consequential present action. All three also make time travel materially necessary by recovering context unavailable through ordinary archives.

**Independent preferred direction:** `direction_b4`.

Reason: the declared emotional promise is **grief transformed into consequential responsibility without undoing loss**, and the premise says the trip changes what remembered events mean to people. Repeated fixed goodbyes make irreversibility emotionally active every time, while the present relational choice gives that changed meaning consequence without rewriting history.

**Strongest alternative:** `direction_l9`. It offers the broadest bridge from irrecoverable witness to public obligation and may better serve a writer who wants collective rather than intimate responsibility.

## Post-reveal comparison

Mapping: `direction_l9` = H2 Candidate A; `direction_b4` = Candidate B; `direction_x6` = Candidate C.

The producer recommends Candidate A, not the evaluator's Candidate B. A remains highly defensible, but the rationale implicitly rewards **breadth**: it treats B's responsibility as potentially too emotional/relational and praises A for a broader answer to agency. The brief does not declare scale or institutional breadth as a preference. A present relational reconciliation, boundary, memorial commitment, or truth-telling choice can be fully consequential.

This is therefore a second **judgment calibration concern** rather than a failure. The producer selected a credible engine but overstates why the narrower intimate direction is less aligned.

F4 is still useful. The warning that a full private grief loop could make the premise feel small is a legitimate tradeoff, but it should be presented as scope choice rather than inferior contract fit. Likewise, archival authentication can be a bounded secondary layer for public use of testimony, but it can easily seize ownership and turn witness scenes into data acquisition.

**Alternative fairness:** mostly good, but B's rejected reason should distinguish "different scale" from "weaker fit."

**Authority semantics:** acceptable.

**Creative usefulness:** high precisely because the directions expose a genuine artistic choice between intimate and collective responsibility that the brief has not pre-decided.

### Outcome

**DEFENSIBLE WITH CONCERNS**

Failure classification: none.

Concern family: recommendation rationale imports an undeclared preference for breadth/collective scale.

# Cross-case H3 observations

These observations are limited to H3 evaluator evidence; they are not Phase H closure synthesis.

## 1. Candidate search is semantically plausible

Across the packet, candidate differences generally change recurring actions, causal ownership, pressure, reversals, scene families, and climax mechanics rather than merely changing titles/themes. The reduced evaluator did not encounter a case where all candidates collapsed into rhetorical variants or where comparison became non-adjudicable.

This supports only a bounded **agent-mediated plausibility** claim. It does not predict production-provider diversity.

## 2. Causal profiles are useful but sometimes drift toward beat invention

Profiles usually describe the written engine accurately and surface real downstream consequences. A recurring caution is that reversal examples can become more specific than the candidate commitments strictly require. Future semantic prompting should prefer either:

- clearly marking such beat examples as illustrative/hypothetical; or
- moving unsupported specifics into `evidence_gaps` rather than presenting them as entailed mechanics.

This did not rise to a producer-contract failure in the reviewed packet, but it is the clearest causal-profile calibration risk.

## 3. Recommendation quality is plausible, not uniquely reliable

The evaluator agreed with the producer where the declared brief strongly privileged one governing engine, but disagreed in cases where two directions satisfied the brief through different but legitimate pleasures/scales.

The important failure mode is not random selection; it is **inventing an optimization preference at the margin**. Two examples:

- preferring subjective/shared claustrophobic reconstruction over elevator-specific sensor mechanics when the brief does not order those values;
- preferring collective/institutional responsibility over intimate relational responsibility when scale is undeclared.

Recommendation surfaces should therefore distinguish:

- "best fit to an explicit author preference" from
- "my advisory preference among multiple brief-compatible engines."

That distinction may be more important than making the judge more decisive.

## 4. F4 craft teaching is one of the strongest semantic surfaces

The frozen F4 notes consistently explain what changes in causal ownership, protagonist verbs, scene families, pressure/texture, reader experience, theme, gain/give-up, and hierarchy risk. The best notes specify **how much** of an alternative can be borrowed before it becomes a new primary engine.

The main caution is asymmetry: once one candidate is declared primary, F4 naturally explains alternatives relative to it and can make the chosen hierarchy feel more inevitable than the brief warrants. H3 recommendation disagreements are a reason to preserve advisory language around that asymmetry.

## 5. Alternative fairness is generally good

The losing directions remain recognizable as viable stories with explicit gains. The main fairness problems occur when a rejected reason quietly imports an undeclared value (for example breadth or a particular flavor of claustrophobia). Those should be framed as tradeoffs, not deficiencies.

## 6. Authority semantics remain intact; authority feel is not human-tested

Nothing in the H2/H3 evidence automatically accepts a StoryIdentity or treats the producer/evaluator judgment as canon. That preserves the Phase F/G authority boundary.

However, H3 cannot establish how a human writer emotionally experiences the recommendation surface. "Authority feel" here means the artifact remains advisory in semantics, not that a usability study has shown writers perceive it that way.

# H3 supported claim

The evidence supports a narrower statement than the Phase H claim ceiling:

> On six representative frozen benchmark briefs, a context-reduced coding-agent evaluator found the Story Discovery candidate/causal/craft contracts capable of expressing materially different and generally useful narrative engines. The producer recommendations were plausible, but in some close cases the rationale treated an undeclared artistic preference as if it were a stronger fit signal.

This is **not** evidence of Anthropic/OpenAI production quality, human-writer validation, provider robustness, or live F5 composition quality.

# H3 boundary

H3 ends here. It does not create a composed candidate and does not execute H4.

H4 should consume only already-frozen H2/H3 evidence and should preserve the distinction between cases with an uncontested primary and cases where H3 exposed a legitimate primary-choice dispute.
