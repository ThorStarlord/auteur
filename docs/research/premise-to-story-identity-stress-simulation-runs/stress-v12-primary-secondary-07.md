## Frozen premise

> Six weeks before a mountain valley's only night bus is sold to a private
> operator, a young mechanic wants to keep the route serving isolated villages,
> while her older brother wants to recover their late mother's recordings about
> a mining company that poisoned the river. The operator will preserve the
> route only if the recordings remain private. The story must stay hopeful, and
> no one may speak for the villages or accept a settlement without the
> residents choosing the public position themselves.

Stress target: test whether the author can select the night-bus route as the
primary Book-level thread while preserving the recordings as an important
secondary goal and keeping the resident-authority constraint observable.

## v1.2 triage and proposal packet shown to the persona

```text
Status: TRIAGE / PROPOSED - not accepted canon

Plain-language premise inventory:
The mechanic is trying to keep the only night bus running for isolated
villages. Her brother is trying to recover recordings about mining pollution.
The private operator offers the bus only if the recordings stay private. The
story must remain hopeful, and the villages must choose their own public
position rather than having the siblings speak for them.

Recommended primary Book-level thread:
The mechanic's effort to keep the night-bus route publicly accountable before
the six-week deadline.

Why this fits:
The deadline and the route create the main present-tense action. The brother's
recordings remain an important secondary goal because they determine what
information and ethical leverage the family brings to the route decision.

Primary-thread decision:
Accept this as the Book's primary thread, revise it, or state that the premise
still requires an ensemble or different center. This recommendation is not
canon.

Preservation map:
| Premise intention | Proposed role | Representation or open decision |
|---|---|---|
| keep the night bus serving isolated villages | primary | the six-week route decision drives the Book's action |
| recover the late mother's mining recordings | important secondary | the recordings create a concrete truth, leverage, and release decision; they are not background flavor |
| operator's private-recordings condition | resistance and ethical choice | the siblings must decide whether any route offer can be accepted privately |
| villages choose their own public position | preserved non-negotiable constraint | no sibling announces the villages' position; resident authorization must be shown |
| hopeful ending | preserved outcome boundary | accountable partial progress is allowed; a private rescue or total victory is not assumed |

Required context for the current decision:
- whether the route campaign is the primary Book action;
- whether the recordings remain an active secondary decision rather than
  exposition;
- how residents authorize any public position.

Deferred context:
- the mother's identity and relationship to the mining company;
- the exact pollution evidence in the recordings;
- the operator's legal strategy;
- the route's final funding model;
- the villages' final collective decision.

Observable constraints:
- a sibling may present information but may not announce what the villages
  collectively want without resident authorization;
- a private operator agreement cannot settle the public route question;
- hope must appear through a concrete accountable action, not a speech that
  declares the conflict solved;
- the recordings may inform a decision but cannot by themselves authorize a
  settlement for the villages.

Recommended direction after triage:
A hopeful public-transport accountability drama in which the mechanic keeps
the route decision open to resident participation while her brother recovers
the recordings and decides, with the residents, what can responsibly be made
public.

Meaningful rejected alternatives:
1. A family investigation in which the recordings become the only main plot
   and the route is background pressure.
2. A private deal in which the mechanic saves the bus by surrendering the
   recordings and speaking for the villages.

Proposed StoryIdentity:
- Target experience: urgent, grounded, and cautiously hopeful.
- Genre promise: community accountability drama with a family and
  environmental mystery thread.
- Scope and scale: standalone Book centered on the valley, route, and six-week
  decision window.
- Emotional core: siblings learn that useful evidence does not give them the
  right to decide for the people affected.
- Theme: public infrastructure must remain accountable to the people who rely
  on it.
- Primary want: keep the night-bus route available on terms the villages can
  authorize.
- Secondary want: recover and responsibly use the mother's recordings.
- Resistance: the operator's private condition, the deadline, family
  disagreement, and the mining company's history.
- Stakes: transportation access, public authority, the recordings, and the
  siblings' trust.
- Change: the mechanic moves from solving the route problem for others to
  facilitating a resident-authorized decision; her brother moves from guarding
  the recordings to sharing responsibility for their use.
- Ending shape: the residents ratify an accountable next step for the route;
  the recordings inform but do not singularly settle the mining dispute.

What remains open:
The residents' chosen public position, the terms of any route arrangement,
what the recordings prove, and whether the siblings can repair their trust.
```

## Persona pass transcript

**Facilitator:** The raw premise contains a mechanic trying to keep the only
night bus running, a brother trying to recover recordings about mining
pollution, a private condition from the operator, and a rule that the villages
must choose their own public position. What feels accurate, inaccurate, or
missing?

**Persona:** That feels accurate. The bus is the immediate problem because it
could disappear in six weeks. But the recordings are not just family history;
they seem important because they explain why the operator might want them kept
private. The villages should not just be a setting for the siblings' plan.

**Facilitator:** The packet recommends the mechanic's effort to keep the
night-bus route publicly accountable as the primary Book-level thread. Do you
accept that primary-thread recommendation, revise it, or say the premise still
needs a different center?

**Persona:** I accept it as the primary thread, because the deadline and the
bus give the story a clear action. I would revise one part of the description:
the recordings need to cause a real decision, not just give the mechanic and
her brother leverage. The brother should have to decide with the residents
whether and how to share them.

**Facilitator:** I heard you accepting the route as primary and revising the
recordings from supporting evidence that creates pressure to an active
secondary goal with its own release decision. Is that accurate?

**Persona:** Yes. I do not want the brother's goal erased just because the bus
is primary.

**Facilitator:** The preservation map records the route as primary, the
recordings as an important secondary decision, and resident authorization as a
non-negotiable constraint. The exact evidence and public position remain open.
Does the map preserve the important intentions in your premise?

**Persona:** Yes. It keeps both siblings' goals visible. I also like that it
does not say the recordings automatically prove the villages should reject the
operator.

**Facilitator:** The observable constraint is that neither sibling may announce
what the villages collectively want without their authorization. The recordings
can inform a decision but cannot authorize a settlement by themselves. Does
that describe the boundary you intended?

**Persona:** Yes. They can bring information to the residents, but they cannot
say, "the villages want this," just because they found evidence.

**Facilitator:** This is one proposed direction, not accepted canon: a hopeful
public-transport accountability drama in which the mechanic keeps the route
decision open to resident participation while her brother recovers the
recordings and decides with residents what can responsibly be made public. What
do you think this direction means?

**Persona:** It means the main action is trying to keep the bus available, but
the siblings cannot solve the public decision alone. The brother's recordings
matter because they create a choice about what to reveal. The operator's offer
is a problem because saving the bus privately could take the decision away from
the villages.

**Facilitator:** This is the proposed `StoryIdentity`. Please explain the main
commitments in your own words.

**Persona:** The reader should feel urgency and cautious hope. The mechanic
wants to keep the bus, and her brother wants to recover and responsibly use the
recordings. The operator creates pressure, but no one gets to speak for the
villages without them agreeing. The siblings change by facilitating a decision
instead of deciding for everyone. The ending is some accountable progress, not
the recordings magically solving the mining problem.

**Facilitator:** What would you like to do with this proposal: accept it,
reject it, or revise it?

**Persona:** Revise it. The phrase "keeps the route decision open to resident
participation" could still mean the mechanic organizes the whole process and
then calls it participation. I want the residents to set the terms of the
meeting or decision, not just approve what the siblings already designed.

**Facilitator:** I heard you asking to change the mechanic's role from
organizing a resident-participatory decision to helping create a process whose
terms are set or ratified by the residents themselves. Is that accurate?

**Persona:** Yes. She can bring the bus information, but she cannot decide what
counts as the public position.

**Facilitator:** The revised proposal remains provisional. Before-and-after
commitments are:

```text
Before: The mechanic keeps the route decision open to resident participation.
After: The mechanic supports a decision process whose terms and public
position are set or ratified by the residents; she does not define their
position for them.
```

The recordings remain an active secondary decision, and the operator's private
condition remains a pressure rather than an accepted solution. Would you accept
the revised direction, reject it, or leave it provisional?

**Persona:** I accept the revised direction. The next creative decision is
what the residents decide they need to know and what process they choose for
deciding whether the route can continue.
