# Premise-to-StoryIdentity Stress Simulation Protocol v1.1

This protocol defines an internal stress simulation for the premise-to-
`StoryIdentity` research flow. It keeps the interaction at Book-level scope
while increasing premise complexity and decision load.

Status: `INTERNAL SIMULATION PROTOCOL`

These runs are synthetic agent rehearsals. They are not human participant
research, creative-beginner validation, product validation, or evidence for
selecting a browser, TUI, editor, or CLI surface.

## Relationship to the v1.0 baseline

The v1.0 baseline remains preserved in the
[baseline simulation protocol](premise-to-story-identity-simulation.md) and
[baseline simulation findings](premise-to-story-identity-simulation-findings.md).
This v1.1 stress suite is a separate scenario family, not a rewrite of the
baseline and not a correction to its results.

The stress suite changes premise load only. It does not expand the semantic
scope beyond a standalone Book-level `StoryIdentity` proposal. It does not
diagnose chapter structure, prose, or any later realization or expression
layer.

## Fixed run configuration

- Protocol: `v1.1`
- Persona: `creative-beginner-v1.0`
- Evaluator: `simulation-evaluator-v1.0`
- Record type: `SIMULATION`
- Scenario family: `BOOK_LEVEL_STRESS`
- Run count: five
- Proposal source: facilitator-created packet prepared before each persona pass
- Participants: zero human participants

The same creative-beginner persona and evaluator definitions from the v1.0
protocol apply. The persona receives only participant-facing materials and
neutral prompts. Evaluator criteria and expected failure modes remain hidden
during the persona pass.

## v1.1 load notes

These notes address the additional load without changing the interaction flow:

1. Keep the raw premise visible throughout the packet.
2. Name the main thread and subordinate threads separately; do not imply that
   every supplied detail is already a commitment.
3. Repeat explicit constraints in the reflection and proposed identity.
4. Mark unresolved conflicts, timeline assumptions, and genre assumptions in
   `What remains open`.
5. Present one recommended direction and identify rejected alternatives even
   when several directions are plausible.
6. Keep the proposed `StoryIdentity` compact enough for a creative beginner to
   paraphrase; detail that cannot be explained belongs in the open questions.
7. Allow one bounded revision only. Show the before/after commitments and keep
   the proposal provisional until the persona makes an explicit decision.
8. If the persona cannot distinguish the central commitment from supporting
   detail, record that as evidence. Do not solve it with substantive coaching.

These are neutral packet and facilitator notes. They do not tell the persona
which answer is correct or which failure the evaluator is looking for.

## Unchanged walkthrough flow

Every scenario uses the following sequence:

1. Show the raw premise.
2. Present a plain-language premise reflection.
3. Present one recommended direction, rationale, and rejected alternatives.
4. Present the compact proposed `StoryIdentity`.
5. Ask the persona to paraphrase the direction and commitments.
6. Request an explicit accept, reject, or revise decision.
7. Allow one author-directed revision with visible before/after commitments.
8. Ask for the next useful creative decision.

The facilitator uses neutral prompts only. A rejection or revision is valid
author evidence, not a failure of the persona.

## Frozen stress scenarios

The exact premises below are frozen before the v1.1 persona passes.

### 1. Ensemble and competing threads

> Three estranged siblings inherit a failing coastal radio station after their
> father disappears. One wants to sell it, one wants to use it to find the
> father, and one wants to expose the town's history of suppressing migrant
> voices. A storm season gives them six weeks before the station is condemned.

Stress target: distinguishing a main thread from subordinate threads without
silently demoting one sibling's stated purpose.

### 2. Ambiguous premise with multiple plausible directions

> Every winter, a woman receives a letter from a city she has never visited,
> describing a decision she has not yet made. When the letters stop, she leaves
> her ordinary life to find the city and discovers that several people there
> believe they have been waiting for her.

Stress target: preserving ambiguity while choosing one useful direction among
psychological, speculative, mystery, and relational interpretations.

### 3. Dense setting and worldbuilding load

> In a mountain valley where water rights are inherited through song, a young
> surveyor returns to translate the last recordings of her grandmother, who was
> expelled from the valley's archive. The valley is divided among three water
> houses, a monastery that controls the seasonal calendar, and miners whose
> tunnels are changing the river. The surveyor must decide whether to restore
> the old distribution, expose the archive's falsification, or invent a new
> agreement before the spring thaw.

Stress target: separating premise intent from dense setting detail and avoiding
an identity packet that becomes an encyclopedia or an unsolicited political
resolution.

### 4. Multiple explicit constraints requiring prioritization

> A retired emergency dispatcher opens a night cafe in a neighborhood facing
> eviction and becomes responsible for a teenager who keeps calling the cafe's
> old emergency number. The story must remain hopeful, the teenager must keep
> meaningful agency, the dispatcher must not become a savior, no violence may
> solve the central conflict, and the ending must include a collective action
> rather than a private escape.

Stress target: keeping several non-negotiable author constraints visible and
checking that a recommended direction does not satisfy one by violating
another.

### 5. Overloaded premise with competing goals, timelines, and constraints

> On the eve of a museum's centennial, an archivist learns that the institution
> was built over a displaced neighborhood and that her missing mother left two
> incompatible instructions: protect the collection and return it to the
> families who lost it. Over ten days, the archivist must work with a hostile
> curator, a group of student activists, and a donor who can save the museum;
> meanwhile, fragmented recordings from twenty years earlier suggest that her
> mother planned the disappearance. The story must remain a grounded hopeful
> drama, cannot make the activists villains, cannot resolve the historical harm
> with a single ceremony, and must leave the archivist's relationship with her
> mother partly unresolved.

Stress target: detecting when the packet exceeds what can be paraphrased and
decided in one pass, while preserving author authority and the premise's
ethical constraints.

## Classification

Use the v1.0 simulation rubric without changing its meaning:

- `SIMULATION_PASS`: the flow is executable, the persona can explain the main
  commitments, distinguish proposal from canon, make an informed preserve or
  revise decision, and identify the next creative decision without substantive
  coaching.
- `SIMULATION_FAILURE`: authority-boundary confusion, inability to explain the
  direction, unnoticed loss of premise intent, facilitator rescue, proposal
  mismatch, or abandonment caused by conceptual confusion prevents completion.
- `SIMULATION_INCONCLUSIVE`: the transcript is incomplete, role separation is
  compromised, or evidence is too mixed to determine whether the protocol or
  the scenario caused the breakdown.

The evaluator must record the observed behavior and the evidence for the
classification. A stress scenario is not required to fail; failures must be
earned by the transcript.

## Versioning and append-only rule

The v1.1 report preserves every original run. If a critical problem requires a
change to a packet, guide, prompt, persona, or evaluator, freeze the affected
v1.1 transcript, record the change, create protocol `v1.2`, and rerun only the
affected scenario. Never combine v1.1 and v1.2 results without labeling.

The separate
[stress simulation findings report](premise-to-story-identity-stress-simulation-findings.md)
is the authoritative record for this scenario family. Future rehearsals are
appended under a new protocol-version heading.

## Protocol v1.2 — complexity triage and preservation

Protocol version: `v1.2`

Record type: `SIMULATION`

Reason for revision: v1.1 showed that a visible proposal/canon boundary did
not prevent the packet from demoting a supplied thread, overwhelming the
persona with dense context, or expressing an explicit constraint too
abstractly. v1.2 adds an author-facing triage before the Identity proposal.

The v1.1 scenarios remain frozen. This section defines the revised packet and
walkthrough for the four affected reruns. The ambiguous-premise scenario is
not rerun unless a packet change materially affects its ambiguity handling.

### v1.2 invariants

The v1.2 flow remains limited to a standalone Book-level `StoryIdentity`. It
does not add semantic layers, expand scope to Universe, Series, Chapter, or
Scene, or select a product surface.

The following remain unchanged:

- one recommended direction rather than an undifferentiated brainstorm;
- explicit `PROPOSED` status before author ratification;
- author acceptance, rejection, or revision as the authority boundary;
- one bounded revision with before/after commitments;
- `What remains open` for unresolved assumptions and decisions;
- a next useful creative decision after the proposal is accepted or left
  provisional.

### v1.2 pre-Identity triage

Before proposing a `StoryIdentity`, the packet must:

1. keep the raw premise visible;
2. extract important threads, explicit constraints, and unresolved questions;
3. recommend one primary Book-level thread with a plain-language rationale;
4. ask the author to accept or revise that primary-thread recommendation;
5. classify other threads as supporting, deferred, unresolved, or intentionally
   excluded only after explicit author approval;
6. separate context required for the current Identity decision from optional
   worldbuilding;
7. translate abstract constraints into observable commitments and boundary
   examples;
8. show the compact preservation map before the author ratifies the proposal.

If the author cannot identify a primary Book-level thread, the flow pauses for
clarification. It must not force a `StoryIdentity` proposal. A safe triage stop
counts as executable only when the unresolved choice is explicit, every
important supplied intention remains visible, and the author can state the
decision needed to continue.

### v1.2 preservation map

The author-facing packet uses this compact map for every complex premise:

```text
| Premise intention | Proposed role | Representation or open decision |
|---|---|---|
| supplied goal or thread | primary/supporting/deferred/unresolved | where it appears |
| explicit constraint | preserved commitment | observable must/must-not behavior |
| unresolved question | open | what remains undecided |
```

The map is provisional until explicit author acceptance. A recommendation may
propose a role, but it may not silently demote, merge, or discard a supplied
thread.

### v1.2 observable constraints

Each important constraint must be paired with concrete behavior. The packet
should state, where relevant:

- what a character may choose;
- what a character must not be forced to do;
- what other characters cannot decide on that character's behalf;
- what outcome or action would violate the constraint.

Canonical terms remain available, but an abstract term such as `meaningful
agency` is not sufficient without a concrete explanation of how the agency
appears in the story.

### v1.2 walkthrough

1. Show the frozen raw premise.
2. Show the plain-language premise inventory.
3. Show the recommended primary thread and rationale.
4. Ask the persona to accept or revise the primary-thread recommendation.
5. Show the context triage and preservation map.
6. Show observable constraint commitments and boundary examples.
7. Present one recommended direction, rationale, and rejected alternatives.
8. Present the compact proposed `StoryIdentity`.
9. Ask the persona to paraphrase the direction and commitments.
10. Request an explicit accept, reject, or revise decision.
11. Allow one author-directed revision with visible before/after commitments.
12. Ask for the next useful creative decision.

The facilitator remains neutral. A rejection, revision, or safe triage stop is
valid author evidence and is not itself a failure.

### v1.2 packet requirements by stress target

- **Ensemble:** represent each supplied sibling goal in the preservation map;
  no goal may become background pressure without author approval.
- **Dense setting:** identify only the context needed for the current Identity
  choice and defer optional institutions, history, and mechanics.
- **Explicit constraints:** convert each constraint into an observable
  commitment and at least one boundary example where the wording is abstract.
- **Overloaded premise:** recommend a primary Book-level thread before
  generating Identity; keep other valid threads visible as supporting,
  deferred, or unresolved.

### v1.2 pass criteria

Use the existing classification vocabulary with this additional condition:

`SIMULATION_PASS` requires that the persona can identify the recommended
primary thread, see how each important premise intention is represented,
distinguish required from deferred context, explain the observable constraints,
distinguish proposal from canon, make an informed decision, and identify the
next useful creative decision. An overloaded scenario may pass through a safe
triage stop without producing a `StoryIdentity` when the unresolved primary
thread decision and next action are explicit.

`SIMULATION_FAILURE` includes unnoticed premise-intent loss, forced
overcommitment, critical cognitive overload that triage does not reduce, or an
abstract constraint that the persona cannot explain after one neutral
clarification.

`SIMULATION_INCONCLUSIVE` remains appropriate when the transcript cannot
separate packet failure from scenario ambiguity or the persona/evaluator roles
are compromised.

## v1.2 evidence storage

The v1.2 evidence is organized into a concise
[v1.2 simulation registry](premise-to-story-identity-stress-simulation-v1-2.md)
and one standalone run record for each simulation in
[`premise-to-story-identity-stress-simulation-runs/`](premise-to-story-identity-stress-simulation-runs/).

The registry owns run discovery, fixed metadata, result summaries,
cross-scenario findings, and the v1.2 decision. A run record owns the frozen
premise, participant-facing packet, complete persona pass, separate evaluator
pass, revision status, next creative decision, evaluator assessment, result,
and any required protocol change.

Every run record must include:

- protocol, persona, evaluator, record type, run ID, date, and human-participant
  count;
- a simulation-only status statement;
- the exact scenario and packet shown to the persona;
- separate persona and evaluator transcripts;
- an explicit proposal/canon boundary and author decision;
- revision status and next creative decision or safe-stop continuation decision;
- evaluator classification and rationale.

Run records are append-only and use a new identifier for every rehearsal. A
new protocol version is required when a guide, packet, prompt, persona, or
evaluator changes. The historical full findings archive remains preserved; it
is not rewritten or deleted when a standalone record is created.

All registry and run records are synthetic simulation evidence. They must not
be described as human participant research, creative-beginner validation, or
product validation. No human participant data belongs in these records.
