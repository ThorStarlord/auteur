I recommend **publish-verified-testimony**.

The **monastery testimony is preserved**, so it can re-enter public memory through authenticated publication while the originals remain secure. This respects the **treaty-protected archive** as the only evidentiary chain and avoids exposing its strongest records. It also directly serves **contested history**: lived memory can publicly challenge the **forged founding record** and the **council’s retracted admission** without requiring the archive itself to be opened or weakened.

The principal tradeoff is delay. Authentication postpones public impact and gives the council’s retraction more time to shape perception. In return, Book 4 gains a durable disclosure whose credibility depends on preserved provenance rather than a single politically vulnerable hearing.

I deliberately excluded the **repaired archive lantern** because its condition does not determine how testimony can safely become public. I also excluded the identity of the falsifier because the decision concerns preserving the evidentiary chain, not proving culpability again.

This is a non-authoritative recommendation only; it creates no canon, Book Direction, or Canonical State.
