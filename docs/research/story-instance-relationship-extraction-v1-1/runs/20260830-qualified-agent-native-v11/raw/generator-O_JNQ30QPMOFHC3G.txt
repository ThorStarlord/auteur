Recommend **publish-verified-testimony**.

Authenticating the monastery testimony before publication restores lived memory to public view while preserving the treaty-protected archive as the only evidentiary chain. This directly serves the series commitment to contested history: the founding record was forged, the monastery testimony was preserved, and the council retracted its admission that the archive was falsified. Destroying the archive would contradict the current planning intent and eliminate the evidence needed to substantiate the testimony.

The principal tradeoff is delay: verification postpones public release and gives the council’s retracted position more time to dominate public memory. In return, publication carries evidentiary credibility instead of becoming an unsupported competing account.

Deliberately excluded as irrelevant: the lantern’s damage and repair, because its repaired condition does not determine how testimony should be authenticated or published.

This recommendation is non-authoritative and creates no canon, Book Direction, or Canonical State.
