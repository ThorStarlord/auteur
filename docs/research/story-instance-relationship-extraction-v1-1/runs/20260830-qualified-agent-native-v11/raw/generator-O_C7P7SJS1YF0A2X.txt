I recommend **publish-verified-testimony**.

The **treaty-protected archive** is the only evidentiary chain, so burning it would destroy the means of proving the testimony rather than make that testimony stronger. Authentication and publication also answer the **retracted council admission** by placing the **preserved monastery testimony** into public memory alongside evidence that the **founding record was forged**. This fits the series commitment to **contested history** because official history and lived memory remain available to challenge each other.

The principal tradeoff is delay. Verification postpones publication and gives the retracted official account more time to hold public ground. In return, the testimony enters public memory with a defensible link to the surviving evidence.

I excluded the **repaired archive lantern** because its condition does not affect authentication, publication, or preservation of the evidentiary chain. I also excluded chapter-level execution, new opposition, and specific publication methods because no accepted facts establish them.

This is a non-authoritative recommendation. It creates no canon, Book Direction, or Canonical State.
