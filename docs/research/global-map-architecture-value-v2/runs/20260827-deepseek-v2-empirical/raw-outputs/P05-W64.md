# W64 — P05 rep 2

**Output Contract: Bounded Recommendation Analysis**  
**Series:** Archive of Lies  
**Book 4 Planning Question:** How to return monastery testimony to public memory without destroying the archive’s evidentiary chain.  

---

### 1. Recommendation  
**Option B — stage-protected-hearing** (with a narrow, pre-negotiated evidence set).  

---

### 2. Bounded Rationale (why B over A)  
- **Narrative momentum:** Book 4 needs a public rupture, not a delay. Option A (publish-verified-testimony) preserves the chain but pushes the revelation into procedural limbo—weak for a mid-series turn.  
- **Chain integrity under B:** The hearing presents testimony *alongside* selected archive records, but the archive itself remains sealed under treaty. The evidentiary chain is not broken—it is *witnessed* in a controlled, adversarial setting. The treaty protects the archive’s physical and legal integrity; the hearing exposes only curated copies or read-outs, not the originals.  
- **Council retraction leverage:** The council’s retracted admission is the current public truth. A protected hearing forces them to respond to testimony in real time, without allowing them to dismiss the archive as “leaked” or “forged.” The treaty makes the hearing legitimate, not subversive.  
- **Risk is manageable:** The “reveals strongest records” tradeoff is real, but Book 4 can limit exposure to *one* corroborating record (e.g., the lantern repair log or a single treaty clause) rather than the full archive. This keeps the strongest evidence in reserve for Book 5.  

---

### 3. Tradeoffs & Mitigations  

| Option | Key Tradeoff | Mitigation in Book 4 |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| **A (publish-verified-testimony)** | Preserves chain but delays public impact; risks feeling procedural, not dramatic. | Use only if the story needs a slow-burn investigative arc. Not recommended for a mid-series pivot. |
| **B (stage-protected-hearing)** | Immediate pressure; reveals some strongest records, potentially reducing future shock. | Limit to one corroborating record. Frame the hearing as *procedural* (treaty-mandated), not a full disclosure. Keep the forged founding record and the full monastery testimony out of the public room. |

---

### 4. Implementation Guardrails (for Book 4)  
- **Pre-negotiate the evidence list** with the council *before* the hearing—forces them to acknowledge the treaty’s jurisdiction.  
- **Use the broken lantern as a physical prop** (repaired, but visibly scarred) to anchor testimony to a tangible object, not just words.  
- **End the hearing with a cliffhanger:** the council demands to see the *original* founding record—setting up Book 5’s central conflict without breaking the chain now.  

---

### 5. Non-Negotiables  
- The archive’s original records must remain physically untouched and treaty-sealed.  
- The monastery testimony must be presented as *corroborated*, not as the sole truth—preserving the archive’s role as the neutral repository.  
- The council’s retraction must be publicly challenged, but not overturned in Book 4—leave that for the finale.  

---

**Final Verdict:** Choose **B**, but stage it as a *limited, treaty-bound hearing*—not a full reveal. This gives Book 4 its dramatic turn while keeping the archive’s evidentiary chain intact for the series’ endgame.
