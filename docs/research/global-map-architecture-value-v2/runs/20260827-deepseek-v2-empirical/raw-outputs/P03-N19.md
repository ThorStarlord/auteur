# N19 — P03 rep 3

**Output Contract: Bounded Recommendation Analysis**  
**Series:** Archive of Lies  
**Book 4 Planning Question:** How to return monastery testimony to public memory without destroying the archive’s evidentiary chain.  
**Options Assessed:** A) publish-verified-testimony | B) stage-protected-hearing  

---

### 1. Bounded Recommendation  
**Recommended Option:** **A) publish-verified-testimony** — with a **phased, two-step execution** (see Section 4).  

**Primary rationale:** Option A preserves the archive’s evidentiary chain (the treaty-protected original remains untouched and verifiable), while Option B risks exposing the strongest records to adversarial scrutiny, potentially re-opening the council’s retraction and destabilizing the treaty. The delay inherent in A is acceptable because the monastery testimony is already preserved; the goal is *public memory*, not immediate pressure. A allows controlled release, verification, and narrative framing without breaking the chain.

---

### 2. Tradeoff Analysis (Bounded to Current State)  

**Option A — publish-verified-testimony**  
- **Preserves chain:** Yes. Original testimony stays under treaty; a certified copy is published. Chain of custody remains intact because the archive’s original is never exposed.  
- **Delays release:** Moderate. Verification (authentication, cross-checking against founding record forgery) takes time. But the delay is *strategic* — it allows the council’s retraction to be countered with a pre-verified document, not a reactive leak.  
- **Risk:** If verification is rushed, the testimony could be dismissed as another forgery (given the founding record was forged). Mitigation: use independent witnesses (monastery elders) and cryptographic/scribal proof.  
- **Narrative fit:** Supports the series’ theme of “archive of lies” by showing that truth can be *published* without *exposing* the archive.  

**Option B — stage-protected-hearing**  
- **Immediate pressure:** High. Public hearing forces council to respond in real time.  
- **Reveals strongest records:** Yes — but this is the flaw. Under treaty, presenting testimony *beside* selected archive evidence means those records become part of the public record. If the council retracts again, the archive’s most damning evidence is now public but *not* protected by the same treaty (since it was voluntarily presented). This breaks the evidentiary chain.  
- **Risk:** The council could argue the hearing was staged, tainting both testimony and archive evidence. The treaty’s protection is weakened because the archive’s “strongest records” are now outside its walls.  
- **Narrative fit:** Creates immediate drama but sacrifices long-term integrity — a classic trap for the series’ protagonists.  

**Verdict:** A is safer for the archive’s integrity; B is riskier but faster. Given the council already retracted once, B invites a second retraction that would be fatal.

---

### 3. Key Assumptions & Constraints (Bounded)  
- **Treaty is absolute:** The archive cannot be opened or its originals moved without breaking the treaty. Any public presentation of originals (Option B) violates this.  
- **Monastery testimony is already preserved** — it is not at risk of loss. The only question is *public memory*, not survival.  
- **Council’s retraction is unstable:** They admitted, then retracted. Any new evidence must be *irrefutable* and *pre-verified* to prevent a third flip.  
- **Forgery precedent:** The founding record was forged. Therefore, any new document must be authenticated by *multiple independent methods* (e.g., ink dating, monastery oral tradition, witness testimony) before release.  
- **Time is not critical:** No immediate external threat (e.g., invasion, destruction) is stated. Delay is acceptable.  

---

### 4. Recommended Execution (Bounded to Option A)  
**Phase 1 — Verification (Books 4A–4B):**  
- Authenticate the monastery testimony using three independent methods: (a) physical analysis (parchment, ink, seals), (b) oral cross-examination of monastery elders, (c) comparison with the *forged* founding record to prove the testimony predates the forgery.  
- Do **not** publish until all three methods agree.  

**Phase 2 — Controlled Publication (Book 4C):**  
- Publish a *certified copy* (not the original) with a public statement: “Original remains under treaty protection; this copy is verified by [three methods].”  
- Release alongside a *neutral summary* of the archive’s own provenance (including the forgery admission) to preempt accusations of bias.  
- Invite independent scholars to inspect the copy (not the original) — this builds public trust without exposing the archive.  

**Phase 3 — Council Response (Book 4D):**  
- Let the council react. If they retract again, the published copy stands as public record, and the archive’s original remains protected — the chain is unbroken.  

---

### 5. Non-Negotiables (Boundaries)  
- **Never present original archive records in a public hearing** (Option B’s core flaw).  
- **Never publish unverified testimony** — the forgery precedent makes this fatal.  
- **Never allow the council to control the narrative** — publish first, then let them respond.  

---

### 6. Final Recommendation Statement  
Choose **Option A** with the phased execution above. It preserves the evidentiary chain, respects the treaty, and converts the monastery testimony into public memory through *verified publication* rather than *exposed origin
