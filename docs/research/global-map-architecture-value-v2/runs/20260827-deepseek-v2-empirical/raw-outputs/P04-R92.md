# R92 — P04 rep 3

**Output Contract: Bounded Recommendation Analysis**  
**Series:** Archive of Lies  
**Book 4 Planning Question:** How to bring monastery testimony back into public memory without losing the archive’s evidentiary chain?  
**Options Assessed:** A) burn-archive | B) publish-verified-testimony  

---

### 1. Bounded Recommendation  
**Recommendation:** Option B — publish-verified-testimony, with a **phased release protocol** (see implementation note).  

**Rationale in one sentence:** Option B preserves the archive’s treaty-protected status and evidentiary chain while allowing the testimony to re-enter public memory through a verifiable, non-destructive path — Option A destroys the very institution the series has spent three books defending.  

---

### 2. Tradeoff Analysis (per option)  

#### Option A — burn-archive  
- **Primary gain:** Testimony becomes the *only* surviving account, forcing public attention and eliminating competing archive narratives.  
- **Primary loss:** Archive ceases to exist — treaty protection becomes moot, evidentiary chain is severed (no cross-referencing, no provenance), and the series’ core tension (protected vs. exposed) collapses.  
- **Secondary risks:**  
  - Public may distrust testimony *because* the archive is gone (absence of corroboration).  
  - Council can claim the burning was a cover-up, not a revelation.  
  - Irreversible — no future plot can restore the archive.  
- **Narrative cost:** High — burns a central setting and symbol.  

#### Option B — publish-verified-testimony  
- **Primary gain:** Testimony enters public memory *with* authentication (monastery seal, archival cross-reference, witness chain) — strengthens credibility without sacrificing the archive.  
- **Primary loss:** Release is delayed (verification takes time), allowing council or other actors to spin counter-narratives in the interim.  
- **Secondary benefits:**  
  - Evidentiary chain remains intact — archive can later be used to verify or challenge the testimony, creating future plot hooks.  
  - Treaty protection is *used* (not broken) — archive becomes a shield for the testimony, not a rival.  
  - Allows for dramatic tension: the delay itself can be exploited by antagonists.  
- **Narrative cost:** Low-to-moderate — requires careful pacing to avoid anti-climax.  

---

### 3. Assumptions & Constraints  
- **Assumption:** The treaty-protected archive cannot be legally or ethically destroyed without breaking the series’ established world logic.  
- **Assumption:** The monastery testimony is currently *preserved* but not *publicly accessible* — its return to memory requires an active act of publication or revelation.  
- **Constraint:** The evidentiary chain (provenance, authentication, cross-referencing) is a core thematic element — losing it would undermine the series’ premise.  
- **Constraint:** Book 4 must not resolve the archive’s core conflict permanently; it must leave room for Books 5+ (per “ongoing” status).  

---

### 4. Implementation Note (for Option B)  
To mitigate the delay risk, use a **two-stage release**:  
1. **Stage 1 (early Book 4):** Publish a *partial* testimony (e.g., a single, self-contained revelation) with provisional authentication — enough to re-enter public memory and create urgency.  
2. **Stage 2 (climax):** Publish the *full* testimony with complete archival verification, timed to counter the council’s retraction.  

This preserves the evidentiary chain while making the delay a plot feature, not a flaw.  

---

### 5. Non-Negotiables  
- The archive must remain intact and treaty-protected at the end of Book 4.  
- The monastery testimony must be publicly known by the end of Book 4 (not merely preserved).  
- The evidentiary chain must be *strengthened*, not broken — the archive and testimony must corroborate each other.  

---

**Final verdict:** Choose **Option B** with the phased release. It honors the series’ established stakes, keeps the archive as a living institution, and turns the testimony into a *verified* truth rather than a desperate last resort.
