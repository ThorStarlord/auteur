### 1. Recommendation Statement

**Option B is recommended.** It is the only option that satisfies the core planning intent—returning to the monastery testimony (ST-F2) without breaking the protected archive (ST-F6)—while also delivering the required compact, specific why-now for both the contested-history cluster (REL-09) and the reactivated testimony (REL-06). Option B achieves this by framing the reactivation as a *defensive necessity* triggered by the cluster's pressure, not as a challenge to the archive's protected status.

---

### 2. Bounded Analysis

#### Option A: Direct Confrontation
- **Pros:** High dramatic tension; clear causal link (REL-05) between retraction and treaty.
- **Cons:** **Fatal flaw.** Directly challenges the archive's protected status (ST-F6), violating the current constraint (REL-07 state-compatibility). This breaks the planning intent and risks invalidating the entire Decision Map's logic. The why-now for the reactivation would be "to expose the truth," which is adversarial to the archive, not compatible with it.

#### Option B: Defensive Reactivation (Recommended)
- **Pros:**
    - **Preserves ST-F6:** The monastery testimony is reactivated not to *attack* the archive, but to *defend* it against external pressure. The testimony becomes corroborating evidence that the archive's protected treaty is legitimate, thereby reinforcing the archive's protected status.
    - **Specific Why-Now (Cluster):** The contested-history cluster (REL-09) is under active pressure (REL-01). The why-now is: *The cluster's integrity is being questioned; the archive must be shown to be built on verified, not fabricated, foundations.*
    - **Specific Why-Now (Testimony):** The reactivation is triggered by a *new external challenge* (e.g., a rival faction or a newly surfaced forgery claim) that specifically targets the founding-record (ST-F1). The monastery testimony (ST-F2) is the only independent witness that can authenticate the founding-record's legitimacy, making its reactivation a direct, necessary response to this specific threat.
    - **Compactness:** The logic is a single, tight chain: *Pressure on cluster → Threat to founding-record → Reactivate monastery testimony as authenticator → Testimony validates founding-record → Cluster integrity restored → Archive's protected treaty remains unchallenged.*
- **Cons:** Lower immediate drama than Option A; requires careful writing to ensure the testimony's reactivation doesn't inadvertently open a new line of inquiry that could threaten the archive.

#### Option C: Indirect/Parallel Reactivation
- **Pros:** Avoids direct conflict with the archive.
- **Cons:** **Weak why-now.** The reactivation would feel arbitrary or coincidental, lacking the specific causal trigger required by the Decision Map. It fails to tie the reactivation to the active cluster pressure (REL-01), making the why-now vague and unfocused. This violates the "compact, specific why-now" requirement.

---

### 3. Risk Assessment & Mitigation

- **Primary Risk (Option B):** The monastery testimony, once reactivated, could be used by antagonists to question the *treaty* itself (REL-05), indirectly threatening the archive.
    - **Mitigation:** Frame the testimony's content as *strictly* corroborating the founding-record's authenticity (ST-F1) and the retraction's validity (ST-F5). Do not have the testimony address the treaty's terms directly. The testimony's role is to prove the *process* was legitimate, not to re-litigate the *outcome*.
- **Secondary Risk:** The reactivation might be seen as a "convenient" plot device.
    - **Mitigation:** The trigger must be a concrete, external event (e.g., a forged document surfaces that claims the founding-record is a fake). The reactivation is a *reaction* to this new evidence, making it a logical, defensive step.

---

### 4. Final Recommendation

**Proceed with Option B.** It is the only option that satisfies the planning intent, maintains state-compatibility (REL-07), and provides a compact, specific why-now for both the cluster and the reactivated testimony. The reactivation is framed as a defensive authentication measure, not an offensive challenge, thereby preserving the protected archive.
