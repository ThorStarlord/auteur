### 1. Bounded Recommendation

**Primary Recommendation:** **Option B — "The Forger's Heir"**

**Secondary Recommendation (Fallback):** **Option C — "The Living Document"**

**Explicitly Not Recommended:** **Option A — "The Silent Witness"**

---

### 2. Reasoning (Bounded to Provided Context)

**Why Option B is strongest:**
- Directly activates the **active consequence** (`founding-record=forged`) by making its origin a *lived, personal* matter — the forger's descendant now carries the burden of that lie.
- Satisfies the planning intent ("make forged founding record matter to lived memory") by converting an abstract archival fact into a **character-driven emotional stake** (inherited guilt, identity crisis, or protective duty).
- Creates a natural **repeated-map tension**: the map (or its absence) becomes a physical trace of the forgery, forcing the protagonist to navigate between the official record and the human cost.
- Does **not** depend on dormant or irrelevant history entries, keeping the causal chain clean and current.

**Why Option C is viable fallback:**
- Also activates the active consequence by making the forged record *functionally alive* — it shapes current law, ritual, or social hierarchy.
- Works if you prefer **institutional/structural stakes** over personal ones.
- Slightly weaker because it risks abstracting the forgery into "systemic issue" rather than *lived memory* (the intent explicitly asks for lived experience).

**Why Option A is rejected:**
- Relies on **dormant** (`monastery-testimony`) and **irrelevant** (`broken-lantern`) history entries — both are explicitly non-current in the provided context.
- Would require resurrecting dormant material, violating the bounded constraint that only the active consequence (`founding-record=forged`) is currently operative.
- The "silent witness" framing implies passive revelation, which undercuts the intent to make the forgery *matter* to lived memory — it would remain an artifact, not a lived force.

---

### 3. Key Assumptions & Caveats

- **Assumption:** "Lived memory" means *personal, embodied, or communal experience* — not merely institutional awareness. Option B best fits this.
- **Assumption:** The repeated-map focus implies the map is a recurring motif; Option B allows the map to be the forger's tool, the heir's burden, and the protagonist's clue — maximizing motif utility.
- **Caveat:** If Book 2's tone is more systemic/political than personal, Option C may outperform B. But given the explicit "lived memory" intent, B is the safer bounded choice.
- **Caveat:** Option B requires introducing a new character (the heir) — ensure this doesn't bloat Book 2's cast beyond scope. If character budget is tight, Option C is the pragmatic pivot.

---

### 4. Confidence & Non-Authoritative Statement

**Confidence:** **High** (given the bounded context — active consequence is singular and clear; dormant/irrelevant entries are unambiguous).

If Book 2 planning later activates the dormant `monastery-testimony` or repairs the `broken-lantern`, Option A becomes viable — but under *current* constraints, it is out of scope.
