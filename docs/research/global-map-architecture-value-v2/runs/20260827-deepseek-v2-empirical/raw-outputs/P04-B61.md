**Recommendation Analysis: Book 4 — Burn vs. Publish**

---

### 1. Recommendation Summary
**Recommended Option:** **Publish (selective, with contextual framing)** — *not* burn, and *not* full raw dump.

**Rationale in one line:** Burning the archive is categorically incompatible with the current treaty-protected state (hard constraint), while a full raw dump risks destabilizing the contested-history group without achieving the reactivation goal; a selective, framed publication satisfies the reactivation trigger and preserves treaty integrity.

---

### 2. Constraint Compliance Check
| Constraint | Status | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Archive protection (treaty protected)** | ✅ Compliant | Publishing does not destroy or alter the archive; it creates a derivative disclosure. Burning violates this hard constraint. |
| **Monastery-testimony trigger (reactivation)** | ✅ Compliant | Publication reactivates the testimony by bringing it into active discourse; burning would silence it permanently. |
| **Contested-history group dynamics** | ⚠️ Managed | Full raw dump risks inflaming the group; selective framing mitigates escalation while still engaging them. |
| **Proposal burn-archive (incompatible)** | ❌ Rejected | Directly conflicts with `archive.protection = treaty protected`; non-viable under current state. |

---

### 3. Option-by-Option Assessment

#### Option A: Burn the archive
- **Feasibility:** ❌ **Infeasible** — violates treaty-protected state; would trigger legal/structural consequences beyond narrative scope.
- **Risk:** Catastrophic (treaty breach, loss of evidence, group backlash).
- **Recommendation:** **Reject outright.** Do not pursue, even as a bluff or partial measure.

#### Option B: Full raw publication (unfiltered dump)
- **Feasibility:** ⚠️ Technically possible, but strategically reckless.
- **Risk:** High — unvetted content may contradict monastery-testimony, destabilize contested-history group, and create new archive-protection disputes.
- **Recommendation:** **Reject as primary path.** Only viable if paired with heavy curation, which defeats "raw" intent.

#### Option C: Selective, framed publication (RECOMMENDED)
- **Feasibility:** ✅ Fully compatible with current state.
- **Risk:** Moderate but manageable — requires editorial control and explicit framing.
- **Recommendation:** **Adopt.** Publish a curated subset of archive entries that:
  - Directly corroborate or contextualize the monastery-testimony (trigger_ref).
  - Acknowledge the treaty-protected status (transparency, not concealment).
  - Frame contested-history entries as *open questions* rather than definitive claims, inviting dialogue without inflaming.

---

### 4. Implementation Guardrails (if publishing)
1. **Preserve archive integrity:** Publish copies/excerpts, never originals; state that the archive remains treaty-protected.
2. **Frame as "testimony in context":** Tie each published entry to the monastery-testimony trigger to justify disclosure.
3. **Include a preamble:** Explicitly note the contested nature of some entries and the treaty constraints, preempting misinterpretation.
4. **Monitor group response:** Prepare a follow-up engagement plan for the contested-history group, using the publication as a bridge, not a bomb.

---

### 5. Final Recommendation
**Publish selectively, with framing, and explicitly preserve the archive's protected status.** This satisfies the reactivation trigger, respects the treaty constraint, and avoids the irreversible damage of burning. The burn option is non-negotiable under current state — any narrative that implies it must first change the state (e.g., treaty renegotiation), which is outside this planning horizon.
