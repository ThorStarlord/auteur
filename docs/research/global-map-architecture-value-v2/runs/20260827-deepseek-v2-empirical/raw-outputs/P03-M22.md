# M22 — P03 rep 1

**Output Contract: Bounded Recommendation Analysis**  
**Series:** Archive of Lies  
**Book 4 Planning Question:** How to return monastery testimony to public memory without destroying the archive’s evidentiary chain?  
**Options Considered:** A) publish-verified-testimony | B) stage-protected-hearing  

---

### 1. Recommendation Summary  
**Recommended Option:** A) publish-verified-testimony — with a staged, limited public reveal (not a full hearing) as a secondary beat.  
**Rationale in one sentence:** Option A preserves the archive’s protected evidentiary chain (treaty intact, original secure) while allowing the testimony to re-enter public memory through an authenticated, controlled release—whereas Option B risks exposing the strongest records to political retraction and treaty breach.  

---

### 2. Tradeoff Analysis  

#### Option A: publish-verified-testimony  
**Pros:**  
- **Chain preserved:** Original testimony stays under treaty protection; only a verified copy is released.  
- **Controlled timing:** Allows the council to prepare a narrative before public pressure peaks.  
- **Reversibility:** If the council retracts again, the archive’s original remains untainted—testimony can be re-verified later.  
- **Narrative flexibility:** You can frame the release as “monastery’s own voice” rather than archive’s claim, reducing institutional liability.  

**Cons:**  
- **Delay:** Verification and authentication take time; public memory may be re-shaped by other actors in the interim.  
- **Partial impact:** Without archive evidence alongside, the testimony may be dismissed as isolated or forged (given the founding record is already known to be forged).  
- **Risk of “second retraction”:** Council could admit then retract again, weakening public trust in the testimony itself.  

#### Option B: stage-protected-hearing  
**Pros:**  
- **Immediate pressure:** Public hearing forces council to respond in real time; testimony gains dramatic weight.  
- **Contextual power:** Presenting testimony beside selected archive evidence creates a compelling, unified narrative.  

**Cons:**  
- **Reveals strongest records:** Under treaty, showing archive evidence in a public hearing may be interpreted as “use” or “exposure,” risking treaty violation and council retraction.  
- **Chain vulnerability:** If the hearing is challenged, the archive’s protected status could be questioned—destroying the very evidentiary chain you aim to preserve.  
- **Irreversible exposure:** Once strongest records are public, they cannot be un-shown; if the council retracts, the archive loses its protective mystique.  

---

### 3. Risk Assessment  

| Risk | Option A | Option B |  
|------|----------|----------|  
| Treaty breach | Low (original untouched) | High (public exposure of records) |  
| Council retraction | Medium (can be framed as independent testimony) | High (hearing invites political counterattack) |  
| Public trust erosion | Medium (delay may breed skepticism) | High (if hearing collapses, trust in both testimony and archive fails) |  
| Evidentiary chain damage | Low (copy released, original secure) | High (strongest records revealed, chain exposed) |  
| Narrative control | High (you control release timing and framing) | Low (hearing is adversarial, unpredictable) |  

---

### 4. Story-Level Consequences  

**If Option A:**  
- Book 4 can focus on the *process* of verification—monastery witnesses, scribes, authentication rituals—building suspense.  
- Public memory shifts gradually; council may attempt to discredit the copy, but the original remains a “trump card” for later books.  
- The lantern (repaired) can be used as a symbolic authentication tool, linking testimony to the archive’s own mythos without exposing records.  

**If Option B:**  
- Immediate dramatic confrontation, but you must then deal with the fallout of treaty violation and possible archive destruction—likely derailing the series’ central premise (protected archive).  
- The strongest records become public, so later books lose their “reveal” power; you’d need to invent new secrets to maintain tension.  

---

### 5. Final Recommendation  
**Choose Option A** as the primary strategy. To mitigate the delay, add a **limited, non-archive public reading** of the testimony (not a hearing, not beside archive evidence) in a neutral venue (e.g., monastery courtyard) with witnesses—this creates public memory without treaty exposure. Save Option B’s full hearing for a later book, only if the council’s retraction becomes untenable and you’re willing to sacrifice the archive’s protected status for a climax.  

**Bounded caveat:** This recommendation assumes the treaty’s definition of “use” excludes publishing verified copies. If the treaty forbids *any* public dissemination, then Option A fails—in that case, pivot to a **private council briefing** (not public) to re-establish testimony internally, then leak via third-party (monastery itself) to avoid archive liability.
