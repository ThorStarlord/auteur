**Recommendation Analysis: P03 — Return to Monastery Testimony Without Breaking Protected Archive**

---

### 1. Feasibility Assessment

**Verdict: Feasible, with a narrow but viable path.**

The planning intent is achievable under the current Decision Map constraints, but it requires precise handling of the **REL-06 reactivation** and **REL-07 state-compatibility** relationships. The core tension is that the monastery testimony (ST-F2) is reactivated *because* DIR-INT4 references it, but the archive-protected treaty (ST-F6) is under a preservation constraint (REL-07). The testimony must be used to *support* the treaty's protected status, not undermine it.

**Key constraints to respect:**
- **REL-07 (state-compatibility):** The treaty must remain preserved and protected. The testimony cannot be used to question the treaty's validity or expose it to revision.
- **REL-06 (reactivation):** The testimony's reactivation must have a clear, specific why-now tied to DIR-INT4's reference.
- **REL-09 (pressure grouping):** The testimony is part of the contested-history cluster (founding-record + admission-retracted + archive-protected). It cannot be isolated from this grouping.

---

### 2. Recommended Approach (Bounded Path)

**Strategy: Use the testimony as corroborative evidence for the treaty's protected status, not as a challenge to it.**

**Step 1 — Anchor the Reactivation (REL-06):**
- The monastery testimony is reactivated *because* DIR-INT4's reference creates a new investigative thread. The why-now is: DIR-INT4's reference exposes a gap in the public record that the testimony can fill — specifically, the testimony contains a detail that *pre-dates* the treaty and explains why the treaty's protection is historically justified.
- This gives the reactivation a specific, non-arbitrary trigger.

**Step 2 — Align with REL-07 (State-Compatibility):**
- Frame the testimony as *supporting* the treaty's protected status. The testimony should contain a historical fact that *reinforces* the treaty's legitimacy (e.g., a witness account of the treaty's signing conditions, or a detail that confirms the treaty's original intent).
- The testimony is not used to *retract* or *challenge* the treaty; it is used to *deepen* the historical context that justifies its protection.

**Step 3 — Maintain REL-09 (Cluster Grouping):**
- The testimony must be presented as part of the same contested-history cluster. It is not a standalone piece; it is one of three elements (founding-record, admission-retracted, archive-protected) that together form the contested-history pressure.
- The testimony's reactivation should *interlock* with the admission-retracted history (ST-F5) — e.g., the testimony corroborates the retraction's context, which in turn explains the treaty's necessity.

**Step 4 — Avoid Breaking the Archive (REL-07 Hard Constraint):**
- The testimony must *not* be used to suggest the treaty is fraudulent, incomplete, or in need of revision. If the testimony contradicts the treaty, it must be framed as a *misunderstanding* or *incomplete record* that the treaty's protection resolves — not as evidence against the treaty.
- The archive remains protected; the testimony is *added* to the archive's context, not used to *alter* it.

---

### 3. Specific Why-Now (Compact)

**For the cluster (REL-09):**
- The contested-history cluster (founding-record + admission-retracted + archive-protected) is active because a current political/legal pressure (e.g., a new claim or investigation) has resurfaced questions about the treaty's origins. The cluster is grouped because all three elements are being cited by different parties to support competing narratives.

**For the reactivated testimony (REL-06):**
- DIR-INT4's reference to the monastery testimony is the trigger. The reference is *new* — it appears in a recently discovered document or a new testimony from another source — and it points to a specific detail in the monastery's records that has never been cross-referenced with the treaty. This creates a why-now: the detail is now relevant because it fills a gap in the current investigation, and it can only be accessed by returning to the monastery.

---

### 4. Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Testimony contradicts treaty → breaks REL-07 | Frame testimony as *corroborative*; if contradiction exists, present it as a *historical misunderstanding* that the treaty's protection resolves. |
| Reactivation feels arbitrary → weak REL-06 | Tie reactivation directly to DIR-INT4's reference; make the reference a *specific* document or event, not a general mention. |
| Testimony isolated from cluster → weak REL-09 | Explicitly connect the testimony to the admission-retracted history (ST-F5) and the founding-record (ST-F1) in the narrative. |
| Archive protection appears threatened → violates REL-07 | The testimony is *added* to the archive's context, never used to *modify* or *challenge* the treaty's protected status. |

---

### 5. Non-Negotiable Boundaries

- **Do not** use the testimony to question the treaty's validity.
- **Do not** allow the testimony to trigger a re-examination of the treaty's terms.
- **Do not** isolate the testimony from the contested-history cluster.
- **Do not** leave the reactivation without a specific, DIR-INT4-driven why-now.
