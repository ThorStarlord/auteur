**Recommendation Analysis (P01)**

---

### 1. **Decision Context (Filtered)**
- **Active Dispositions:** `contested-history` (DIR-SC1) and `founding-record forged` (ST-F1) are the only live constraints for Book2.  
- **Excluded (with why-now):**  
  - `commitment-falsifier unresolved` (DIR-SC2) → deferred to Book2’s end; not a current driver.  
  - `monastery-testimony preserved` (ST-F2) → dormant; setup for Book4, no current pressure.  
  - `broken-lantern` (ST-I1) → irrelevant; never supports continuity.  
- **Trigger:** `DIR-INT2` activates `founding-record` as a live plot lever.  
- **Relationship Pressure:** `REL-01` (contested-history) pushes Book2 toward public correction; `REL-02` (founding-record) sets up the falsifier reveal.

---

### 2. **Planning Intent vs. Active Dispositions**
- **Intent:** Make the forged founding record matter to *lived memory* (i.e., how characters experience, resist, or are shaped by the lie).  
- **Alignment Check:**  
  - `contested-history` requires the lie to be publicly challenged or internally doubted.  
  - `founding-record forged` requires the record to be *operational*—not just a background fact, but a force that alters choices, relationships, or stakes.  
- **Gap:** The intent is currently *thematic* (lived memory) but lacks a *mechanical* hook to bind it to the active dispositions. Without a concrete action, the record stays inert.

---

### 3. **Bounded Recommendation (P01)**
**Option:** Anchor the forged record to a *specific, irreversible consequence* that forces a character to act against their own memory or testimony.

**Why this fits:**
- **Activates `contested-history`:** The record’s forgery is exposed *through* a lived-memory contradiction (e.g., a witness remembers the founding differently, but the record overrides them). This makes the lie *felt* in daily life, not just abstract.
- **Uses `founding-record forged` as a pressure valve:** The record isn’t just a document; it’s a *weapon* used to silence or coerce. For example, a character’s land claim, inheritance, or identity is denied because the forged record says otherwise.
- **Triggers `DIR-INT2` naturally:** The trigger fires when a character *tries to correct* the record but finds the forgery is legally or socially entrenched—forcing a choice between compliance and rebellion.

**Concrete move (bounded to P01):**  
Introduce a scene where a minor character (or a protagonist’s ally) is denied a right or status *because* the forged record is cited as authoritative. The protagonist must decide whether to challenge the record (risking exposure of the larger lie) or accept the injustice (preserving the lie’s stability). This makes the record *matter* to lived memory by making it a direct obstacle.

---

### 4. **Constraints & Risks**
- **Do not resolve `commitment-falsifier` yet**—keep it open for Book2’s climax. The P01 move should *deepen* the falsifier’s shadow, not close it.
- **Avoid over-using `monastery-testimony`**—it’s dormant; referencing it now would violate the why-now filter.
- **Risk:** If the record’s consequence is too abstract (e.g., “it changes the historical narrative”), it won’t land as *lived*. Keep it personal, material, or relational.

---

### 5. **Next Step (P02)**
After P01, test the move against `REL-01` (pressure trajectory) and `REL-02` (setup for falsifier). Ask: *Does this consequence escalate the public correction pressure, and does it plant a specific clue for the falsifier’s eventual reveal?* If yes, proceed. If no, adjust the consequence to be more entangled with both relationships.

---

**Verdict:** Recommend the **irreversible-consequence anchor** for P01. It binds the forged record to lived memory, activates both active dispositions, and sets a clear trajectory for Book2’s correction arc.
