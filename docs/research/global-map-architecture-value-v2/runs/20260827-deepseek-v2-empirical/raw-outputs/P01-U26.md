# U26 — P01 rep 3

**Recommendation Analysis — Book 2, P01 (Repeated-Map-Focus-v2-r1)**

---

### 1. **Primary Recommendation**
**Option A — “The Forger’s Confession” (Lived-Memory Catalyst)**

---

### 2. **Reasoning (Bounded to Provided Context)**

**Why Option A is optimal given the constraints:**

- **Directly activates the active consequence** (`founding-record=forged`). The planning intent is to make the forged record matter to *lived memory* — not just institutional history. A confession from the original forger (or a descendant) converts a static archival fact into a personal, emotional, and community-level rupture. This is the most efficient path from “forged document” to “lived memory” because it introduces a human agent whose memory directly contradicts the record.

- **Respects the dormant entry** (`monastery-testimony=preserved`). The confession can be framed as *corroborating* or *challenging* the monastery’s preserved testimony, but the monastery testimony remains dormant — it is not required to be activated. This keeps the current-state evidence intact (dormant stays dormant) while allowing the forger’s confession to stand as the new active driver.

- **Avoids the irrelevant entry** (`broken-lantern`). Option A does not require the lantern to be repaired, lit, or referenced. It remains irrelevant, preserving the bounded scope.

- **Creates a natural Book 2 arc**: The confession forces the protagonist to re-examine the founding record, which was previously treated as authoritative. This generates conflict between institutional truth (archive) and personal truth (memory), which is the core of the “contested-history” theme carried from Book 1.

---

### 3. **Why Other Options Were Rejected (Brief)**

- **Option B — “The Monastery’s Hidden Copy”**: This would require activating the dormant `monastery-testimony` entry, which violates the current-state evidence (dormant must remain dormant unless explicitly triggered). It also shifts focus away from the forged record itself toward a secondary source, diluting the planning intent.

- **Option C — “The Lantern’s Engraving”**: This requires the `broken-lantern` to become relevant, which is explicitly marked as irrelevant. It would introduce a deus ex machina and violate the bounded scope.

- **Option D — “The Archivist’s Dilemma”**: While plausible, this keeps the conflict *institutional* (archivist vs. record) rather than *lived* (community/personal memory). It risks remaining abstract and does not fully satisfy the planning intent of making the forgery matter to lived memory.

---

### 4. **Implementation Notes (Non-Authoritative)**

- **Introduce the forger as a named character** with a specific motive (e.g., political pressure, family protection, or ideological alignment). This makes the confession feel earned, not arbitrary.
- **Anchor the confession to a specific scene** (e.g., a deathbed confession, a discovered letter, or a public recantation) to give it dramatic weight.
- **Ensure the confession does not fully resolve the contested history** — leave ambiguity about whether the forger’s memory is reliable, so Book 2 can sustain tension.
- **Do not reference the broken lantern** in any scene; keep it fully irrelevant.

---

### 5. **Risk / Mitigation**

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Confession feels like a plot device | Give the forger a personal stake in the founding record (e.g., their family’s honor, a lost loved one). |
| Lived memory becomes too localized (one person) | Have the confession ripple outward — the protagonist must decide whether to share it, and with whom. |
| Contradicts Book 1’s established tone | Keep the confession grounded in the same narrative voice and world logic as Book 1. |

---

### 6. **Final Verdict**
**Option A** is the only option that satisfies all three constraints simultaneously: it activates the active consequence, respects the dormant entry, and ignores the irrelevant entry — while directly fulfilling the planning intent of making the forged founding record matter to lived memory.
