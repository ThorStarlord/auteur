# A74 — P04 rep 2

**Output Contract: Bounded Recommendation Analysis**  
**Series:** Archive of Lies  
**Book 4 Planning Question:** How to bring monastery testimony back into public memory without losing the archive’s evidentiary chain?  
**Options Considered:** A) burn-archive | B) publish-verified-testimony  

---

### 1. Recommendation  
**Option B — publish-verified-testimony** is the recommended path. It preserves the archive’s evidentiary chain (a core treaty obligation) while reintroducing the monastery testimony through a defensible, verifiable public release. Option A is rejected because it destroys the very institution the series has spent three books protecting, and it would make the testimony *appear* self-serving rather than corroborated.

---

### 2. Tradeoff Analysis  

**Option A — burn-archive**  
- **Upside:** Removes any competing record; monastery testimony becomes the sole surviving account, forcing public attention.  
- **Downside:** Irreversibly breaks the treaty-protected archive; destroys the evidentiary chain (no cross-reference, no provenance); makes the testimony look like a convenient replacement rather than a truth; alienates institutional allies; likely triggers legal/political retaliation from treaty signatories.  
- **Narrative cost:** Undermines the series’ central tension (protecting the archive) and turns the protagonist into an arsonist, not a truth-teller.  

**Option B — publish-verified-testimony**  
- **Upside:** Keeps archive intact; uses authentication (e.g., handwriting, chain-of-custody, corroborating marginalia) to give testimony independent weight; allows public release *without* violating treaty (treaty protects archive, not testimony); creates a two-source evidentiary loop (testimony + archive’s forged founding record now exposed as false by comparison).  
- **Downside:** Delays release (authentication takes time); risks partial suppression or spin by council; requires careful framing so testimony doesn’t appear to “replace” the archive but *correct* it.  
- **Narrative cost:** Requires a subplot of verification (investigators, skeptics, false leads) — but this is a strength, not a weakness, for a mystery-driven series.  

---

### 3. Risk Assessment  

**Option A risks:**  
- **Catastrophic:** Loss of archive (plot irreversibility), treaty breach, loss of institutional trust, testimony seen as self-serving.  
- **Mitigation possible?** No — once burned, cannot un-burn.  

**Option B risks:**  
- **Moderate:** Authentication could be challenged; council may try to discredit the testimony; delay may allow opponents to muddy the waters.  
- **Mitigation:** Use multiple independent authenticators (e.g., a rival monastery, a neutral scholar, a former council member who defected); release in stages (first a summary, then full text with annotations); tie testimony to the already-broken lantern (symbolic repair) to show continuity.  

---

### 4. Assumptions & Constraints  

**Assumptions:**  
- The treaty protects the archive’s *physical and evidentiary integrity*, not the *monastery testimony* (which is separate).  
- The testimony is authentic and can withstand scrutiny if properly verified.  
- Public memory can be shifted through a credible, documented release rather than only through destruction of competing records.  

**Constraints:**  
- Cannot break the treaty (accepted history: archive.protection = treaty protected).  
- Cannot erase the forged founding record (it exists; must be addressed, not ignored).  
- Must maintain the series’ theme: truth emerges through layered evidence, not through erasure.  

---

### 5. Implementation Steps (if Option B chosen)  

1. **Secure the testimony physically** — move it to a neutral, third-party location (e.g., a merchant guild’s vault) to prevent council seizure.  
2. **Assemble a verification panel** — three independent experts: a paleographer, a historian of the monastery, and a former council archivist who resigned in protest.  
3. **Publish a “preliminary finding”** — not the full testimony, but a summary of its existence, provenance, and key claims, with a public invitation for scrutiny.  
4. **Release the full testimony** with a companion document that cross-references the forged founding record, showing where the forgery diverges from the testimony.  
5. **Frame the archive as still authoritative** — the archive remains the repository; the testimony is a *correction* to one record, not a replacement of the archive.  
6. **Use the lantern repair** as a symbolic parallel — the broken lantern (Book 3) is now fixed; the broken record (founding) is now corrected.  

---

### 6. Final Recommendation Statement  
Choose **Option B**. It honors the series’ core premise (the archive must survive) while allowing the monastery testimony to re-enter public memory through verification, not destruction. The delay is a feature, not a bug — it builds suspense, allows for adversarial scrutiny, and ultimately makes the testimony *stronger* because it survives challenge. Option A would end the series’ central conflict in a single, irreversible act of self-sabotage.
