# P12 — P02 rep 2

**Recommendation: Option B (P02-B)**

---

### 1. Decision Context

- **Active Pressure:** Contested-history (REL-01)
- **Current State:** Admission-retracted (ST-F5) is current via REL-04 supersession; named-falsifier (ST-F3) is resolved via REL-03
- **Trigger:** DIR-INT3 activates the currentness of the retracted admission
- **Planning Intent:** Respond to council's accepted retraction
- **Constraint:** Rationale must jointly use (a) current retraction and (b) resolved falsifier

---

### 2. Option Analysis (Bounded)

| Option | Fit with Current Retraction (ST-F5) | Fit with Resolved Falsifier (ST-F3/REL-03) | Risk to Series Pressure (REL-01) | Transition Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Weak – treats retraction as isolated event; does not leverage falsifier resolution | Partial – references falsifier but not as resolved foundation | High – leaves contested-history unresolved; council may re-open | Low – no forward path to ST-F6 |
| **B** | Strong – uses retraction as the *current* public stance, anchored by the resolved falsifier as the underlying proof | Strong – explicitly builds on REL-03 resolution as the stable ground beneath the retraction | Moderate – keeps pressure alive but managed; retraction is defensible, not fragile | High – creates bridge to ST-F6 (next treaty) via REL-05 |
| **C** | Moderate – uses retraction but over-relies on it without falsifier grounding | Weak – treats falsifier as background, not as resolved evidence | High – retraction alone is vulnerable to re-contestation | Moderate – stalls without new treaty logic |

---

### 3. Recommendation

**Select Option B.**

**Rationale (joint use of current retraction + resolved falsifier):**
- The **current retraction (ST-F5)** is the operative public position—the council has accepted it, and DIR-INT3 makes it active. Option B responds directly to this accepted state.
- The **resolved falsifier (ST-F3 via REL-03)** provides the evidentiary backbone: the named falsifier was definitively resolved, so the retraction is not an empty reversal but a grounded correction. Option B pairs these two: the retraction is *current* because the falsifier is *resolved*—the falsifier's resolution is what makes the retraction credible and stable.
- This joint use prevents the retraction from appearing as a mere political retreat (which would re-ignite REL-01) and instead frames it as a fact-based correction, keeping contested-history pressure active but contained.
- Option B also sets up **REL-05** (not yet active) as the natural next step: the retraction, backed by the resolved falsifier, explains the need for the next treaty—making the transition to ST-F6 plausible.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- **Option A** fails because it does not bind the retraction to the resolved falsifier, leaving the contested-history pressure unmanaged and the retraction unsupported.
- **Option C** fails because it under-weights the resolved falsifier, making the retraction appear arbitrary and vulnerable to re-contestation.

- Foreshadow REL-05 by noting that the retraction's evidentiary basis will require formalization in a subsequent treaty—this keeps ST-F6 on the horizon without activating it prematurely.
- Avoid re-opening ST-F4 (public-admission) as a live issue; treat it as superseded history only.
