**Recommendation Analysis: P03 — Return to Monastery Testimony Without Breaking Protected Archive**

---

### 1. Decision Context (Bounded)

- **Series Pressure:** Contested-history active (REL-01). The narrative must engage with historical dispute without resolving it into a single, authoritative truth.
- **Active Transitions (Book 4):**
  - **ST-F1** (founding-record forged) — grouped history, contested.
  - **ST-F2** (monastery-testimony reactivated) — **why-now** must be specific and internally driven (DIR-INT4 reference).
  - **ST-F5** (admission-retracted history) — explains treaty, causal to treaty (REL-05).
  - **ST-F6** (archive-protected treaty) — protected by current constraint (REL-07 state-compatibility).
- **Active Relationships:**
  - **REL-09** — founding-record + admission-retracted + archive-protected form one contested-history cluster; treaty is present evidence.
  - **REL-10** — thematic tension across the cluster.
  - **REL-06** — reactivation of monastery testimony (dormant → active).
- **Planning Intent:** Return to monastery testimony **without breaking** the protected archive (i.e., do not invalidate, expose, or compromise ST-F6's preservation constraint).

### 3. Recommended Approach (Option C — Procedural Key)

**Narrative Logic:**
- The archive's protection of the treaty (ST-F6) is not arbitrary — it is governed by an internal rule (REL-07). In Book 4, an anomaly emerges: the archive's own index (DIR-INT4) references the monastery testimony as a *procedural prerequisite* for the protection to remain valid.
- The monastery testimony is **not** about the treaty's content. It is about the *method of preservation* — a ritual, a cipher, or a condition that the archive's protection mechanism depends on.
- Reactivation (REL-06) is therefore **internally forced**: the archive itself points to the monastery as the only source that can explain why the protection exists. This satisfies the "why-now" with a specific, internal trigger (DIR-INT4), not an external event.

**Why This Does Not Break the Protected Archive:**
- The testimony is used to *maintain* the archive's protection, not to question it. The characters must retrieve the testimony to *keep the treaty safe* (state-compatibility, REL-07).
- The contested-history tension (REL-01) is preserved because the testimony's *content* remains ambiguous — it explains the *mechanism* but not the *truth* of the founding record (ST-F1) or the retraction (ST-F5). The cluster (REL-09) stays contested; only the *protection* is clarified.

**Thematic Tension (REL-10):**
- The tension shifts from "which history is true?" to "what does it mean to preserve a history we cannot verify?" — a richer, more specific iteration of the series' contested-history pressure.

---

### 4. Bounded Recommendation

**Adopt Option C.** Return to the monastery testimony as a **procedural key** to the archive's protection mechanism, reactivated by an internal archival reference (DIR-INT4). This:
- Satisfies the planning intent (return to testimony without breaking archive).
- Provides a compact, specific why-now for both the cluster and the reactivation.
- Preserves contested-history pressure (REL-01) and thematic tension (REL-10).
- Reinforces, rather than threatens, the protected treaty (ST-F6).

**Non-authoritative note:** If the series later requires the archive to be *challenged* (not broken), Option C leaves that door open — the testimony's procedural role can be revealed as incomplete, forcing a future, more dangerous confrontation. But for P03, this is the safest, most coherent path.
