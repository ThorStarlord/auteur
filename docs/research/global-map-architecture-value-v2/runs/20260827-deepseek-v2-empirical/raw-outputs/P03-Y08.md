### 1. Recommendation Summary
**Recommended Option:** A) publish-verified-testimony  
**Confidence:** High (85%)  
**Core Rationale:** Option A preserves the archive’s evidentiary chain (treaty-protected original remains secure) while allowing the monastery testimony to re-enter public memory through an authenticated, independent channel. Option B risks collapsing the archive’s protective treaty by exposing its strongest records to adversarial scrutiny, which could trigger council retraction or treaty breach.

---

### 2. Option Analysis

#### **Option A: publish-verified-testimony**
- **Primary Benefit:** Maintains the archive’s integrity as a sealed, treaty-protected source. The testimony is authenticated externally (e.g., via monastic seals, cross-referenced copies, or witness corroboration) and published as a *separate* evidentiary artifact, not as an archive record. This creates a parallel public narrative without weakening the archive’s legal shield.
- **Secondary Benefit:** Delays release, allowing time to craft a narrative that aligns with the council’s retracted admission without forcing a direct confrontation. The delay can be framed as “due diligence” to preempt accusations of fabrication.
- **Key Tradeoff:** The delay may allow opponents to fill the vacuum with counter-narratives. Also, if the testimony is published *without* archive linkage, it may be dismissed as unverified hearsay—unless the authentication process is visibly rigorous.
- **Risk Mitigation:** Pair the publication with a public statement that the archive remains protected *because* the testimony is now independently verified, thereby reinforcing the treaty’s purpose.

#### **Option B: stage-protected-hearing**
- **Primary Benefit:** Immediate public pressure—showing testimony beside archive evidence creates a dramatic, undeniable juxtaposition that could force the council to re-admit the truth.
- **Secondary Benefit:** Uses the treaty as a stage, implying the archive is so secure that even its strongest records can be displayed without risk.
- **Key Tradeoff:** This reveals the archive’s most sensitive records, inviting scrutiny of their provenance. If any record is challenged (e.g., the forged founding record), the entire chain collapses—and the treaty may be voided by the council as “no longer protecting a credible archive.”
- **Risk Mitigation:** None sufficient. The treaty’s power depends on the archive being *unexamined*; exposing records invites examination, which is the archive’s fatal weakness.

---

### 3. Bounded Recommendation
**Select Option A**, with the following constraints:
- **Authentication must be independent** of the archive itself (e.g., monastic seals, third-party historians, or surviving witnesses) to avoid circularity.
- **Publication timing** should be tied to a political event (e.g., a council session) to maximize impact without appearing reactive.
- **Do not reference the archive’s contents** in the published testimony—only state that the archive remains protected and that the testimony stands on its own merits.
- **Prepare a fallback narrative** if the council attempts to discredit the testimony: emphasize that the archive’s protection is *unaffected* by external publications, preserving the treaty’s legal standing.

**Explicitly reject Option B** because it converts the archive from a protected repository into a contested exhibit, risking the treaty and the entire evidentiary chain.

---

### 4. Contingency Notes
- **If Option A fails** (testimony dismissed): pivot to a “leak” of a *single* non-critical archive record (not the strongest) to re-ignite public interest, while maintaining the treaty’s core protection.
- **If the council preemptively attacks the testimony:** release a pre-prepared statement from the monastery confirming the testimony’s authenticity, and remind the council that their retracted admission is a matter of public record—without invoking the archive.
- **If the treaty is threatened by external actors:** invoke the treaty’s own arbitration clause to delay any forced inspection, buying time for the published testimony to gain independent traction.

---

### 5. Final Verdict
**Proceed with Option A.** The archive’s protection is the linchpin of the entire evidentiary chain; Option B sacrifices that linchpin for short-term pressure. Option A allows the testimony to re-enter public memory as a *verified external truth*, while the archive remains a silent, protected guarantor—not a target.
