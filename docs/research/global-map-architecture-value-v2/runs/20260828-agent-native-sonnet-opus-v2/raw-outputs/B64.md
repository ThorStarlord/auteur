Recommendation: **witness-account**

Why: The series commitment `contested-history` requires every Book to expose conflict between official history and lived memory — witness-account does this directly by putting the living witness's testimony against the forged record. This also builds on Book 1 Realization that the monastery preserves a testimony and Book 1 Realization that the founding record was forged: the material already exists to stage this confrontation. Book 1 Direction "The Missing Ledger" established that the central conflict is authenticating the record while choosing witnesses, and that stakes involve publishing too soon destroying witnesses versus waiting erasing truth — witness-account continues that same tension into Book 2 rather than deferring it, keeping lived memory (not institutions) at the center, consistent with the series pressure that public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness.

Principal tradeoff: Centering the witness's account makes lived memory central to Book 2's exposure of the fraud, but it exposes the witness earlier — heightening the risk named in the series pressure (that surfacing truth gives archivists reason to erase witnesses) and raising the stakes described in Book 1 Direction around publishing too soon.

What I excluded: cover-up-trace, because tracing the institutional cover-up keeps institutional history central rather than lived memory, which sits less directly with `contested-history`'s requirement to expose conflict with lived memory, and it delays the witness's account rather than using the testimony already established as preserved in Book 1. I did not rely on the broken lantern fact or the falsifier-identification commitment, as neither bears on which of these two options better serves lived memory in Book 2.

This is a non-authoritative planning recommendation — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
