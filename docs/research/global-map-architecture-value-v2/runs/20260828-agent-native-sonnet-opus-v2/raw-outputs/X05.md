**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The series commitment `contested-history` requires every Book to expose the conflict between official history and lived memory — the monastery testimony is exactly this lived-memory material, so surfacing it satisfies the standing commitment. But the current state carries two facts that make sequencing matter: the archive is treaty-protected as "the only evidentiary chain" (Book 3), and the council previously admitted the record was falsified only to retract that admission (Book 2). That retraction shows public disclosure of contested material has already backfired once here — an admission was made and then withdrawn, which is the series pressure ("every public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness") playing out directly. Publishing the testimony only after authentication keeps the treaty-protected chain intact while still honoring `contested-history`, without repeating the retraction pattern by putting unverified testimony into a live, contestable hearing.

**Principal tradeoff:** Delay. Verification takes time, and during that time the testimony stays out of public memory — leaving the series open question ("Can truth survive without becoming instrument of power?") unresolved a while longer. That's the cost of protecting the chain.

**What I excluded:** stage-protected-hearing, because presenting the testimony beside "selected" archive evidence necessarily foregrounds which records are strongest — this is the kind of exposure that previously triggered the council's retraction, and risks giving "hidden archivists reason to erase another witness" (series pressure) before verification can secure the testimony's standing. I did not weigh any option beyond the two presented, and did not treat the lantern repair as relevant to this choice — it bears on the archive's physical state, not on how testimony re-enters public memory.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
