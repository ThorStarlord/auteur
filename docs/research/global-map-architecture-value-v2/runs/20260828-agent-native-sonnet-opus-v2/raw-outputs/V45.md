Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony

Why: The series commitment contested-history requires every Book to expose conflict between official history and lived memory, which favors surfacing the monastery testimony rather than leaving it dormant. But the current state shows the council already retracted its admission and the archive is treaty protected as the "only evidentiary chain" — established in Book 3's Realization. Since the founding record is forged and the retraction already inflames tension around the archive's authority, introducing the testimony through a route that keeps the original archive secure avoids compounding the fragility the retraction created. Authenticating and publishing the testimony while the protected archive keeps the original secure honors both the exposure obligation (contested-history) and the protective commitment established in Book 3, without requiring the treaty-protected archive to be opened or its evidentiary chain disturbed.

Principal tradeoff: Preserving the evidentiary chain comes at the cost of delayed release — the testimony's public impact is slower and less immediate than a direct confrontation would produce.

What I deliberately excluded: stage-protected-hearing, because presenting the testimony beside selected archive evidence risks exposing the archive's strongest records at a moment when the council has just retracted its admission — a combination that plausibly gives "hidden archivists reason to erase another witness" (the series pressure). I did not treat the lantern repair or breakage as relevant to this choice, since nothing in the accepted history ties the lantern's condition to testimony release or archive access. I also did not invent any reason the treaty protection could be waived or reinterpreted — the facts state only that it protects the archive as the sole evidentiary chain, nothing about conditions for exception.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
