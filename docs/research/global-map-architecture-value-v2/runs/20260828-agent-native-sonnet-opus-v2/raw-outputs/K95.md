Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony.

Why: The series commitment contested-history requires exposing conflict between official history and lived memory, not eliminating the record of that conflict — destroying the archive would remove the very evidentiary chain the treaty was created to protect (per the Book 3 treaty that made archive protection = treaty protected, and the council's retracted admission, which still needs a documented record to be checked against). The monastery testimony has been preserved since Book 1 and remains available to authenticate rather than substitute for the archive. Publishing the testimony while keeping the archive intact upholds both the founding-record forgery exposure begun in Book 1-2 and the protection commitment established in Book 3, satisfying the series pressure (hidden archivists erasing witnesses) by adding a witness account without giving them a justification (archive loss) to point to.

Principal tradeoff: publish-verified-testimony preserves the evidentiary chain but delays public release until verification is complete, leaving a window during which the testimony remains unavailable to the public and archivist opposition may act.

Excluded: burn-archive — rejected because it directly negates the treaty-protected status of the archive as the only evidentiary chain (Book 3 Realization) and would destroy the same forged founding record whose falsification chain (identified in Book 2) is needed to authenticate the testimony in the first place; it also contradicts contested-history, which calls for exposing conflict, not deleting one side of the record. I did not weigh the lantern repair/breakage facts, as they bear on physical archive events, not on the public-memory/evidentiary-chain question posed here.
