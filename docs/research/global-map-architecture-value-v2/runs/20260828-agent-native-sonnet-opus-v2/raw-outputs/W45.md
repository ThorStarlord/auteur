Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony.

Why: The series commitment "contested-history" requires exposing conflict between official history and lived memory, not erasing evidence of it. The monastery testimony has been preserved (Book 1) precisely so it could eventually surface; destroying the archive would discard the treaty-protected evidentiary chain established in Book 3 to protect the archive after the council's retracted admission — undoing that protection contradicts the planning intent to return the testimony "without breaking the protected archive." The falsified founding record (Book 1/2) and the council's retraction (Book 2/3) mean the archive itself is part of the contested history the series promise points at; publishing the testimony alongside the still-intact archive keeps both sides of that conflict visible, which serves the promise better than eliminating one side.

Principal tradeoff: Preserving the evidentiary chain requires delaying public release of the testimony until verification is complete — truth-telling is slowed in exchange for keeping the archive's authority intact.

Excluded as not relevant: burn-archive — while it would make the testimony unavoidable, it destroys the treaty-protected archive established in Book 3 specifically to prevent this kind of loss, directly conflicting with the stated planning intent and with no accepted fact indicating the archive itself should be sacrificed. The archive lantern's repaired condition was also excluded as not relevant to this choice — it is a physical detail with no bearing on the evidentiary or publication question.

This is a planning recommendation only; it does not constitute a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
