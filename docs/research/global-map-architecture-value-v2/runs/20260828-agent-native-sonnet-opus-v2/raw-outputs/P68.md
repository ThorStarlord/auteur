Recommendation: force-council-hearing

Why: The series commitment `contested-history` requires every Book to expose conflict between official history and lived memory — a public hearing that forces the council to answer, using the now-named falsifier as leverage, keeps that exposure of conflict active and visible. The council already admitted the archive record was falsified once (Book 2) before retracting; that prior admission shows the council can be compelled to speak on the record, and the falsifier being named gives Book 3 the concrete instrument to force another answer rather than starting from nothing. This also sits closer to the series pressure — that public correction gives hidden archivists reason to erase another witness — since compelling the council to answer keeps the confrontation on institutional terms rather than immediately exposing an individual witness.

Principal tradeoff: The council controls the forum and timing of the hearing, so accountability stays centered on the falsified record and the retraction, but the council retains procedural power to shape, delay, or blunt the confrontation.

Excluded: publish-witness-account — while it would give the witness an independent, unretractable record, the series pressure explicitly ties public correction to retaliation risk against witnesses, and nothing in the accepted history establishes protections or standing for the witness to absorb that exposure; recommending it would mean introducing an unsupported assumption about the witness's safety or resources rather than reasoning from accepted facts.

Facts/commitments relied on: series commitment `contested-history`; series pressure about public correction and erasure of witnesses; Book 2 Realization that the council publicly admitted the falsification; Book 2 Realization that the council later retracted; current state that the falsifier is named.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not create Book Direction or canon and does not modify Canonical State.
