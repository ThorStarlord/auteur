**Recommendation: publish-verified-testimony**

**Why:** The series commitment `contested-history` requires Book 4 to expose conflict between official history and lived memory — the monastery testimony (preserved since Book 1) is exactly that lived-memory counterweight to the forged founding record. The council's admission-then-retraction shows public correction is fragile and reversible under this series' pressure (public corrections invite erasure of witnesses). Book 3's treaty establishes the archive as the sole protected evidentiary chain — that protection was the explicit want behind Direction "The Protected Archive" and is now accepted fact (`archive.protection = treaty protected`). Authenticating and publishing the testimony while leaving the original secured under the treaty satisfies the exposure mandate without placing the only evidentiary chain at risk, honoring both the Book 3 outcome and the series pressure about erasure following public correction.

**Principal tradeoff:** Preserves the evidentiary chain intact, but delays public release of the testimony — leaving the series' open question ("can truth survive without becoming instrument of power?") unresolved a while longer, and the retracted council admission uncorrected in the interim.

**Excluded:** stage-protected-hearing — presenting testimony beside selected archive evidence would surface the archive's strongest records publicly, directly courting the series pressure (visibility invites erasure) and risking the treaty-protected chain the Book 3 Realization just secured; the immediacy it buys doesn't offset that exposure risk given the retraction precedent. I did not invoke the repaired lantern or the falsifier's identity (Book 2) — neither bears on how testimony re-enters public memory, only on how the forgery was discovered.

This is a planning recommendation only — it does not constitute a Book Direction and does not modify Canonical State.
