## 5. Owner's original decision question

> As the D16–D23 operative network enters its next phase, should HW and Eclipse
> retain tight operational centralization for coordination and effectiveness, or
> strengthen distributed authority, independent custody, and explicit
> exit/refusal rights even at some cost to speed?

## 6. Source context used to derive frames

The accepted Series Direction says the network expands through distinct methods
while testing whether integration preserves agency. Its declared pressure is:
every successful integration increases coordination and capability while making
independent refusal harder to imagine.

Relevant accepted meanings include:

- D18 / Calista: mutual instrumentalization alongside unresolved authentic
  doubt; useful integration may become jurisdiction; no final answer to whether
  the network is a cage.
- D19 / Wren revised realization: shared intelligence, logistics, emergency
  backup, and interoperability without absorption of Wren's jurisdiction.
- D20 / Elena: a platform restores documentation and care capacity while
  concentrating access; restoration can become dependence.
- D23 / Iris: transactional access makes dependence explicit; the integrated
  system knows and connects more than local actors; the cage question remains
  unresolved.

These are evidence inputs, not proof that any one governance arrangement is
correct.

## 7. Candidate Decision Frames

### Frame A — governance configuration under shared infrastructure

**Decision question:** What allocation of coordination and authority lets the
network retain capability without making independent refusal merely nominal?

**Possible configurations:**

1. Tightly centralize operational coordination and related authority.
2. Federate shared infrastructure while retaining local jurisdiction, custody,
   and refusal boundaries.
3. Distribute authority and custody with explicit exit/refusal rights, accepting
   the owner-stated possibility of coordination or speed cost.

**Directly stated decision dimensions:** operational centralization,
coordination, effectiveness, distributed authority, independent custody,
exit/refusal rights, and speed cost.

**Interpretively grounded dimensions:** agency, jurisdiction, dependency, and
shared infrastructure.

**Candidate core tension:** collective capability through integration versus
meaningful independent agency or jurisdiction.

**Assumptions to test:** operational centralization entails centralized
authority; local custody entails agency; explicit rights are exercisable; one
topology should govern every function; centralization itself causes speed or
effectiveness.

### Frame B — safeguards and boundaries within shared capability

**Decision question:** Which functions, decisions, custody boundaries, and
refusal rights should remain local while the network shares coordination and
capability?

This frame treats the question as more than a central-versus-distributed binary.
It makes room for centralized coordination with local authority/custody,
centrally maintained opt-in services, federated interoperability, and
function-specific allocation.

**Candidate tensions:** coordination versus jurisdiction; shared support versus
dependence/control; system knowledge versus local custody; speed versus usable
refusal; integration versus preservation of distinct functions.

**Uncertainties:** custody of what; refusal by whom and of what; exit from
which relationship; who holds which authority; which functions actually require
central coordination; what effectiveness means in this decision.

### Frame C — narrative stress test rather than institutional design

**Decision question:** What concrete Book 9 pressure should test whether an
operative can refuse, retain custody, or exit while the network faces a real
coordination demand, and what consequence should remain unresolved?

This is an alternative reading, not a claim about the owner's intent. It keeps
the cage question and D18's ambiguity alive by treating institutional
configurations as story variables rather than as a final constitution to choose.

**Candidate stakes:** whether the story can demonstrate the cost and limits of
shared capability without prematurely resolving the question of agency,
jurisdiction, or the cage.

## 8. Decision-Frame Socratic Critic findings

An independent critic reviewed the original question, source context, and the
three candidate frames without being told which frame the lead preferred.

| Finding | Evidence ceiling |
| --- | --- |
| Coordination, effectiveness, centralization, distributed authority, custody, exit/refusal, and speed cost are directly supplied by the owner question. | SUPPORTED |
| Agency, jurisdiction, dependence, and shared infrastructure arise from the Series promise/pressure and D18–D23 evidence. | SUPPORTED |
| Frame A silently equates operational centralization with centralized authority. | SPECULATIVE |
| A network-wide central-versus-distributed binary is incomplete: D19 already supplies shared support with retained jurisdiction. | SUPPORTED |
| Joint, conditional, revocable, and overlapping authority are missing from Frames A and B. | INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED |
| The umbrella tension is capability through integration versus preserved agency, but decision-level mechanisms must remain separate. | SUPPORTED |
| Formal rights versus practical ability to use them is grounded but derived language; merely nominal is not an accepted source claim. | INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED |
| Centralization being faster or more effective, distribution imposing a speed penalty, and local custody guaranteeing agency are not historical conclusions. | SPECULATIVE |
| Frame C better preserves the accepted refusal to settle the cage question. | INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED |

The critic further identified that the eventual relevance role of the same
source would change with the frame: D19 may be a federation precedent under
Frame A, a boundary requirement under Frame B, or a fragile exception/stress
condition under Frame C. This is why no six-case bridge analysis has yet been
performed.

## 9. Lead reconciliation before Owner Gate A

and C because they organize the same evidence differently and would materially
change later relevance claims.

4. Preserve the accepted refusal to settle whether the network is a cage.

   to distinguish evidence from conclusion, not as permission to invent a
