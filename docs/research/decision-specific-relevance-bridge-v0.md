## 5. Owner's original decision question

> As the D16–D23 operative network enters its next phase, should HW and Eclipse
> retain tight operational centralization for coordination and effectiveness, or
> strengthen distributed authority, independent custody, and explicit
> exit/refusal rights even at some cost to speed?

## 6. Source context used to derive frames

The accepted Series Direction says the network expands through distinct methods
while testing whether integration preserves agency. Its declared pressure is:
every successful integration increases coordination and capability while making
independent refusal harder to imagine.

Relevant accepted meanings include:

- D18 / Calista: mutual instrumentalization alongside unresolved authentic
  doubt; useful integration may become jurisdiction; no final answer to whether
  the network is a cage.
- D19 / Wren revised realization: shared intelligence, logistics, emergency
  backup, and interoperability without absorption of Wren's jurisdiction.
- D20 / Elena: a platform restores documentation and care capacity while
  concentrating access; restoration can become dependence.
- D23 / Iris: transactional access makes dependence explicit; the integrated
  system knows and connects more than local actors; the cage question remains
  unresolved.

These are evidence inputs, not proof that any one governance arrangement is
correct.

## 7. Candidate Decision Frames

### Frame A — governance configuration under shared infrastructure

**Decision question:** What allocation of coordination and authority lets the
network retain capability without making independent refusal merely nominal?

**Possible configurations:**

1. Tightly centralize operational coordination and related authority.
2. Federate shared infrastructure while retaining local jurisdiction, custody,
   and refusal boundaries.
3. Distribute authority and custody with explicit exit/refusal rights, accepting
   the owner-stated possibility of coordination or speed cost.

**Directly stated decision dimensions:** operational centralization,
coordination, effectiveness, distributed authority, independent custody,
exit/refusal rights, and speed cost.

**Interpretively grounded dimensions:** agency, jurisdiction, dependency, and
shared infrastructure.

**Candidate core tension:** collective capability through integration versus
meaningful independent agency or jurisdiction.

**Assumptions to test:** operational centralization entails centralized
authority; local custody entails agency; explicit rights are exercisable; one
topology should govern every function; centralization itself causes speed or
effectiveness.

### Frame B — safeguards and boundaries within shared capability

**Decision question:** Which functions, decisions, custody boundaries, and
refusal rights should remain local while the network shares coordination and
capability?

This frame treats the question as more than a central-versus-distributed binary.
It makes room for centralized coordination with local authority/custody,
centrally maintained opt-in services, federated interoperability, and
function-specific allocation.

**Candidate tensions:** coordination versus jurisdiction; shared support versus
dependence/control; system knowledge versus local custody; speed versus usable
refusal; integration versus preservation of distinct functions.

**Uncertainties:** custody of what; refusal by whom and of what; exit from
which relationship; who holds which authority; which functions actually require
central coordination; what effectiveness means in this decision.

### Frame C — narrative stress test rather than institutional design

**Decision question:** What concrete Book 9 pressure should test whether an
operative can refuse, retain custody, or exit while the network faces a real
coordination demand, and what consequence should remain unresolved?

This is an alternative reading, not a claim about the owner's intent. It keeps
the cage question and D18's ambiguity alive by treating institutional
configurations as story variables rather than as a final constitution to choose.

**Candidate stakes:** whether the story can demonstrate the cost and limits of
shared capability without prematurely resolving the question of agency,
jurisdiction, or the cage.

## 13. Phase B — six bounded bridge analyses

The following analyses use the owner-validated Frame B. They are research
interpretations, not ontology relations or production recommendations.

### 13.1 D18 — Calista

**Source:** D18 changes the network to peer_ambivalence_integrated. Its accepted
meaning is mutual instrumentalization with unresolved authentic doubt; its
central engine says useful integration may become jurisdiction; its open
question remains “Are we the cage now?”

**Decision dimensions:** authority/jurisdiction, dependency, refusal, practical
exercisability, and preservation of distinct function.

**Proposed relevance:** boundary case and pressure contribution; it complicates
the assumption that integration either resolves dissent or leaves dissent
outside the system.

**Mechanism:** authentic doubt remains inside an integrated architecture. That
shows that internal critique and unresolved ambiguity can coexist with shared
capability, but it does not establish that Calista can refuse a specific
directive, retain custody of a record/resource, or exit a service or
relationship. The decision therefore needs to distinguish the presence of
internal dissent from an exercisable authority boundary.

**Creative consequence:** a Book 9 arrangement that preserves disagreement
without specifying who may refuse what risks treating dissent as expressive
rather than operational. This is an interpretive consequence, not an accepted
future event.

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** if D18 had resolved doubt in favor of
integration, the pressure to distinguish dissent from refusal would weaken. If
D18 had rejected integration entirely, the question of preserving agency
inside shared infrastructure would be less directly tested. This changes the
reasoning landscape but does not prove a particular governance answer.

**Evidence ceiling:** source meaning and the existence of unresolved pressure
are SUPPORTED; boundary/complicates relation is INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED;
claims about actual refusal, custody, or exit are NOT_TESTED.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes. If the decision is limited to allocation of
concrete resources rather than the status of dissent, D18 may only preserve a
thematic/open-question pressure and have no defensible operational bridge.

### 13.2 D19 — Wren federated protection

**Source:** The accepted D19 revision gives Wren shared intelligence,
logistics, emergency backup, and interoperability without absorbing her
operational jurisdiction.

**Decision dimensions:** coordination, capability/effectiveness,
authority/jurisdiction, custody, and function-specific allocation.

**Proposed relevance:** boundary case, feasibility evidence, and a limited
precedent for shared infrastructure with retained local jurisdiction.

**Mechanism:** support functions are shared while Wren's operational
jurisdiction remains unabsorbed. This separates coordination from authority in
one concrete authored arrangement. The source does not specify custody of every
record/resource, the terms of refusal, portability of support, or exit from the
arrangement, and it does not establish that this pattern scales network-wide.

**Creative consequence:** the current decision can ask which functions can be
federated while the operative retains authority, rather than choosing one
topology for all functions. The arrangement may be a fragile exception or a
model; the evidence does not decide which.

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** if D19 had absorbed Wren's jurisdiction, the
hybrid configuration would lose its strongest concrete support and the
coordination/authority separation would become harder to defend. If the shared
support had not existed, the capability side of the hybrid would be less
grounded. Neither counterfactual proves scalability.

**Evidence ceiling:** shared support without jurisdictional absorption is
SUPPORTED; federation as a decision precedent is
INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED; custody, refusal, exit, and scalability are
NOT_TESTED.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes, if Book 9 concerns a different function whose
authority boundary is unlike Wren's protection jurisdiction.

### 13.3 D20 — Elena

**Source:** D20 makes documentation and care capacity a platform/system
support. Its direction states that a faction platform values work while
concentrating access; restoration can become dependence; it asks whether
platform access can remain independent.

**Decision dimensions:** capability/effectiveness, coordination, dependency,
custody, authority, refusal, exit, and function-specific allocation.

**Proposed relevance:** capability evidence plus dependency risk evidence; a
custody/access boundary question.

**Mechanism:** shared documentation/care can increase collective capacity while
concentrated access changes who can reach or control the service. Dependence is
interpretive but grounded by the authored conflict and stakes. The source does
not establish that shared access transfers authority, that records are centrally
owned, or that service withdrawal is possible.

**Creative consequence:** a function-by-function arrangement should specify
whether platform participation is opt-in, whether records/resources remain
portable or locally controlled, and what refusal/exit means for the service.
Those are research questions, not proposed canon.

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** if platform access remained independent and
portable, the dependency concern would weaken while the capability benefit
could remain. If the platform could not restore capacity, the capability case
would weaken. The counterfactual identifies decision-sensitive dimensions
without proving authority transfer.

**Evidence ceiling:** capability and authored access-concentration concern are
SUPPORTED; dependency/custody implications are
INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED; authority transfer and exercisable refusal/exit are
NOT_TESTED.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes, if the Book 9 decision excludes documentation
and care services from its scope.

### 13.4 D23 — Iris

**Source:** D23 integrates computational optimization into system context. Its
meaning says transactional access makes dependence explicit and that the
network knows and connects more than local actors. The direction preserves the
unresolved cage question.

**Decision dimensions:** capability/effectiveness, coordination, dependency,
custody, authority/jurisdiction, refusal, exit, and practical exercisability.

**Proposed relevance:** capability evidence, information/custody asymmetry risk,
and unresolved-question reactivation.

**Mechanism:** an integrated computational capability may change bargaining
conditions when access is transactional and system knowledge exceeds local
knowledge. This is more than lexical similarity because it bears on who can
make an informed choice and what dependence a local function carries. The
source does not identify who owns data, who may revoke access, or whether an
operative can refuse computation while retaining unrelated support.

**Creative consequence:** Book 9 may need to test whether a coordination demand
can be refused without surrendering access to indispensable knowledge or
infrastructure. This preserves the cage question rather than answering it.

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** if computation remained local, the coordination
and capability configuration would change and the information-asymmetry risk
would weaken. If access were non-transactional and portable, dependency would
mean something different. These are diagnostics, not evidence that the network
is a cage.

**Evidence ceiling:** computational integration and explicit dependence concern
are SUPPORTED; custody/asymmetry and refusal implications are
INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED; actual control or exit mechanics are NOT_TESTED.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes, if computation is not part of the current
decision's operative function.

### 13.5 Persistent pressure — living infrastructure

**Members:** the accepted Series commitment carries the D16–D23 integration
sequence: operational integration, successor scale, peer ambivalence, care
infrastructure, platform system, analysis scale, code-grounded infrastructure,
and computational integration. The D19 revision changes the relevant member to
federated protection.

**Common pattern:** distinct operative methods repeatedly become faction
infrastructure while their distinct functions are nominally preserved. This
pattern is grounded in the accepted commitment and transitions. The stronger
claim that successful integration progressively makes independent refusal harder
to imagine is the authored Series pressure; treating it as a concrete
authority/custody mechanism is interpretive.

**Decision dimensions:** capability/effectiveness, coordination, dependency,
authority/jurisdiction, custody, refusal, exit, and function-specific
allocation.

**Proposed relevance:** pressure contribution and tradeoff restructuring, not
option selection.

**Mechanism:** accumulated integration increases the number of functions whose
capability depends on shared infrastructure, which can make local authority,
custody, refusal, and exit harder to exercise in practice. The evidence
supports the accumulation and the pressure statement; it does not establish
that every function has the same dependence or that a single topology follows.

**Creative consequence:** the current decision should be decomposed by
function, service, and authority boundary. A network-wide “centralize” or
“distribute” answer would erase the distinctions the pressure makes visible.

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** remove one integration member and the cumulative
pressure is reduced but not eliminated; change D19's retained jurisdiction and
the pressure becomes more one-sided toward absorption. This suggests
path-dependence and overdetermination, not a single decisive fact.

**Evidence ceiling:** membership, sequence, and declared pressure are
SUPPORTED; common-pattern-to-practical-authority mechanism is
INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED; universal dependency or a required topology is
SPECULATIVE.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes, if the Book 9 choice is intentionally local to
one function and does not reason over accumulated network pressure.

### 13.6 D18 → D19 relation

**Source relation:** The accepted D18 transition changes the state from
successor_scale_integrated to peer_ambivalence_integrated. D19's original
transition names peer_ambivalence_integrated as its before-state; the revised
D19 realization changes the state to federated_protection_integrated. The
derived Map labels the causal relation D18 supports D19.

**Decision dimensions:** authority/jurisdiction, coordination, dependency,
function-specific allocation, and practical exercisability.

**Proposed relevance:** relation exists as ordered state dependency; its
decision-specific semantic mechanism is interpretive and should be weaker than
the deterministic predecessor relation.

**Mechanism:** D18 establishes an integrated system in which unresolved doubt
and mutual instrumentalization remain active. D19 then supplies a concrete
shared-support arrangement that retains Wren's jurisdiction. This makes D19
readable as a boundary response to the problem D18 leaves open, but the source
does not state that D18 caused D19's governance design or that D19 resolves
D18's ambiguity.

**Creative consequence:** later analysis should not merely say “D18 supports
D19.” It should distinguish the ordered dependency from the interpretive
question: does D19 show how shared support can preserve jurisdiction under the
ambiguity D18 established, or is it only a neighboring example?

**Counterfactual diagnostic:** if D18 had not established peer ambivalence,
D19's retained jurisdiction would still be a support arrangement but would bear
less directly on the unresolved integration/agency pressure. If D19 lacked
retained jurisdiction, the relation would read more as an instance of
absorption than as a boundary case.

**Evidence ceiling:** predecessor state and source ordering are SUPPORTED;
“D18 makes D19 a boundary response” is INTERPRETIVE_BUT_GROUNDED; causal
authorship, scalability, and resolution of the cage question are SPECULATIVE
or NOT_TESTED.

**No-bridge possibility:** yes. The safe result may be “relation exists but
mechanism unknown” if the owner does not accept the boundary-response reading.

## 19. Reasoning operations required

The owner evidence identifies reasoning operations that are not supplied by a
flat fact-to-meaning explanation:

1. Parse the current decision into function-specific dimensions without

2. Align accepted narrative meaning with the relevant decision dimensions.
3. Classify a source's role as configuration instance, pressure contribution,
   access/dependence risk, trajectory/stakes signal, qualification, or no
   bridge.

5. Name transfer conditions: which function, authority holder, custody object,
   refusal/exit object, and exercisability condition are in scope.
6. Use counterfactuals as diagnostics while preserving cumulative,
   redundant, path-dependent, and symbolic relevance.
7. Preserve alternative interpretations and allow NO DEFENSIBLE BRIDGE.
8. Require owner confirmation when a useful interpretation exceeds
   deterministic source order or accepted meaning.

These are reasoning/workflow requirements, not a proposed production schema.

## 20. Cross-case semantic patterns

The six cases show recurring patterns, but they do not establish a universal
ontology:

- Shared capability can coexist with retained jurisdiction in one function
  (D19), so coordination and authority must remain separate.
- Access concentration and transactional integration can make dependence a live
  question (D20, D23) without establishing custody or control.
- Internal doubt can preserve an unresolved governance pressure (D18) without
  becoming operational refusal.
- Accumulated integration can restructure the decision tradeoff at Series

  rather than simply extending an absorption trajectory.
- Ordered state adjacency is deterministic history, while semantic causation
  and “support” remain interpretive unless separately declared or confirmed.
- The same source can hold several roles, and one source can have no bridge for
  a function outside its authored scope.

Recurring vocabulary such as jurisdiction, dependency, custody, refusal, exit,
precedent, and pressure is driven by this Book 9 decision. It is not yet shown
to be generally necessary narrative semantics across the campaign.

## 23. H1/H2/H3/H4 comparison

### H1 — current representation appears sufficient for tested meaning

**Supported in a bounded sense:** accepted explanations, commitments,
transitions, and provenance supplied enough narrative meaning for useful
owner-confirmed bridges in D19, D20, and the persistent pressure, with limited
value in D18 and D23.

**Not generally established:** the representation alone did not deterministically
derive decision relevance, and D18→D19 had no defensible semantic-causal bridge.
H1 cannot claim general sufficiency or automatic author value.

### H2 — a small recurring representation gap exists

**Not admitted by this evidence:** missing custody objects, authority holders,
refusal objects, and exercisability details are decision-specific unanswered
questions, not demonstrated universal narrative primitives. The revised-chain
contradiction is a source/reconciliation issue, not proof of a new ontology
concept.

H2 remains reopenable only if multiple owner decisions show the same distinction
cannot be recovered or elicited without canonical representation.

### H3 — decision relevance remains substantially interpretive

**Best-supported disposition:** useful bridges require a validated Decision
Frame, explicit relation/mechanism reasoning, evidence ceilings, alternatives,
and owner confirmation. D19 is useful with a narrow scope; D18 and D23 are
limited; D20 and the pressure are useful after correction; D18→D19 is no
bridge. This unevenness is evidence for an interpretive workflow, not a
deterministic universal relation.

### H4 — DecisionRelevanceBridge is the wrong durable abstraction

**Partially supported as an architectural guardrail:** a persisted bridge object
would overstate the evidence and encourage universal relation types. The
research vocabulary remains useful for discussing candidate interpretations, but
the evidence favors a proposed-and-owner-confirmed analysis workflow over a
canonical or automatically reusable bridge entity.
