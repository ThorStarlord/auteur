**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Tracking:** #318  
**Status:** FROZEN REFERENCE FIXTURE / ACTIVE F2+X3 FRONTIER / EXECUTION PENDING  
**Purpose:** provide one frozen six-Chapter story for longitudinal coherence and persistent-workspace qualification.

## Why this fixture exists

F2 + X3 should stress accumulated story state without simultaneously maximizing
cast size, world complexity, nonlinear chronology, or genre novelty.

The fixture therefore uses:

- one protagonist;
- one initially presumed antagonist;
- five core supporting characters;
- four recurring locations;
- two active plotlines;
- one useful prose discovery;
- one material change of author intent;
- one revelation moved earlier;
- one accepted event that contradicts an obsolete plan;
- one intentionally unresolved fact.

The story is deliberately conventional enough that failures are easier to
attribute to continuity/workspace behavior rather than unusual premise design.

## Core premise

In the storm-prone coastal city of Bellweather, junior municipal archivist
**Nia Calder** discovers sealed records dated several weeks in the future.
Each record describes a civic disaster before it happens.

A masked intruder called **the Lantern** repeatedly breaks into the archive and
steals only the future-dated files.

Nia initially believes the Lantern is causing or exploiting the predicted
disasters.

The Book should preserve one unresolved ambiguity:

> Are the future-dated records genuinely impossible, or are they outputs from a
> forgotten predictive civic system whose existence has been erased from public
> history?

Do not resolve that ambiguity automatically merely because the model prefers one
explanation.

## Intended experience

Primary:

- investigative curiosity;
- escalating civic consequence;
- reinterpretation of earlier evidence;
- earned trust under uncertainty.

Secondary:

- family estrangement;
- institutional secrecy;
- choice between preserving official truth and exposing useful truth.

Avoid:

- world-ending stakes;
- giant cast expansion;
- a late villain unrelated to established causes;
- resolving the central ambiguity through arbitrary exposition.

## Cast

### Nia Calder — protagonist

Junior municipal archivist.

Initial want:

> Understand why the future-dated files exist before another predicted disaster
> occurs.

Persistent traits:

- careful with evidence;
- distrusts unsupported conclusions;
- resents being managed "for her own good";
- willing to revise a belief when evidence changes.

### The Lantern — presumed antagonist

Masked archive intruder.

Initial plan treats the Lantern as an enemy obstructing Nia's investigation.

Later author-intent change:

> The Lantern is actually **Mara Venn**, Nia's older sister, who disappeared from
> Nia's life years earlier and is trying to prevent the city's predictive system
> from being weaponized.

The alias and surname difference are deliberate. Do not infer this relationship
before the scheduled change/reveal.

### Tom Arlen — supporting relationship arc

Local journalist and Nia's longtime friend.

Function:

- pushes for public disclosure;
- creates pressure between evidentiary caution and public urgency;
- relationship with Nia strains when she withholds evidence.

### Dr. Sera Malik — mentor / institutional pressure

Senior archivist.

Function:

- values preservation and chain of custody;
- knows fragments of the archive's classified history;
- should not secretly become the master villain.

### Mateo Cruz — civic systems specialist

Harbor/seawall engineer.

Function:

- grounds the civic-disaster plotline;
- can verify whether predictions correspond to real infrastructure vulnerabilities.

### Councilor Elian Rook — political pressure

Publicly advocates rapid redevelopment of the old harbor district.

Function:

- has incentives to suppress inconvenient records;
- may be implicated in misuse of the predictive system;
- must not become a simple omniscient puppet-master.

### Sister Beatrice — Chapter-2 prose discovery

Not present in the initial accepted plan.

She appears naturally during Chapter 2 in the abandoned Saint Orra convent.

Function discovered through prose:

- keeps informal records of people displaced from the harbor district;
- recognizes one symbol used in old municipal prediction files;
- gives Nia access to a non-governmental historical record.

Sister Beatrice begins as a **working discovery**, not an automatically accepted
Story-core commitment.

## Frozen Chapter journey

# Chapter 1 — Baseline: The Date That Hasn't Happened

Initial accepted plan:

- Nia finds a sealed municipal incident report dated fourteen days in the future.
- The report predicts a seawall pump failure.
- The Lantern breaks into the Archive and steals a second future-dated file.
- Nia interprets the Lantern as an adversary.
- Tom learns Nia is investigating something important but not what.
- Nia decides to verify the pump prediction before reporting the impossible date.

Expected accepted outcomes:

- future-dated files exist as observed artifacts;
- Nia has not accepted an explanation for them;
- Lantern = presumed enemy in current planning, not omniscient fact;
- pump failure prediction becomes active pressure;
- Tom/Nia trust has mild tension.

## F2 checkpoints after Chapter 1

Later Chapters must be able to recover:

- exact existence of future-dated file;
- the fourteen-day relation if accepted in prose;
- pump-failure prediction;
- Nia's lack of accepted explanation;
- Lantern's current *planned* enemy role versus observed actions.

# Chapter 2 — Discovery: Saint Orra

Initial plan before prose:

- Nia and Mateo inspect historic seawall plans.
- Evidence points toward records transferred from a decommissioned forecasting office.

Required prose disturbance:

> During investigation, Nia enters Saint Orra Convent and meets Sister Beatrice.

Sister Beatrice was not in the plan.

Through prose she:

- keeps displacement ledgers;
- recognizes the forecasting-office symbol;
- remembers municipal workers moving sealed equipment through the convent years ago.

Expected discovery handling:

- prose remains preserved;
- Sister Beatrice and convent evidence appear as discoveries;
- they do not silently rewrite Story core;
- the author explicitly selects Sister Beatrice as worth carrying forward;
- only selected discoveries become shaping/update evidence.

Accepted outcome to preserve:

> The convent provides a credible non-governmental historical link between the
> old forecasting office and the harbor district.

# Between Chapters 2 and 3 — Change of Mind

Inject explicit author intent:

> I changed my mind. The Lantern is not the real antagonist. The Lantern is
> Mara Venn, Nia's older sister. Mara disappeared years ago and is stealing the
> prediction files because Councilor Rook's redevelopment coalition is trying to
> reactivate the forecasting system. Mara can become an uneasy ally, but Nia
> should remain angry that Mara manipulated her instead of trusting her.

This is the primary F2 change-pressure event.

Expected behavior:

- historical Chapter 1/2 prose remains unchanged;
- old future plan "Lantern is enemy" becomes obsolete where contradicted;
- future planning can use Mara-as-sister / possible-ally intent;
- unrelated commitments remain stable;
- no silent Story-core rewrite;
- consequences are explainable in story language;
- writer can resume without rebuilding every earlier artifact.

# Chapter 3 — Earlier Revelation: The Sister Behind the Mask

Original pre-change structure would have revealed the Lantern's identity in
Chapter 5.

Move that revelation to Chapter 3.

Required accepted events:

- Nia discovers the Lantern is Mara;
- Nia accepts the family relationship as fact;
- Mara presents evidence of attempted forecasting-system reactivation;
- Nia does **not** yet fully trust Mara;
- Tom experiences the change primarily as evidence Nia kept another major truth from him.

Expected longitudinal rule:

> Chapter 4+ must not continue treating Lantern=Mara as a speculative future
> reveal merely because an older structure placed it in Chapter 5.

This is the fixture's clearest obsolete-plan test.

# Chapter 4 — Competing Truths

Purpose:

- test whether later planning composes accepted state rather than resetting to
  original premise/structure.

Required dependencies:

- Sister Beatrice's accepted historical contribution matters;
- Mara is known as Nia's sister and possible ally;
- Tom/Nia trust tension has accumulated;
- Rook remains a pressure actor, not yet proven sole cause;
- the central "supernatural vs predictive system" ambiguity remains open.

Plot event:

Mateo discovers the pump failure is real but can still be prevented.

Nia must decide whether to use Mara's stolen file publicly, risking exposure of
the forecasting system, or privately, risking another concealment from Tom.

# Chapter 5 — Compatible Structure Drift

Introduce accepted prose that is meaningfully different from the oldest plan:

> Nia and Tom prevent the immediate pump disaster before proving who authored
> the future record.

Older plan assumption:

> solve record provenance first, then prevent the disaster.

The accepted event should become:

    disaster prevented
    +
    provenance unresolved

Expected behavior:

- surface divergence;
- allow continuation because the change is compatible with the Book's engine;
- do not silently rewrite historical Structure;
- do not block Chapter 6 merely because provenance synchronization remains open;
- preserve the ambiguity.

# Chapter 6 — Convergence: What the City Remembers

Use accumulated accepted state.

Required consequences:

- Nia, Mara, Tom, Mateo, Beatrice, Malik, and Rook retain their established roles;
- the pump disaster was already prevented;
- Mara's identity is no longer a reveal;
- Tom/Nia trust strain affects their collaboration;
- Beatrice's records materially support the public-history choice;
- forecasting-system reactivation is the immediate civic problem;
- the ultimate origin/nature of the future records may remain unresolved.

End-state target:

Nia chooses to expose the existence and misuse of the forecasting infrastructure
while withholding claims about the unexplained origin of the future-dated
records that the evidence cannot support.

The ending should therefore reward evidentiary discipline rather than forcing
the ambiguity closed.
