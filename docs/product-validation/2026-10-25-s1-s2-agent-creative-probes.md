## 1. Evaluation method and strict claim boundary

The simulated author supplied **no already-selected plot or character canon**. I generated original fictional candidate directions, then evaluated each against an adversarial causal-distinctness and evidence-provenance checklist. These are agent-authored examples, **not** proof of independent model consistency, superior performance compared with an unstructured prompt, actual author preference, literary effectiveness, market demand, or live backend compatibility.

Labels: **AUTHOR_INPUT** = stated within the fictional test prompt only; **AGENT_PROPOSAL** = new creative invention; **TEXT_OBSERVATION** = explicitly present in the original synthetic manuscript; **HYPOTHESIS** = one interpretation that might fit; **AUTHOR_CORRECTION** = simulated later direction; **UNKNOWN** = not established; **WORKING** = never accepted canon. No commercial or audience evidence was supplied, used, or inferred.

We looked for **different major activities, reversals and climax mechanics**, not distinct proper nouns; conditional multi-chapter runway; explicit setup/exposition cost; source-specific evidence in manuscript analysis; correction handling; and no automatic canonical write.

## 2. S1 — Two opposite no-premise fixtures

### Fixture A: warm academy, no plot

**Exact fictional AUTHOR_INPUT:** “I want a warm, beautiful academy; intelligent companionship; a meaningful mystery; and generous heroism that protects other people's good lives. I have not chosen a protagonist, magical system, conflict, scale, or plot.”

For comparison only, assume a **standalone 8–12-chapter fantasy Book**. The horizon is an evaluator assumption, **not** the simulated author's commitment.

| Candidate / authority | Renewable causal engine and actual scene work | Material reversal / climax mechanics | Intended experience, cost, and conditional runway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1 — The Repair Covenant** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | In a protective academy, each student restoration of damaged infrastructure redistributes future responsibilities and exposes earlier hidden compromises. Scenes revolve around diagnosis, repairs, negotiation, and new obligations produced by previous victories. | A heroic candidate can win a final examination by personally mastering the protective system **or** reveal and distribute its operation among ordinary residents; the second resolution preserves community independence. | **Promise:** belonging and generous competence. **Cost:** administrative and engineering explanations may weaken mystery and wonder. **Runway:** each solution changes public obligations, plausibly sustaining several arcs; risk of procedural repetition after the institution stabilizes. |
| **A2 — The Letters That Do Not Agree** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | The academy's sealed archive receives apparently authentic accounts of events that have not yet happened. Classmates test contradictory predictions, collect provenance, and revise hypotheses; false certainty itself causes consequential choices. | A predicted catastrophe was intensified by the institution's insistence on one authorized reading; the climax forces characters to decide whether to dismantle prediction authority or use it selectively to protect people. | **Promise:** intellectual mystery and trusted companionship. **Cost:** paradox exposition and ominous revelations can damage the desired peaceful warmth. **Runway:** each interpretation creates a new error/evidence problem; finite once the archive's cause and authority are resolved. |
| **A3 — The Sanctuary of Visiting Seasons** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | The academy temporarily shelters communities displaced by changes between neighboring enchanted regions. Each arrival brings genuine needs and incompatible customs; the core characters must practice hospitality, mediate conflict, and build institutions that enable guests to flourish independently. | The academy can remain a prestigious safe enclave that selectively grants shelter, or share knowledge and civic power until the wider region can sustain multiple sanctuaries; the climax turns on relinquishing exclusive importance. | **Promise:** a beautiful place made desirable through acts of care. **Cost:** many visitors and settings can dilute recurring emotional attachments. **Runway:** new arrivals and long-term consequences renew tensions, but unrelated episodes would become filler without returning obligations. |

**Why these are causally distinct:** A1's major actions change resource/authority distribution; A2's change what can be believed and acted upon; A3's change mutual belonging and multi-community governance. Their reversals and climaxes would require different scenes. **No chosen winner.** The fictional author might reject A2 as overly ominous or A3 as too episodic without the agent pretending that preference has already been expressed.

**Smallest next author decision:** Is the academy primarily a place for **collaborative repair**, **knowledge under uncertainty**, or **ethical hospitality**? That question distinguishes plot generators, not just mood labels. If unanswered, all options remain WORKING.

### Fixture B: bleak and time-limited voyage, no ending

**Exact fictional AUTHOR_INPUT:** “A bleak voyage must race a deadline. A rescue will be costly. One witness is unreliable. I have not chosen the reason, cost, truth, politics, or ending.”

For comparison only, assume **one novella or compact novel**, not a series. Neither the rescuee nor the witness has an author-selected identity.

| Candidate / authority | Renewable/finite causal engine and scene work | Reversal / climax mechanics | Reader promise, cost, and conditional runway |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B1 — The Lifeboat Ledger** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | Every detour to rescue another vessel spends finite fuel and space; witness statements conflict with navigation records. The crew must assess physical evidence while spending resources that cannot be replenished. | A nearly successful journey exposes a second group in distress; the climax is a public triage decision whose moral consequences survive arrival, not simply discovering who lied. | **Promise:** escalating material sacrifice and responsibility. **Cost:** repeated fuel arithmetic may reduce characters to logistical tokens. **Runway:** strong and finite for a novella; a multi-book expansion would need a different governing engine. |
| **B2 — The Unreliable Route** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | Uncharted water reacts to remembered routes; the sole guide's contradictory accounts change apparent hazards. Each correction to the map costs the guide a specific memory, so navigation competence competes with identity preservation. | A reliable map could save the crew only by emptying the guide of personal memory; the climax chooses between exact arrival and a riskier route that preserves a person. | **Promise:** epistemic dread and intimate sacrifice. **Cost:** memory-water mechanics are invented, heavy to explain and may violate a realist target if the author later specifies one. **Runway:** deep but bounded by the guide's memories and the voyage deadline. |
| **B3 — The Ceasefire Passage** (AGENT_PROPOSAL) | A rescue crosses a disputed naval boundary; testimony about its timing could unravel a fragile ceasefire. The crew must choose whom to shelter, whose statements to expose, and what trust or legitimacy to spend at each port. | The witness may be inaccurate, compromised or deliberately protective; the final choice is whether to disclose evidence that saves a particular person but destabilizes many others. | **Promise:** political/moral tension and contested testimony. **Cost:** needs more faction exposition than a pure survival voyage. **Runway:** the compact voyage can carry several escalations; a long series would depend on new obligations after arrival. |

**Distinctness check:** B1's central currency is finite material survival, B2's is personal knowledge/memory, B3's is political legitimacy. Material change in rescue action, reversal and climax survives superficial setting renaming. **No universal idea score** and no automatic selection.

**Premise Fitness caution:** A warm academy may fit a long Book when repair/hospitality renew consequences, whereas a time-limited voyage can be excellent precisely because it *ends*. This is a **scope-fit judgment**, not a declaration that one premise is artistically better. Efficacy estimates are hypothetical; story effectiveness requires realized prose and reader evidence.

### S1 adversarial self-review

- **Source attribution:** Only the two exact fixture sentences are AUTHOR_INPUT. All academies' engines, archives, memory-water, navigation costs, fictional institutions and moral forks are **AGENT_PROPOSAL**. No real audience reception or popularity is asserted.
- **Meaningful separation:** A1 vs A3 both concern communal care, but their actions differ: **repair/obligation reallocation** versus **hospitality/shared civic belonging**. This is the closest pair, deserving a later test with actual detailed scenes. If their scene patterns converge, the diversity claim weakens.
- **Thematic conflict:** A2 risks undermining a cozy target; B2 may move a bleak voyage into fantasy without permission; B3 has higher exposition load. These are real tradeoffs, not cosmetic "weaknesses."
- **Not tested:** whether an *independent* coding agent consistently delivers this diversity, whether the Story Opportunity protocol outperforms plain generation, whether any author chooses a variant, or whether a full 12-chapter story can sustain the proposed engines.
- **S1 provisional outcome:** The **documented method can guide at least one source-labeled, noncanonical worked example without adding runtime code**. Insufficient evidence to justify a permanent Story Opportunity database, forced intake UI, automatic favorite selector, or new semantic layer.

## 3. S2 — Manuscript-first story archaeology and correction

### Frozen original synthetic text (source IDs)

These are **newly written fictional paragraphs for the test only**, not third-party manuscript content. Freeze the words and IDs; later analysis must point to **these** source IDs.

**P1 — Quayside rescue:** “At the first harbor bell, a figure in a yellow coat cut the burning ferry's mooring rope. From the rain-soaked quay, apprentice Neri thought the figure was Mara. The figure pulled a traveler from the water and slid a brass key into his coat.”

**P2 — Official account:** “Captain Ilen told the night crew the fire had been discovered after the second bell. Beneath the wet dock, a spare mooring line was coiled and dry. The captain forbade anyone to bring the rope into his office.”

**P3 — Conflicting testimony:** “In the lighthouse office, the rescued traveler swore he had seen Mara already in the ferry's hold at the first bell. Mara said, ‘At the first bell I was at the outer beacon, two miles away.’ No one in the room had a working clock.”

**P4 — Ambiguous motive:** “That evening Ilen returned a brass key to Mara. ‘They will say you saved him to protect yourself,’ he said. Mara answered, ‘He was not the only person in the water.’ She did not explain whose key it was.”

### Observed source claims versus hypotheses

| ID | Narrow TEXT_OBSERVATION | Supported by | Do **not** infer as fact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O1 | A yellow-coated figure cut a mooring and rescued a traveler; **Neri thought** it was Mara. | P1 | The figure is definitely Mara or the rescuer caused the fire. |
| O2 | Ilen's account dates discovery after second bell, while P1 narrates the rescue at first bell. | P1, P2 | Ilen intentionally lied; first-bell rescue proves he already knew of the fire. |
| O3 | A spare rope was dry and Ilen forbade bringing it into his office. | P2 | The rope is planted evidence or linked to arson. |
| O4 | The traveler places Mara in the ferry hold while Mara claims to be at the outer beacon at the first bell; no working clock was present in the office. | P3 | Either speaker's account is proven true; exact chronology can be settled. |
| O5 | A brass key appears in P1 and P4, and Mara suggests other people were in the water. | P1, P4 | Both keys are certainly identical, the key unlocks the ledger, or there were confirmed additional victims. |

### Competing *working* interpretations before correction

**I1 — Protection through concealment (HYPOTHESIS, not canon).** The rescue may be one operation in a covert effort to protect additional people aboard; the captain may be suppressing evidence to prevent a public hunt for them. The brass key might open a shelter or lockbox. **Source support:** P1 rescue and key, P2 embargo on rope, P4 suggestion of multiple people. **Missing:** there is no observed second survivor, protected group, or meaning of the rope. **Plot pressure:** trace missing passengers versus harbor authority; climax could force disclosure or protective secrecy. **Tradeoff:** danger of romanticizing concealment without demonstrating its consequences.

**I2 — Rescue as strategic cover (HYPOTHESIS, not canon).** The rescue may be real but double as concealment of a theft or staged departure; a witness might be accurate about Mara's presence while Mara conceals another obligation. **Source support:** conflicting accounts in P3, key transfer in P1/P4, captain's rope prohibition in P2. **Missing:** no theft, arson, destination, or motive is observed. **Plot pressure:** evidence-gathering and moral complicity; climax might expose the rescue as mixed charity and self-protection. **Tradeoff:** a twist-first reading could invalidate the intended generosity without any source justification.

The two interpretations would entail different recurring scenes: **I1** follows protective logistics and competing disclosure obligations; **I2** follows evidentiary contradictions, possible culpability and contested rescue motives. Both retain the ambiguity of P1/P3. Neither creates Story Identity, Structure, Realization, or accepted prose.

### Simulated author correction C1 (new explicit direction)

**AUTHOR_CORRECTION (fictional, supplied after the initial interpretation):** “Mara did **not** start or arrange the fire. The brass key opens a temporary shelter, **not** any ledger room or valuables storage. The traveler's reason for making a potentially false accusation must remain unresolved. Keep the original text of all four paragraphs.”

### Revised analysis after C1

- **Preserved TEXT_OBSERVATION:** O1–O5 remain true as text observations; no prose edits, fabricated timestamps, or changes of cited IDs. O5 still does not prove that the key in P1 and the key in P4 are one object, although C1 gives the purpose of the brass key.
- **Newly explicit AUTHOR_CORRECTION:** the fire was not arranged by Mara; the brass key's purpose is shelter. These are **author-supplied constraints**, not discoveries inferred from P1–P4. They are part of the simulated analysis context but have **not** been accepted through Auteur's canonical workflow.
- **I1 updated:** shelter-based protection is more plausible; who needs protection and why the captain fears the rope remain **UNKNOWN**. The traveler may still be confused, defensive or strategically misleading; no motive is asserted.
- **I2 revised/rejected in original form:** a version dependent on Mara setting the fire or the key unlocking stolen records is **incompatible with C1**. A narrower alternate hypothesis—Mara concealed a separate mission while genuinely rescuing the traveler—could remain open, but is still not supported as fact. Do not repurpose contradictory facts into a new certainty merely to preserve the dramatic twist.
- **Mechanism recommendation:** the material currently affords a **testimony/provenance mystery under protection pressure**; whether the final Book should emphasize sanctuary ethics or mixed motives remains an **author choice**. A next existing-owner handoff, if explicitly selected, would be a *candidate* Story Identity or Structure hypothesis with P1–P4/C1 provenance, not a direct canonical write.

It does **not** establish reliable multi-chapter extraction or justify a new parser, permanent inferred-story store or an automatic prose-to-canon workflow.

## 4. Cross-probe findings and strategic effect (P5 input)

1. **Different creative jobs:** S1 explores *which story to make before a premise exists*; S2 explores *which story may already be implicit in written prose*. Neither is identical to the current Story Realization -> Quick Draft UI repair.
2. **Mechanisms matter more than labels:** useful opportunity diversity changes actions, reversals, and climax currencies; useful manuscript interpretation separates witnessed text from invented motive.
3. **Author intention is not recovered by confidence:** even a source-compatible inference stays a hypothesis until the author explicitly selects it. A later correction must narrow the model's claims rather than erase earlier text.
4. **Current documented protocols are useful as operating guidance:** this exploratory exercise used them without implementing another state model. That favors **NO NEW RUNTIME** for S1/S2 at this evidence level.
5. **What remains unproven:** no independent host-agent comparisons, no true Auteur UI/HTTP flow for S1/S2, no repeated creative quality or human reader evidence, no controlled superiority over unstructured brainstorming, no general whole-Book archaeology reliability.

**Decision recommendation:** **NO_MODEL_CHANGE / REAFFIRM** the already-selected connected creative-thread repair as current bounded product work. Keep Story Discovery Companion and Manuscript-First Story Archaeologist as distinct, evidence-gated candidates. Do not promote F3/F4/F5/F7, add a new semantic layer, create a new Story Opportunity runtime/store, or enforce a premise questionnaire. If future concrete usage surfaces a repeated failure, compare the smallest existing-owner context/presentation improvement against new architecture *only after* that failure is reproduced.
