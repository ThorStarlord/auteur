# Premise Fitness

**Status:** product/design contract; documentation-first  
**Scope:** context-dependent evaluation of a working premise before Story Discovery  
**Authority:** advisory / noncanonical; no universal premise score or automatic winner

## Purpose

Premise Fitness asks:

> Is this particular premise fit for the narrative job the author intends it to do?

The answer is conditional on the intended story environment. A premise that is
excellent for a short horror story may be badly matched to a twelve-volume
series. A premise that is expensive to establish in a novella may be efficient
for an epic because the same world, institutions, and conflicts can generate
many later stories.

Premise Fitness therefore rejects context-free questions such as:

> Is this a good premise?

and replaces them with:

> Can this premise plausibly generate the intended genre promise, target
> experience, and narrative horizon at an acceptable complexity and setup cost?

This is a pre-Identity analytical contract. It does not create another semantic
layer and does not make a Story Opportunity or premise canonical.

---

## 1. Auteur operational vocabulary

The terms efficacy, effectiveness, and efficiency are used here as Auteur
operational vocabulary. They are not claimed as universal terms from narratology.

### Premise efficacy

Question:

> Under competent execution, can this premise naturally generate the intended
> narrative experience?

Efficacy is about potential affordance.

Examples:

- Can a mystery premise naturally generate questions, evidence, competing
  hypotheses, revelation, and reinterpretation?
- Can a romance premise naturally generate attraction, relational resistance,
  increasing vulnerability, intimacy, and consequential commitment?
- Can a progression premise naturally generate visible advancement, challenge,
  new capability, status transformation, and escalating consequence?

Premise efficacy can be reasoned about before the story is written, but only as
a bounded design judgment.

### Story effectiveness

Question:

> Did the realized story actually produce the intended effect?

Effectiveness belongs later in the lifecycle.

A premise can have high efficacy while the finished story is ineffective because
the structure, realization, or expression mishandles it.

Therefore:

    premise efficacy
    !=
    realized story effectiveness

Audience effectiveness requires claim-appropriate audience evidence. A Blueprint
or premise alone cannot establish that readers were actually moved, surprised,
satisfied, frightened, amused, or compelled to continue.

### Premise efficiency

Question:

> How much narrative cost must be paid to produce the intended value at the
> intended scope?

Efficiency does not mean "shorter is always better."

Relevant costs may include:

- setup burden;
- exposition burden;
- entity count;
- world-rule burden;
- causal complexity;
- continuity/maintenance burden;
- payoff latency;
- additional machinery required to continue the story.

A premise may have high startup cost but low marginal expansion cost. That can be
efficient for a long series even when it would be inefficient for a short story.

---

## 2. Narrative runway / capacity

Premise Fitness needs a fourth concept beyond efficacy, effectiveness, and
efficiency:

> **Narrative runway** — how much meaningful story the premise can continue
> generating before its governing engine exhausts itself or requires arbitrary
> replenishment.

The useful question is:

> How many meaningful state transformations can this premise generate without
> introducing unrelated new machinery merely to keep the story alive?

A low-runway engine often looks like:

    problem
    -> complication
    -> complication
    -> repetition
    -> padding
    -> final solution

A renewable engine looks more like:

    problem
    -> choice
    -> changed state
    -> new pressure caused by that change
    -> new choice
    -> deeper changed state
    -> new pressure
    -> ...

Runway is not a quality judgment. Some excellent premises are deliberately
designed for one concentrated transformation.

---

## 3. Premise-to-scope fit

Efficiency and runway only make sense relative to narrative horizon.

A useful continuum is:

    flash / short story
    -> novelette
    -> novella
    -> novel
    -> long novel
    -> multi-book arc
    -> long-running series

### Short-form fit

A premise intended for short fiction often benefits from:

- rapid activation;
- low setup cost;
- few required entities;
- one dominant contradiction or pressure;
- limited world-model burden;
- one or a small number of major transformations;
- little need for renewable long-horizon machinery.

A premise may be excellent precisely because it exhausts itself cleanly.

### Novel fit

A novel generally requires more developmental space:

- several stages of escalation;
- changing consequences;
- enough relationship or thematic development;
- meaningful reversals;
- subproblems that still serve the central engine;
- a climax that transforms the initial problem.

A premise can therefore be excellent for a short story yet too thin for a
novel.

### Series fit

A long series needs more than "more plot." It needs renewable sources of
consequence.

Common sources include:

- character progression;
- relationship evolution;
- world discovery;
- institutional conflict;
- status change;
- expanding antagonist pressure;
- new domains of capability;
- persistent unanswered questions;
- consequences that open new choices rather than merely delay closure.

A premise with substantial setup cost can still be efficient for a series if the
established world and engine continue producing useful stories at low marginal
cost.

---

## 4. Scope mismatch is not premise failure

A Premise Fitness finding should distinguish:

    weak premise
    from
    strong premise / wrong scope

Example:

> A condemned prisoner receives one conversation with the future version of the
> person he murdered.

This may support:

- strong emotional pressure;
- rapid activation;
- one major moral transformation;
- low setup burden.

It may therefore be highly fit for short or novella form while offering little
natural multi-volume runway.

By contrast:

> Humanity discovers that every inhabited planet contains a different
> descendant civilization created by forgotten human colonization projects.

This has high setup and world-model cost, but it can generate:

- repeated discovery;
- political conflict;
- civilizational comparison;
- alliances;
- ideological pressure;
- large-scale mystery;
- multiple local arcs beneath a persistent macro question.

The second premise may be less efficient for a short work and far more efficient
for a long series.

## 5. Genre-conditioned efficacy

Premise efficacy is conditional on intended genre promise.

### Mystery

Useful premise affordances may include:

- unanswered governing questions;
- evidence generation;
- competing hypotheses;
- information asymmetry;
- revelation;
- reinterpretation.

### Romance

Useful premise affordances may include:

- attraction;
- relational incompatibility or barrier;
- increasing vulnerability;
- changing intimacy;
- consequential commitment;
- meaningful relational resolution.

### Horror

Useful premise affordances may include:

- vulnerability;
- uncertainty;
- threat;
- loss of control;
- dread escalation;
- confrontation with the feared condition.

### Progression fantasy

Useful premise affordances may include:

- visible advancement;
- repeated meaningful challenge;
- capability transformation;
- status transformation;
- increasing problem scale;
- new costs created by growth.

### Political drama

Useful premise affordances may include:

- multiple capable actors;
- incompatible interests;
- leverage;
- changing alliances;
- asymmetric information;
- consequences of power.

A premise can therefore have high efficacy for one genre and low efficacy for
another without being intrinsically defective.

---

## 6. Genre is necessary but not sufficient

Premise Fitness should consider at least these declared target dimensions:

- **genre / genre mix** — what kind of promise must be delivered;
- **scope / length horizon** — how long the engine must operate;
- **target experience** — what the audience is meant to experience;
- **mode / aesthetic framing** — how the experience should register;
- **architecture complexity** — focused, layered, maximalist, ensemble, etc.;
- **genre posture** — conventional, subversive, deconstructive, or another
  accepted posture;
- **audience intent** — niche, specialized, broad, or otherwise declared.

These are not all required fields in one future schema. They are the conceptual
environment against which fitness is evaluated.

---

## 7. Multi-genre and long-form engine hierarchy

A long work does not need every genre dimension to be equally primary.

Premise Fitness should inspect engine hierarchy.

Example:

    PRIMARY ENGINE
    political progression fantasy

    SECONDARY ENGINE
    romance

    TERTIARY ENGINE
    mystery

    LOCAL MODE
    horror during incursions

The analysis should ask:

- Does the premise sustainably feed the primary engine?
- Do secondary engines have natural attachment points?
- Do secondary engines reinforce or compete with the primary engine?
- Does each additional engine require costly independent setup?
- Can one event serve several engines without making the story illegible?

A premise can be broad without being incoherent when the hierarchy is clear.

## 8. Premise cost model

Efficiency should be diagnosed through qualitative costs rather than a universal
"effects per word" formula.

### Activation cost

How much must be established before the distinctive value begins?

### Exposition cost

How much conceptual or world knowledge must the audience learn?

### Entity cost

How many characters, factions, locations, systems, timelines, or institutions
must remain cognitively active?

### Causal complexity cost

How many interacting mechanisms must be understood before outcomes make sense?

### Continuity cost

How much state must the author and system preserve across the work?

### Payoff latency

How long can important promises remain unresolved before value is returned?

### Expansion cost

How much new machinery must be invented to generate the next major arc?

### Marginal story cost

After the engine is established, how expensive is each additional meaningful
arc?

The last distinction is particularly important for series design:

    high startup cost
    + low marginal story cost
    can be efficient for long-form work

---

## 9. Core Premise Fitness dimensions

A future analysis may inspect the following dimensions without collapsing them
into one numerical score.

### Genre efficacy

Can the premise naturally generate the primary genre promise?

### Engine strength

Does the central contradiction, desire, system, or situation reliably generate
action and consequence?

### Activation / compression

How quickly can the distinctive value of the premise become available?

### Narrative runway

How many meaningful transformations can occur before the engine exhausts itself?

### Engine renewability

Does resolving one pressure naturally create another, or must unrelated pressure
be imported?

### Expansion capacity

Can the premise grow into new domains, scales, relationships, or conflicts
without abandoning its identity?

### Complexity cost

How much cognitive, structural, and continuity burden is required?

### Payoff density

Can the work discharge meaningful promises at suitable intervals rather than
requiring all value to be deferred to the end?

### Multi-engine compatibility

Do primary and secondary genres/engines reinforce one another?

### Differentiation

Does the premise produce materially distinct possibilities rather than merely
reskinning its influences?

### Scope fit

Does the engine's cost, density, and runway match the intended length horizon?

---

## 10. No universal Premise Fitness score

Premise Fitness must not produce by default:

- "Premise quality: 87/100";
- "series potential: 93%";
- "commercial viability: A+";
- bestseller probability;
- a weighted automatic winner.

The dimensions are heterogeneous and often trade against one another.

For example:

    Premise A
    high activation efficiency
    high short-form efficacy
    low series runway

    Premise B
    high setup cost
    moderate activation efficiency
    high series runway
    high expansion capacity

Neither is universally better.

The correct conclusion may be:

> A is better matched to the declared novella target; B is better matched to the
> declared multi-book target.

That is a fit judgment, not a global quality ranking.

## 11. Efficacy and effectiveness across the lifecycle

The same narrative should be evaluated differently as evidence accumulates.

### Premise / Story Opportunity stage

Reasonable claims:

- predicted genre efficacy;
- predicted scope fit;
- predicted efficiency;
- predicted runway;
- likely complexity costs.

### Story Discovery / Identity stage

Reasonable claims:

- whether a chosen story engine actually exploits the premise's affordances;
- whether target experience and genre commitments make the target clearer;
- whether the opportunity has become more or less demanding.

### Structure stage

Reasonable claims:

- designed escalation sustainability;
- planned setup/payoff burden;
- planned engine renewal;
- whether the promised scope is actually populated with causal machinery.

### Realization / Expression stage

Reasonable claims:

- actual event-level consequence;
- whether planned complexity became confusing or remained legible;
- whether activation and payoff latency match the realized work.

### Audience-evidence stage

Only here may claim-appropriate evidence support statements about:

- experienced effectiveness;
- comprehension;
- engagement;
- satisfaction;
- memorability;
- recommendation behavior.

Therefore:

    predicted efficacy
    -> designed efficacy
    -> realized effectiveness
    -> audience-observed effectiveness

These are different evidence levels.

## 12. Relationship to Story Opportunity Discovery

Story Opportunity Discovery asks:

> What story might be worth developing?

Premise Fitness asks:

> Is this working premise fit for the particular narrative job we intend?

Recommended upstream flow:

    author tastes / references / audience evidence / criticism
                         |
                         v
              Story Opportunity Discovery
                         |
                         v
               candidate opportunities
                         |
                         v
                   Premise Fitness
                         |
                         v
             scope / genre / cost tradeoffs
                         |
                         v
             selected working premise / brief
                         |
                         v
                   Story Discovery

Premise Fitness may send an opportunity back for revision.

Examples:

    strong premise / wrong scope
    -> change intended horizon
       OR
       strengthen renewable engine

    strong genre hook / excessive activation cost
    -> simplify entry requirements

    strong primary engine / weak secondary-engine compatibility
    -> demote or remove a secondary genre
       OR
       redesign the connection

All outputs remain working / noncanonical.

## 13. Relationship to Genre Packs

Genre Packs and Premise Fitness solve different problems.

### Genre Pack

Question:

> What promises, conventions, engine families, failure modes, subversion points,
> escalation patterns, and resolution patterns characterize this genre?

A Genre Pack is reusable knowledge.

### Premise Fitness

Question:

> Can this particular premise use that genre knowledge effectively at the
> intended scale and posture?

Premise Fitness may consume Genre Pack knowledge for:

- genre promise;
- likely engine families;
- common escalation patterns;
- known failure modes;
- resolution expectations;
- intentional subversion points.

But:

    Genre Pack
    !=
    Premise Fitness analysis

and:

    genre alignment
    !=
    premise quality

Genre Packs remain reusable knowledge. Premise Fitness remains a
story-specific, advisory analysis.

---

## 14. Conventional, subversive, and deconstructive posture

Genre knowledge does not inherently bias a story toward conventional mass
appeal.

The same Genre Pack can support different adherence postures.

### Conventional

The premise is expected to deliver recognizable genre promises largely through
established mechanisms.

### Subversive

The premise preserves enough of the genre promise for the audience to recognize
the form while deliberately violating or transforming selected expectations.

### Deconstructive

The premise may use the genre's own assumptions, promises, or conventions as
objects of examination and pressure.

Deconstruction therefore needs genre knowledge rather than replacing it:

    know the expectation
    -> understand its function
    -> identify what happens when it is stressed or denied
    -> design the consequence deliberately

Premise Fitness should evaluate the premise against the **declared posture**.

A deconstructive premise should not be marked unfit merely because it violates a
conventional default when the violation is the intended engine.

---

## 15. Mass appeal is a separate axis

Genre adherence and audience breadth should remain separate.

    genre posture:
    conventional <-> subversive <-> deconstructive

is not the same axis as:

    audience intent:
    specialized / niche <-> broad

A conventional work can be niche.

A deconstruction can have broad appeal.

A Genre Pack may contain widely recognized audience promises, but that does not
make the pack a mass-appeal optimizer.

When MANA is available, it may later provide audience-effect evidence about a
selected narrative architecture. Premise Fitness should not duplicate MANA or
turn genre conformity into a proxy for mass appeal.

## 16. Candidate diagnostic shape

A future executable feature may use a report shaped approximately like this.
The schema is a documentation hypothesis, not a frozen API.

    premise_fitness:
      authority_status: "WORKING / NOT ACCEPTED"

      target:
        primary_genre: mystery
        secondary_genres:
          - romance
        scope: series
        planned_horizon: "5 books"
        genre_posture: subversive
        audience_intent: broad

      findings:
        genre_efficacy:
          state: supported
          rationale: >
            The core situation continuously produces uncertainty, evidence
            conflicts, and revelation opportunities.

        activation:
          state: tension
          rationale: >
            Three institutions and two timelines must be established before the
            central mystery becomes legible.

        narrative_runway:
          state: supported
          rationale: >
            Each solved local mystery reveals a deeper institutional layer and
            changes faction incentives.

        engine_renewability:
          state: supported
          rationale: >
            Resolution changes the political state and creates the next
            investigation rather than resetting the same problem.

        complexity_cost:
          state: tension
          rationale: >
            The multi-faction model may exceed the declared low-friction entry
            goal.

        scope_fit:
          state: supported
          rationale: >
            High startup cost is offset by several renewable conflict and
            discovery domains appropriate to a multi-book horizon.

      tradeoffs:
        - "Reducing faction count improves activation but lowers political depth."
        - "Moving the romance to secondary status preserves the mystery engine."

      nonclaims:
        - "Does not establish reader effectiveness."
        - "Does not predict commercial success."
        - "Does not select a winner automatically."

The exact terminology may change if a runtime feature is later warranted.

## 17. Coding-agent protocol

When asked whether a premise is "good," an Auteur agent should first recover or
state the target environment.

Prefer:

> Good for what genre, experience, scope, and posture?

If the target is known, the agent should:

1. identify the governing engine implied by the premise;
2. identify the primary genre promise;
3. inspect activation/setup costs;
4. estimate qualitative runway and renewability;
5. inspect expansion capacity;
6. inspect primary/secondary engine compatibility;
7. compare those properties with the declared scope;
8. surface tradeoffs and unknowns;
9. distinguish design predictions from evidence about a realized story;
10. avoid aggregate quality scoring.

If the story is already written, the agent should not continue calling later
evidence "premise effectiveness." It should distinguish premise affordance from
realized story effectiveness.

## 18. Implementation posture

The current selected responsibility is documentation-first.

Implement now:

- this Premise Fitness vocabulary;
- premise-to-scope fit;
- genre-conditioned efficacy;
- efficiency/cost model;
- runway and renewability;
- boundary with Story Opportunity Discovery;
- boundary with Genre Packs;
- conventional/subversive/deconstructive posture;
- lifecycle separation between efficacy and effectiveness.

Do not implement yet:

- a Premise Fitness numeric score;
- a bestseller or commercial-success predictor;
- an automatic premise winner;
- word-count formulas pretending to establish ideal length;
- a new canonical artifact;
- a new semantic layer;
- automatic Genre Pack mutation;
- a dedicated runtime before repeated use demonstrates the need.

---

## 19. Design principle

Premise Fitness should answer:

> Is this premise capable, sustainable, and economical for the particular story
> we intend to build?

It should not answer:

> Is this premise universally good?

The governing principle is:

    premise
    x intended genre promise
    x target experience
    x narrative horizon
    x complexity preference
    x genre posture
    x audience intent
    -> context-dependent fitness profile

The multiplication sign is conceptual, not an executable scoring formula.

A premise is fit **for an intended narrative environment**, not universally
superior.
