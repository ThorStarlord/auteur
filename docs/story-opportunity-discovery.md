# Story Opportunity Discovery

**Status:** product/design contract; documentation-first  
**Scope:** upstream creative search before premise-centered Story Discovery  
**Authority:** advisory / noncanonical; no automatic story selection or acceptance

## Purpose

Story Opportunity Discovery answers a different question from ordinary story craft:

> **What story is worth making in the first place?**

Auteur already has strong machinery for taking a premise or Discovery Brief and
exploring multiple Story Identity directions. Story Opportunity Discovery sits
one step earlier. It helps an author or delegated agent turn preferences,
influences, audience observations, criticism, and unmet narrative desires into
candidate story opportunities that are sufficiently abstracted and
differentiated to enter the existing Story Discovery workflow.

The intended progression is:

    author preferences / influences / audience evidence / criticisms
                                  |
                                  v
                        Story Opportunity Discovery
                                  |
                                  v
                  candidate story opportunities / premises
                                  |
                                  v
                           Story Discovery
                                  |
                                  v
                       candidate Story Identities
                                  |
                                  v
                        explicit author acceptance
                                  |
                                  v
              Identity -> Structure -> Realization -> Expression

Story Opportunity Discovery is not a sixth semantic layer. It is a
**pre-Identity creative-search activity** that produces noncanonical working
material.

---

## 1. Story selection and story craft are related but distinct

Choosing the idea and crafting the chosen story are both forms of narrative
craft, but they solve different problems.

### Story selection / creative search

Questions include:

- What kinds of stories does the author actually want to experience or create?
- Which influences contain portable mechanisms rather than merely attractive
  surface details?
- What already-successful stories demonstrate audience demand?
- What recurring complaints may reveal unsatisfied narrative desires?
- Which combinations create a causally distinct opportunity rather than a
  reskin?
- Which candidate has enough narrative runway to justify further development?

### Story design / execution

Questions include:

- What is the story's governing engine?
- Who wants what and against what resistance?
- What are the stakes, pressures, reversals, and climax mechanics?
- What structural progression best realizes the intended experience?
- What concrete events happen?
- How should those events be rendered in prose?

The boundary is:

    "What should I build?"
    !=
    "How should the selected thing work?"

Story Opportunity Discovery owns the first question. Existing Story Discovery,
Identity, Structure, Realization, and Expression own the later questions.

---

## 2. Creativity need not mean invention from nothing

Human creative search commonly includes:

- remembering stories, characters, systems, scenes, moods, or relationships the
  author liked;
- combining attractions from multiple works;
- preserving a desired audience experience while changing the mechanism;
- noticing recurring weaknesses in a genre or popular work;
- asking whether an existing tradeoff can be solved differently;
- recombining familiar mechanisms into a new causal system.

Auteur should model this as **abstraction + recombination + differentiation**,
not as copying.

A useful transformation is:

    reference work
    -> attractive surface element
    -> desired experience
    -> underlying narrative mechanism
    -> known tradeoffs / failure modes
    -> portable design principle
    -> new combination

Example:

    Source A
    "I love the constant recontextualization of earlier events."
            |
            v
    portable mechanism:
    progressive revelation changes the meaning of prior evidence

    Source B
    "I love repeated failure that teaches the protagonist something useful."
            |
            v
    portable mechanism:
    failure produces durable strategic knowledge

    Source C
    "I love multi-faction political conflict."
            |
            v
    portable mechanism:
    several capable actors pursue incompatible objectives

    Observed failure mode
    "Late victory can feel unearned."
            |
            v
    design constraint:
    final success must depend causally on accumulated knowledge and choices

    Synthesis
            |
            v
    A political mystery where failed interventions expose deeper institutional
    truths, and accumulated causal knowledge is the resource that makes the
    final victory possible.

The synthesis is useful because it transfers mechanisms while changing the
specific characters, settings, events, prose, imagery, and other
source-specific expression.

## 3. Three evidence streams must stay separate

Story Opportunity Discovery must never silently collapse personal taste,
popularity, and craft interpretation into one claim.

### 3.1 Author preference

Examples:

- "I love rival relationships."
- "I want hidden-civilization discovery."
- "I dislike endings where the protagonist wins by coincidence."
- "I want overwhelming power without eliminating strategic tension."

This is evidence about **the author's values**, not about mass audiences.

### 3.2 Cultural / audience evidence

Examples:

- a work sold unusually well;
- a genre is growing;
- a trope is frequently discussed;
- a criticism recurs across many audience discussions;
- a particular promise appears repeatedly in successful works.

This is evidence about **observed reception or market behavior**, subject to
source quality, sampling bias, marketing/distribution effects, timing, franchise
effects, and other confounds.

Popularity does not prove that one narrative mechanism caused the popularity.

### 3.3 Craft interpretation

Examples:

- "The hidden-school premise probably lowers entry friction by giving the
  audience an understandable institutional frame."
- "Readers may be reacting to lost suspense rather than to protagonist power
  itself."
- "The complaint that side characters became irrelevant may reflect a single
  competence hierarchy swallowing previously distinct contribution domains."

These are **interpretations or heuristics**, not direct audience facts.

The required separation is:

    OBSERVATION
    "This work is popular."

    INTERPRETATION
    "This mechanism may contribute to the appeal."

    DESIGN MOVE
    "Try a new story that preserves the mechanism while changing its expression."

One must not be silently promoted into another.

## 4. Recommended epistemic labels

A Story Opportunity artifact or analysis should distinguish at least:

- **AUTHOR_PREFERENCE** — explicitly supplied author taste or constraint;
- **SOURCE_OBSERVATION** — a fact or bounded observation about a reference work;
- **AUDIENCE_EVIDENCE** — sourced reception, popularity, review, or market signal;
- **CRITIQUE_CLAIM** — a criticism reported by an identifiable source or sample;
- **CRAFT_INTERPRETATION** — an explanation of why something may work or fail;
- **CRAFT_HEURISTIC** — a reusable design rule or recommendation;
- **PROJECT_EVIDENCE** — evidence from the current story/project;
- **UNKNOWN** — unresolved or unsourced.

These labels are epistemic provenance. They are not numerical confidence scores.

## 5. Influence decomposition

When an author supplies a reference such as "I like Work X," the agent should
not immediately copy plot ingredients.

Instead, decompose the attraction.

### Step A — identify the stated attraction

Ask or infer conservatively:

- character dynamic?
- emotional experience?
- world premise?
- progression system?
- mystery structure?
- aesthetic?
- social fantasy?
- institutional structure?
- pacing?
- theme?
- climax mechanism?

If the author does not know why they like it, treat that as a valid discovery
problem rather than inventing certainty.

### Step B — identify the audience experience

Examples:

- escalating competence;
- paranoid uncertainty;
- romantic yearning;
- belonging to a prestigious institution;
- awe at hidden scale;
- fear of losing social status;
- pleasure of collection;
- repeated recontextualization;
- cathartic vindication.

### Step C — identify the mechanism

Examples:

- rivalry supplies recurring comparison pressure;
- information is revealed in layers that invalidate earlier explanations;
- a collection loop gives visible acquisition progress;
- institutions create persistent status and obligation structures;
- capabilities are specialized so characters retain non-substitutable value;
- failure changes the protagonist's information state.

### Step D — identify tradeoffs

Every mechanism has costs.

Examples:

- escalating power may reduce physical uncertainty;
- mystery withholding may weaken character intimacy;
- large casts may dilute attachment;
- strict progression may make outcomes feel mechanical;
- institutional complexity may increase cognitive load.

### Step E — abstract away the source expression

A portable mechanism should survive a change of:

- names;
- setting;
- lore;
- character identities;
- scene sequence;
- distinctive objects;
- terminology;
- prose;
- exact relationship expression.

If the "mechanism" disappears when those surface elements are removed, it is
probably still too close to source expression.

## 6. Criticism interpretation

Criticism is evidence to analyze, not a prescription to obey.

A complaint such as:

> "The protagonist is too powerful."

can represent several different underlying failures:

    power itself is unwanted
    OR
    outcomes became predictable
    OR
    antagonists lost meaningful leverage
    OR
    power has no cost
    OR
    supporting characters lost relevance
    OR
    the story kept using physical conflict after physical conflict stopped
    carrying uncertainty

Only the first interpretation implies "make the protagonist weaker."

The agent should therefore process criticism through:

    literal complaint
    -> possible failed expectation
    -> candidate causal mechanisms
    -> intentional tradeoff check
    -> preservation constraint
    -> alternative design responses

### Criticism-analysis questions

For each criticism:

1. What exactly is being complained about?
2. Is the complaint isolated or recurrent in the available evidence?
3. What audience expectation may have failed?
4. What mechanism could have caused that failure?
5. Is the criticized property also responsible for some of the work's appeal?
6. Would "fixing" the complaint destroy the desirable experience?
7. Can the value be preserved while the failure mode is moved elsewhere?
8. What alternative explanations remain plausible?

### Example

    Critique:
    "Side characters stop mattering."

    Possible mechanism:
    combat-power escalation becomes the only consequential competence axis.

    Preserve:
    progression fantasy and protagonist growth.

    Transform:
    important conflicts increasingly depend on information, legitimacy,
    relationships, logistics, institutional access, or specialized expertise.

    Opportunity:
    a progression system where advancement increases power while simultaneously
    expanding the number of non-substitutable strategic domains.

This is stronger than simply reducing the protagonist's power.

## 7. Popularity-informed opportunity discovery

An author may deliberately start from successful works or popular genre
patterns.

That is a valid form of creative and product discovery, but the inference must
remain bounded.

Use:

    observed popularity / repeated demand
    -> identify plausible audience promise
    -> identify mechanisms that may deliver it
    -> inspect recurring tradeoffs and complaints
    -> preserve the promise
    -> alter the mechanism or combination
    -> generate distinct opportunities

Do not use:

    popular story
    -> copy plot
    -> fix review complaints
    -> guaranteed better story

Popularity can reflect factors unrelated to narrative architecture, including:

- marketing;
- distribution;
- existing franchise strength;
- platform placement;
- production quality;
- star power;
- timing;
- social identity;
- network effects;
- adaptation exposure.

Story Opportunity Discovery may use popularity as **opportunity evidence**, not
as causal proof.

---

## 8. Preference-driven opportunity discovery

The author may have no market goal at all.

A valid input can simply be:

> "I love these things."

Example:

    hidden civilizations
    + identity mystery
    + competent ideological rivals
    + civilization-scale stakes
    + intimate romantic rivalry

The task is then to search for combinations where these attractions reinforce
one another causally.

The agent should ask:

- Which element can become the governing engine?
- Which elements are compatible as supporting mechanisms?
- Which combinations create collisions rather than mere coexistence?
- Which element supplies escalation?
- Which supplies emotional attachment?
- Which supplies uncertainty?
- Which supplies differentiation?
- What must be omitted to avoid incoherent maximalism?

The output is still a story opportunity, not a canonical Story Identity.

## 9. Story Opportunity synthesis

A useful synthesis should state four things explicitly:

### KEEP

Which experience or mechanism is deliberately retained?

### AVOID

Which failure mode or tradeoff should not be reproduced?

### TRANSFORM

How will the retained mechanism work differently here?

### DIFFERENTIATE

Why would the resulting major scenes, choices, pressures, or climax mechanics be
different from the reference works?

Example:

    KEEP:
    visible progression

    KEEP:
    large persistent cast

    AVOID:
    growth making everyone except the protagonist narratively irrelevant

    TRANSFORM:
    progression expands access to specialized domains instead of collapsing all
    value into combat rank

    DIFFERENTIATE:
    the protagonist becomes physically dominant early, while later victories
    depend on allies whose information, legitimacy, logistics, and social access
    cannot be substituted by raw power

## 10. Candidate Story Opportunity contract

A future executable feature may use a shape like the following. This is a
**documentation hypothesis**, not a frozen API.

    story_opportunity:
      title: "Knowledge Is the Progression"

      author_preferences:
        - statement: "I want visible competence progression."
          basis: AUTHOR_PREFERENCE
        - statement: "I want supporting characters to remain important."
          basis: AUTHOR_PREFERENCE

      influences:
        - source_label: "Reference A"
          admired_surface: "Repeated revelations"
          desired_experience: "Earlier scenes keep changing meaning"
          portable_mechanism: "Layered revelation recontextualizes prior evidence"
          basis: CRAFT_INTERPRETATION

        - source_label: "Reference B"
          admired_surface: "Failure loops"
          desired_experience: "Learning feels earned"
          portable_mechanism: "Failure produces durable strategic information"
          basis: CRAFT_INTERPRETATION

      audience_evidence:
        - observation: >
            Progression stories show sustained audience demand in the selected
            market.
          source_ref: "<external evidence ref>"
          basis: AUDIENCE_EVIDENCE

      critique_inputs:
        - claim: "Late power scaling makes secondary characters irrelevant."
          source_ref: "<review/discussion ref>"
          basis: CRITIQUE_CLAIM
          interpretation: >
            A single escalating competence hierarchy may be swallowing
            non-protagonist contribution domains.
          interpretation_basis: CRAFT_INTERPRETATION

      preserve:
        - "visible advancement"
        - "persistent ensemble"

      avoid:
        - "all consequential problems reducing to combat rank"

      synthesis: >
        A progression mystery where advancement grants access to deeper causal
        knowledge and institutions, while major allies retain exclusive
        competence domains that raw power cannot replace.

      differentiators:
        - "knowledge progression changes what problems can be perceived"
        - "allies retain non-substitutable strategic domains"
        - "climax requires accumulated causal understanding, not maximum force"

      narrative_runway:
        opportunities:
          - "successively deeper institutional layers"
          - "alliances become more valuable as the protagonist grows"
        risks:
          - "information complexity may outpace emotional attachment"

      unresolved_questions:
        - "setting"
        - "protagonist identity"
        - "specific central conflict"

      authority_status: "WORKING / NOT ACCEPTED"

The durable principle is more important than the eventual field names.

## 11. Candidate generation

Generate multiple opportunities that differ **causally**, not merely
cosmetically.

Useful diversity dimensions include:

- governing conflict;
- protagonist action pattern;
- causal owner;
- information architecture;
- progression mechanism;
- relationship engine;
- institutional pressure;
- reversal mechanism;
- climax mechanic;
- cost structure.

A candidate is not meaningfully distinct merely because it changes:

- names;
- setting skin;
- visual aesthetic;
- title;
- superficial trope labels.

Practical test:

> If selecting Candidate B would not materially change the major actions,
> pressures, reversals, or climax the author later writes, the opportunity set is
> probably not diverse enough.

This intentionally mirrors Story Discovery's causal-distinctness rule while
operating one stage earlier.

---

## 12. Opportunity comparison and selection

Story Opportunity Discovery may compare opportunities, but it must not invent a
universal "best idea" score.

Useful comparison dimensions include:

- **author desire** — how strongly the opportunity expresses declared taste;
- **intended audience promise** — what experience the opportunity appears able
  to support;
- **differentiation** — whether the causal design is meaningfully distinct;
- **narrative runway** — whether it can sustain escalation and development;
- **mechanism coherence** — whether the combined mechanisms reinforce rather
  than fight one another;
- **execution burden** — what complexity the author must actually realize;
- **known tradeoffs** — what desirable properties may be lost;
- **evidence quality** — which claims are preference, sourced observation,
  interpretation, or unknown.

Do not collapse these into a weighted numerical score by default.

A comparison should expose tradeoffs such as:

    Opportunity A:
    closer to declared author taste; lower conceptual novelty; clear long-form
    progression.

    Opportunity B:
    stronger differentiation; higher information complexity; less certain
    emotional center.

    Opportunity C:
    clearest audience promise; greater risk of resembling the reference works
    unless the climax mechanism is changed.

The final selection remains an author or delegated-owner decision under the
applicable authority policy.

## 13. Boundary with Story Discovery

Story Opportunity Discovery and Story Discovery are complementary.

### Story Opportunity Discovery

Input:

- tastes;
- influences;
- criticisms;
- audience/market evidence;
- genre opportunities;
- partially formed desires.

Question:

> "What story might be worth developing?"

Output:

- candidate opportunity;
- candidate premise;
- opportunity constraints;
- differentiators;
- unresolved questions.

### Story Discovery

Input:

- a premise or structured Discovery Brief.

Question:

> "What causally distinct story directions could this become?"

Output:

- candidate Story Identities / narrative architectures;
- comparative evidence;
- advisory recommendation;
- explicit acceptance boundary.

The handoff may include the optional Premise Fitness step:

    Story Opportunity
    -> candidate premise / brief
    -> Premise Fitness
    -> scope / genre / cost tradeoffs
    -> selected working premise / brief
    -> Story Discovery

Premise Fitness asks whether a premise is fit for its intended genre, experience,
scope, complexity, and posture; it does not decide universal idea quality. See
[Premise Fitness](premise-fitness.md).

Story Opportunity Discovery must not bypass Story Discovery by silently turning
a synthesized opportunity into accepted Story Identity.

---

## 14. Boundary with Story Lens

Story Lens interprets a supplied premise.

Story Opportunity Discovery may operate **before a stable premise exists**.

    Story Opportunity Discovery
    "What should I make?"

    Story Lens
    "What does this premise already suggest?"

    Story Discovery
    "What distinct story directions could this premise become?"

No Story Opportunity artifact should be presented as though Story Lens had
derived it from the premise.

## 15. Boundary with MANA

Mass-Appeal Narrative Architecture (MANA), when available, serves a different
function.

Story Opportunity Discovery works primarily from the **input/search side**:

> What candidate narrative opportunity is promising enough to explore?

MANA works from the **audience-effect side**:

> What audience-facing effects does a candidate narrative architecture appear to
> support, given the evidence currently available?

Potential future composition:

    preferences + references + audience evidence
                    |
                    v
         Story Opportunity Discovery
                    |
                    v
            candidate opportunities
                    |
                    v
              Story Discovery
                    |
                    v
        candidate narrative architectures
                    |
                    v
                   MANA
                    |
                    v
       audience-effect tradeoff evidence
                    |
                    v
            explicit human selection

MANA must inform comparison rather than become an automatic winner function.

This document does not depend on the MANA implementation being merged or
available.

## 16. Boundary with existing craft systems

Story Design Packs, Genre Packs, Tutor guidance, Structure diagnostics, and
other craft systems teach or evaluate **how a selected story can work**.

Story Opportunity Discovery may consume reusable craft knowledge, but it should
not duplicate those systems.

Examples:

    Story Opportunity Discovery:
    "Combine collection pleasure with relationship-dependent acquisition."

    Story Design Pack:
    "What design principles make a companion-collection system emotionally
    durable?"

    Story Discovery:
    "What complete story engines can realize the selected opportunity?"

    Structure:
    "How should pressure, reversals, and payoff be sequenced?"

## 17. Coding-agent operating protocol

A coding or narrative agent can follow this method even when its model already
contains broad cultural knowledge.

### Step 0 — establish the evidence boundary

For every reference or criticism, determine whether it is:

- supplied directly by the author;
- present in repository knowledge;
- sourced from current external research;
- remembered from model training / latent knowledge only;
- unknown.

Latent model knowledge may seed **hypotheses**. It must not be presented as
current market evidence or audience consensus without a source appropriate to
the claim.

### Step 1 — collect author attractions

Represent what the author likes or dislikes without immediately explaining it
away.

### Step 2 — decompose references

For each influence:

    surface attraction
    -> desired experience
    -> candidate mechanism
    -> tradeoff
    -> portable abstraction

### Step 3 — interpret criticism

For each criticism:

    literal complaint
    -> failed expectation hypotheses
    -> causal hypotheses
    -> intentional-tradeoff check
    -> possible design responses

### Step 4 — separate evidence types

Never convert:

    "I liked this"

into:

    "audiences prefer this"

or:

    "this work was popular"

into:

    "this mechanism caused the popularity"

### Step 5 — synthesize mechanisms

Combine mechanisms, not merely nouns.

Weak:

    wizard school + time loop + political houses

Stronger:

    institutional belonging
    + failure-generated information
    + multi-actor incompatible goals

### Step 6 — differentiate causally

Require candidate opportunities to imply materially different major actions,
pressures, reversals, or climax mechanics.

### Step 7 — expose tradeoffs

Do not hide what each synthesis sacrifices.

### Step 8 — produce noncanonical opportunities

Output working opportunities, premises, constraints, and unresolved questions.

### Step 9 — hand off to Story Discovery

Do not directly accept Story Identity.

## 18. How an agent should use prior knowledge

Capable coding agents often already know a great deal about:

- famous stories;
- genre conventions;
- common tropes;
- broad critical interpretations;
- frequently discussed audience complaints;
- narrative theory.

That knowledge is useful as **search priors and hypotheses**.

It is not sufficient by itself for claims such as:

- "this is the current consensus";
- "audiences generally hated this";
- "this trope is currently rising";
- "this specific criticism is widespread";
- "this work succeeded because of mechanism X."

For changing or source-sensitive claims, the agent should use appropriate current
research when tools and authority permit.

If current research is unavailable, the correct output is:

    hypothesis / remembered pattern
    + explicit uncertainty

not fabricated source certainty.

---

## 19. Copyright, imitation, and differentiation boundary

Story Opportunity Discovery is an abstraction workflow, not an imitation
workflow.

The agent should prefer:

- mechanisms;
- functional relationships;
- audience experiences;
- structural tradeoffs;
- generic genre conventions;
- high-level design principles.

The agent should avoid treating distinctive source expression as a reusable
design component, including:

- copied prose;
- copied scene sequences;
- unique terminology;
- source-specific lore;
- distinctive character identities;
- near-identical signature situations;
- trivial renaming of protected expression.

The goal is:

    learn from why something works
    !=
    reproduce what the source specifically expressed

---

## 20. Relationship to Auteur authority

All Story Opportunity outputs are noncanonical.

They may be:

- inspected;
- compared;
- revised;
- combined;
- discarded;
- converted into candidate premises or Discovery Briefs.

They may not silently:

- create accepted Story Identity;
- modify Structure;
- alter accepted project state;
- become "author preference" merely because an agent generated them;
- become an automatic commercial recommendation.

Recommended authority label:

    WORKING / NOT ACCEPTED

Existing Story Discovery and Story Identity acceptance remain authoritative.

## 21. Implementation posture

The current selected responsibility is **documentation-first**.

Implement now:

- this product/design contract;
- architecture placement;
- boundary with Story Discovery;
- evidence/epistemic rules;
- coding-agent operating protocol;
- noncanonical opportunity-artifact hypothesis.

Do **not** implement yet:

- third-party story scraping;
- automatic review mining;
- a market-data service;
- a giant hardcoded encyclopedia of story criticisms;
- automatic MANA scoring;
- automatic Story Opportunity winner selection;
- a new canonical artifact;
- a new semantic layer;
- a new Beginner first-screen step.

A future runtime is warranted only when actual use shows that authors or coding
agents need durable opportunity state or repeated automated synthesis beyond
what a documented method already provides.

---

## 22. Anti-patterns

Do not:

- treat "popular" as synonymous with "well crafted";
- treat one criticism as consensus;
- treat criticism as an instruction;
- optimize away the feature that created the original appeal;
- copy surface content when a mechanism can be abstracted;
- confuse author taste with audience evidence;
- confuse audience evidence with causal explanation;
- confuse craft interpretation with scientific fact;
- generate cosmetic candidate variants and call them distinct opportunities;
- convert a working opportunity directly into canon;
- add numerical "idea quality" scores without a claim-appropriate future need;
- use model memory as though it were current sourced market research.

---

## 23. Design principle

Story Opportunity Discovery should help an author or agent move from:

> "I like these things, I notice these patterns, and I see these weaknesses."

to:

> "Here are several causally distinct stories I might genuinely want to build,
> why each exists, what it preserves, what it transforms, and what remains
> uncertain."

It should never pretend that creativity can be reduced to:

> "copy what is popular and repair the reviews."

The correct abstraction is:

    taste
    + evidence
    + interpretation
    + abstraction
    + recombination
    + differentiation
    -> candidate story opportunity
